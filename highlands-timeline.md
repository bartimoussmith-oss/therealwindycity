# The Highlands Annexation — Complete Procedural Timeline
## PUDC-26-84 (Annexation) · PUDC-26-85 (Zone Change to BP) · PUDC-26-116 (FLUM Amendment)
### Cheyenne Planning Commission & Governing Body, April – September 2026

*Compiled from: City of Cheyenne case portal (Connect Cheyenne), Granicus agendas/minutes/staff memos, and press coverage (Wyoming Tribune Eagle via News Letter Journal, Gillette News Record/Wyoming News, Cap City News, County17, Cowboy State Daily). Names normalized; spelling of Microsoft's Rachel Irving varies by source (Irwin/Irving/Irwing).*

## The project at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Property** | 3,459.99 acres east of South Greeley Highway, south of the Sweetgrass subdivision ("The Highlands") |
| **Seller** | The Lummis family (family of U.S. Sen. Cynthia Lummis); sale to Microsoft closed by mid-July 2026 |
| **Buyer/developer** | Microsoft — long-term data-center campus, phased over 10–20 years |
| **Scale** | ~20% of the pre-annexation land area of the City of Cheyenne; one of the largest single annexations in city history |
| **Case numbers** | PUDC-26-84 (annexation) · PUDC-26-85 (zone change AG→BP, ~3,340.7 of the 3,460 acres) · PUDC-26-116 (future land use map amendment + urban service boundary) |
| **Case planner** | Connor White, Planning & Development (director: Charles Bloom) |
| **Applicant's agent** | Brad Emmons / AVI (the firm Miller later targets on conflict-of-interest grounds) |

**The statutory machinery (why so many meetings):** Wyoming law requires a W.S. §15-1-402 compliance public hearing; Cheyenne practice runs ordinances through three readings by the Governing Body, shuttling each reading through the **Public Services Committee (PSC)** between council votes, with the **Planning Commission (PC)** reviewing the assigned zoning and the comprehensive-plan (FLUM) amendment. A FLUM amendment needs a majority of the PC's *full* membership (4 of 7); anything less is a disapproval the council can only overcome by overruling the PC under **W.S. §15-1-506** — which is exactly what happened here.

## The spine: June 1 → July 13

### ① June 1, 2026 — Planning Commission (regular meeting)
*The quiet derailment that shaped everything after it.*

- PC considers the Highlands package: annexation report, **assigned AG–Agricultural zoning**, the **zone change to BP** (PUDC-26-85), and the **FLUM amendment** (PUDC-26-116: Agricultural/Rural + Urban Residential → Mixed-Use Employment, plus urban service boundary expansion).
- **FLUM amendment: deemed disapproved.** Only 5 of 7 commissioners present; the result was 3–2 *in favor* — but state law requires 4 yes-votes of the full board for a plan amendment, so it exited as a **negative recommendation** (staff memo: "voted 3-2 against"; planning director Bloom later explains the quorum arithmetic at the July 13 meeting). This is the failure items 19–20 later "overrule."
- **BP zone change (PUDC-26-85): PC formally recommends DENIAL.** With the FLUM deemed disapproved, staff flipped to recommending denial, and the Commission adopted the denial recommendation on a voice-roll vote. On July 13, when Miller asks whether the council has "overruled your planning commission officially," the dais answers that item 15's passage *"was"* the overrule, with items 19–20 to follow.
- **Assigned AG zoning (item H): recommended approval 3–0** — the only Highlands item the PC endorsed.
- **Reed Land Addition (PUDC-26-121, 387 acres AG→BP south of I-80, also Microsoft):** PC **explicitly recommends denial** on June 1 (per Miller's July 13 account, uncontradicted) — the council approves it anyway on July 13, 8–1.
- *Same era, same dynamics:* the PC's thin attendance is a recurring theme — with commissioners Tripp and Hibben absent around this period, a single dissent was enough to sink a different 98-acre annexation endorsement, and that developer later rescinded entirely (see Parallel Threads).

### ② June 8, 2026 — Governing Body (regular meeting)
*Public hearing + introduction.*

- **W.S. §15-1-402 compliance hearing** for the 3,459.99 acres — hours of testimony; **no vote is taken at the hearing itself**.
- Mayor Collins, repeatedly: the hearing is about annexation criteria, not future development — *"Annexation doesn't do anything but change the color of the map."*
- **Councilman Wolfe** presses Planning Director Bloom on annexing in phases; Bloom: state law draws no small/large distinction, staff evaluate only serviceability and statutory criteria.
- **Heather Madrid** (petition organizer): *"The math isn't mathing… how is this protecting the health, safety and welfare if people don't live there yet?"*
- **Microsoft** — Rachel Irving (land development & permitting): the parcel is a long-term expansion area, phased **10–20 years**; *"Large contiguous parcels… are increasingly rare."* Lucas Downing (community affairs): $4.7M donated to Wyoming nonprofits since 2018.
- **Introduction (first readings) of the ordinance set:** annexation (agenda item 22), assigned AG zoning (item 23), and the applicant-requested **BP zone change** — per planner Connor White, all "for introduction" this night. (Also on this agenda's final approvals: the separate 1,652.6-acre Cheyenne/Campstool business-park rezoning — see Parallel Threads.)

### ③ June 15, 2026 — Public Services Committee (noon, Council Chambers)
**Video:** [PSC 06-15-26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e8pByyWP7RE) (4:24:48) — full transcript; **Miller present all afternoon, 10 sync-indexed turns** (his first: a "formal audit" of a *gaming-overlay* ordinance, citing a "veracity index" of the June 8 transcript).
*The committee clears the runway.*

- PSC **recommends approval** of the Highlands annexation, the AG assigned zoning, and the BP zone change (~3,000 of the 3,460 acres) — advancing all to second reading June 22 (on the video, the BP item carries **2–1 with Laybourn the lone no**). Also advances the **394-acre Swan Ranch Rail Park rezone** and the **387-acre Reed Land Addition rezone**.
- **The Southwest Drive MUB→LI package dies here** (items 25, 36, 37) — pre-scripted no-second after the developer's withdrawal request, the circuit's only killed package; and the "Cheyenne 98" annexation is rescinded by its developer the same afternoon (per Cap City).
- **Laybourn is the lone committee dissent**; the majority's stated rationale — property rights trump land-use concerns so long as code is met.
- **FLUM items postponed two weeks** (to July 6 PSC): the map can't be updated until the annexation/zoning are final — sequencing that later funnels everything into the July 13 endgame.

### ④ June 22, 2026 — Governing Body (second readings, ~5½-hour meeting)
*The first floor fight.*

- **Highlands annexation approved on second reading**, subject to final reading July 13.
- **Wolfe–Laybourn motion: postpone four months AND require Microsoft to pay $50 million over five years** for public improvements (community-benefits agreement). **Fails 7–3** (only Wolfe, Laybourn, Moody in favor).
- Also approved on second reading: the **Reed 387-acre BP rezone** ("400-acre parcel south of I-80") and the **Swan Ranch BP expansion** — the full Microsoft/BP cluster moving in lockstep.
- **Public testimony:** Irving — *"At its core, annexation is about planning."* **Charles Miller (remote)** — the council is enabling *"premeditated regulatory arbitrage"* and *"granting municipal jurisdiction to a 3,500-acre ghost project… advancing this blank check while ignoring catastrophic environmental warnings"* (water and electricity). Elizabeth Marvin — the *"If You Give a Mouse a Cookie"* analogy. Trades and Microsoft workers (Mullikan, Agosta) counter with jobs/stability testimony.

### ⑤ July 6, 2026 — Public Services Committee
*Wolfe's compromise dies in committee.*

- PSC **recommends approval of everything for third/final reading** (annexation, AG zoning, BP zone change, and the FLUM resolutions).
- **Wolfe (not a committee member) brings a "compromise":** don't delay the approval — just push the **effective date to year-end**, during which no groundwork could begin, preserving time to negotiate benefits. Councilman Rinne: *"Didn't we just give away our leverage?"* Wolfe concedes the point but argues the council could extend again in November. LEADS CEO Betsy Hale discusses voluntary infrastructure contributions growing as plans firm up. **Committee rejects it; Laybourn the only yes.**

### ⑥ July 13, 2026 — Governing Body (final consideration; 7 hr 44 min; adjourned 1:41 a.m.)
*The night this project has been traced through in the companion documents.*

| Item | Action | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 13 | Highlands annexation, 3rd reading | **Approved** — after Moody's amend effective date to Jan 25, 2027 (**failed**) and his move to recommitt to PSC in January (**failed**) |
| 14 | Assigned AG zoning (holding zone, all 3,460 ac) | **Approved 6–3** (Wolfe, Laybourn, Moody no) |
| 15 | Zone change AG→BP, 3,340.7 ac (PUDC-26-85) | **Approved 6–3** (same) — the effective overrule of the PC's non-recommendation |
| 16 | Reed Land Addition 387 ac AG→BP (PUDC-26-121) | **Approved 8–1** (Moody no) — overruling the PC's June 1 denial recommendation |
| 19 | Resolution overruling PC disapproval of FLUM amendment (W.S. §15-1-506) (PUDC-26-116) | **Adopted 6–3** (same three no) |
| 20 | Resolution amending the Future Land Use Map / urban service boundary | **Adopted 6–3** (same three no) |
| — | Items 17–18: *next* Roundtop/Happy Jack annexation + BP zoning, 1st reading | Referred to PSC (the pipeline's next cluster) |

- Effective date: **upon publication** (the Moody delay having failed) — the Highlands entered the city in late July 2026, ending a roughly **two-month, six-meeting** gauntlet that press described as "volleying between the City Council and its Public Services Committee, with each meeting lasting hours."
- The 500-acre AG buffer between Sweetgrass and the BP zone survives (confirmed by staff at the meeting).
- Miller's 19 speaking turns this night are documented in `miller-comments-2026-07-13.md`.

## Pattern summary (the whole arc in one table)

| Date | Body | Highlands action | Anti-slowdown scoreboard |
|---|---|---|---|
| May 2026 | Governing Body | — | Moody 12-mo moratorium **fails** (Wolfe, Laybourn support) |
| Jun 1 | Planning Commission | FLUM 3–2 → **deemed disapproval**; BP zone change: no positive rec; Reed: **denial rec** | — (attendance does the damage) |
| Jun 8 | Governing Body | §15-1-402 hearing + **1st readings** | "Color of the map" framing adopted |
| Jun 15 | Public Services Cttee | **Recommends approval** (FLUM items punted 2 wks) | Laybourn lone no |
| Jun 22 | Governing Body | **2nd reading approved** | Wolfe/Laybourn 4-month + **$50M CBA motion fails 7–3** |
| Jul 6 | Public Services Cttee | **Recommends final approval** | Wolfe effective-date compromise **rejected** (Laybourn only yes) |
| Jul 13 | Governing Body | **3rd readings + overrules all approved**; adjourn 1:41 a.m. | Moody Jan-25-2027 date **fails**; recommittal **fails** |

**Net result:** six public bodies' worth of process, three failed slowdown attempts (plus the May moratorium = four), two planning-commission override votes, one 7-hour-44-minute finale — and a 3,500-acre annexation that took roughly three months from petition to publication. The only formal losses the project suffered anywhere in the chain were *inside* the Planning Commission, and the council overruled every one of them.

## Post-adoption epilogue (added 2026-08-23, from the user's mega_file research tail)
- **Ordinance No. 4687** = the Highlands annexation instrument; the BP rezoning passed as a separate ordinance (number still unidentified — PUDC-26-85 is the case file).
- **~Aug 3–7, 2026** — after ~3 weeks of signature collection, a **referendum petition on Ord. 4687** is submitted to the city clerk (W.S. 22-23-1005: 10% of qualified electors, 20 days from first publication).
- **Aug 10, 2026 — GB:** approves July 27 minutes; **Roundtop/Happy Jack annexation 3rd reading** on the agenda (the Cox Ranch cluster — postponed Apr 27 to Sept 14 — advancing); after a private executive session on the referendum petition, the council **votes to ask a court whether W.S. §15-1-409 (60-day annexation appeal) is the exclusive remedy, crowding out referendum**.
- **Deadlines:** §15-1-409(c) annexation appeal — **60 days from the ordinance's effective date** (= publication under §15-1-408, ≥20 business days after the §15-1-405(a) hearing, unless the ordinance states otherwise) → if first publication was ~July 18, the bar is **~mid-September 2026**. The 30-day APA theory (16-3-114) from the 12:30 a.m. July 14 vote effectively lapsed **~Aug 13**.
- **Unresolved:** first-publication date + publisher's affidavit; BP-rezoning ordinance number; approved-minutes memorialization date (City Clerk, 2101 O'Neil Ave.; Wyoming Tribune Eagle legal-notice archive).
- **~July 17–18** — Ord. 4687 first published (back-solved from the petition deadline; affidavit pending) → **§15-1-409(c) 60-day appeal bar ≈ Sept 16, 2026**.
- **Aug 3–7** — Referendum petition (organizer M. Lee Hasenauer; 2,739 needed; targeting 7,000; city-registered voters only) submitted; deadline Fri Aug 7.
- **July 27 GB** — Miller reappears (40 statements, video S49bb5GfUro): North Range 34-ac second readings; chair's "you've got to be prepared, sir."
- **Aug 10 GB** — Approves July 27 minutes; North Range 3rd readings (items 6–7); after exec session, **8–1 (Moody no)** vote for City Attorney Brodie to seek a Laramie County District Court declaratory judgment: Title 22 referendum vs. §15-1-409 appeal. Esquibel: a successful referendum only pushes the project to "the county with less regulation." Clerk continues signature count (prior counts: 90+ days).
- **Aug 14** — Secretary of State Chuck Gray's letter: don't "undermine the clear, statutory process laid out in W.S. 22-23-1005… let the people of Cheyenne have a voice." Mayor rebuts (Cap City 8/14–15).
- **Fall:** Sept 7 PSC (Cox Ranch 2nd reading) · Sept 14 GB (Cox Ranch report; appeal bar ~Sept 16) · Sept 28 GB (Cox Ranch final) · November referendum if certified.
