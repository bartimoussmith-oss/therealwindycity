Time is of the essence given the deadline for relo-
cation of the Municipal Courts by February 1, 
2018.  Therefore, we propose to get an “early 
start” on the Project by beginning work with the 
City on September 15th (about one month prior to 
the final execution/approval of the Design Services 
Agreement).  With this early start, we can assist 
with the CMAR selection process (have CMAR can-
didates selected for interviews), have completed 
our Scoping Meeting, and  have reviewed Concept 
Design options for the Temporary Relocation all 
prior to the October 11th date.  We will accomplish 
these tasks and refrain from sending our first in-
voice to the City until after our contract is fully-
executed.   
During the Scoping Meeting we wish to identify all 
the City’s objectives, both operational and finan-
cial, that they may be in addition to the primary 
purpose of relocation the Municipal Courts to 
2101 O’Neil.  
Our Design Team has already started to examine 
2101 O’Neil in terms of how and where the Addi-
tion(s) may be placed on the building, and how 
and where we might temporarily relocate the Mu-
nicipal Court.  Through our initial investigation, we 
are confident that our Design Team has a very use-
ful preliminary understanding of what it will take 
to meet, or exceed, the Court’s minimum needs in 
its Temporary Relocation.  Our Team will be very 
prepared to “hit the ground running”.   
As shown on the Municipal Court Temporary Relo-
cation (MCTR) Early Start Schedule on the follow-
ing page, we would also propose that the CMAR 
be allowed to have an “early start” on the Project 
prior to the approval and signing of its Agreement 
with the City.   If that arrangement would be ac-
ceptable, we would have about 2 weeks to work 
with the CMAR before we would complete the 
Construction Documents for the temporary reloca-
tion.   
In addition to advising the Design Team during the 
preparation of Design and Construction Docu-
ments for the temporary relocation, this “early 
start” by the CMAR could well have the added 
benefit of allowing the CMAR to have completed 
its Schematic Design Cost Estimate for the Addi-
tion|Renovation a day, or two, before the City 
would have to approve of the CMAR’s construc-
tion cost for the Temporary Remodel.  This way 
the City’s approval of the cost of construction for 
the Temporary Relocation is made in the context 
of the preliminary cost estimates of the Addi-
tion|Renovation 
The MCTR Early Start Schedule on the following 
page allows approximately 47 days for construc-
tion of the temporary relocation.  While this is an 
unusually short duration, our Design Team will 
provide a design that will not only strive to mini-
mize costs, but will also be possible to construct 
within this time frame.  And, the CMAR will have 
been advised of this Schedule prior to selection, 
and will not be surprised if it finds it has to staff 
the job more aggressively to allow completion by 
January 29th. 
The proposed schedule for your Addition and/or 
Renovation (A|R) Project is also provided in this 
Section.  The anticipated or desired end date of 
the overall A|R Project was not defined in the RFP, 
and the proposed A|R Schedule can be slightly 
modified to accommodate the City as needed. 
Our Design Team has extensive experience main-
taining and improving project schedules.  One of 
our methods is to expedite the Plan Review by 
working with those authorities early in the prelimi-
nary/schematic design.  They will be aware of our 
design intent before final submittal to ensure the 
design concept is agreeable prior to minor issues 
becoming roadblocks.

Mayor Orr opened a public hearing for the City of Cheyenne’s Annual Action Plan Fiscal 
Year 2018-2019 prepared by the Cheyenne Housing and Community Development Office.  
Deanne Widauf, Housing and Community Development, provided a brief overview of the history 
and purpose of the Community Development Block Grant program, noting the proposed plan is 
consistent with the 5-year Comprehensive Consolidated Plan, and stated the anticipated allocation 
funding amount is $398,442.00 with $1,000.00 in program income and $56,000.00 in recaptured 
funding. Ms. Widauf explained the Tier system being utilized and reviewed information on the 
grant application process; public notifications and opportunities for input, and role of the Advisory 
Council in reviewing and recommending projects to be funded.  She stated nine projects are being 
proposed and discussed the category type, funding amount and purpose for each project, and 
commented on program administration funding. Upon inquiry, Ms. Widauf advised the 
neighborhood involved in one of the projects (a street paving project) was located north of Dell 
Range, between Frontier Mall and Yellowstone Road.  [City Clerk’s note: Councilman Laybourn 
joined the meeting] Patrice Gapen, Housing and Community Development Advisory Council 
member, stated the number of applications submitted were more than what could be funded, and 

that the Advisory Council unanimously agreed on the projects to recommend.  Ms. Gapen also 
noted the area that will receive paved roads had previously been a county pocket.  Brief comment 
was made on county pockets.  There being no further comments or input and purpose of the hearing 
having been met, Mayor Orr closed the hearing.

ORDINANCE – 2nd READING – Amending Chapter 8.56, Noise Control, Section 
8.56.060, Permit for relief from designated levels, of Title 8, Health and Safety, of the Code of the 
City of Cheyenne, Wyoming.  Astrid, The Plains Hotel, expressed concern with vehicle noise 
levels and speeding in the area of The Plains Hotel, and questioned whether the proposed ordinance 
would negatively impact the situation.  Reporting for Finance Committee, Mr. Luna moved to 
approve on second reading, seconded by Mr. Cook.  Upon inquiry, Carol Intlekofer, City Clerk, 
explained the proposed ordinance is affiliated with an ordinance change last year which 
streamlined business licensing processes, and that a change to this City Code section was 
inadvertently omitted from the previous ordinance, noting the proposed change is essentially a 
housekeeping measure.  She further explained the ordinance’s effect will result in the Police Chief 
being authorized to determine conditions affiliated with a noise permit instead of the Mayor.  
Motion carried.  Voting “yes” – all members of the governing body present.

RESOLUTION – Acknowledging receipt of the Reed Avenue Rail Corridor Master Plan. 
Speaking in support of the Master Plan were: Vicki Dugger, Downtown Development Authority; 
Cari Cuffney (who also voiced support on behalf of Danielle and Mark Cullins who could not be 
present); Dave Teubner, Warehouse Twenty One; Andrew Rose, property co-owner in the West 
Edge vicinity; Amy Spieker; Affie Ellis, State Senator, District 8, and Astrid, owner, The Plains 
Hotel.  Comments included the following topics:  Master Plan’s importance to development of the 
West Edge area; financial investments by business property owners; types of businesses that may 
be interested in locating in the West Edge; investing in revitalization of the downtown as well as 
the West Edge, and difference between acknowledging receipt vs. adoption of the plan.  Tony 
Lucero, A & C Feed, voiced his concerns regarding the Master Plan and future access to property 
he owns along the rail corridor.  Reporting for Public Services Committee, Mr. Roybal moved to 
acknowledge receipt, seconded by Mr. Shanor.  Discussion followed on the 6th Penny ballot 
question that was approved for the Reed Avenue Corridor project; acknowledging receipt vs. 
adoption of the report; railroad easement that must be acquired, and moving forward with the 
Master Plan.  During discussion and upon inquiry, Sylvia Hackl, City Attorney, provided her 
interpretation of the difference between “acknowledging receipt” vs. “adoption” of the plan.  
William Powell Haley, property owner in the area, questioned the effect of a railroad easement 
relative to his property boundaries.  Providing information in response to inquiries were:  Tom 
Mason, Director, Metropolitan Planning Organization, noting the plan reflects a 35% preliminary 
design, and Troy Russ, Kimley-Horn and Associates, planning and design engineering consultants, 
who provided information on working with the railroad regarding an easement, and determining 
any private property access issues.  Mr. Laybourn moved to amend the pending motion from 
“acknowledging receipt” to “adopting”.  Motion died due to lack of a second.  Comment was made 
that funding for the project was in place through voter approval of the 6th Penny ballot question.  
Motion to acknowledge receipt carried.  Voting “yes” – all members of the governing body present. 
(#5924)
