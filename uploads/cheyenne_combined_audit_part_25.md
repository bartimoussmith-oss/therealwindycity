BACKGROUND
On February 2, 2021, Dan Hart Patrol (DHP), the earthworks contractor (contractor) for the Happy Jack
Landfill Cells 1 and 2 Construction and Cell 3 Excavation Earthworks Project (Project), submitted request
for information (RFI) 002 to notify the City of unanticipated increases in fuel costs.
On March 15, 2021
DHP submitted a letter from their counsel which accompanied with RFI 003 to provide further detail
regarding RFI 002. The letter stated:
“0 HP is requesting the per unit price be increased due to the unforeseen, uncontrollable and non
seasonal spike in the price of diesel fuel.
DHP is likely to incur a substantial loss if the per unit
prices are not adjusted to accountfor the extraordinary increase in fuel cost. Under Section 41 of
the Contract, the increase in dieselfuel cost resulted in a force majeure event because this is “an
occurrence beyond the control of the parties”... which “dissuade[s]
... the performance required
by” DHP due to the near certainty that DHP will lose money.”
Further discussions with DHP indicated prices for both diesel and gasoline escalated as the commodity
price for crude oil increased due to growth in demand and supply chain limitations. Specifically, the prices
escalated when the nationwide easing of Covid restrictions led to increased demand for all crude oil
derivatives and the closure of several Texas refineries, due to a severe winter storm, restricted supply of
refined products. The WTI Price for crude increased from $36.76 on September 8, 2021 to $61.35 on
April 21, 2021 and had a year to date high of $66.09 on March 8,2021.
A force majeure notice was not received from the suppliers. The suppliers have the ability to supply the
fuel but, as with any commodity, increased demand for the same or less supply increases costs.
A near doubling of the WTI Crude Price and consequential increase in diesel and gasoline prices is not a
fluctuation that DHP anticipated. The increase in prices is not part of the normal, summer seasonal price
increase. Rather, to date, the increase in prices occurred between the months of September 2020 and
April 2021.

Solid Waste Professionals of Wyoming (SWPW) evaluated several options for the City to consider
regarding this matter as described below.
OPTIONS
There are two options that the City has regarding DHPs request for a fuel price adjustment and include
taking no action or issuing a change order to compensate DHP for the unforeseeable fuel price increases
as described below:
Option 1
— No Action
In the event the City took no action, DHP has indicated they would claim force majeure as
described in Section 41 of the Agreement and cancel the agreement. If force majeure is valid, the
project would then need to be re-bid, which would result in further delays and additional project
costs. When the project was bid, the next lowest bidder’s bid was $879,465.72 less than DHP’s
bid.
Option 2
- Approve A Change Order to Allow for Fuel Price Increases
SWPW evaluated several methods to manage fuel price increases used by the Wyoming
Department of Transportation (WYDOT), Abandoned Mine Lands (AML) Program, and private
industry.
Based on our review of the previously described methods, a Fuel Escalation Force
Account could be incorporated into the contract via change order.
The following calculation
method was used to determine the force account amount and describes how fuel price increases
would be accounted for each month:
The off-road diesel price of $1.45 per gallon that was provided at the time of bidding will
be used as the base price. Should the average price of delivered red-dyed off-road diesel
vary by more than five percent (5.0%) from the base price ($1.38
- $1.52), the Contractor
(DHP) may request compensation related to that fuel price variance according to the
following conditions.
Upon submittal of the payment request, the Contractor shall
provide the Engineer copies of actual invoices for fuel delivered to and utilized on the site.
At the end of each month, the total of any adjustments, positive or negative will be
calculated.
If the average price of red-dyed diesel fuel does not vary by more than five
percent (5.0%) from the base price, including Wyoming Sales Tax, no adjustment in
Contract Price related to fuel prices shall be requested by the Contractor. Variances will
be calculated for each month and the total variance from the start of the project will be
calculated and agreed upon in writing. Adjustments will only be considered and allowed
for red-dyed
diesel fuel consumed
by earthmoving or off-road equipment.
No
adjustments will be considered for oils, greases, gasoline, and that portion of diesel fuel
consumed by light-duty vehicles or delivery costs.
Based on our discussions with several fuel suppliers, the cost of off-road diesel fuel is expected to
stabilize at $2.35 per gallon. In order to not incur multiple change orders due to rising fuel costs,
an assumed peak fuel price was set at $2.60 per gallon for purposes of determining a potential
change order value.

Geosynthetic prices increase due to spike in
polypropylene resin costs
Polypropylene (PP) resin and other plastic resins have experienced a rapid price
increase over the past three months, affecting the cost of geosynthetic products,
according to Fred C. Chuck, P.E., executive director of the Geosynthetic Materials
Association (GMA) at Industrial Fabrics Association International (IFAI). “Market
conditions continue to remain highly volatile, with resin availability and pricing
changing daily,” says Chuck.
According to a February 9 article in The Plastics Exchange Market Report, “At least
a half-dozen resin producers have experienced Force Majeure conditions, creating
severe shortages for both polyethylene and polypropylene resins. Consequently,
spot resin trading has been hyperactive as resin processors, many on supply
allocation, have flocked to the spot market as they scramble to procure material.”
The report continues, “Through the years, we have seen several hurricanes and
petrochemical/plant fires, tariffs, economic crisis, waves of new production,
sudden plant outages, numerous Force Majeure declarations and huge swings in
energy and feedstock costs, but none of the previous markets, resulting from
these sometimes catastrophic catalysts, compare to the extreme environment we
currently engage [in].” The combination of these events has accumulated over the
past two to three quarters, heavily impacting the industry.
Chuck further elaborates that the worldwide demand for personal protective
equipment (PPE) to shield against the COVID-19 outbreak has caused the demand
for spun-bond and needle punched nonwovens to increase dramatically. He notes,
“As the economy slowed, the number of vehicular miles driven around the world
has plummeted, causing oil refineries to scale back on production. This has had the
side effect of lowering production of propylene, the basic feedstock of the
geosynthetics raw material polypropylene.”

According to Lilli Manolis Sherman, senior editor of Plastics Technology,
“Polypropylene prices in December moved up a total of 14C/Ib, bC of which was
in step with propylene monomer prices and 4C went toward boosting supplier
margins. Moreover, suppliers were looking for another 5—6C/lb for January in
addition to any change in the monomer contract price.”
The Plastics Exchange Market Report also notes, “PP Copolymer has been the
scarcest of materials and soared $.16/lb in January, while homopolymer resins
climbed $.17/lb, catching up a penny to CoPP, which had built an extra $.04/lb
premium during the 2nd half of 2020. The rise in spot prices closely matched the
January contract increase.”
“Several geosynthetics manufactures are experiencing a reluctance on the part of
agencies and owners not accepting the price increase pass through from the
contractors, creating a pressure point in the industry,” notes Chuck, adding, “The
construction cycle operates with projects quoted in a ‘low-bid’ environment, but
do not require material procurement for months. In the past, distributors have
been able to hold pricing during the longer cycle as the resin price has been less
volatile.”
GMA and Geosynthetics magazine will continue to monitor this issue and will
provide future information to address the wider concerns of the GMA
membership.
