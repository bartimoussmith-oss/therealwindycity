RECOMMENDED MOTION: 
 
Move to approve the rezoning of a portion of the southwest quarter of Section 23, Township 14 North, 
Range 66 West, of the 6th P.M., Cheyenne, Wyoming from County A-2 Agricultural to MR Medium 
Density Residential as shown in the attached zone change map, noting that the project meets the review 
criteria for approval. 
 
APPLICABLE CITY CODE SECTION(S) AND PLANS: 
 
 
UDC 2.2.1 Zoning Map Amendment 
 
PlanCheyenne 
 
BACKGROUND: 
 
This zone change application proposes changing the zoning from County A-2 Agricultural to MR Medium 
Density Residential for 35.7 acres of vacant land to be developed as a single-unit residential potentially 
including single-family homes and duplexes, with associated streets and open areas.     
 
SURROUNDING LAND USE AND ZONING: 
 
 
ANALYSIS OF APPLICATION CONFORMANCE WITH REVIEW CRITERIA: 
 
Should the Planning Commission wish to recommend approval of the Zoning Map Amendment, the 
Commission must make the following findings required in UDC 2.2.1.d. A detailed analysis is below: 
 
1) The application is in accordance with the development policies and physical patterns 
identified in the Comprehensive Plan.  (UDC 2.2.1.d.1) 
 
PlanCheyenne encourages growth within the Urban Service Boundary and the maximization of 
existing infrastructure. This property is within the Urban Service Boundary and existing 
infrastructure exists in the neighborhoods to the south and west. Development will require 
infrastructure extensions and improvements.   
 
The Future Land Use Designation for this property is Urban Residential on the Future Land Use 
Map updated by Connect 2045. The optimal zoning for the proposed future land use designation 
is any of the residential zones, including the MR zone.

2) The application is compatible with nearby property, specifically considering existing zoning,

The existing zoning to the south and west is mostly MR and within the City limits of Cheyenne. 
The proposed MR zone would be consistent with the current City zoning. The Future Land Use 
Map, as updated by Connect 2045, encourages Urban Residential type development in this area. 
The MR zone is considered one of the optimal zones for Urban Residential style development.   
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 2. 
   
3) The suitability of the subject property for development according to the standards and uses of 
the existing zoning district, compared to the suitability of the subject property for 
development according to the standards and uses of the proposed zoning district (UDC 
2.2.1.d.3) 
  
The current County A-2 zone allows limited development options and is intended for low-
intensity uses. Generally, parcels in this zone district are not expected to connect to urban 
services. The proposed annexation and zone change to MR would allow the land to develop with 
urban-style residential uses with connections to urban services (such as water and sewer).  
 
In order to develop under City jurisdiction, a City zone is required. 
   
The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 3. 
 
4) The ability of the subject property and surrounding vicinity to be developed according to the 
Subdivision Standards in Article 4 considering the proposed zoning (UDC 2.2.1.d.4) 
 
The MR zoning allows larger blocks with anticipated residential development. The subject 
property is topographically challenging, and areas likely would qualify for the natural feature 
exception when platted. The approved Preliminary Plat for this area  
 single-family  
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 4. 
 
5) The ability of the application to support the Intent and Applicability statements of the 
proposed zoning district and achieve the Design Objectives of any specific standards. (UDC 
2.2.1.d.5) 
 
The MR zone is intended to develop with primarily residential uses, including a mix of density 
and dwelling types within a neighborhood. Full access to public infrastructure and city services is 
expected. The MR zone is applicable to Urban Residential areas where more compact 
neighborhood development patterns are desired. The subject property will require the 
extension of City services, but such services are stubbed out to the property. The property is in 
an area designated Urban Residential.

BACKGROUND: 
 
The applicant is requesting annexing of 8,831 square feet of land to use as access for the Mission Village 
Senior Housing development. The County Assessor presently shows the tract of land as one small parcel. The 
land is currently undeveloped in the County. A zone change is proposed concurrently with this annexation. 
Both applications are anticipated to come to final consideration before the Governing Body at the same 
meeting.  
 
Land annexed to the City requires a City zone. The petition to annex has been accompanied by a submission 
to change the zoning from County MR Medium-density Residential to the City zone of MR Medium-density 
Residential.  
 
SURROUNDING LAND USE AND ZONING: 
 
 
The property is currently zoned County MR Medium-density Residential in the County. The property is 
concurrently proposed to be annexed and rezoned to the MR Medium-density Residential. The MR zone is 
generally described as a primarily residential zone with compact neighborhood development patterns. 
The district generally allows a variety of residential uses with some smaller-scale civic or commercial 
uses.      
 
ANALYSIS OF APPLICATION CONFORMANCE WITH REVIEW CRITERIA: 
 
To accept the petition to annex and approve the annexation Ordinance, the Governing Body must make 
the following findings required in WS 15-1-402; a detailed analysis of which is below: 
 
1) An annexation of the area is for the protection of the health, safety and welfare of the 
persons residing in the area and in the city or town.  [WS 15-1-402 (a) (i)] 
 
The annexation of this property will increase the health, safety, and welfare of the occupants of 
future development once it is built. By being in City limits, the property will receive access to 
City Fire and Police and sanitation services. The property will also be connected to City water 
and sanitary sewer instead of having wells and septic systems.

BACKGROUND: 
 
The applicant is requesting annexing of 81.8 acres of land for future residential and commercial 
development. The County Assessor presently shows the tract of land as part of one larger unplatted parcel. 
The land is currently undeveloped in the County. A zone change is proposed concurrently with this 
annexation. Both applications are anticipated to come to final consideration before the Governing Body at 
the same meeting.  
 
Land annexed to the City requires a City zone. The petition to annex has been accompanied by a submission 
to change the zoning from County A-2 Agricultural to the City zone of AG Agricultural. While the AG zone 
does not allow urban-style uses, it is appropriate as a holding zone until development plans are finalized. The 
jurisdiction prior to finalizing the development patterns. A preliminary plat for the area, with lots and blocks 
designed for residential and commercial development, was approved in August 2020. It is anticipated that a 
final plat and a rezoning application would come before the Governing Body within a year of this annexation. 
 
SURROUNDING LAND USE AND ZONING: 
 
 
The property is currently zoned A-2 Agricultural in the County. The property is concurrently proposed to 
be annexed and rezoned to the AG Agricultural. The AG zone is generally described as an agricultural or 
natural area zone, where most typical urban-style uses are prohibited.  The AG zone is applicable to 
areas where significant natural lands or open spaces are to be protected, or as an interim or holding 
zone for areas that may be further developed in the long term, but where minimal development activity 
in the interim is acceptable and will not prematurely establish development patterns.

ANALYSIS OF APPLICATION CONFORMANCE WITH REVIEW CRITERIA: 
 
To accept the petition to annex and approve the annexation Ordinance, the Governing Body must make 
the following findings required in WS 15-1-402; a detailed analysis of which is below: 
 
1) An annexation of the area is for the protection of the health, safety and welfare of the 
persons residing in the area and in the city or town.  [WS 15-1-402 (a) (i)] 
 
The annexation of this property will increase the health, safety, and welfare of the occupants of 
future development once it is built. By being in City limits, the property will receive access to 
City Fire and Police and sanitation services. The property will also be connected to City water 
and sanitary sewer instead of having wells and septic systems.   
 
The application complies with this review criterion. 
 
2) The urban development of the area sought to be annexed would constitute a natural, 
geographical, economical and social part of the annexing city or town. [WS 15-1-402 (a) (ii)] 
 
This property, once developed, will constitute a natural, geographic, economic, and social part 
of the city. The contiguity to the west and the proximity to urban-style development to the 
south makes the parcel a natural geographic part of the City. The development of this property 
will benefit those who live within the City and will be a part of the social and economic fabric 
that is Cheyenne.   
 
The application complies with this review criterion. 
   
3) The area sought to be annexed is a logical and feasible addition to the annexing city or town 
and the extension of basic and other services customarily available to residents of the city or 
town shall, within reason, be available to the area proposed to be annexed. [WS 15-1-402 (a) 
(iii)] 
 
The area is a logical and feasible addition to the City due to the reasons previously listed. All City 
services normally available to residents will also be made available. Police and fire protection 
will be extended upon annexation. City water and sewer lines exist in the vicinity and could be 
extended with the development of the property. 
 
The application complies with this review criterion. 
 
4) The area sought to be annexed is contiguous with or adjacent to the annexing city or town, or 
the area meets the requirements of W.S. 15-1-407. [WS 15-1-402 (a) (iv)] 
 
The area proposed for annexation are about 8.2% contiguous, along the western edge.   
 
The application complies with this review criterion.

Naming and Memorial Policy
It is the policy of the City of Cheyenne (City) that the naming of new and renaming of existing
facilities be reserved for exceptional circumstances and that the naming process comply with the
guidelines and procedures set forth in this policy.
Purpose:
These policies and procedures are intended to guide a) any individual or community group that is
interested in having a park, building, or major feature named for a significant person, event, or
place, b) any individual, group, or business that is interested in having their significant donation
(park, building, major feature) named, and c) decision makers. 
Definitions:
Parks. All traditional designed parks, natural open spaces, historic sites, golf courses,
specialized parks (e.g. Botanic Gardens, Skate Park), and trails under the department’s
jurisdiction or management 
Buildings. Significant structures that house City functions and programs
Major Features. Major, permanent components of City facilities, (e.g. ball fields, swimming
pools, tennis courts, playgrounds, amphitheaters, fountains, artwork or physical features lakes).
Rooms within Buildings are considered to be Major Features.
Amenities. Smaller furnishings and facilities (e.g. Benches, small fountains, tables, etc.)
Amenities are not formally named. Recognition for donated amenities is covered under the Gift
and Memorial Policy.
Naming or renaming City facilities is often complex as naming is a powerful and permanent
identity for a public place. Excessive naming of individual features and facilities can be
confusing to the public.
Approval of naming requests is a prestigious, cautious process that typically involves meeting
with the appropriate City Department, petition process and verification, public meetings held in
conjunction with governing body meetings, and resolution approval. 
The policy of the City is to reserve the naming or renaming of facilities to those circumstances
which tradition and practice have shown to best serve the interests of the City and assure a
worthy and enduring legacy for the City. To this end, the City supports consideration of naming
requests in the following three broad categories:
Exceptional Individuals - recognition of an exceptional City leader or a dedicated supporter of
the City

Historic Events, Places, and Persons – recognition of a major historical event or place or
person(s), local landmarks, prominent geographical locations of historic, social and cultural
significance
Major Gifts - recognition of community generosity in the form of gifts of time, skills, resources
and products, and money donated by public and private foundations, individuals, businesses 
General Principles. In considering any proposal to name or rename a Park, Building, or Major
Feature, the following will be considered: 
a) Lasting historical, cultural, and social significance 
b) Name engenders a strong and positive image
c) Name memorializes or commemorates people, places, or events of enduring importance to the
community or the nation
d) Name engenders significant ties of friendship and mutual recognition and support within the
community and/or with those outside of the community
e) Name is identified with some major achievement or the advancement of the public good
within the community or the nation
f) Name is particularly suitable for the park, building, or major feature based on the location,
history, or surrounding neighborhood
g) Name has symbolic value that transcends its ordinary meaning or use and enhances the
character and identity of the park, building, or major feature
h) Ensure any naming request that accompanies a corporate gift will not result in undue
commercialization 
Renaming Parks, Buildings, and Major Features. Proposals to rename facilities are not
encouraged and should be entertained only after fully investigating and considering the potential
impact of dropping the current name. Names that have become ingrained or widely accepted in
the community should not be abandoned unless there are compelling reasons and strong public
sentiment for doing so.
Historical or commonly-used place names should be preserved wherever possible.
Naming or Renaming for Exceptional Individuals. The following guidelines apply to naming
or renaming requests that result from either a community process or major gift.
Typically, naming rights are not conferred until the named person is deceased. Typically, such
naming after a person should not occur until the person has been deceased for at least two (2)
years and that person’s historical significance and good reputation have been secured in the
history and lore of the community and/or the nation.
Priority for naming facilities after deceased persons should be given in the following order to
those who have significant and lasting contributions:
·
to the City of Cheyenne; or
·
to the nation.
Naming Parks and Buildings after national or international figures should be rare and only upon
a substantial demonstration of the figure’s connection to or special importance in the Cheyenne
community or the State of Wyoming.
Naming of facilities after people or a group of people who perish in or survive a tragic event or
war should be considered only well after the public shock generated by the tragic event or war
has lessened.

Potential sites for such memorials should be focused on facilities that are more known for their
serene and contemplative nature rather than active recreational locations, such as playing fields
and recreation centers.
Emphasis should be placed on the contributions or heroic actions of these people during their
lifetime, rather than the circumstances of their death or survivorship.
  
Naming and Renaming for Historic Events, Places, and Persons. When a facility is located
near or otherwise associated with events, places, and people of historic, cultural, or social
significance, it is appropriate to consider naming such facilities after such events, places, and
people. The relationship of the facility to the events, places, and people of historic, cultural, or
social significance should be demonstrated through research and documentation. Such naming
will follow the public input and Council approval process. 
Naming and Renaming for Major Gifts. Typically, the donation of gifts to the City of
Cheyenne should be reward in itself, with recognition being given as set forth in the Gift Policy.
Occasionally, a gift will be made to the City that is of such magnitude and generosity that
naming of such new park or facility in honor of or at the request of the benefactor will be
considered. Any request to rename an existing park or facility that is associated with a major gift
to expand or refurbish that park or facility must meet the naming guidelines but is discouraged.
Threshold. As a guideline but not a limitation, the threshold for naming rights on parks or
buildings would include one or preferably more of the following:
·
deeding to the City most if not all of the land on which the park or building to be named
will be situated 
·
payment of one-half or more of the capital costs of constructing a park or building to be
named (depending on the availability of matching funds or grants)
·
long-term endowment for the operation, repair and maintenance of the donated park or
building, 
·
provision of significant program costs for facilities that will serve City program and
facility needs
Likewise, as a guideline but not a limitation, the threshold for naming rights on major features
would include one or preferably more of the following:
·
payment of the capital costs for constructing and installing a major feature
·
some long-term endowment for the operation, repair and maintenance of the donated
major feature 
·
provision of significant program costs for any major feature giving rise to or supporting a
City program or operation
Other Requirements. In no case shall naming or renaming be considered unless the major gift
is deemed acceptable in accordance with the Gift Policy and allowable under the provisions of
City governance. 
 
Naming for Persons. Benefactors seeking naming rights for major gifts will be encouraged to
follow the guidelines herein. An exception will be considered on its own merits. A major feature

that has been donated or refurbished may be named for a living person(s) provided that said
person(s) is of good reputation.
Whatever contract accompanies the gift and naming rights should address all conditions
applying to the naming, including time limits for naming of features. However, in addition, the
City reserves the right to rename any park, building, or major feature if the person for whom it is
named turns out to be disreputable or subsequently acts in a disreputable way.
Naming for Entities. A corporation, association, and other legally created entity making a major
gift may request that the name of that entity be associated with the name of the park, building, or
major feature which is the subject of the major gift. Each request is evaluated on its own merits
by the City.
In making a recommendation for a corporate name, the reputation of the company and
community support should be considerations. Corporate logos, brands, insignias, may be used as
part of any name association or naming. No direct advertising is allowed. Renaming of any
existing park or building is discouraged even when associated with a major enhancement gift.
The City reserves the right to remove an entity name association on a park or building or to
rename a major feature if the entity turns out to be disreputable or subsequently acts in a
disreputable way.
Other Considerations.
Typically, a park should not be subdivided for the purposes of naming unless there are readily-
identifiable physical divisions (major roads, waterways, etc.) in the park and other compelling
reasons for having more than one name for a park. This limitation on naming a park should not
prevent giving a different name for a trail, building, or major feature located in or near the park.
However, care should be taken in giving a name to a trail, building, or major feature that is
different from the name of the park so that confusion is not caused for the users of the differently
named facilities.
Facilities that are held by the City through a short-term lease or use agreement or improvements
that have a limited life span or occupancy should not be named.
All signs on parks and buildings must meet the City’s or responsible Department’s graphic and
signage standards. No specialized signage will be displayed.
If a new park or building is completed and no suggestion for a name has come from the
community or in association with a gift, the City will recommend a name, using any organized
public process (i.e. nominations, contest, etc.) that they decide upon.

Gift Policy
It is the policy of City of Cheyenne (City) to actively encourage gifts that reinforce its mission
and core services. Acceptance and recognition of gifts (property, goods or cash) must comply
with the guidelines and procedures set forth in this policy.
Purpose: 
This policy is intended to help potential donors understand how the City decides whether to
accept a proposed gift and how that donor can be recognized and thanked; to guide decisions of
the City, elected officials, and staff.
The City has benefited from a rich legacy of community generosity. Over the decades Cheyenne
residents and businesses have given gifts of their time, materials, products, and money. Public
and private foundations have invested in the City. A donation can dramatically increase the
quality of a program, park, or building or make the difference during economically lean times.
The City encourages and facilitates public and private gifts, bequests, and such contributions that
enhance and support services, parks, buildings, programs, and goals of equity. The City wants to
assure donors that their gift will be well-maintained and recognized. These policies outline the
criteria for accepting gifts and recognizing donors.
Definitions
Gift: A gift is a freely given donation of property, goods, or cash to the City, preferably with no
expectation of return. If the gift is contingent upon a special request or restriction, the gift is
considered as being subject to a “condition”. Property can range from land and buildings to
smaller goods. Cash could include actual cash, discounts, or investments. Gifts may be donated
for a specific purpose or may be general in nature.
Naming Benefits. Naming is the permanent name associated with a park, building, or major
features within a park or building and is an exceptional way in which to recognize a substantial
gift. See the Naming & Memorial Policy.
Corporate Sponsorship. Corporate sponsorship differs from gifts; it is financial or inkind
support from a for-profit entity for a specific City event, project, program and site in exchange
for tangible and intangible marketing benefits to both the sponsor and the City. See the
Corporate Sponsorship Policy.
General Principles: The following principles are intended to help potential donors understand
the process and complexity for the City in accepting gifts.
a) The City works closely with each donor to provide meaningful recognition. The final decision
is made by the City and is guided by established recognition and signage guidelines and
applicable policies.  A variety of methods providing gift recognition is encouraged and naming
benefits are reserved for the most compelling contributors to the City or major donors.

b)  The City must have adequate operating budget and staffing to accept, implement, and
maintain the gift. Consequently, gifts that require levels of service, maintenance, or staffing
beyond current levels shall consider necessary funding for the operating budget. 
c)  Gifts are encouraged that help eliminate any disparity in the quality of facilities and services
across the City.
d)  In order to promote departmental priorities and the goal of equity, the City and its partners
will proactively offer potential donors a variety of meaningful and relevant gift programs or
ideas, such as tribute walls, bench programs, donor plaques and work to ensure wide geographic
distribution.
e.) Gifts must be given with the understanding that:
·
they become the property of the City and are subject to the laws, policies and procedures
that govern the City and the department.
·
the department is not obligated to replace the gift if it is stolen, vandalized, worn out,
irreparably damaged or destroyed or, in the case of plant material, dies.
·
gifts must bear no emblem of, or reference to, firearms, tobacco, alcohol, or sexually
explicit materials.
f.)  Once accepted, the City reserves the following rights with respect to the gift:
·
to remove or return any donated item
·
to determine where the improvement will be located and when it will be installed (unless
a specific location is agreed upon in advance)
·
to determine the life span of the improvement and to remove it when appropriate
·
to determine style and design of the improvement to fit City specifications, historic and
environmental context
·
to require the groups or individuals involved to meet City standards of liability and
insurance coverage if volunteer labor is used
Guidelines for Accepting Gifts
The City welcomes these generous gifts as an opportunity to enhance services as long as they are
consistent with City and departmental policies and regulations; respect the physical beauty of
public spaces; and reaffirm the City’s mission and core services. In examining any proposed
gifts, contemplate if the project could be built or happen otherwise, and consider the following
individually and collectively:
·
Gift is consistent with the mission, values, priorities, and master plans of the City
·
Gift has community or neighborhood support
·
Gift meets departmental specifications, such as construction quality
·
Gift enhances existing programs and facilities
·
Gift reinforces the City’s priorities
·
Gift addresses the Department’s goal of equity of services and facilities across the City
·
Adequate budget and staffing to maintain the gift
Conditional gifts may include requests for naming parks, recreation facilities, or buildings (see
Naming and Memorial Policy for details on naming) or requests for permitting priority or

access to facilities built or improved as a gift. When considering requests for permitting priority
or access, ensure that the request does not:
·
negatively impact public access in a way that exceeds the value of the gift or the public
good served by the gift
·
negatively impact equity of services across the City
·
have the effect of turning a public facility into essentially a private one
·
negatively impact existing programs, activities, and uses
Other gifts subject to conditions will be considered on a case-by-case basis. These types of gifts
include but are not limited to the following:
·
Any monetary gift that is time limited or requires subsequent donor approval of project
plans
·
Any monetary gift that is restricted to a specific use
·
A real property gift subject to defensible fee, reversionary interest, or life estate
·
Any gift that requires or restricts the public use or access to the gift in a manner not in
conformance with federal or state law, City Charter or Ordinances, or department
policies and rules and regulations
·
Any gift that requires action by the City and/or department
·
Any gift that requires a substantial financial commitment or a “maintenance effort” by
the City or department as a prerequisite for or in association with a gift
Procedures for Accepting a Gift
The City may approve or decline any proposed gift.
In addition, any gift of real or personal property must be approved by the affected Department
Director and be supported by the governmental process.  
Any proposed major gift that substantially impacts the public use of parks or building, impacts
the City’s resources, or has an accompanying condition requested with it must be approved by
the City. If a gift includes the request to name a public park, building, or major feature, it must
follow the Naming and Memorials Policy.
The process for donating a gift to the City is to:
·
Contact the City Clerk directly about any proposed gift (except for trees)
City Clerk
2101 O’Neil Avenue
Cheyenne, WY 82001
·
A City staff member will work directly with the donor for the appropriate reviews
and recognition for each gift.
Procedures and Guidelines for Recognition of a Gift
The City wants to recognize the generosity of its donors in a meaningful way. At the same time,
the City strives to minimize visual impacts in parks, buildings, and major features. The staff will
work with the donor to select a variety of options to thank and recognize them for their gift. That

can include recognition in marketing materials, City website, collective gift walls, or small
plaques. The following guidelines apply to all gift recognition:
·
All forms of recognition must meet City design and maintenance guidelines.
·
The City will establish and follow guidelines for the size, material, color and location of
a plaque or other name recognition media.
·
Often many people may be “behind the scenes contributors” to a major gift (e.g., elected
officials, staff members, or community leaders). However, permanent recognition, by
name, should be limited to the donor(s).
·
Permanent recognition of donors in public spaces and parks must have minimal visual
impact, be tasteful, and appropriate to the scale of the place and gift. Plaques will be
directly connected to the gift or in a collective display area.
·
Permanent recognition in a park or building can include the name of individuals,
organizations, and corporations. Product names and company logos are considered
advertising and are not acceptable. If a gift is for a series or group of improvements (e.g.,
new signs, trail markers, play equipment components) recognition will be limited to one
primary location.
·
Other visible recognition objects will be limited to areas recognized as “built”
environments (e.g. benches, picnic tables, buildings, play areas, ball fields, tennis courts).
·
Overall guidelines for accepting gifts and naming requests are covered in City policies:
Gift Policy, Corporate Sponsorship Policy, and Naming and Memorial Policy.
Procedures and Guidelines for Permanent Recognition. These are guidelines for permanent,
public recognition of gifts. The City will also recognize donors and gifts in a variety of ways
such as printed materials and announcements. 
Internal Department Process and Materials for Recognition of Gifts
Simple wording:
·
“Dedicated to….” “In honor of…..” “In celebration of....”
·
No logos
Dates:
·
Date of dedication or installation (no birth/death dates)
·
City should have a master plan of general areas where benches could be added and other material gifts
(shelters, equipment, plazas, playgrounds, flower beds, etc.)
Recognition for multiple gifts (e.g., a series of trail markers, many signs, all the equipment) will be consolidated into
one primary place. Recognition attached to gift if possible.
·
Kept in scale to not detract from the gift or context
·
Connected to an architectural element (in the wall, pavement, boulder)
·
Details (materials, font, graphics determined by City)
·
Logos: no company logos or brands incorporated into permanent recognition on display in a park or
recreation facility
Signs 
Standard City design preferable for large gifts (naming rights).  Smaller gifts will be recognized by plaques,
interpretive materials, or other appropriate recognition.  No logos.
