EXECUTIVE SUMMARY:   
 
This is an application for a future land use map amendment at the southeast corner of the 
intersection of US Highway 30 and Whitney Road. Currently, the land use designation for this 
area is Community Business. PlanCheyenne incorporated this future land-use designation based 
on the existing zoning that was part of the initial Saddle Ridge development. A request has been 
submitted by the current developer for Saddle Ridge, 7th Filing to change to land use designation 
to Urban Residential. This designation would allow the area to be developed residentially instead 
of commercially.  
 
According to W. S. § 15-1-601, all zoning must be made in accordance to a comprehensive plan. 
The property is currently zoned CB Community Business to follow the future land use designation 
of Community Business. Residential is not one of the uses intended in the Community Business 
district and thus a zone change to residential would not be following the comprehensive plan. A 
change to Urban Residential in the future land-use plan would allow for a zone change to a 
residential zone. 
 
This amendment process allows for additional public input when the land-use is changing 
significantly from previous expectations. The review criteria provides a framework for a public 
discussion about the land-use change. With regard to this proposal, PlanCheyenne certainly 
supports integrating commercial uses into neighborhoods. This proposal represents less 
commercial activity in the area than previously planned. The applicant has identified the market 
for commercial property has not been able to produce activity in this area that would be 
conducive with the existing neighborhood. Commercial activity is still contemplated in the area, 
with commercial plans still in place at the southwest corner of Whitney Road and US-30, although 
these plans have not been vested with development applications. Commercial zoning also is in 
place immediately to the east. In summary, this land-use change is relatively minor in nature and 
there remains commercial zoning in place to help serve the neighborhood. This would appear a 
reasonable approach moving forward. 
 
Due to inconclusive support from the review criteria found in PlanCheyenne, staff 
does not have a recommendation on whether to change to future land use map. 
 
Following the review criteria for amendments to PlanCheyenne, staff supports a 
modification to the future land use map, converting a portion of land previously 
anticipated for commercial development to residential development.

FUTURE LAND USE MAP AMENDMENT REVIEW CRITERIA: A Plan Amendment may be approved by the 
Governing Body based upon the following considerations: 
 
BENEFIT - The proposed amendment is beneficial to the community or addresses issues not foreseen 
during the planning process. 
 
Currently, the land use designation for this property is Community Business. According to 
PlanCheyenne, this category is generally found at the interchanges of interstates or at the 
intersection of an arterials and/or state highways. The intersection of US Highway 30 and 
Whitney Road meets this criterion. 
 
That being said, it is not the only Community Business in the area. There is also this category on 
the northwest corner of this intersection. Due to that, more Community Business is not 
necessary. Changing this area to Urban Residential as requested does not necessarily benefit the 
community but it does not hurt it either. 
 
COMPATIBILITY - The proposed amendment is compatible with the surrounding area, and the goals and 
policies of the Plan. 
 
The proposed amendment is compatible with the surrounding area. To the south and east is 
Urban Residential designated areas with a similar build out as proposed for this subject area. The 
ability to change to respond to market forces is one of the policies of PlanCheyenne (ref. Policy 
1.1.B). While changing to Urban Residential is not necessarily more flexible than Community 
Business it the uses it is intended for, it does adjust the property to what the market is currently 
demanding. There is the potential that in the future the market may demand something other 
than residential at this property, for which the land use designation would not work. 
 
IMPACTS - The proposed amendment will address and mitigate impacts on transportation, services, and 
facilities. 
 
The proposed amendment should not increase traffic on the adjoining roads in terms of overall 
volume. It will affect the volume of traffic during peak hours as it will add more homes. 
Commercial uses, while they show slightly higher overall volumes of traffic, do spread the traffic 
out throughout the day instead of focusing the traffic during peak hours. 
 
SERVICES - The proposed amendment will have minimal effect on service provision, including adequacy or 
availability of urban facilities and services, and is compatible with existing and planned services. 
 
Water and sanitary sewer could potentially be impacted by a change of use at this location. This 
is due to residential uses typically having a greater impact on sanitary sewer capacity than 
commercial uses. These changes will have to be accounted for with any proposal in the area 
moving forward.  
 
ANNEXATION - The proposed amendment does not jeopardize the City’s ability to annex the property. 
 
The property is already a part of the City so this amendment would not jeopardize any 
annexation potential. 
 
URBAN SERVICE BOUNDARY - The proposed amendment is consistent with the logical expansion of the 
Urban Services Area.

The area is located within the Urban Service Boundary and thus meets that criterion of the Urban 
Residential category. 
 
UNINTENDED CONSEQUENCES - Strict adherence to the Plan would result in a situation neither intended 
nor in keeping with other key elements and policies of the Plan. 
 
Strict adherence to the Plan would mean this property must develop in a commercial way and 
would not be able to develop residentially. According to the applicant, this may result in the land 
remaining undeveloped for the foreseeable future, with market demand not sufficient to generate 
compatible commercial development at this time.   
 
PUBLIC WELFARE - The proposed plan amendment will promote the public welfare and will be consistent 
with the goals and policies of PlanCheyenne and the elements thereof. 
 
A change to Urban Residential will not promote further public welfare amongst the community. A 
reoccurring theme in PlanCheyenne is mixing uses throughout neighborhoods (ref. Principle 1.1, 
2.2, 3.4). By changing to Urban Residential, the potential for mixing uses immediately within the 
neighborhood is eliminated without further change. As previously noted, there are commercial 
uses at the northwest corner of the intersection of US-30 and Whitney Road. A change won’t 
completely eliminate commercial from area.

ATTACHMENT A
PROCEDURES AND GUIDELINES
FOR CHEYENNE DOWNTOWN DEVELOPMENT AUTHORITY
DISTRICT SPECIAL ELECTIONS (PROPERTY ASSESSMENT)
A.
WYOMING STATUTES GOVERNING ELECTION
1.
Wyo. Stat. §15-9-217 (b)
NOTE: Various areas of Wyo. Stat. §§22-23-801 through 22-23-809, Municipal Special Elections,
were also considered in compiling these procedures and guidelines.
B.
CITY ORDINANCES AND RESOLUTIONS GOVERNING ELECTION
1.
Resolution authorizing special election
2.
Resolution adopting procedures and guidelines for conduct and canvassing of
elections
3.
Ordinance submitting special election ballot question to qualified property owners
4.
Resolution adopting election Canvassing Board results.
C.
GENERAL PROCEDURES
1.
Cheyenne Downtown Development Authority shall submit, pursuant to a specific
date set by the City Clerk, a letter to the City Clerk requesting the City of Cheyenne
conduct an election pursuant to Wyo. Stat. §15-9-217 (b).  Letter shall include the
following information:
a.
Date of proposed election
b.
Wording of question to be placed on election ballot
c.
Effective term of assessment (not to exceed four (4) years) if majority vote
received
d.
Confirmation that a list of qualified voters will be submitted when the
Laramie County Assessor completes the most recent update of DDA District
property owners database (1 original by alpha and 1 original by address; 3
copies of each)
(i)
Persons owning real property within the District, excluding real
property used exclusively for residential purposes, or
(ii)
Persons obligated for the payment of general property taxes pursuant
to tax assessment notification as issued by the Laramie County
Assessor or the Wyoming Department of Revenue.
e.
Confirmation that the names of three (3) persons who may serve as
election judges; and names of any alternate judges will be submitted not
less than one month prior to the election.

NOTE: Any election information (ballot question, issues, general info, absentee, etc.) provided as
a courtesy to property owners is the responsibility of the Downtown Development Authority
Board and its members.
2.
The City Clerk will submit a resolution for governing body approval for the
special election date, and specify procedures and guidelines to be utilized in the
conduct of the election.
D.
ELECTION GUIDELINES 
(DDA District shall be considered  as one (1) voting precinct for election purposes)
1.
Voters; election site
a.
All qualified voters of non-residential property within the boundaries of
the Cheyenne Downtown Development Authority District per tax
assessment listing provided by the Laramie County Assessor, are eligible
to vote per Section C (d) above.  Each qualified voter will be allowed one
(1) vote per the number of property tax assessment notices the qualified
voter receives for  real property, excluding property used exclusively for
residential purposes, within the District as issued by the Laramie County
Assessor or the Wyoming Department of Revenue.
b.
A listing of qualified voters of non-residential properties within the
District, as compiled by the Laramie County Assessor, will be used by the
judges of the election in determining the voter roster.  A copy of the
election ballot, along with a map outlining the established boundaries of
the Cheyenne Downtown Development Authority District, will be posted
at the election site.  Voters will be required to sign an affidavit stating their
authority to cast a vote(s) at the polling site on the date of election
reflecting: (1) ownership of non-residential property within the District, or
(2) authority as the person obligated for payment of general property taxes
pursuant to tax assessments issued by the Laramie County Assessor and
state assessed properties that receive assessment through the Wyoming
Department of Revenue.
c.
Sole polling place for the election will be at a location determined by the
Cheyenne Downtown Development Authority Board with polls to be open
from 9:00 a.m. until 3:00 p.m. on the date of election.
d.
Upon request, voters may request an absentee ballot(s) from the City
Clerk’s Office, and cast their vote through the absentee ballot process.
e.
Tabulation of  ballots cast will be by manual tabulation by the canvassing
board, or as determined by the City Clerk.
2.
Election judges
a.
There shall be three (3) election judges on the polling premises during the
time established for the election (9:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m.).  Judges will be
owners of real property, business proprietors or employees of same within
the District.  Judges may serve voluntarily without compensation, or they

may be compensated at the rate allowed by Wyo. Stat. §22-8-116 (election
code).
b.
The judges of the special election shall discharge the same duties as the
judges of regular elections.
c.
Judges will be provided with accessibility to telephones, rest rooms,
beverages, etc. at the polling place, and lunches will be provided or judges
will be reimbursed for luncheon expenses.  Judges must remain at the
polling site during election hours.
3.
Absentee voting
a.
Absentee voting on the question is permitted and votes cast will be
included in the manual tabulating of the ballots, or as determined by the
City Clerk.  The ballots will contain the same question as stated on the
ballots available at the polling place, and will provide the absentee elector
the opportunity to indicate his or her vote of “for” or “against” the ballot
question.
b.
Any qualified voter who will be absent on election day, or who can not be
present at the polling place on election day, may vote by absentee ballot
upon making application in person, in writing, or by telephone, to the City
Clerk’s Office, 2101 O’Neil Ave., Cheyenne, WY 82001, telephone (307)
637-6346, and by furnishing the following information:
C
Name in full,
C
Name in which the property is held,
C
How applicant is associated with the property/entity,
C
Election for which an absentee ballot is being requested,
C
Location of property within the District, and
C
If absentee ballot will not be obtained in person, the address
to which the absentee ballot is to be mailed.
NOTE: Request for an absentee ballot may be made on behalf of an absentee qualified elector if
the prescribed information listed above is furnished.  The absentee voter will be entitled to vote
upon executing an affidavit, indicating the applicant is entitled to vote in the election, with said
affidavit printed on the reverse side of the return ballot envelope to be returned to the City
Clerk’s Office.
c.
Requests for absentee ballots may be made not sooner than 25 days prior
to the election date and not later than the close of business of the City
Clerk’s  Office on the day prior to the election date.
4.
Publications; ballots
a.
The date of the special election, location of the polling place and specified
election procedures will be proclaimed by ordinance by the Governing
Body of the City of Cheyenne not more than 45 days nor less than 30 days
before the date of the election.  In addition to publication of the ordinance,
including a map outlining the established boundaries of the Cheyenne

Downtown Development Authority District, the City Clerk will cause the
Proclamation and Notice of Election and Public Notice of Elector
Qualifications portion of the ordinance, including a map of the district, to
be published at least twice (2) in a newspaper of general circulation within
the city prior to the election date. 
b.
All ballots, including absentee, will be prepared by the City Clerk or the
Cheyenne Downtown Development Authority.
E.
POST ELECTION PROCEDURES
1.
Canvassing Board
a.
Canvassing Board members shall be comprised of the Deputy City Clerk
and two (2) other persons from the community designated by the Mayor. 
Community members will serve without compensation. 
2.
Canvassing
a.
Votes will be tabulated and canvassed by members of the Canvassing
Board immediately upon closure of the polls at 3:00 p.m. on the day of the
election.  Results will be certified by the Board to the City Clerk.
b.
All records of the election will be kept by the City Clerk for the required
records retention time frame for special municipal elections.
c.
The Governing Body of the City of Cheyenne will certify by resolution the
Canvassing Board’s findings and results of the election. The City Clerk
will provide a certified copy of the resolution to the Laramie County
Assessor.
(1)
If the majority of votes cast are in favor of the special assessment,
the City Clerk will include in the certified copy of the resolution to
the Laramie County Assessor, the effective term of the assessment.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY 
INTRODUCTION  
The Pershing Boulevard Complete Streets Plan focuses on improvements to multimodal access and safety 
along Pershing Boulevard between Evans Avenue and Logan Avenue in Cheyenne, Wyoming.  The plan 
identifies needs and prioritizes improvements to make it easier, safer and more appealing to walk and 
bike within and through the corridor.      
The City of Cheyenne partnered with the Cheyenne Metropolitan Planning Organization (MPO) to engage 
residents, key stakeholders and partner agencies through a comprehensive Public Outreach. The outreach 
included handing out informational flyers along the corridor, individual business interviews, a walking 
audit of the corridor, a project website, two community outreach workshops, and presentations to the 
MPO Technical Committee. The public outreach component of this project helped identify the qualitative 
issues as experienced by local business owners and users of Pershing Boulevard.  
EXISTING CONDITIONS ASSESSMENT 
The vehicular Level of Service analysis indicates that the intersections along the corridor are operating 
without significant delay. Pedestrians and bicyclists traveling within and along Pershing Boulevard face 
significant challenges. Volumes of bicycles and pedestrians are relatively low along this corridor.  This 
does not necessarily reflect a low demand by these modes, but rather insufficient infrastructure to create a 
comfortable and safe environment for people to walk or bicycle along Pershing.  Particular issues for 
bicyclists and pedestrians include: 
• 
Poor sidewalk quality 
• 
ADA accessibility 
• 
Lack of Access management 
• 
Lack of pedestrian crossing opportunities 
• 
Misaligned intersections 
• 
Insufficient lighting, particularly pedestrian-scale 
• 
Lack of street furniture 
• 
Narrow and attached sidewalks 
• 
Sidewalk slope exceeds 2% at driveways, making it difficult for mobility impaired users to navigate

Study redevelopment opportunities throughout the corridor.

INTRODUCTION 
Pershing Boulevard is a key commercial corridor in the heart of Cheyenne, serving residents and visitors of 
a range of ages and abilities.  This corridor serves Miller Elementary School, Carey Junior High School, a 
number of small businesses, residential neighborhoods, a 12-screen movie theater, Gold’s Gym, the 
Wyoming State Bank, three city cemeteries, and the Cheyenne Workforce Center.  Pershing is also an 
important transportation connection in Cheyenne, as it is one of the few uninterrupted east-west corridors 
in the city. 
The need for this plan became evident following concerns expressed by the neighboring businesses, 
general public and individuals with disabilities who frequently cross the intersection of Pershing Boulevard 
and Duff Avenue. In response to the concerns, the City of Cheyenne and the Cheyenne Metropolitan 
Planning Organization (MPO) recognized a need to explore Complete Streets options along this section of 
Pershing Boulevard to make it easier, safer and more appealing to walk and bike.   
Complete Streets are designed and operated to enable safe access for all users, including people of all 
ages and abilities on foot, bikes, cars, and buses. Complete Streets make it easy to cross the street, walk to 
shops, and bicycle to work.  Complete Streets essentially define the character of a street. A successful 
streetscape helps to create an inviting environment, encourage economic development, stimulate private 
sector investment and enhance the existing positive features. Each streetscape is unique and there is no 
one-size-fits-all description, but ingredients that may be found in a complete streetscape include 
sidewalks, bike lanes, parking lanes, crosswalks, pedestrian lighting and signals, and traffic calming 
measures such as curb extensions and medians. Everything that is found in the space between buildings 
on each side of the street can be considered part of the streetscape realm. 
This report documents the review of existing and proposed plans for the Pershing corridor, existing 
conditions of the study area, bicycle and pedestrian issues, as well potential opportunities.

LITERATURE REVIEW 
The Pershing Boulevard Complete Streets Plan began with a review of Cheyenne’s existing plans and 
studies.  The reviewed plans date back to 2009 so some of the improvements identified in the summaries 
have already been implemented.  A summary of each of the plans is below.    
1) East Pershing Boulevard Corridor and Intersections Plan (July 2009) 
This study looks at East Pershing Boulevard from Dunn Avenue to Converse Avenue and considered 
intersection improvements on this section of Pershing.  At the intersection of Pershing Boulevard and 
Concord Road/ Logan Avenue, the study proposed to realign the intersection to a standard four-leg 
design.  This will require acquiring right of way and razing a portion of the school building at the 
northeast corner.  “This improvement is expected to improve traffic flow, safety, reduce traffic queuing 
and enhance pedestrian crossing of the intersection.”  There were also some mixed use redevelopment

This study area encompasses Pershing Boulevard from Evans Avenue on the west to Logan Avenue on the 
east, with a deeper look at the two blocks between Duff Avenue and Dunn Avenue.  The Pershing 
Boulevard Corridor can be seen as three distinct character areas between Evans Ave. and Logan Ave. The 
western portion of the corridor is influenced by the intersection of Evans Ave. and Pershing Blvd. from

points fronting Pershing Blvd. Most parcels have parking fronting Pershing Blvd. A number of residential 
parcels also front the corridor through this area as well. Streets along the south portion of the corridor are 
oriented northwest/southeast and southwest/northeast in a grid pattern. Street intersections along the 
south of the corridor are misaligned to the north and enter at an angle, creating visibility issues for 
turning movements.

VEHICULAR LEVEL OF SERVICE CRITERIA 
To measure and describe the operational status of the local roadway network and corresponding 
intersections, transportation engineers and planners commonly use a grading system called level-of-
service (LOS) put forth by the Transportation Research Board’s HCM 2000.  LOS characterizes the 
operational conditions of an intersection’s traffic flow; ranging from LOS A (indicating free flow traffic 
conditions with little or no delay) to LOS F (representing over-saturated conditions where traffic flows 
exceeds the design capacity, resulting in long queues and delays). These grades represent the perspective 
of drivers and are an indication of the comfort and convenience associated with driving. Although LOS A 
through C are desired levels, LOS D is considered acceptable in urban conditions. Traffic conditions with 
LOS E or F are generally considered unacceptable and represent significant travel delay, increased 
accident potential, and inefficient motor vehicle operation. The LOS is determined differently depending 
on the type of control at the intersection.  

Pershing Boulevard Complete Streets Plan  
P a g e | 19 
At signalized intersections, the operation analysis uses various intersection characteristics (such as traffic 
volumes, lane geometry, and signal phasing) to estimate the intersection’s volume-to-capacity (v/c) ratio.  
For signalized intersections the HCM defines the intersection LOS as the average delay per vehicle for the 
overall intersection, which includes all approaches.   
At unsignalized intersections, the operation analysis uses various intersection characteristics (such as 
traffic volumes, lane geometry, and stop-controlled approaches) to estimate the intersection’s volume-to-
capacity (v/c) ratio.  For unsignalized intersections the HCM defines the intersection LOS as the average 
delay per vehicle for the worst approach intersection.

Preferred Cross-Section 
The alternatives were evaluated in a workshop with City Staff and at the first public workshop. It was 
determined that the roadway width from curb to curb should remain as it is in the current existing 
condition. On-street bike lanes were seen as a less feasible means of integrating bike facilities, and it was 
determined that a multi-use pedestrian/bike path in a detached condition that integrates some street 
trees is most desirable. 
The preferred street cross-section involves integrating a detached 6’ walk on the south side of Pershing 
Blvd. with a 4’ planting buffer, 10’-6” travel lanes, a 14’ planted median/center turn lane and an 8’ multi-
use path on the north side of Pershing Blvd. This multi-use path on the north side will tie into the East 
Pershing Blvd. constructed condition. Street trees are integrated on the north side of Pershing Blvd. in 
grates where feasible.

CONCLUSION 
An analysis of Pershing Boulevard from Evans Avenue to Logan Avenue indicates a number of deficiencies 
in the corridor that keep it from being a complete street that is safe and comfortable for users of all ages 
and abilities.  Given the traffic volume, speed and width of Pershing Boulevard, the corridor lacks the 
presence of sufficient bicycle and pedestrian facilities. An analysis of the Level of Service reveals that all 
intersections are operating without significant vehicular operational issues. 
The walking audit as well as business interviews identified a need for improved access management, sight 
line, quality of sidewalks for a better pedestrian experience, and ADA conformity.   
Alternatives to address the deficiencies and desires from the community were presented and evaluated by 
City staff and at the community workshops.  It was determined that the roadway width from curb to curb 
should remain as it is in the current existing condition. On-street bike lanes were seen as a less feasible 
means of integrating bike facilities, and it was determined that a multi-use pedestrian/bike path in a 
detached condition that integrates some street trees is most desirable. 
The preferred street cross-section involves integrating a detached 6’ walk on the south side of Pershing 
Blvd. with a 4’ planting buffer, 10’-6” travel lanes, a 14’ planted median/center turn lane and an 8’ multi-
use path on the north side of Pershing Blvd. This multi-use path on the north side will tie into the East 
Pershing Blvd. constructed condition. Street trees are integrated on the north side of Pershing Blvd. in 
grates where feasible. 
It was determined that the ultimate preferred cross-section should be phased for project implementation 
and that the first phase should address pedestrian safety.  This phase involves integrating a pedestrian 
crossing at Duff Ave. and Pershing Blvd to facilitate crossing within the heavily used commercial area here. 
This involves installation of a Rectangular Rapid Flashing Beacon (RRFB) set of signals on either the east or 
west side of Duff Ave. This involves integrating a 6’ refuge median as well on the west side of Duff Ave. or 
a 10’ refuge median on the east side of Duff Ave. Restriping lanes to 10’ will be required in either version 
of this concept. 
The costs of implementing and maintaining these longer term improvements identified by the 
participating members of the community can often be expensive and burdensome to municipalities. As a 
result, this study breaks out these potential future improvements into phases which can be implemented 
through a variety of creative public and private funding sources in the future. The intent is however to 
have a plan or a road map for this area so that if and when funding opportunities arise, the City and 
community leaders have a vision and corresponding design ideas that they can utilize to move forward.

CHEYENNE - About a dozen local residents and business owners rallied Wednesday afternoon in 
front of Hoy's Drugs on East Pershing Boulevard.
They gathered to raise awareness of the need for a pedestrian crosswalk across Pershing Boulevard 
at Duff Avenue.
They say the intersection is unsafe.
"This is about life and death," Todd Anderson said.
He is the owner of Elite Cleaners on Pershing Boulevard.
"It's a real, real challenge to get across here," Anderson said, gesturing toward the heavy lunch-time 
traffic zipping down Pershing Boulevard.
"It's a traffic issue and a speeding issue, big time. It's dangerous. There have been children hit (by

Dr. Marty Carroll, an optometrist at the Cheyenne Vision Clinic near the intersection, agreed it can

"This is a community area, and there are a lot of older people who live around here who need to get

The situation at the intersection is made worse by the presence of nearby schools, Carroll said.
"When school gets out, you see swarms of kids trying to cross the street from Dairy Queen, and

The Cheyenne Metropolitan Planning Organization is in the process of studying the intersection as

Acting city engineer Nathan Beauheim added that once the final report is presented "and we get 
buy-in from the governing body, we will start the process of looking for funding."
In the meantime, Councilwoman Annette Williams is pushing for the council to set aside funds in 
the 2016 fiscal year to build the crosswalk.
She attempted Wednesday to amend the city's 2016 fiscal year budget proposal to include nearly

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY:   
This preliminary plat is for a residential subdivision at the southeast corner of Ridge Road and Summit 
Drive.  The primary developable area is intended for multifamily development conceptually contemplating 
around 60 units. For the subdivision, the applicant is proposing a zoning of NR-3 High Density Residential 
which is supported by PlanCheyenne.  Eventually, an annexation application, zone change application, 
and final plat application will have to come before the Governing Body for approval.  A Conditional Use 
Approval from the Board of Adjustment is also required for Multi-family development.   
 
The land currently includes two single family units which are intended to remain for the near term and 
multiple mobile homes which are planned to be removed at time of development.  The proposed 
development is immediately north of the developing Thomas Heights Subdivision. With the Final Plat the 
applicant should address an approach to the traffic signal land dedication condition and clarify their 
approach with easements Cheyenne Light Fuel and Power may want.  Staff would also want to clarify 
expectations related to the sanitary sewer lift station and how it should be reflected on the final plat.    
 
 
 
Due to meeting the review criteria found in Section 2.1.2 of the Cheyenne Unified 
Development Code (UDC), staff recommends approval of the Summit Ridge 
preliminary plat, advancing it to the Governing Body with the following condition: 
 
1. Please dedicate a 20’x20’ triangle of additional right-of-way on the corner of Summit 
& Ridge to provide additional right-of-way for future signalization and other 
intersection improvements.

PLAT DATA:  In reviewing, providing comments, and making recommendations and decisions on a 
proposed preliminary plat, the Staff, Planning Commission and City Council shall use the following 
criteria: 
 
PLANCHEYENNE - The application is in accordance with the development policies and physical patterns 
identified in the PlanCheyenne.  
 
In the future land use plan, PlanCheyenne, this area has been identified as urban transitional which 
supports the transition of rural residential into higher densities in the City.    PlanCheyenne also supports 
the transition utilizing existing capacity and the water and sewer system. 
 
 
PLANNING - The application reflects sound planning and urban design principles with respect to existing 
and potential future development in the vicinity, including consideration of current and future streets, 
open spaces, blocks and lots, and public utilities or facilities.  
 
This project is near previous investments made by the City and Community which include North 
Cheyenne Community Park with primary park facilities just over ¼ mile away and the recent 
development of Summit Drive which was coordinated by the MPO with sidewalk curb and gutter. 
 
The proposal accommodates joint access to existing residential units in Lots 1 and 2 also providing 
opportunities for redevelopment on these lots.   
 
With the annexation the expectation is the adjacent rights of ways and intersections will be included in 
the annexation proposal to eliminate jurisdictional inconsistencies.   
 
STANDARDS - Division of land meets the purposes, intent, and all applicable standards in Article 4. 
 
Transportation Network: 
This is a redeveloping area which has limited opportunities for a transportation network.  The 
remaining undeveloped land in the area bounded by Summit Drive, Ridge Road and College Drive 
is relatively small.  To the south Thomas Heights is already developing and a connection is not 
possible and to the east are a number of homes precluding a logical street extension to the east.  
The proposed preliminary plat does provide access to numerous parcels and is a reasonable 
approach given the constraints in the area.  
 
Ridge Road is a Minor Arterial Street which currently does not include curb, gutter or sidewalk.  
Site development may require improvements to Ridge Road to current standards.  Ridge Road 
improvements would need to be coordinated with Thomas Heights improvements to the south.  
Summit Drive is also a minor arterial and sidewalk curb and getter has already been installed as a 
result of an MPO Surface Transportation Program – Urban (STP) project which was able to 
leverage federal funds to build the road in coordination with Laramie County and WYDOT. 
 
A traffic signal may be warranted in the future.  The property corner is unusually close to the 
street at this intersection and does not provide room for future installation of a traffic signal.  The
