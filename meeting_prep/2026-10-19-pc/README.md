## The one item: PLTA-26-5 — Data Centers Text Amendment

### What it actually is
A Unified Development Code (UDC) text amendment that would:
- Create **UDC 1.1.8** (Public Records; No Confidentiality Agreements) — bars City officials from signing NDAs with developers over development records.
- Create **UDC 1.1.9** (Development Agreements) — formalizes that development agreements need City Council approval.
- Create **UDC 5.7.1 "Data Centers"** — the core of the amendment: a dedicated specific-use-standards section for data centers (noise, water, sewer, surface discharge, electricity, natural gas, construction, lighting, records/agreements, fees, state/federal law).
- Create **UDC 5.8.9 "Construction Uses"** (Temporary Construction Use standards) — new rules for construction staging, dust, screening, lighting, traffic, and **"Workforce Accommodation Quarters"** (temporary worker housing/RV sites) tied to large builds like data-center campuses.
- Amend **UDC 1.4.3** (Defined Terms) to add "Discernable Noise," "Temporary Construction Use," and "Workforce Accommodation Quarters."
- Amend **UDC 4.5.3** (Water and Sanitary Sewerage) and **4.5.4** (Utilities) to formalize existing Board of Public Utilities (BOPU) practices around capacity review, cost-sharing, and closed-loop cooling.

Prepared by Seth Lloyd, AICP, Senior Planner. Full text reconstructed in `supporting_documents/STAFF_REPORT_PLTA-26-5.md` from the City's own staff report PDF.

### Why it's back on the agenda tonight
It was heard and **postponed on October 5, 2026** along with a related-but-separate item, PLTA-26-3 (Power Generation Facilities). Commissioners wanted more discussion of:
- Independent sound studies and how "discernable noise" is verified,
- Setbacks between data centers and residential property lines,
- How facilities of different sizes should be treated (one-size-fits-all vs. tiered standards).

PLTA-26-3 (power generation / backup generators) was postponed separately to **November 2, 2026** — it is a related case (same meeting, overlapping subject matter, same staff) but it is **not** on tonight's agenda. Don't conflate the two in public comment or coverage.

No new staff report has been posted for the Oct 19 date — the same staff report (dated/updated September 30, 2026, noted on the Oct 5 agenda as correcting "a typo in proposed 5.7.1.b") appears to be the operative document carried forward.

### The headline number: 45 dBA at night
The most quotable detail: Planning & Development Director Charles Bloom told the Oct 5 Commission, "if you have an operation, it cannot be generating 45 decibels at that residential property line that's nearest to it... This is a number we were able to get from national federal guidance." Per the staff report, that 45 dBA nighttime residential limit is a **reduction from the City's current (Aug. 20, 2025) interpretive policy**, which allows 55 dBA — staff concluded their own existing policy level "is likely too high," citing WHO guidance that nighttime road-traffic noise shouldn't exceed 45 dBL and that peer cities set nighttime limits in the 40–50 dBA range.

Full before/after noise table in `supporting_documents/STAFF_REPORT_PLTA-26-5.md` and `supporting_documents/EXISTING_2025_NOISE_POLICY.md`.

### Other notable substantive provisions
- **Rooftop cooling fans must be fully enclosed** in an acoustically attenuated penthouse — staff says "the majority of sound problems experienced in the Cheyenne region have come from these types of units," requiring sound barriers taller than the buildings themselves.
- **Closed-loop (non-evaporative) cooling required** for data-center water use unless BOPU/Council finds an evaporative system necessary — a direct response to the water-use controversy that has dogged Cheyenne's data-center boom all year (see background file).
- **Emergency generator testing capped at 30 min/week/generator, 9 a.m.–4 p.m. only**; generators may run up to 144 hours (6 days) during an actual utility outage without being treated as "noise."
- **New low-frequency ("dBC minus dBA") noise standard** — a response to a loophole where machinery can be quiet by dBA-only measurement but still disruptive (and the report notes this could even apply to future generations of data-center hardware "specifically designed to avoid dBA regulations").
- **New "Workforce Accommodation Quarters" use** — formal zoning category for temporary worker housing/RV camps tied to a single project, modeled on peer Front Range jurisdictions (Fort Collins, Broomfield, Centennial, Aurora, Douglas County, CO cited for similar construction-lighting/shielding rules).
- **Explicit transparency clause (new 1.1.8):** the City is formally barred from signing NDAs/confidentiality agreements with developers and all development records are public records — reads as a direct, if unstated, response to this year's criticism (see background file) that the City has been less than forthcoming about data-center deals (Microsoft's verbal-only/undisclosed terms, the ~3-month delay in disclosing the Meta "Project Cosmo" water contamination, etc.).

### Public comment on record so far
Per the staff report itself: *"This item was advertised in the paper for the October 5, 2026, Planning Commission. No comment has been made by the general public on this proposal."* As of the Sept. 28, 2026 report date, staff had received zero public inquiries or comments — this is true even though Cheyenne's data-center fights generally (Orchard Hills, Microsoft, the moratorium vote) drew large, sustained public turnout all year. Tonight is the first real opportunity for the public to weigh in on this specific text amendment.

## ⚡ LIVE UPDATE: the hearing actually happened — see `live_transcript/`
A live transcript of tonight's actual hearing (user-provided, meeting clock ~18:04–19:58)
has been processed into `live_transcript/PC_MEETING_LIVE_NOTES.md`. It supersedes the
"zero public comment" expectation from the written staff report — the hearing drew roughly
20 in-person/phone speakers plus extensive Commission discussion. Headline developments:
- Staff revised the noise rules again since Oct. 5 (now measures at any potentially affected
  nearby property, not just abutting parcels; tightened the dBC loophole to "20 or more").
- An independent acoustician, **Rob Rand** (funded by the Albany County Conservancy),
  recommended dropping the residential nighttime limit from 45 dBA to **40 dBA** and daytime
  from 55 to **45 dBA** — staff said on the record it has no objection to the Commission
  adopting those numbers.
- Extensive testimony centered on one specific, already-operating facility — a **CleanSpark**
  Bitcoin-mining data center at Camp Stool — as the real-world case study for noise, not a
  hypothetical.
- Councilman Mark Bowie proposed a detailed set of floor amendments (1,000-ft setbacks,
  monthly water/electricity-usage reporting, NDA restrictions extended to nonprofits/EDOs,
  on-site-only wind/solar); Councilwoman Kathy Emmons pushed back, urging the Commission to
  pass the staff draft as-is rather than "upend the whole thing."
- **The transcript ends before any motion or vote** — outcome unknown, check for an update.
