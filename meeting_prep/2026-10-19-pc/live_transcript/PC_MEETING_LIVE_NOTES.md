# Processed notes — Planning Commission live hearing on PLTA-26-5, October 19, 2026
Source: `RAW_TRANSCRIPT_PLTA-26-5_hearing.md` in this folder (user-provided live transcript,
meeting clock ~18:04–19:58). **Transcript ends before any motion or vote** — treat everything
below as the record of debate/testimony only, not a final outcome, until a vote is confirmed.

This supersedes/supplements `../supporting_documents/STAFF_REPORT_PLTA-26-5.md` (the Sept. 30
written staff report) with what actually happened live at the hearing.

## 1. What staff changed since the October 5 postponement

Director Bloom and Senior Planner Seth Lloyd described four substantive revisions made after
additional outreach to BOPU, City Engineering, and Cheyenne Fire Rescue:

1. **Noise now measured at "any potentially affected nearby location," not just abutting parcels.** A data center could meet its own district's noise standard at its own property line, then exceed a *different* (tighter) threshold once sound reaches a nearby residential district — this revision makes clear the tighter threshold governs regardless of adjacency.
2. **Low-frequency (dBC) threshold tightened from "exceeds by 20 dBC" to "20 dBC or more"** — closing a one-decibel loophole (a facility measuring exactly 21 dBC over the dBA reading would not have been "exceeding by 20" under the old wording).
3. **BOPU utility-cost language clarified** — codifies that developers must cover all costs required for system improvements (already common practice).
4. **Data center standards incorporate NFPA (National Fire Prevention Association) fire-safety mandates by reference** (already adopted through the fire code) and require **digital/3D interior mapping** for data centers — already a current Fire Marshal/Cheyenne Fire Rescue practice, now being codified.
5. **Temporary Construction Use thresholds clarified**: off-site facilities over **1 acre** and on-site facilities over **5 acres** trigger dust/air-quality/erosion/grading-permit requirements and give Engineering authority to require a traffic study for any development proposing **more than 100 employees on site at a given time**.

## 2. Key Q&A clarifications on the record

- **The "LEADS NDA" question (Commissioner Laird):** Does the new 1.1.8 (no-NDA/public-records) provision affect the Mayor's LEADS-board confidentiality document? **Staff: No.** Staff characterized the LEADS document as not technically an NDA but a "non-disclosure and confidentiality agreement to the extent allowed by law" — meaning anything otherwise subject to a public-records request remains discoverable regardless. This is a direct, on-the-record staff answer to one of this year's standing transparency criticisms, and it did **not** result in any change to that existing LEADS arrangement.
- **Fire safety track record (Commissioner Hibbins / Fire Chief Dykstra):** Cheyenne has had **zero data-center fires locally** to date; Chief Dykstra noted modern facilities have high-end suppression/life-safety systems and the public typically wouldn't hear about an incident unless those systems were compromised.
- **Noise-measurement methodology still pending City Attorney review** (not yet in the ordinance text): ANSI standard adoption, time-weighting approach (day/hour weighting rather than a year-long airport-style average, since data-center cooling-driven noise peaks seasonally), equipment type (Type 1 vs. Type 2 accuracy), and tamper/monitoring logistics. Per Lloyd: the **City Attorney is reviewing measurement method, duration, and equipment — not the dBA/dBC numeric limits themselves.**
- **Enforcement history disclosed for the first time:** A Bitcoin-mining ("crypto-mine") facility exceeding noise limits was brought into compliance via a **negotiated power-supply reduction**, coordinated with the utility (not unilateral City action) — the utility reportedly preferred partial power usage over a full shutoff. Available UDC enforcement tools also include utility-service cutoff and fines; Board of Adjustment can impose tighter conditional-use noise limits case by case.
- **Two other noise-related code provisions exist outside this UDC amendment**: a municipal-code general-nuisance noise standard enforced by Police (threshold: **80 dB**), and a City Clerk-administered noise-permit process for events (e.g., fireworks) that may exceed normal limits.
- **Unincorporated Laramie County has no numerical noise limit at all under state statute** — meaning annexation into the city *tightens*, not loosens, the applicable noise standard for a given parcel, per staff.
- **Table 1-2's "All Other Zone Districts" category is explicitly intended to cover all residential property, inside or outside city limits** — staff confirmed on the record that the proposed 45 dBA nighttime standard is meant to protect county residential properties adjacent to a city data center, not just properties inside Cheyenne.
- **Setbacks and use-table placement (where data centers are allowed) were explicitly deferred.** Staff recommended handling data-center size tiers, buffering, and use-table placement (e.g., restricting large-scale data centers' eligibility within Light Industrial) as a **separate, future text amendment**, not folded into PLTA-26-5, to avoid "biting off too much with one ordinance." Director Bloom confirmed active Cheyenne data centers currently exist in LI, CB, DC (Downtown Commercial), HI, BP, and MUB zones.
- **Staff acknowledges a documented case of a sound study passing on paper but failing after construction** — cited generally (facility not named in this transcript) as the reason for requiring post-construction/complaint-driven enforcement measurement in addition to pre-construction sound studies.
- **Construction-phase noise is NOT currently covered by this ordinance's discernible-noise standard**, and staff flagged a real drafting tension: applying it uniformly could make ordinary single-family home construction technically non-compliant. Staff and commissioners discussed, but did not resolve, adding a **large-scale-construction-specific** noise/time-of-day standard (possibly tied to project duration or employee count) rather than a blanket rule.
- **Construction lighting is currently a loophole in the zoning code** — the ordinance would require construction sites to meet the same downward-shielding/light-trespass standards as permanent buildings, with the sole carve-out being taller allowable pole height for on-site security lighting.

## 3. Dr. Rob Rand's acoustic-consultant report — the night's most consequential outside input

Rob Rand (Rand Acoustics), an independent acoustician, was retained/funded by the **Albany County Conservancy** (per member Anne Brand's testimony) specifically to review the city's staff reports on this amendment. His written comments, discussed repeatedly by commissioners, recommended:
- Lowering the **nighttime residential limit from 45 dBA to 40 dBA**.
- Lowering the **daytime residential limit from 55 dBA to 45 dBA**.
- Applying the standard to residential **uses**, not just residential **zones** (i.e., protecting an actual home regardless of its underlying zoning designation).
- Replacing the term "discernible noise" with "**regulated noise**" (staff disagreed with this specific change, since "discernible" is used UDC-wide and changing it would require a document-wide find/replace).
- Formatting the dBC/dBA relationship as a table rather than narrative text (staff called this functionally identical to what's already proposed).
- Defining measurement methodology by **ANSI standard**.

**Staff's response, on the record (Seth Lloyd):** reviewed the full report; called most of it "grammatical"; found the substantive recommendations (40/45 dBA, use-vs-zone framing) reasonable and stated no objection — noting that because large/hyperscale data centers tend to produce similar sound output day and night, a stricter nighttime number (40) functions as the practical controlling limit regardless of whether daytime also drops to 45. Staff explicitly invited the Commission to adopt Rand's numbers if it wished: *"If the Planning Commission wants to go to the levels recommended by [Rand], I don't think that would be a problem."* **No vote had been taken on this as of the transcript's end.**

## 4. Dominant real-world example raised all night: CleanSpark (Camp Stool)

Unlike the written staff report's largely hypothetical framing, live testimony repeatedly centered on one **specific, named, currently-operating facility**: a Bitcoin-mining data center operated by **CleanSpark** at Lot 1, Block 1, Camp Stool 7th Filing (near I-80/Camp Stool Road, just east of the Lowe's Distribution Center), on a parcel originally sold by Camp Stool Land Company. Per three generations of the Volk family testifying:
- Noise is audible up to roughly a half-mile away under normal conditions, and reportedly as far as Pershing under high-wind conditions.
- The City measured approximately **70 dBA** at the site — but operators reportedly throttle down fan units when they anticipate measurement, undermining enforcement reliability.
- 30-foot sound-mitigation walls were installed around the (now 10-acre) site, reducing noise outside the fence line but not for workers inside (who wear hearing protection), while creating their own visual/appearance complaints from neighbors and the landowner (Camp Stool Land Company says it "never approved the walls" under its own covenants).
- A second, unrelated crypto-mining facility on the **west side** of town (referenced as near North Range Business Park / "Bison Business Park") has a parallel noise-complaint history; staff is in regular direct contact with nearby county residents and had not received a complaint "since the summer," tentatively attributed to cooler weather reducing cooling-fan load.
- Both crypto-mining sites were explicitly distinguished by Director Bloom from "the data centers" proper — in his words, noise problems have come disproportionately from cryptocurrency-mining operations specifically, more than from the larger hyperscale/cloud data centers, "which we hadn't had those problems with... as much as we've had with the cryptocurrency facilities."

## 5. Themes raised repeatedly by the public, not fully resolved in the draft as of tonight

- **Light pollution** — raised by at least five speakers (Edmonds, Al Gates, Katherine Stevens, implicitly others) as a significant, under-addressed impact; several described multiple nearby facilities (Microsoft, a Lowe's-area facility, a new Bitcoin-mine site, a large center near the John Deere factory, one south of I-80, one near Sierra Trading Post) creating a cumulative glow visible at night ("like dawn... any time of the day"). No lighting-specific numeric standard for *operational* (non-construction) data-center lighting was discussed as being added to this amendment.
- **Cumulative/multi-facility noise impact** — raised explicitly by Janet Tolman and implicitly by Wendy Volk: if two or more data centers each individually comply with the 45 dBA limit, does their combined effect at a shared residential boundary still comply? Not answered on the record.
- **County-resident disenfranchisement** — a recurring, emotionally significant theme (Edmonds, Pixley, Tolman, Donna Frost, Katherine Stevens): county residents living adjacent to city-annexed data-center parcels bear noise/light/traffic/wildlife impacts but have no vote in Cheyenne city elections or this Commission's decisions; at least one speaker referenced pursuing legislative change to address this.
- **Topography/propagation variability** — Dr. Lori Wayne Chestnut's on-site test near a Chalk Bluff Road resident (3.3 miles from Microsoft's Bison Business Park) found audible, tangible (felt) noise at that specific property despite the resident's own hearing impairment, while nearby properties reportedly do not register the same impact — suggesting Cheyenne/Wyoming's rolling topography channels sound unpredictably and that uniform distance-based assumptions may not hold.
- **Size-tiered regulation** — Heather Madrid's detailed ask (megawatt-based size tiers, by-right only for small facilities, conditional-use + cumulative-impact review for larger ones) echoes the exact question Commissioners Laird and Hibbins separately raised with staff (should "data center" be split into large/small or hyperscale/accessory sub-categories in the use table). Staff's consistent answer: appropriate for a **future, separate use-table amendment**, not this one.
- **Setbacks** — multiple members (Madrid: 1 mile; Councilman Bowie's floor amendment: 1,000 feet from schools/churches/homes) requested a real, numeric setback; the current draft, per public comment, does not contain one (beyond the use-table/zoning-district system itself).
- **Wildlife impact** (pronghorn antelope behavior disruption) — raised once, by Anne Smith, not otherwise addressed.
- **Construction-phase impacts** (early-morning truck noise, undisclosed phased-build timelines/power draw) — raised by multiple speakers; partially addressed by the new Temporary Construction Use standards (5.8.9) but noise specifically is still unresolved for construction, per the Commission's own discussion above.
- **Water/cooling-system oversight** — Donna Frost specifically asked who regulates alternative cooling systems if closed-loop proves too costly for an operator; not directly answered in this segment of the transcript.

## 6. Charles Miller's own delivered comment (verbatim, for the record)

See full text in `RAW_TRANSCRIPT_PLTA-26-5_hearing.md`. Summary of the three-part legal/procedural framing used: (1) asked whether the still-pending noise-measurement protocol will be adopted as ordinance text or as a UDC §1.4.7 "supplemental professional standard" (which cannot modify/contradict code), and under what approval process; (2) tied the noise *definition* directly to the existing rule that discernible noise reclassifies a data center as an Industrial use, asked staff not to treat the measurement protocol as non-substantive cleanup, requested a defined operational-verification process (building on staff's own admission that at least one site passed its paper sound study but failed in operation), and asked for the exact WHO/EPA citations, the lower thresholds raised that night, the dBC-minus-dBA rationale, and whether sub-20Hz frequencies are assessed; (3) asked staff to clarify the authority, findings, decision-maker and appeal route for the new §2.3.1 subdivision-waiver pathway being used for Article 5 exceptions (evaporative-cooling exception, generator exemptions, development-agreement authority), and asked the Commission to use its §2.4.1 authority to recommend modifications or postpone if the measurement protocol isn't finalized, rather than treat it as settled.

## 7. Councilman Mark Bowie's floor amendments (Council member speaking as public/elected input, not a Commission motion)

Full detail in the raw transcript. Headline items: (a) expand the no-NDA clause to cover nonprofits, economic-development agencies, and chambers of commerce, void existing agreements of that kind, and add a termination/resignation penalty clause for staff/elected officials who violate it; (b) a hard **1,000-foot setback** from schools, churches, AND residential homes (tightening "OR"/"AREA" language to "AND"/"HOMES"); (c) require site plans to disclose **estimated monthly water usage (gallons)** and **estimated monthly electricity usage (watts)**; (d) require new data-center development to **pay for its own power and system-expansion costs without increasing costs to other utility customers** (citing unspecified Trump/Gordon executive orders), and change "may be required" to "shall be required" throughout; (e) add "coal" alongside natural gas in the energy-source section; (f) **restrict on-site wind/solar generation to the facility's own property only**, with no small-scale or industrial-scale wind/solar allowed to serve a data center unless generated on-site, subject to DEQ standards; (g) require a **30-day public comment period** on data-center development agreements.

**Councilwoman Kathy Emmons** responded immediately and directly: supported moving forward with the **staff draft as written**, arguing Bowie's amendments would "completely upend the whole thing" and require restarting the ordinance from scratch, while still endorsing the safeguards already built into the staff version.

## 8. THE VOTE — UPDATED: the Commission is adopting this in pieces, and tightening it as it goes

A second transcript segment covering the actual motion and vote has been received and added to
`RAW_TRANSCRIPT_PLTA-26-5_hearing.md`. Key developments:

- **Water/cooling language resolved (not literally a vote yet, but consensus reached):** The
  "closed-loop cooling" fight flagged back on Oct. 5 is over. Councilman Mark Rennie, who'd
  pushed for a strict closed-loop mandate, met with BOPU's Bryce Dorr and agreed to **narrower,
  more technology-neutral language**: instead of mandating "closed loop" systems by name
  (which could lock out future, even more water-efficient technology), the draft now
  **prohibits direct evaporative cooling using a potable water supply** — achieving the same
  practical intent (no evaporative water loss to cooling) without freezing the code to one
  specific technology. BOPU's stated reason for resisting a rigid closed-loop mandate: the
  "energy-water nexus" requires case-by-case evaluation of on-site water use vs. water
  consumed during the power generation that supplies the facility.
- **Tonal noise:** staff agreed, on the record, to a general directive to address tonal noise
  "to the extent possible" (raised by nearly every public commenter who mentioned Rob Rand),
  but explicitly **did not commit to specific enforceable language that night**, citing the
  practical difficulty of Hertz-band-specific regulation.
- **MOTION:** Commissioner Laird moved the full staff-recommended package (verbatim staff
  recommendation language) as a single motion. Seconded.
- **AMENDMENT — adopt Dr. Rand's noise table:** Commissioner Tripp moved to replace the
  staff's own (already-tightened) Table 1-2 with **Rob Rand's recommended, even lower numbers**
  — **40 dBA nighttime / 45 dBA daytime at residential property lines** (down from staff's
  45/55, and down from the pre-existing 2025 policy's 55/60). **PASSED 4–1** (Mathia, Tripp,
  Hibbins, Connor yes; **Laird — the commissioner who made the main motion — voted no**).
  This is a real, numeric win for the public-comment/Rand-report campaign, not just a stated
  intention — it is now part of the pending motion.
- **PROCESS CHANGE:** Commissioners Mathia and Connor, citing the volume of tonight's public
  comment on lighting and noise, successfully moved to **"divide the question"** — breaking
  the single omnibus motion into separate per-section votes (1.1.8, 1.1.9, 1.4.3, 4.5.3, 4.5.4,
  5.7.1, 5.8.9) rather than an up-or-down vote on the whole package. **PASSED 5–0 unanimous.**
- **Section-by-section votes so far:**
  - **1.1.8 Public Records; No Confidentiality Agreements — APPROVED 5–0, unanimous, as written.**
  - **1.1.9 Development Agreements — APPROVED 5–0, unanimous, as written.**
  - **1.4.3 Defined Terms — IN PROGRESS.** Already includes the adopted Rand noise table.
    Commissioner Connor then proposed **narrowing the daytime window from 7am–10pm to
    7am–9pm**, so the stricter 40 dBA nighttime limit kicks in an hour earlier (9 p.m.
    instead of 10 p.m.) — an explicit follow-through on her earlier floor comment that the
    original time windows "forgot about small children." **Transcript ends before a second
    or vote on this amendment.**
  - **4.5.3 (Water/Sewerage), 4.5.4 (Utilities), 5.7.1 (Data Centers), 5.8.9 (Construction
    Uses) — NOT YET REACHED.** None of Councilman Bowie's floor amendments (1,000-ft setback,
    monthly water/electricity reporting, NDA scope expansion to nonprofits/EDOs, on-site-only
    wind/solar) had been formally moved or voted on as of this transcript's end.
- **Also resolved on the record:** rooftop/elevated cooling fans (the specific equipment
  behind the loudest crypto-mining complaints tonight) are addressed directly in 5.7.1 —
  prohibited unless fully enclosed in an acoustically attenuated mechanical penthouse. Staff
  distinguished this from the separate, broader question of industrial-zone design standards
  (fence height, aesthetics), which it called "probably independent of the data center
  conversation" and more appropriate for a future, separate amendment.

**Status: the Commission is actively adopting PLTA-26-5 section-by-section, has already voted to
make it stricter than staff proposed (Rand's table), and has two sections fully approved
(1.1.8, 1.1.9) with the rest — including the core 5.7.1 Data Centers section — still pending as
of the last transcript provided.** Flag any further transcript segment covering 1.4.3's final
