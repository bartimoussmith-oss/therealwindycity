# Press coverage of the October 5, 2026 Planning Commission meeting (where PLTA-26-5 was postponed to tonight)

## Primary account: Wyoming Tribune-Eagle (Alyssa Tolman), republished by Laramie Boomerang and Yahoo News
- Laramie Boomerang: https://www.wyomingnews.com/laramieboomerang/news/power-generating-facility-data-center-udc-amendments-postponed-at-cheyenne-planning-commission/article_b81e4095-9e38-4ff6-ab2b-3bc2557380c6.html
- Yahoo (fuller reprint, byline Alyssa Tolman, Wyoming Tribune-Eagle, published Oct 11, 2026): https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/power-generating-facility-data-center-udc-amendments-postponed-at-cheyenne-planning-commission-035900669.html

**Headline:** "Power generating facility, data center UDC amendments postponed at Cheyenne Planning Commission"

Key reported facts:
- Two separate UDC amendments were on the Oct. 5 agenda: **PLTA-26-3** (Power Generation Facilities) and **PLTA-26-5** (Data Centers). Both were postponed "due to the complexity of the proposals." Commissioners wanted additional info from utility providers, further discussion of noise/water regulations, and consideration of how facilities of different sizes should be treated.
- **PLTA-26-3 (power generation — NOT tonight's item, postponed instead to Nov. 2, 2026):**
  - Previously postponed once already, Sept. 8, 2026, over loophole concerns (small-scale generation combined across multiple facilities to dodge regulation).
  - Revised proposal: community power generation exempt from certain review if ≤2.5 MW and no discernible noise/primary pollutants.
  - Backup generators allowed to run up to 144 hours during an emergency.
  - Commissioner **Boyd Wiggam** questioned whether that time limit is sufficient during prolonged outages.
  - Planner **Seth Lloyd**: "Our concern here is that the more we extend that out, the more we're going to have people claiming emergencies that really aren't... We want to make sure we don't have diesel generators running all the time."
  - Commissioner **Tony Laird** raised the "load shed" loophole: data centers are often asked by utilities (Black Hills Energy / High West Energy) to switch to backup generators during peak demand to prevent rolling blackouts — but that may not count as an "emergency" under the proposed language, meaning companies that cooperate with utilities could be unfairly penalized by the 144-hour cap.
  - Local utilities Black Hills Energy and High West Energy were reportedly **not formally consulted** on these specific questions before the item came back Oct. 5, though Director Charles Bloom said the city "did make sure to keep Black Hills and High West in the loop" generally.
  - Commissioner Justin Tripp motioned to postpone to Nov. 2 for utility-provider input; Chair Boyd Wiggam did not vote.
- **PLTA-26-5 (data centers — tonight's item):**
  - "The proposed regulations would codify several existing city policies while adding new requirements for data center developments, including standards for noise, water use, utilities, lighting, construction activity and emergency generators."
  - Director **Charles Bloom**, on the noise cut: *"The intent of it is that if you have an operation, it cannot be generating 45 decibels at that residential property line that's nearest to it... This is a number that we were able to get from national federal guidance."*
  - Data centers required to conduct sound studies showing noise levels at potentially affected properties; staff suggested the Commission add language clarifying that **both standard and low-frequency noise** would be measured (this became the dBC-minus-dBA provision in the staff report).
  - Rooftop fans prohibited unless acoustically enclosed; emergency generator testing limited to 30 minutes per generator per week; new requirements for temporary construction uses (lighting, dust, stormwater, hazardous materials, etc.).
  - Water use was "another major topic" — the amendment prohibits evaporative cooling systems in favor of closed-loop systems, raising concerns about increased electricity use as a tradeoff for reduced water use.
  - Commissioners ultimately **postponed to Oct. 19** (tonight) to get more information on independent sound studies, setbacks, and how differently-sized data centers should be treated.
