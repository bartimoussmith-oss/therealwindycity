## REQUEST
A Text Amendment amending the Unified Development Code (UDC) creating 1.1.8 Public Records; No Confidentiality Agreements, 1.1.9 Development Agreements, 5.7.1 Data Centers, and 5.8.9 Construction Uses; and amending 1.4.3 Defined Terms, 4.5.3 Water and Sewerage, and 4.5.4 Utilities.

**CASE NUMBER:** PLTA-26-5
**PURPOSE:** Amendments to clarify current regulations and codify current policies around data center and construction requirements.
**PREPARED BY:** Seth Lloyd, AICP, Senior Planner

## RECOMMENDED MOTION
Move to recommend the Governing Body approve text amendments to the Unified Development Code creating 1.1.8 Public Records, No Confidentiality Agreements, 1.1.9 Development Agreements, 5.7.1 Data Centers, and 5.8.9 Construction Uses; and amending 1.4.3 Defined Terms, 4.5.3 Water and Sewerage, and 4.5.4 Utilities as outlined in this staff report and recommended by staff, noting the amendments meet the review criteria of UDC 2.4.1.d; and further, the Planning Commission authorizes the City Attorney's Office to make minor, non-substantive, technical, or legal corrections to the draft ordinance prior to final consideration by the City Council, provided such changes do not, in the determination of Planning staff, materially alter the intent of the ordinance or conflict with this recommendation.

## BACKGROUND
The City's UDC was adopted by the Governing Body on January 23, 2012 (Ordinance No. 3943, effective April 30, 2012). It is reviewed annually under UDC 1.1.7. The City Council, Planning and Development Director, and City Engineer are authorized under UDC 2.4.1.b to bring forward text amendments on their own initiative; UDC 1.4.4 also directs any use that can't be classified by the use table to go through the text-amendment process.

This amendment addresses city-wide standards for public transparency, noise, utilities, specific use standards for data center development, and supplemental provisions for long-term temporary construction activity.

Key framing from staff: *"One element of data center development that was not originally anticipated was the sheer scale of construction impacts... data centers (and other, similar large-scale construction projects) have impacts that last years, not weeks; and at a significantly greater scale."*

Staff considered amending the existing definition of "Data Center" (UDC 1.4.4.f) but decided it already excludes small server-room-type facilities in ordinary office buildings, since it's limited to buildings "specifically designed or modified to" house data/transaction-processing equipment.

## SUMMARY OF CHANGES: Article 1

- New **1.1.8 Public Records; No Confidentiality Agreements** and **1.1.9 Development Agreements**.
- New definitions in **1.4.3**: "Discernable Noise," "Temporary Construction Use," "Workforce Accommodation Quarters."

Staff's own framing: *"City Staff do not currently sign non-disclosure agreements nor withhold documents required to be released through a public records request... The proposed section would not change how the City functions, it just demonstrates to the public and prospective developers what can and cannot be done by City staff."*

On the noise definition specifically, staff is **lowering** its own existing policy:

> "A reduction in the allowed nighttime noise level in residential areas to 45dBA. The policy document had a sound level higher than this and staff research has subsequently... found the policy document's level is likely too high. The World Health Organization recommends that road traffic noise not exceed 45dBL at night. Multiple other communities which have set nighttime dBA levels set them in the 40dBA to 50dBA range."

And on low-frequency noise:

> "Staff proposes a dBC minus dBA restriction on noise... future data center technologies specifically designed to avoid dBA regulations could possibly [cause problems], too."

### New definition — Discernable Noise (1.4.3)
For uses classified as industrial, or non-industrial uses that consistently run on-site machinery, "discernable noise at the property line" shall not exceed:

| Adjacent Zone (and Qualifying Conditions) | Daytime (7am–10pm) | Nighttime (10pm–7am) |
|---|---|---|
| City Zone Districts: AG, CB, BP, P, LI, HI, non-residential PUDs | ≤ 65 dBA | ≤ 60 dBA |
| Unincorporated County Zone Districts: CB, LI, HI, P, MU, non-residential PUDs, LU tracts over 10 acres (undeveloped/agricultural), LU tracts developed with utility/non-residential uses | ≤ 65 dBA | ≤ 60 dBA |
| **All Other Zone Districts (i.e., residential)** | **≤ 55 dBA** | **≤ 45 dBA** |

Also counted as "discernable noise at the property line":
(a) any consistent sound where the dBC measurement exceeds the dBA measurement by 20 dB;
(b) any short burst of sound exceeding Chapter 8.56 (Noise Control) of municipal code.

> **Compare to the current (Aug. 20, 2025) policy this replaces** — see `EXISTING_2025_NOISE_POLICY.md`: that memo set non-residential limits at 70/65 dBA (day/night) and all-other-zone limits at 60/55 dBA (day/night). This amendment tightens non-residential to 65/60 and residential to 55/45.

New definitions:
- **Temporary Construction Use** — construction trailers, material/equipment storage yards, staging areas, worker parking, concrete/asphalt batch plants, authorized for the life of an active building/site permit, removed on completion or expiration.
- **Workforce Accommodation Quarters** — temporary residential/support facilities tied strictly to an active development project: modular housing, RV sites with utility hookups, resident-only amenities (dining, laundry, wellness, recreation, admin offices, security, shuttle service, utility infrastructure).

## SUMMARY OF CHANGES: Article 4 (Utilities)

Edits to **4.5.3 Water and Sanitary Sewerage** and **4.5.4 Utilities** codify existing BOPU practice: plat/site-plan approval contingent on demonstrated adequate water/sewer/utility capacity and easements; new development must pay its proportional share of system-expansion costs as determined by BOPU or applicable utility providers; utility statements on service capacity "may be required." Also newly exempts **high-voltage primary electric transmission lines operating at 115kV or above, sub-transmission lines, and associated utility equipment subject to utility engineering constraints** from the standard undergrounding requirement.

## SUMMARY OF CHANGES: New 5.7.1 Data Centers (the core of the amendment)

Staff's framing: "This section largely is a collection of references to other sections of code, or specifically states that data centers do not receive preferential treatment under code (e.g. the City does not waive impact fees)."

Specifically, this section:
- Prohibits rooftop cooling fans unless enclosed (new standard) — *"The majority of sound problems experienced in the Cheyenne region have come from these types of units and efforts to mitigate the noise levels of these elevated units have necessitated the creation of large scale sound barriers higher than the site's structures themselves."*
- Requires a sound study (already current policy).
- Exempts equipment testing and interior lot lines within a single project from sound requirements (already current policy).
- Requires closed-loop cooling systems (already current BOPU policy).

### Full text of new UDC 5.7.1 Data Centers

In districts where data centers are allowed, they shall meet the following standards in addition to all other lot and building standards for the district:

**a. Noise.** All properties shall meet the noise requirements of 5.1.4 for data center uses or receive appropriate approvals for an Industrial use, with the following modifications:
1. *Rooftop fans.* Prohibited unless fully enclosed within an acoustically attenuated mechanical penthouse that satisfies property-line dBA/dBC limits.
2. *Sound study required.* Every data center shall submit a sound study showing, at minimum, plan view of the site and sound levels in all directions and any potentially affected properties, including sound levels under emergency operating conditions.
3. *Exempt Activities* (not considered "discernable noise at the property line"):
   (a) sound discernable only at interior property lines between developments under common ownership or a common site plan;
   (b) emergency generator testing, provided it doesn't exceed 30 minutes/week/generator, and testing occurs only 9:00 a.m.–4:00 p.m.;
   (c) emergency generator operation during an actual utility-supply failure, for up to 144 hours (six days) per event.

**b. Water.** Data centers using water for cooling shall be approved by BOPU and are **required to use closed-loop water cooling systems** unless BOPU or City Council determines an evaporative system is necessary due to operational redundancy, water-quality management, utility-infrastructure constraints, or present/future impacts on regional water availability.

**c. Sanitary sewer.** All data centers must be approved by BOPU for sanitary sewer systems and usage; capacity/safe operation verified by BOPU.

**d. Surface discharge.** Must meet stormwater/SWPPP and environmental-health requirements. Water used by a data center may never be discharged onto the surface of the property unless approved via a Subdivision Standards Waiver (2.3.1).

**e. Electricity.** Must demonstrate access to adequate electricity capacity (4.5.4). Any on-site continual power-generation facility on/near a data-center campus must meet all requirements of the applicable power-generation type (i.e., ties into the companion PLTA-26-3 power-generation standards).

**f. Natural Gas.** Any large-scale natural-gas facility proposed on a data-center site must demonstrate adequate natural-gas capacity (4.5.4).

**g. Construction Activities.** Must meet the new 5.8.9 Temporary Construction Use requirements.

**h. Lighting.** Must meet UDC 6.4 lighting requirements (or 5.8.9 while under construction).

**i. Agreements.** City shall not withhold public records nor enter into an NDA with any data-center development (per new 1.1.8).

**j. Fees and Required Improvements Applicable.** All impact fees, application fees, penalty/enforcement fees, and required site/subdivision improvements apply to data centers — no special treatment.

**k. State and Federal Laws Applicable.** All federal/state regulations apply; City may request evidence of compliance.

## SUMMARY OF CHANGES: New 5.8.9 Temporary Construction Use

Staff's framing: *"Construction uses have largely received a 'pass' in meeting zoning code requirements... the sheer size and quantity of recent data center construction sites have demonstrated the flaws in giving an undefined 'pass,' but the rules proposed would apply to any construction site, including large-scale construction sites of alternate industrial uses."*

New-construction site plans must submit documentation as part of the site-plan application showing construction plans. Off-site construction staging in non-industrial areas requires conditional-use approval. Sites must show how they mitigate dust; screen from residential sites; manage stormwater; manage construction traffic; connect to utilities; and reclaim any off-site staging areas.

Lighting note: temporary construction lighting is subject to the same shielding/light-trespass limits as permanent uses, but temporary fixtures may exceed standard height limits so poles can direct light downward and avoid off-site glare — modeled on **Fort Collins, Broomfield, Centennial, Aurora, and Douglas County, Colorado**.

### Key provisions of 5.8.9
- **b. Applicability/process:** For construction associated with developments subject to site-plan approval and exceeding 60 days, approved in conjunction with the site plan via site layout + narrative; Director may impose standards to mitigate neighbor/transportation/worker-housing impacts.
- **c. Submittal Requirements:** scaled site layout (boundaries, structures, adjacent uses, temporary-structure footprints, staging/stockpile areas, access/circulation, screening/lighting/dust measures, site-restoration plan) plus a narrative addressing each standard below.
- **d. Relationship to Zone District:** on-site temporary construction uses (except Workforce Accommodation Quarters) permitted in all zoning districts upon building permit. Off-site temporary uses and Workforce Accommodation Quarters require Conditional Use approval (2.2.4) in all districts except BP, LI, HI.
- **e. Standards:**
  1. *Dust/Air Quality* — actively suppress fugitive dust; cover/water stockpiles over 4 feet.
  2. *Residential Buffers* — 10-ft setback from residential lot lines for sites under 2 acres; 50-ft for sites 2+ acres.
  3. *Signage* — non-residential sign rules apply; perimeter fence-wrap screening exempt from sign-area limits if non-commercial/project-description content; OSHA/safety postings fully exempt.
  4. *Stormwater* — SWPPP required (UDC 3.2).
  5. *Temporary Lighting* — fully shielded, downward-directed, capped at 0.5 foot-candles at property lines; height limits may be exceeded if shielding standards met.
  6. *Traffic Management* — approved Traffic Control Plan, non-residential access routes, track-out control pads, daily roadway sweeping.
  7. *Utilities* — adequate water/power/sanitary/fire-protection connections required before operations start.
- **f. Site Reclamation & Financial Security:** all temporary structures/equipment/gravel pads/stockpiles/waste removed and site restored within 30 days of construction completion or permit expiration. Off-site temporary uses require a performance bond/letter of credit/cash escrow equal to **125% of an engineer's estimated cleanup/regrading/re-vegetation cost** before permit issuance.

## ANALYSIS OF CONFORMANCE WITH REVIEW CRITERIA (UDC 2.4.1.d)

1. **Comprehensive Plan:** *"PlanCheyenne did not consider data center development, nor industrial development at the size and scope presented by the data center interest in the community."* Staff cites PlanCheyenne Policy 1.1.B (responsiveness to changing market conditions), Foundation 7 (utility infrastructure/capacity), and Principal 4.7 (mitigating industrial truck traffic) as support.
2. **State Statutory Requirements:** Cites Wyo. Stat. 15-1-601 (municipal zoning authority to regulate location/use of buildings/structures/land for trade, industry, etc.) as authorizing basis.
3. **Health, Safety, and Public Welfare:** *"Most of these elements are already in code, but significant noise and construction operations were poorly defined. The impacts of noise and construction can negatively affect the public health, safety, and welfare if not properly managed."*
