# Background: the 2026 Cheyenne data-center fight that PLTA-26-5 sits inside

PLTA-26-5 is a narrow technical UDC amendment (noise/water/construction standards), but it did not arise in a vacuum. It is the regulatory tail end of a year-long, high-conflict series of data-center land-use fights in Cheyenne. This file is background/context only — **none of the events below are PLTA-26-5 itself**, and none should be presented as if they are part of tonight's agenda item.

## Timeline of the broader saga (2026)

- **March 2026 — Meta "Project Cosmo" water contamination.** A rare-bacterium contamination of the city water system was traced to Meta's "Project Cosmo" data center. Critics say it was not disclosed to the public for roughly three months. (Referenced across 2026 Cheyenne coverage; see Cap City News archives, e.g. "BOPU to release timeline on Cheyenne wastewater contamination" linked from https://capcity.news/latest-news/2026/07/14/cheyenne-passes-microsoft-data-center-land-annexation-after-more-than-5-hours-of-discussion/.) This incident is the backdrop for why PLTA-26-5's new closed-loop-cooling-required / evaporative-cooling-restricted standard (5.7.1.b) and new water/sewer provisions (4.5.3/4.5.4) draw heightened public attention even though staff describes them as "already current BOPU policy."
- **April–July 2026 — Microsoft's land acquisitions and annexations.** Microsoft purchased land from the family of Wyoming Sen. Cynthia Lummis for a planned data-center campus. A ~3,200-acre package was announced in spring 2026; a ~3,459.99-acre annexation — reported as the **largest annexation in city history** — was approved after roughly two months of Council/Public Services Committee back-and-forth, passing in July 2026 after "more than 5 hours of discussion" at the final vote. (https://capcity.news/latest-news/2026/07/14/cheyenne-passes-microsoft-data-center-land-annexation-after-more-than-5-hours-of-discussion/) A June 2026 Microsoft land-use package reportedly failed to get required Planning Commission votes at one stage.
- **May 2026 — Data-center moratorium proposal fails 9-1 on City Council.** A proposed moratorium on new data-center development failed by a 9-1 vote. (Referenced in prior-session research, `2026-09-14-council/`, and corroborated by national data-center-tracking coverage.)
- **June 2026 — Public panel figures.** A public panel reported roughly **27 data centers operating, under construction, or announced in Cheyenne** (about 10 operating, 5 under construction, 9 announced) as of mid-2026; ~31 statewide in Wyoming.
- **September 2026 — Orchard Hills (Trammell Crow Company) fight intensifies.** Trammell Crow's 162-acre Orchard Hills data-center proposal, adjacent to the existing Meta campus and the Orchard Valley residential neighborhood, is denied by the Planning Commission (unanimous) on a related future land-use map item, but City Council overrules that denial. Company (per Nakashima) commits on the record to a 500+ ft buffer and "zero-spill lighting" as binding conditions, plus a promised Wallick/Parsley road expansion. Residents (e.g., Donna O'Donnell) raise "domino effect" concerns about future industrial encroachment, dust, light pollution, constant noise.
  - **Sept 21, 2026 — Hitching Post District annexation (adjacent controversy, different site).** Landowner and Wyoming House District 7 Rep. Doug Samuelson tells the Public Services Committee that the 652.67-acre Hitching Post District — future home of the PRCA Hall of Fame and Museum of the American Cowboy — will **never** contain a data center as a matter of deed/use restriction, explicitly distancing that project from the data-center wave. (https://capcity.news/community/city/2026/09/21/landowner-says-hitching-post-district-future-site-of-prca-forbids-data-centers-as-use/)
  - **Sept 28, 2026 — "AI Data Center Revolt" bus tour stop.** A national protest campaign against "massive industrial development" in the form of data centers staged a stop at the Cheyenne Municipal Building (2101 O'Neil Ave.), part of a cross-country tour. (https://capcity.news/community/2026/09/23/lobby-group-protesting-data-centers-plans-sept-28-cheyenne-stop-during-national-tour/)
- **Sept 30 / Oct 5, 2026 — PLTA-26-5 and PLTA-26-3 first come to the Planning Commission** (PLTA-26-3 already once-postponed from Sept 8), both postponed again — PLTA-26-5 to tonight, PLTA-26-3 to Nov. 2.
- **Oct 5–12, 2026 — Orchard Hills final votes.** City Council advances Orchard Hills on second reading Oct. 5 (annexation + two zoning changes, overruling the PC's unanimous denial); Councilmembers **Mark Moody** and **Larry Wolfe** vote against every item. On **Oct. 12**, Council rejects a last-minute bid to postpone and finalizes the annexation package on third reading after "more than two hours of testimony." (https://capcity.news/community/city/2026/10/12/rejecting-last-minute-postponement-bid-cheyenne-finalizes-orchard-hills-annexation/)
- **Oct 17, 2026 — Orchard Hills fully approved.** Council takes the final vote on all four remaining Orchard Hills items (annexation, ag-zoning, light-industrial rezoning, and a future land-use map amendment from mixed-use residential to mixed-use employatment) — all pass on third reading with no amendments or delays, despite sustained resident opposition. (https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/orchard-hills-data-center-approved-035900693.html)
- **Oct 19, 2026 (tonight) — PLTA-26-5 returns to Planning Commission.** This is the only item on tonight's PC agenda.

## Why this context matters for reading PLTA-26-5
1. **The "no NDA / public records" clause (new UDC 1.1.8) reads as a direct response to this year's transparency criticisms** — Skybox's relationship with King Ranch was characterized on the record (Sept. 14, 2026 PSC meeting) as "verbal conversations only," with no written lease and no written "Commitment to Cheyenne" at that time; the Meta water-contamination disclosure delay (~3 months) was separately criticized. Staff's own framing ("just demonstrates to the public and prospective developers what can and cannot be done") suggests this section may be more about reassurance than substantive change, since staff says the City doesn't currently sign NDAs anyway.
2. **The closed-loop cooling requirement (new 5.7.1.b) is a direct response to water-use controversy** — in the Sept. 14, 2026 PSC meeting, the Mayor stated data centers used 95 million of 7.17 billion gallons (1.3%) of city water last year, a figure publicly disputed by at least one commenter ("Wolf") who rebutted the Mayor's broader claim that DEQ and "19 agencies" vet data centers, noting DEQ only regulates backup-generator air permits and stormwater — not water use or wildlife impact.
3. **The 30-min/week generator testing cap and 144-hour emergency-operation allowance in 5.7.1.a directly mirror the parallel, still-pending PLTA-26-3 (Power Generation Facilities) amendment** — the same 144-hour figure, same "is it really an emergency" tension (utility-requested load-shedding vs. genuine outage) flagged by Commissioner Tony Laird on Oct. 5 for PLTA-26-3, applies equally to data centers' own backup generators under 5.7.1.a(3)(c). PLTA-26-3 remains unresolved (postponed to Nov. 2), so expect this same tension to resurface for PLTA-26-5 tonight even though it's not the generator-specific text amendment.
4. **Zero public comment on the record as of the Sept. 28 staff report is a striking contrast with the Orchard Hills, Hitching Post, and Microsoft fights**, all of which drew heavy public turnout and testimony throughout 2026. It suggests this technical text amendment may be flying under the radar relative to the annexation/zoning fights that have dominated headlines — tonight may be the first real public test of that.
5. **Case planners Charles Bloom and Seth Lloyd are the same staff duo** handling essentially every one of 2026's data-center-adjacent code fights (PLTA-26-3 power generation, PLTA-26-5 data centers, and — per the same-night PSC meeting record — related electric-fence and special-use items).
