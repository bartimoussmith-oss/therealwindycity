# Item 19 — UDC §6.2.8, unpaved/gravel parking — FINAL script
# (reconstructed from live delivery, meeting clock ~13:29-16:21; this is the
# version actually spoken on the record)

Charles Miller, item 19.

Before the substance, on the record: Director Bloom confirmed this ordinance
was one combined ordinance when it went to the Planning Commission, then split
into two afterward. That's exactly why Dr. Aldrich couldn't find the exceptions
language in tonight's packet a few minutes ago. I'd note this is the same kind
of document mismatch WyFresh Farms raised in writing against item 18 — that the
slide shown at the August work session didn't match the posted materials.
That's two separate, dated instances of the same problem: one from the public,
one from the dais.

Second, Director Bloom told this committee this ordinance has been on the UDC
work plan for the last couple of years and was pulled out of the agricultural
ordinance for this cycle. By staff's own account, this isn't actually one of
the four agricultural ordinances — it's a standalone parking policy bundled
with items 18, 20, and 21 in today's agenda and in the public-input comment
count. That matters because this is also the only one of the four the Planning
Commission split on 3-2, and Director Bloom just described it live as broader
than the staff report suggests — covering lots over 10 spaces and landscaping
relief, not just small infill lots under 10. I'd ask directly: did the 3-2 vote
happen with the benefit of this broader framing, or on a narrower ordinance
than what's being presented today?

Third, I just heard Dr. Rinney ask the right question: could a business with no
walk-in or drive-up customers — his example was a plumbing shop that only
sends crews out — qualify for gravel parking simply by declaring it doesn't
serve walk-in members of the public? Staff began to answer that it would not
qualify, but the full reasoning wasn't finished on the record. I'd ask that the
complete answer be stated clearly before a vote, because this is the same
vagueness problem WyFresh Farms raised in writing on item 18, asking how "small
scale" and "intermittent" are defined and at whose discretion. If council
members themselves are testing this ordinance for loopholes minutes before
voting on it, that's a sign the standard needs a clear line, not just a
director's case-by-case discretion.

On ADA: Dr. Emmons already got a clear answer — ADA spaces stay paved, with a
compliant path to the door or trailhead, spelled out in subsection C. That part
is resolved. Given a mislabeled document, an ordinance staff says isn't really
part of the ag package it's riding with, the only 3-2 vote among the four, and
a live loophole question still being worked out on the dais, I'd ask this
committee to take up item 19 on its own, not in the same motion as 18, 20, and
21.

## ADDENDUM — the Frontier Days connection (researched, held in reserve)

Not delivered live; prepared after the above remarks as a deeper, requested
"dig in harder" connection, flagged explicitly as an inference/question, not an
asserted fact.

Item 34(f) tonight is a Grant of Easement between the City and F.E. Warren AFB
for a brand-new 36.5-acre Park-N-Ride on the old base golf course (access via
Patriot Drive/Gate 1), which the easement's own Exhibit A says is granted
"exclusively for construction activities in anticipation of and support for the
2027 Cheyenne Frontier Days season." That's a near-exact match to two of item
19's new exceptions: "Low-Usage or Overflow Parking" (peak use under 55 times a
year, explicitly including overflow parking for "Entertainment venues... and
similar outdoor and assembly uses" — CFD runs about 10 days a year), and
"Regional Stormwater Management" (relevant given paving 36.5 acres would be a
major drainage and cost question). The easement was approved as to form on
October 7th — five days before this ordinance was even introduced to Council on
October 12th. The easement term itself runs one year, November 1, 2026 to
November 1, 2027, so it's an annual-renewal instrument, not a standing grant.
No document reviewed states whether the new lot will be paved or gravel — this
is a well-grounded question, not a confirmed fact.

Ready-to-read add-on paragraph (use now as a preview, or hold for item 34(f)):

"One more thing for the record on item 19: tonight's agenda also includes item
34(f), a new easement with F.E. Warren Air Force Base for a 36.5-acre Park-N-Ride
built specifically for the 2027 Cheyenne Frontier Days season. That's a
low-usage, overflow, outdoor-assembly parking lot of exactly the kind item 19's
new exceptions are written for. I'd ask staff directly: is the CFD Park-N-Ride at
F.E. Warren going to rely on the exceptions in this ordinance, and if so, will it
be paved or gravel?"

## Outcome (confirmed live)
Passed 2nd reading unanimously. Before the motion, Councilman Rinney said on
the record: "I would like to point out that this is listed as item 19, a
separate item from the others that are probably more ag-related" — echoing the
closing ask above almost verbatim. A member of the Mayor's Council for People
with Disabilities (Greg Smith) also spoke in support of the ADA answer already
given.
