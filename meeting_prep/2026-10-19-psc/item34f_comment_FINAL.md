# Item 34(f) — Grant of Easement, City of Cheyenne / Dept. of the Air Force,
# F.E. Warren AFB — Frontier Days Park-N-Ride — FINAL script (v3, CONFIRMED)

Charles Miller, for the record, item 34(f).

Engineer Cobb just confirmed on the record what I came on tonight to ask: this
is a 2,868-stall lot, only 39 of which — the ADA stalls — are paved. In his own
words, quote, "this is an aggregate parking lot... it is not paved other than
the ADA areas." This committee passed an ordinance earlier on this very agenda
— item 19 — that creates a brand-new exception for exactly this kind of lot:
"Low-Usage or Overflow Parking," for uses under 55 times a year, naming
overflow parking for "Entertainment venues... and similar outdoor and assembly
uses" by name, plus a new "Regional Stormwater Management" exception for lots
this size. I'd ask staff to confirm directly, on the record, that this is the
mechanism making nearly 2,900 gravel stalls possible, because nothing in either
item's packet draws that line for the public.

Second, Ms. Hill just told this committee no public dollars are involved and
that Frontier Days is funding the parking lot entirely. I'd ask the committee
to separate two things staff already put on the record: construction cost and
liability. Cobb said the City accepts this property "as is" and assumes
responsibility for insurance, compliance, and site restoration — and that the
Air Force can terminate this easement on 120 days' notice. If Frontier Days is
paying to build 2,868 gravel stalls, I'd ask who is paying the City's insurance
and restoration obligation if the Air Force exercises that 120-day clause,
because "no public dollars" so far has only been said about construction, not
about the liability this committee is being asked to accept tonight.

Third, Cobb also disclosed two agreements still to come: a construction
agreement between the City and CFD, and a long-term lease between the City and
the Air Force or CFD for ongoing maintenance — meaning tonight's one-year
easement is just the first of at least three instruments on this project. I'd
ask that those two follow-on agreements come back to this committee, not just
to the governing body, given the surfacing and liability questions raised here
tonight.

Finally, the timing: this easement was approved as to form on October 7th —
five days before item 19 was introduced to Council on October 12th. I'm not
suggesting one caused the other. I am asking whether these two items were ever
discussed together internally, because tonight is the first time that
connection has been stated on the record.

## CONFIRMED facts (as of this transcript)
- 2,868 total stalls; 39 ADA stalls concrete paved; **"this is an aggregate
  parking lot... it is not paved other than the ADA areas"** — Tom Cobb,
  City Engineer, on the record. This resolves the open question from v2.
- Entry/access off Randall, near Gate 1.
- Two more agreements still coming: (1) City/CFD construction agreement,
  (2) long-term City/Air Force-or-CFD maintenance lease. Tonight's easement is
  a 1-year term only — first of at least three instruments.
- Mary Kay Hill (CFD Board of Directors), public comment: no public dollars
  involved "at this point"; CFD is funding the parking lot portion entirely;
  City is lead/signatory on the easement; future phase 2 is a pedestrian bridge
  from the lot to CFD grounds, with Laramie County and the State of Wyoming as
  additional partners at that stage; stated goal is relieving neighborhood
  traffic/safety pressure south of CFD grounds and improving the park-and-ride
  visitor experience.
- Not yet addressed on the record: who bears cost if the Air Force exercises
  its 120-day termination right, given the City (not CFD) holds the insurance/
  restoration obligation under the easement terms.

## Outcome (confirmed live — delivered, staff responded directly)
Delivered nearly verbatim from v3. Engineer Cobb responded directly on the
record:
1. Jurisdictional rebuttal: the easement property is OUTSIDE Cheyenne city
   limits (Air Force jurisdiction), so the City's UDC - including item 19 -
   has no legal application there. The aggregate surfacing is driven by the
   Air Force's own on-site detention requirements, not by item 19's
   exceptions. "There is no relationship... this is just a timing issue."
   This cuts the legal-mechanism claim (item 19 does NOT actually enable this
   lot), though it does not deny the coincidence/timing point, which Cobb
   himself labeled "a timing issue" rather than disputing outright.
2. Liability question NOT directly answered: Cobb deflected to "the
   agreements coming forthright" and addressed only construction-phase
   contractor insurance ("contractor will be responsible with their insurance
   to cover anything during construction... standard agreements with
   anyone"). The long-term insurance/restoration obligation under the 120-day
   Air Force termination clause remains unaddressed - worth tracking when the
   construction agreement and long-term lease come before committee.
Committee moved toward a motion afterward; no further public comment.

CORRECTION FOR THE RECORD if referencing this item again: do not repeat the
claim that this lot "relies on item 19's exceptions" as established fact -
staff rebutted that specific mechanism (jurisdiction). The accurate framing
going forward is: UDC item 19 does not apply to this easement property
(outside city limits), the gravel surface is an independent Air Force
decision, and the only unresolved thread is long-term liability/insurance
once the City's 1-year easement gives way to the promised longer-term lease.
