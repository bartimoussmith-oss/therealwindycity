### February 9, 2026 amendments (from the Record of Proceedings)

1. **Dr. Aldrich: change "or" to "and" in 1.28.020(b)(2)**, so a warrant would require both nonconformity *and* an immediate risk to health or safety. **Failed.** Only Dr. Aldrich and Mr. Moody voted yes.
2. **Dr. Aldrich: remove "determine, discover or" from 1.28.020(b)(3)**, so an inspection could no longer be justified as "necessary to determine, discover or verify." **Carried 6–4.**
   - Yes: Dr. Aldrich, Mayor Collins, Mr. Esquibel, Mr. Moody, Dr. Rinne, Mr. White.
   - No: Dr. Emmons, Mr. Laybourn, Mr. Segrave, Mr. Wolfe.
3. **Mr. Moody: define "area"** as large open spaces, parking areas, and exteriors of a single commercial or abandoned property. **Failed.** Only Dr. Aldrich and Mr. Moody voted yes. Mr. Wolfe raised a point of order about verbal amendments. The Mayor ruled them permitted under Robert's Rules and asked that future amendments be in writing.
4. The substitute as amended carried unanimously. The main motion as amended carried, with Esquibel, Moody, and Rinne voting no.

## What the adopted text says (Feb 5, 2026 substitute)

Source: [Proposed Substitute, Feb 9, 2026 packet](https://github.com/therealwindycity/cheyenne-archives-2025-2026/blob/main/1770624000Feb%20%209%2C%202026%20-%20City%20Council%20Meeting/Supporting_Documents/1770624000Feb%20%209%2C%202026%20-%20City%20Council%20Meeting%20-%20Proposed%20Substitute%20-%20Proposed%20Substitute.pdf)

- **Who issues warrants:** "A **municipal judge** may issue an administrative inspection warrant only upon proper application of the fire chief, the fire marshal or the chief building official" (§1.28.010.A).
- **Before applying:** the city must show it attempted personal contact at the property and sent a letter to the owner's last known address (§1.28.020).
- **Probable cause:** required, supported by a sworn affidavit (§1.28.020.A).
- **Reporting:** within 10 business days after a warrant is executed, the applying official must give the Governing Body a written incident report. The report includes the application and affidavit, with personally identifying information limited.

## Main public objections on the record

- **Municipal judge versus district court judge.** Charles Miller argued that W.S. 35-7-1046 provides for administrative inspection warrants issued by a district court judge or commissioner, so a municipal judge lacks the authority. He also argued the draft lacked pre-notice, citing *City of Los Angeles v. Patel* (2015). See {{yt:city-council-2026-01-12@01:10:32}}, {{yt:city-council-2026-01-12@01:11:01}}, and {{yt:city-council-2026-01-26@00:33:43}}.
  - **Outcome:** the adopted text still names a municipal judge. The substitute added pre-application contact and letter requirements.
  - **Note:** W.S. 35-7-1046 sits in Title 35, Chapter 7, the Wyoming Controlled Substances Act. Whether it governs municipal code inspections is a legal question this page does not decide.
- **"Determine, discover" as a license for fishing expeditions.**
  - Patricia McCoy presented a written strike sheet and cited the Wyoming Constitution's privacy protections (captioned "Oil versus State"): {{yt:city-council-2026-02-09@02:18:16}}.
  - Charles Miller called 1.28.020(B)(3) "a fatal defect… the poison pill" ({{yt:city-council-2026-02-09@02:42:03}}) and supported the strike ({{yt:city-council-2026-02-09@03:06:42}}).
  - **Outcome:** Dr. Aldrich's amendment to strike the words carried 6–4.
- **Reporting.** A member recommended putting the reporting requirement in the city code so it would be permanent ({{yt:city-council-2026-02-09@03:25:58}}). Miller later thanked the council for "codifying a reporting requirement for administrative warrants" ({{yt:city-council-2026-02-09@04:21:04}}).
