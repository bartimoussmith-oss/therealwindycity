# THE ADVERSARY'S CODE
## Vol. VI — The Complete Legal Cross-Reference: The Entire Miller Narrative Read Through Every Body of Law It Touches

**Task:** one treatise that cross-references constitutional law (federal and Wyoming), statutory law (U.S. Code, Wyoming statutes, county and municipal codes), common-law doctrine, administrative law, civil-procedure and access-to-justice law, and the jurisprudence of direct democracy — against the full Miller record (Vols. I–V: noise war, warrants, Flock, scanner night, the meeting circuit, Highlands, the referendum, August 10, and the $0.00 lawsuit kit).

**Citation discipline (three registers, marked throughout):**
- **[R]** = Record-verified: facts/quotes from the session's transcripts, agendas, and page log.
- **[C]** = Canonical authority: standard, checkable citations to real cases and statutes.
- **[M]** = Movement-cited: citations as they appear in Miller's own documents — **audited** here as ✓ confirmed / ≈ plausible-but-glossed / ✗ unverified-or-mislabeled.

**Headline audit results before the body:** the movement's citations are substantially *real* — *Foster's Inc. v. City of Laramie*, 718 P.2d 868 (Wyo. 1986) ✓; *Wilson Advisory Committee v. Bd. of County Comm'rs*, 2012 WY 163 ✓ (verified: Wyoming Supreme Court reversed for **failure to make required findings**, holding the record "insufficient to permit judicial review" — though the "Topanga Analytical Bridge" label is the movement's gloss, borrowed from *Topanga Growth Council v. ## PART I — THE SPEECH DOCKET: FORUM, PETITION, AND THE BOT

**1.1 The two constitutions.** Wyoming's own free-speech clause, Art. 1, § 20 [C] — "Every person may freely speak, write and publish on all subjects, being responsible for the abuse of that liberty" — predates and is textually broader than the First Amendment's speech clause (it adds *publish*). Miller's Apr 20 formal notice [R] pleaded "the First Amendment of the United States Constitution"; his Aug 19 petition [R] added Wyo. Const. Art. 1, § 20 to Count II. A Wyoming court could read § 20 independently — a state-ground ruling the U.S. Supreme Court could not review (*Michigan v. Long*, 463 U.S. 1032 (1983) [C]).

**1.2 The forum doctrine — and the case that controls here.** A city-council public-comment period is a **designated/limited public forum**; the government may impose content-based *topic* and time limits but **may not discriminate among viewpoints**. The controlling authority in Wyoming's circuit is ***Make Salt Lake v. Salt Lake City Corp.*, 875 F.3d 1312 (10th Cir. 2017)** [C]: removal of speakers from a council comment period for criticizing officials by name was a viewpoint-discriminatory, plausibly unconstitutional application, while topic/time rules survived. Supporting pillars: *City of Madison Joint Sch. Dist. v. Wis. Emp. Rel. Comm'n*, 429 U.S. 167 (1976) [C] (a public body open for speech may not silence a viewpoint); *Board of Airport Comm'rs v. Jews for Jesus*, 482 U.S. 569 (1987) [C]; against him: *Minn. State Bd. for Cmty. Colls. v. Knight*, 465 U.S. 271 (1984) [C] (a body may listen to whom it chooses — content choice, not viewpoint penalty).

**Application [R]:** the Apr 20 record is his best exhibit — developer Glissmeyer on the data-center business plan, LEADS' Hale on data-center water, then Chairwoman Emmons to a resident: *"this issue is not about data centers. This is about the annexation."* Same topic, opposite valence, differential ruling — the *Make Salt Lake* pattern nearly on all fours. The muting/bypass record (Apr 21, 27 [R]) adds a *custom*: under *Monell* (Part II.4) a persistent practice is policy. **Grade: B+/A− as pleaded.** The discipline: viewpoint claims lose when the chair's ruling is defensible *scope* policing (*Knight*); the transcript comparison — developer-allowed vs. critic-cut — is what converts scope into discrimination.

**1.3 The bot question as a legal question.** There is **no law requiring human authorship of public comment** — no statute, no ordinance, no doctrine [C]. Anonymous and pseudonymous speech is protected (*McIntyre v. Ohio Elections Comm'n*, 514 U.S. 334 (1995); *Watchtower Bible & Tract Soc'y v. Village of Stratton*, 536 U.S. 150 (2002) [C]); the AI dimension is squarely the ACUS mass-comment problem Vol. II priced. The city's lawful tools were always content-neutral time/manner/place rules (topic, duration, turn order) — never identity tests. Wolfe's Apr 21 demand that Miller appear in person "so that we can actually verify that he's not just a chat GPT robot" [R] proposes an **identity qualification on speech** with no doctrinal footing; under *Make Salt Lake* it is evidence, not a remedy. Mirror image: a councilman's own vote is not his personal speech (*Nevada Comm'n on Ethics v. Carrigan*, 564 U.S. 117 (2011) [C]) — which cuts *against* councilmembers who claim speech rights as a defense for how they run the microphone.

**1.4 Petition-clause protection — both directions.** Miller's campaign is Petition Clause activity at maximum intensity; retaliatory response is analyzed under *Mt. Healthy City Sch. Dist. v. Doyle*, 429 U.S. 274 (1977) [C] (but-for motivation). The city's Aug 10 suit is *also* petitioning (litigation = protected activity, *BE&K Constr. Co. v. NLRB*, 536 U.S. 516 (2002) [C]), and his counter-strategy (Part VIII) invokes the same clause — his pre-drafted **antitrust disclaimer** [R] is his acknowledgment that he lives adjacent to *Trucking Unlimited* territory. **1.5 The gag proposal** (Wolf, May 26 [R][pg] — one agenda item per commenter) would be a speaker-based, not content-based, restriction — presumptively unconstitutional in a designated forum; as evidence, it is ¶34 of the federal pleading [R].

## PART II — THE POLICING DOCKET: VAGUENESS, PRETEXT, CAMERAS

**2.1 The noise ordinance [R] and the vagueness doctrine.** His Sept 22, 2025 memorandum [R] is a competent void-for-vagueness brief: *Grayned v. City of Rockford*, 408 U.S. 104 (1972) [C] (fair notice + anti-arbitrary-enforcement prongs); *Coates v. City of Cincinnati*, 402 U.S. 611 (1971) [C] ("annoying" conduct ordinance void); *Papachristou v. City of Jacksonville*, 405 U.S. 156 (1972) [C]; *City of Chicago v. Morales*, 527 U.S. 41 (1999) [C]. A subjective "unreasonable noise" standard with no dBA limit is genuinely vulnerable *as applied*; the counter-control is *Kovacs v. Cooper*, 336 U.S. 77 (1949) [C] (noise ordinances are regulable). Wyoming courts apply federal vagueness analysis with the *Grayned* prongs. **Grade: B as enforced.** His deeper NVR claim:

**2.2 The NVR dragnet [R].** Stops of vehicles displaying a notarized bill of sale: W.S. § 31-2-205(d) [M]✓ makes the displayed bill presumptive compliance territory; a seizure requires reasonable articulable suspicion (*Terry v. Ohio*, 392 U.S. 1 (1963) [C]); **any** objective violation supports a stop (*Whren v. United States*, 517 U.S. 806 (1996) [C]), and even reasonable **mistake of law** suffices (*Heien v. North Carolina*, 574 U.S. 54 (2014) [C]) — which is the state's best answer to a bill-of-sale stop. The custom theory (persistent stops over compliant papers) is *Monell* territory with a *Yick Wo v. Hopkins*, 118 U.S. 356 (1886) [C] patina (enforcement "so administration… is unequal" is unconstitutional), and modern selective-enforcement doctrine (*Oyler v. Boles*, 368 U.S. 448 (1962); *Village of Willowbrook v. Olech*, 528 U.S. 562 (2000) "class of one" [C]) supplies the equal-protection leg. **Grade: B− (proof-dependent — needs stop data, which is exactly what his WPRA demands were for).**

**2.3 Flock [R].** Single plate reads in public: no search (*United States v. Knotts*, 460 U.S. 276 (1983) [C]; third-party doctrine, *Smith v. Maryland*, 442 U.S. 735 (1979) [C]). Thirty days of 139,176 vehicles across 23 cameras [R] implicates aggregation: *United States v. Jones*, 565 U.S. 400 (2012) (GPS) and *Carpenter v. United States*, 585 U.S. ___, 138 S. Ct. 2206 (2018) [C] (CSLI; "seismic shifts in digital technology"). No federal circuit has squarely held ALPR networks *per se* searches; the Panopticon document's [R] mosaic argument is the honest current state of the law — a *Carpenter* extension awaiting a test case. **2.4 Monell scaffolding** (for the muting *and* the stops): *Monell v. Dep't of Soc. Servs.*, 436 U.S. 658 (1978); *Pembaur v. Cincinnati*, 475 U.S. 469 (1986) (a single act by a final policymaker is policy — the mayor runs the microphone) [C].

## PART III — THE HOME: THE WARRANTS WAR AS THE RECORD'S BEST LAW

**3.1 The doctrine.** Administrative inspections require warrants when consent is refused (*Camara v. Municipal Court*, 387 U.S. 523 (1967); *See v. Seattle*, 387 U.S. 541 (1967) [C]), on **"administrative probable cause"** — not individualized suspicion but "reasonable legislative or administrative standards." Commercial premises get less protection (*New York v. Burger*, 482 U.S. 691 (1987) [C]); post-fire entry and plain-view have their own line (*Michigan v. Tyler*, 436 U.S. 499 (1978) [C]); implied-consent-by-permitting sits atop the unconstitutional-conditions problem his "Permit Trap" section names [R] — the exact terrain of *Nollan/Dolan/Koontz* logic (conditions on benefits) [C], by analogy.

**3.2 His B.3 argument, graded.** His "Compliance Loophole" thesis [R] — that a warrant grounded only on the *need to verify* compliance inverts probable cause into a recursive fishing license — is a serious *Camara* scope argument (area-inspection standards were meant to be *neutral plans*, not individualized curiosity). **It was also mooted by his own victory [R, user-confirmed]:** "determine and discover" was **struck as a direct result of his campaign**; courts will now never rule on it. The legal price of winning: the record's best constitutional argument is unripe forever. What survived enactment — reporting codified, guardrails, the 2-of-4 abandonment definition, daytime/no-knock limits [R] — is, ironically, a statute *better-drafted than most*, because its author's adversary read it clause by clause.

**3.3 The Wyoming frame [R/M].** Peace-officer enumeration (W.S. § 6-1-104 [M]✓), the defensive-force officer-exception (W.S. § 6-2-602(c)(iii) [M]✓), and the stand-your-ground/no-knock collision his "Silent Entry" section theorizes — all real codification, all correctly characterized as Wyoming's *submit-first-litigate-later* posture.

## PART IV — LAND AND WATER: THE HIGHLANDS DOCKET

**4.1 The zoning constitution.** *Village of Euclid v. Ambler Realty*, 272 U.S. 365 (1926); *Nectow v. Cambridge*, 277 U.S. 183 (1928) [C]; modern framework after *Lingle v. Chevron U.S.A.*, 544 U.S. 528 (2005) [C] (due process asks whether the ordinance is arbitrary, not whether it "substantially advances" aims).

**4.2 The findings line — his Count I's true ancestry.** The national anchor is *Fasano v. Bd. of County Comm'rs of Washington County*, 507 P.2d 23 (Or. 1973) [C]: rezonings are **quasi-judicial**, applicant bears the burden, and the body must make findings connecting evidence to decision. California's *Topanga Growth Council* [C] gave the requirement its enduring metaphor: the agency "must bridge the analytic gap between the raw evidence and ultimate decision." **Wyoming's real anchor, verified:** *Wilson Advisory Committee v. Bd. of County Comm'rs of Teton County*, 2012 WY 163 [M]✓ — the Wyoming Supreme Court **reversed an approval because the body failed to make findings its own regulations required, rendering the record "insufficient to permit judicial review."** The movement's framing ("Topanga Analytical Bridge Standard, formally adopted…") is a gloss [M]≈ — the case is about regulatory findings-deficit, not a named California import — but the substantive proposition (findings must exist and must connect) is Wyoming law. Pair with *Foster's Inc. v. City of Laramie*, 718 P.2d 868 (Wyo. 1986) [M]✓ (rudimentary due process at required hearings; de novo judicial review of zoning with presumption for the decision below) and the federal findings canon, *SEC v. Chenery Corp.*, 332 U.S. 194 (1947) [C] (the body must disclose its actual reasoning; post-hoc rationalization is impermissible).

**Application [R]:** a June 1 Planning Commission denial → July 13 council override, in the developer's own words ("detailed design has not yet been developed"; "I do not have any of that information at my disposal" — Irwin [R]), is a findings-case prototype: *Wilson Advisory* requires the bridge; an evidentiary void is the bridge's absence. His Count I is the strongest merits count in the kit. **Grade: A− as a legal theory; outcome turns on whether the council's roll-call recitals count as "findings."** Wyoming's annexation findings statute (W.S. § 15-1-402 [M]≈ as cited in the record [R]) supplies the statutory hook; the Planning-Commission-vote statute he invoked Apr 20 (W.S. § 15-1-505 [M]≈, "four statutory votes") governs the override path he calls the "Stansberry exploit."

**4.3 Segmentation.** "Illegal project segmentation" is not a freestanding Wyoming cause of action [C]; it is NEPA's doctrine (*Kleppe v. Sierra Club*, 427 U.S. 390 (1976); 40 C.F.R. § 1508.25 [C]) borrowed into state siting law. Wyoming's real vehicle is the **Industrial Siting Act, W.S. § 35-12-101 et seq.** [C] — DEQ Industrial Siting Council jurisdiction above cost thresholds (periodically adjusted), with mitigation fees to affected counties. The record's claim [R] — that BP zoning holds the project under the ISA threshold, with Bloom and Wolf conceding the exemption on the floor — is a *policy* critique of a *lawful* classification decision: "evasion" of review thresholds by slicing is the classic segmentation grievance, and the lawful-but-unwise band is where it lives. **Grade: B− as law, A as politics.**

**4.4 Water and the common law.** His 16,000-home/5.6M-gal-day arithmetic [R] meets Wyoming prior-appropriation (*v. the "eight homes" metaphor* [R]) — a factual dispute in a quasi-judicial record, not a tort (yet). The strict-liability ancestors for the Sept 21 event are *Rylands v. Fletcher* (1868) and, pointedly, *Indiana Harbor Belt R. Co. v. American Cyanamid*, 916 F.2d 1174 (7th Cir. 1990) (Posner, J.) [C] — ultrahazardous rail transport — aimed at the *private* defendants, not the city (Part IX).

## PART V — DIRECT DEMOCRACY: THE REFERENDUM WAR

**5.1 The statute.** W.S. § 22-23-1005 [M]✓ (verbatim in the record [R]): a municipal ordinance is referable on petition of **10% of qualified registered electors**, filed within **20 days of first publication**, setting the ordinance forth in full. The 22-23-1003–1008 machinery [C] then forces repeal-or-election. Threshold arithmetic (≈43,000 votes cast → ≈4,300 signatures; 3,300+ submitted [R]) is the referendum's open factual question — as is the formality ground the statute hands opponents ("set forth the ordinance in full").

**5.2 The referendum-zoning jurisprudence.** Zoning *by* referendum is constitutional — the referendum is the people legislating (*City of Eastlake v. Forest City Enters.*, 426 U.S. 668 (1976) [C]) — and *Eastlake* holds the due-process clause does not require pre-referendum hearings for legislative acts. The outer limits: a referendum scheme delegating unbounded neighbor discretion is void (*Washington ex rel. Seattle Title Trust Co. v. Roberge*, 278 U.S. 225 (1938) [C]); structuring referenda to burden disfavored classes violates equal protection (*Hunter v. Erickson*, 393 U.S. 385 (1969); *James v. Valtierra*, 402 U.S. 137 (1971) [C] marking the boundary). None of the invalidating lines applies to a garden-variety annexation referendum — the city's case must be statutory (signatures, form, subject-matter), not constitutional.

**5.3 The doctrinal trap inside his own pleading.** Count II pleads the annexation hearings as **quasi-judicial** (to import *Foster's* due-process protections); the referendum presupposes the ordinance is **legislative** (referenda reach legislation; many jurisdictions hold administrative/quasi-judicial land-use decisions *not* referable) [C]. The city's strongest brief writes itself from the movement's own papers: *"Petitioners' Count I contends these ordinances are quasi-judicial; quasi-judicial decisions are not subject to referendum; petition fails as a matter of law."* His Count II will have to be litigated *in the alternative* — a genuinely difficult posture, and the single best example in this record of advocacy doctrine colliding with itself.

**5.4 August 10 and the "legal ghost."** His theory (unagendized walk-on authorization → suit unauthorized → "ghost") [R] collides with ratification/curative practice: a body can simply **re-vote in public**, and courts hesitate to void municipal action for procedural misstep absent prejudice [C]. His real remedies were always: (a) the OMA (Part VI), and (b) election-law challenge to the DJ action's authorization — both slower than the referendum clock. The city's mirror-image exposure: suing citizens to invalidate a petition is petition-clause-adjacent activity with poor optics and *Eastlake*-era doctrine against it on the merits.

## PART VI — SUNSHINE: THE OPEN MEETINGS AND RECORDS ACTS

**6.1 Corrected statutory map (audit finding).** The Public Meetings Act, W.S. § 16-4-401 *et seq.* [C]: § 402 (open meetings/actions), **§ 403 = the anti-circumvention clause** ("Communication outside a meeting, including… sequential communications among the members of an agency, shall not be used to circumvent the purpose of this act" — verified text), § 404 (meeting types/notice), §§ 405–406 (executive sessions), **§ 408 = penalties** (civil penalty **not to exceed $750** per member, knowing/willful misdemeanor exposure, with the objection-in-minutes safe harbor — verified). **The kit's mapping is loose [M]≈:** its "16-4-403(a)" public-action cite and "16-4-404" agenda cite do not track the codification; the substance survives, the pin-cites do not.

**6.2 The Aug 10 count, rigorously.** Executive session with counsel over litigation strategy is a *permitted* executive session [C]; the exposure is the **unagendized public action** taken on emergence [R]. But the OMA's text requires *notice of meetings*, not itemized public agendas — itemization is a Cheyenne self-imposed practice [R] — so the count rests on purpose-circumvention (§ 403) and any ordinance-based agenda rule, not a statutory agenda mandate. **Grade: B−** — real, but narrower than the movement's "null and void" rhetoric. Live Wyoming precedent for § 403's teeth: the 2025 Evanston no-confidence-letter investigation (four councilmembers found in violation for *sequential deliberations*; $750/member exposure) [C] — which is, with the record's usual symmetry, an OMA enforcement action about a *no-confidence* document.

**6.3 The Records Act.** WPRA, W.S. § 16-4-201 *et seq.* [C]: presumption of disclosure; fees under § 16-4-204 limited to defined costs [M]≈ (the (e) fee-shift/penalty path his Stage 2 invokes needs current-text confirmation); the Governor's public-records **ombudsman** path is real practice. His $3,000 estimate fight [R] is a textbook § 204 "actual cost" dispute, and his templates are close to what counsel would file. **Grade: A− as process work.**

## PART VII — THE $0.00 LAWSUIT: ACCESS-TO-JUSTICE LAW

**7.1 The constitutional floor.** Fees that deny access where fundamental interests are at stake violate due process (*Boddie v. Connecticut*, 401 U.S. 371 (1971); *M.L.B. v. S.L.J.*, 519 U.S. 102 (1996) [C]); ordinary civil fees survive (*United States v. Kras*, 409 U.S. 434 (1973); *Ortwein v. Schwab*, 410 U.S. 656 (1973) [C]) — so the constitutional argument is weak, and the statutory one is strong: **7.2 Wyoming's own machinery (verified).** W.S. § 5-3-206 sets the district-court filing fee (**$160**, not the kit's $120 — audit finding [M]✗ on amount); the Judicial Branch publishes an official **Affidavit of Indigency and Request for Waiver of Fees and Cost** (Self-Help Packet 10), free to file, administered at the clerk's window exactly as Stage 3 instructs [R]. The kit's "W.R.E.F.S. Rule 5" citation is **unverified and likely a mislabel** [M]✗ — the affidavit packet, not a rule of that name, is the operative law-in-practice. **The $0.00 claim is therefore institutionally true.**

**7.3 Pro se doctrine.** *Haines v. Kerner*, 404 U.S. 519 (1972) (allegations held "to less stringent standards than formal pleadings drafted by lawyers"); *Erickson v. Pardus*, 551 U.S. 89 (2007) [C]. **7.4 UPL.** Publishing blank forms with disclaimers is the protected self-help-publishing line (the *Nolo* / *Quicken Family Lawyer* Texas episode, 1999 [C]); his "civic auditing group, not a law firm / this is not legal advice" recitals [R] are precisely the compliance posture of that industry — drafted by someone who had read the doctrine (or had it read to him).

## PART VIII — THE KILL MECHANISM: TITLE, CHILL, AND Noerr

Stage 3's theory [R] — parallel free suits → underwriters refuse the risk → financing freezes — runs through real property doctrine: marketable title; standard-policy exceptions; quiet title (W.S. § 1-37-201 [C]) and lis pendens practice. Litigation does not automatically cloud title, but **underwriters price uncertainty**, and pro se suits cost the defense real money — the deterrence is economic, not doctrinal. The exposure runs the other direction: petitioning (including litigation) is antitrust-protected (*Eastern R.R. Presidents Conf. v. Noerr Motor Freight*, 365 U.S. 127 (1961); *United Mine Workers v. Pennington*, 381 U.S. 657 (1965) [C]), unless objectively baseless (*Professional Real Estate Investors v. Columbia Pictures*, 508 U.S. 49 (1993)) or aimed at the *process* itself (*California Motor Transp. Co. v. Trucking Unlimited*, 404 U.S. 508 (1972) [C]). A coordinated campaign of colorable-if-losing suits sits inside *Noerr* protection; his **pre-drafted antitrust disclaimer** [R] is the tell that the author knew exactly where that line runs. Wyoming has **no anti-SLAPP statute** [C] — the city cannot cheaply tax the strategy; symmetry, again.

## PART IX — THEIR SIDE OF THE LEDGER: IMMUNITIES AND THE DUTY TO WARN

**9.1 Legislative immunity (the kit's unpriced wall).** Local legislators hold **absolute immunity** for ordinance sponsorship and voting (*Bogan v. Scott-Harris*, 523 U.S. 44 (1998); *Supreme Court of Va. v. Consumers Union*, 446 U.S. 719 (1980) [C]). The $750-personal-fines demand [R] survives only as a **statutory** OMA penalty (not a tort), and the § 1983 counts against councilmembers for *voting* largely do not survive at all. Chairing decisions (muting) occupy murkier administrative-qualified-immunity ground [C]. **The kit never mentions *Bogan* — its most consequential omission.** **9.2 Sept 21.** Failure-to-warn against the city faces *DeShaney v. Winnebago Cnty.*, 489 U.S. 189 (1989) and *Town of Castle Rock v. Gonzales*, 545 U.S. 748 (2005) [C] (no federal duty to protect/warn absent special relationship) plus the Wyoming Governmental Claims Act, W.S. § 1-39-101 *et seq.* [C] with its discretionary-function and emergency immunities. The actionable theories live against the *private* side — pipeline operator (PHMSA corrective action [R]; *Rylands/Indiana Harbor* strict liability; negligence per se off the hazmat regs) — with one rigor trap the movement has not priced: **49 U.S.C. § 1154(B) bars admission of most NTSB reports in civil litigation** [C]. The Silent Bomb's federal exhibits are investigative catalysts, not admissible evidence.

## PART XI — THE VERDICT OF THE LAW

Read as one docket, the law does something to this narrative that rhetoric never could: it **sorts it**. The campaign's legal spine — findings, forum, fees, sunshine, access — is sound to strong, and its citation discipline outperforms its live-improv garbles by an order of magnitude. Its soft tissue — "null and void" rhetoric, personal fines, the ghost lawsuit — dissolves on contact with *Bogan*, ratification doctrine, and notice-versus-agenda text. And its deepest tension is doctrinal, not factual: **the same pleading cannot both worship the referendum (legislative) and mourn the hearing (quasi-judicial) without choosing.** The law of the Miller record is thus the record in miniature: one apparatus, two minds — the drafter who cites *Wilson Advisory* and *Foster's* correctly, and the improviser whose transcript garbles the case names; the movement that strikes "determine and discover" from the code and then must live with never hearing a court say it was right. The city's mirror lesson is blunter: everything it did to him with the microphone is pled somewhere; everything it could have done with the *findings* is what would have won. The most uncomfortable finding of this volume: **on the law as it actually exists, the strongest count against the City of Cheyenne was never the one anyone shouted about — it is a June 2026 zoning vote taken over an admitted evidentiary void, and it is exactly the kind of count Wyoming's Supreme Court has reversed before.**
