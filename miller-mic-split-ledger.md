## §2 — THE CAP TABLE (what each door pays, and where each door stops)

The user's question in one table: *if each violation becomes its own case, what is each case capped at?*

| Claim vehicle | Per-incident cap | Who pays | Fees? | The cap's source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| §1983 — viewpoint (V-1) | **$1 nominal** per count absent proven compensables; compensatory only with proof (dignitary: low four figures; distress requires real proof) | City | **Yes — §1988, uncapped, discretionary** | nominal-damages doctrine; *Farrar v. Hobby*, 506 U.S. 103 (1992) [C] |
| §1983 — retaliation (V-2) | same nominal structure; compensatory slightly more plausible (access deprivation) | City (Bogan bars members for votes; operational acts individual) | §1988 | *Bogan*; *Mt. Healthy* [C] |
| §1983 against City — punitive damages | **$0 — barred** | — | — | *Newport v. Fact Concerts*, 453 U.S. 247 (1981) [C] |
| WPRA knowing withholding (V-13) | **$750 per official per episode** | official(s) personally | **No fees — ever** (verified, *McDaniel*) | W.S. 16-4-201 et seq.; LSO/RCFP [S] |
| OMA violation (V-12) | **$750 per member per action**; safe harbor for members who objected in minutes | members personally | none in statute | W.S. 16-4-408 [S/C] |
| Wyoming constitutional (art. 1 §§ 6/20) | declaration/injunction; damages uncertain | City | American Rule — no fees absent statute | [C] |
| Small-claims vehicle (state penalties) | court's jurisdiction cap (**$6,000** — canonical, verify at filing) | — | statutory | W.S. ch. 1-21 [C] |

**The three structural facts the whole ledger turns on:**
1. **The microphone incidents are fee-fuel, not damage-fuel.** A point of order is not $750. The $750 quantum belongs to records and meetings claims. The mic cuts live in §1983, where the *damages* are one dollar and the *money* is the fee statute.
2. **§1988 fees are uncapped and may exceed the judgment many-fold** — *Riverside v. Rivera*, 477 U.S. 561 (1986) [C] — but are discretionary and can be cut to zero on nominal-only wins, *Farrar* [C]. The fee award is where a tribunal punishes or rewards the *structure* of the case. Fragment badly and *Farrar* eats the portfolio.
3. **State penalty claims are the only true per-incident cash**, and they carry no fees — which in the $0.00 model is a feature (each costs $160, or $0 with the indigency packet, and pays up to $750 per official per episode).

## §3 — THE LITERAL SPLIT MODEL (each violation its own lawsuit — priced honestly)

Model A: M-1 through M-8, each filed as its own federal action (8 dockets), plus per-episode state penalty filings.

- **Filing economics:** 8 federal actions at the civil filing fee (or IFP at $0 under his model) + 28 U.S.C. §1915(e)(2) frivolousness screening on each IFP filing; each state penalty filing $160/$0.
- **Preclusion cascade:** claim preclusion bars claims *arising from the same transaction or occurrence* that were or could have been brought. M-2/M-3/M-4 are one evening, one policy, one pattern — one transaction. File M-2 alone and lose, and M-3/M-4 may die with it. File M-2 alone and *win*, and the city tendering relief can moot the twins. **The Apr. 27 cascade is one case wearing three coats.**
- **Consolidation:** Fed. R. Civ. P. 42(a) — common questions → the court consolidates 8 dockets into one anyway; the filer has bought 8 captions and 1 case.
- **Fee fragmentation:** *Hensley v. Eckerhart*, 461 U.S. 424 (1983) [C] — unrelated claims treated separately; overlapping work across 8 dockets gets scrubbed as duplicative. Eight $1 judgments invite eight *Farrar* haircuts, or one global haircut for the vanity of eight captions.
- **The vexatious threshold:** serial IFP filings arising from the same institutional dispute is the classic prefiling-order profile (*DeFonbrook*-line inherent power; Wyo. R. Civ. P. 11 for the state track). Eight is not fifteen — but the trajectory is the sanction.
- **Model A verdict:** maximum gross captions, minimum net recovery. **The literal split destroys the value it tries to multiply.** Priced below for completeness:

**Model A cash ceiling (generous assumptions):** 8 × $1 nominal = **$8**; muted compensables (M-6 disablement) low four figures; fees: duplicative-scrubbed lodestar on 8 small files, *Farrar*-reduced — realistically a fraction of one consolidated lodestar; state penalties: only where episodes exist (not mic-dependent). **Net: the worst architecture in the ledger.**

## §4 — THE MAXIMIZATION ARCHITECTURE (what Miller would actually build)

**Model B — "each violation its own case" read correctly: each incident its own COUNT, inside one engineered action.** One federal complaint, *City of Cheyenne* as sole defendant, styled as a pattern case:

**Count structure (the complaint's table of contents):**
- Counts I–k (one per individuated mute event, M-1 series): First Amendment retaliation, each its own count, each its own $1, each its own fee-bearing judgment line.
- Counts for M-2/3/4 (pleaded as one count with three incorporated incidents, or three counts — either survives preclusion because filed together): viewpoint discrimination in comment administration.
- Count: M-5 (May 26 one-item gag) — content-based restriction of a designated forum.
- Count: M-6 (comment disablement) — retaliatory forum closure; *Lindke v. Freed* supplies the state-action frame for any digital component [C].
- Count: M-7 (podium threat) — express threat as adverse action; individual-capacity defendant (operational act; *Bogan* does not reach it).
- Count: M-8 (asymmetry pattern) — the *Make Salt Lake* count, the case's spine.
- Prayer: nominal per count; declaratory; **injunction reforming comment administration** (the forward-looking relief that keeps fee entitlement intact).

**Why one action multiplies the money:**
1. **One prevailing-party fee petition prices the whole pattern.** Every hour, every transcript, every exhibit bills once — a consolidated lodestar with no duplicative-work scrub. Hensley rewards related-claims unity.
2. **The pattern is the compensable thing.** Eight isolated dollar verdicts look like a gadfly; one injunction plus eight counts looks like a civil-rights practice. Same facts, doubled settlement leverage.
3. **Preclusion is disarmed** when everything is in one file — nothing left behind to bar.
4. **Removal/consolidation is preempted** — there is nothing for the city to consolidate; its own DJ action (below) is the only pending docket.

**The fee engine, priced (scenarios):**

| Scenario | Counts | Nominal | Hours (lodestar basis) | Blended rate | §1988 fee exposure | State penalty track | Total city-side exposure |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 8–10 | ~$10 | 200 | $275 | ~$55,000 | $0–$4,500 (WPRA, if episodes pleaded) | **~$60K** |
| Base | 10–14 | ~$14 | 400 | $325 | ~$130,000 | $4,500–$9,000 | **~$140K** |
| Maximum | 14+ w/ injunction | ~$15 | 600+ | $375 | ~$225,000+ | up to $7,500/OMA action **if** serial-deliberation evidence lands | **~$230K+** |

*The Base scenario (~$140K) is not a coincidence — it is Vol. II's original weighted yield, now rebuilt through the microphone record instead of the records record. The model has two roads to the same number, which is what convergence looks like.*

**The state track, run in parallel (per-episode cash only, no fees, no lawyers):**
- WPRA: one filing per *episode* (each knowing withholding; each nonresponsive production), naming custodian(s) personally — $750 per official per episode. This is where literal splitting is *correct*: separate statutory violations are separate claims by design, each below the fee-radar, each $160/$0 to file. Example pricing: 3 episodes × 2 officials = **$4,500 ceiling**.
- OMA: dormant until the §403 serial-deliberation evidence exists; if it lands, one tainted action × 10 members × $750 = **$7,500**, plus the nullity remedy (the only count that reaches Ord. 4687 — worth more than every fee in this ledger).

## §5 — THE CLOCK AND THE TRAP (the two timing facts that dominate everything above)

1. **The DJ-suit counterclaim trap.** The city's Aug. 10 authorized declaratory-judgment action is coming. Under Fed. R. Civ. P. 13(a) / Wyo. R. Civ. P. 13(a), once he is a party to a pending case, claims "arising from the same transaction or occurrence" become **compulsory counterclaims — assert them there or lose them forever.** The mic-record claims and the referendum fight share a transactional core (the same policy war, the same meetings, the same speakers). If the city files first and names him, his entire M-series portfolio must be filed as counterclaims in *the city's chosen forum*, on the city's chosen clock — or waived. Vol. I predicted the trap ("a defendant in his own trap"); this ledger prices it: **the difference between filing his pattern case first and being filed upon first is roughly the entire lodestar plus forum control.**
2. **Limitations.** §1983 borrows Wyoming's four-year catch-all in this posture [C]; no incident in the M-series is near a bar. The real deadline is not limitations — it is the DJ caption dropping.

**Operational bottom line, stated as the file's discipline requires:** the microphone record maximizes as **one federal pattern action with count-per-incident, filed before the city's DJ caption lands**, plus a parallel ribbon of per-episode state penalty filings that are too small to consolidate and too cheap to abandon. The maximum realistic city-side exposure is the **~$230K ceiling scenario**; the expected value is the **~$140K base**; and both numbers are fees, not damages — which is the entire point, and the entire risk (*Farrar* can zero a fees-only win; one adverse merits ruling on the pattern could preclude the rest and hand the city a validation precedent the movement would feel for years).

## §6 — What the Model Refuses

- It does not mint counts from the slurs (E-1) — government/campaign speech; struck on sight.
- It does not file the eight-caption split — preclusion, consolidation, *Farrar*, and the prefiling-order profile make it the one architecture that pays less the harder it is worked.
- It does not price the OMA $7,500 before the §403 evidence exists.
- It does not confuse the two markets: **state claims buy remedy; federal claims buy fees; the referendum — which he cannot even sign — buys the ordinance.** Every maximization in this ledger is subordinate to that third one, and the file knows it.
