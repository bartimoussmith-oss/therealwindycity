The most uncomfortable finding, again, is dual. The page's cause catalogue is real and checkable — the consent-agenda cameras, the pathogen that shut down the purple pipes, the walk-on vote, the missing agenda, the one-mile circle the public was never told about. And the page's method is openly instrumental: pre-written testimony, templated complaints, copy-paste litigation, an explicitly actuarial theory of victory ("title insurance companies will refuse to underwrite the land"), and at least one wholesale amplification of an unsourced death claim. It is simultaneously the most rigorous public-records operation in the city and a purpose-built pressure machine — the man's duality, industrialized.

The last line of Vol. I survives, and gains its coda: *before the trap, there was a scanner app, a burglary, and a neighbor who called him a bot.* After the trap, there is a library, an armory, and — as of 12:23 this morning — a statewide question about who keeps the liability.
