## III. Goal 1 — Void the Highlands Package (Primary Operation)

### 3.1 The precedent-seeding ladder

His sequencing across the whole evening was architectural:

```
Items 8/9  (2.69 ac residential)      → plant the "quasi-judicial / findings" standard on a trivial item
Items 10–12 (26–400 ac business park) → show the same defects repeating at scale
Items 13–16 (3,460 ac Highlands)      → argue the defects are now "structural" and knowing
Items 19–20 (FLUM override)           → tie the planning commission's denial into the chain
```

He announced the design himself at 1:03: *"The standard you apply to this 2.6-acre vote will be cited in the appellate record for the 30,000-acre votes. Govern yourselves accordingly."* This is a **consistency trap**: if the council rubber-stamps the small parcel with no findings, the big parcels are indistinguishable; if it suddenly generates findings for Microsoft but not for the widow's 2.69 acres, that selectivity *is itself* the arbitrariness exhibit. Either branch produces copy for his brief. Note the elegance: he wins the argument by losing the votes.

### 3.2 His best exhibit is a councilmember

That is not a coincidence of scheduling. **The most litigation-valuable sentence spoken all night came from a member of the body itself, and Miller immediately moved to valorize, preserve, and amplify it** — rewarding the witness, coaching him to continue, and framing the council's own internal dissent as the objective truth a court will recognize. ### 3.3 The noise-standard device — his cleverest engineering

The item-13/15 argument is a genuine piece of legal engineering, whatever its ultimate merit:

1. Take the city's **own UDC definition** (industrial use = data centers producing noise discernible at the property line) and its **own August 2025 noise standards** (requiring acoustic measurements under full-load conditions);

3. Conclude: the city *cannot* legally make the findings a business-park classification would require — *"a mathematical and legal impossibility"* — so every yes vote is knowingly cast over an admitted data void.

Whether or not a court ever accepts step 3, steps 1–2 mean **every approving voter is now on record voting over an acknowledged evidentiary gap.** He designed the argument so that winning the vote creates the liability. That is the signature of litigation strategy, not civic input. [STRONG INFERENCE]

## VI. Goal 4 — Delegitimize the Machine (The Institutional Track)

The final hour (voucher audit + other-business finale) abandoned parcel-level arguments entirely and attacked the *system*:

1. **LEADS/NDAs = "unlawful delegation of municipal police powers"** — zoning strategy laundered *"through a private 501(c)(6) to hide the details from the taxpayers."* This is the argument with the most political explosiveness (it explains *why* the public "can't know" — secrecy), and it converts every NDA question ever asked into a constitutional count.
2. **The AVI conflict of interest** — the zoning agent for the Highlands applications simultaneously receiving city payments ($10,548 + $9,859) in the same voucher report. This is his single most concrete allegation, and notably it requires **no court**: an ethics complaint is cheap, requires no standing, and triggers investigations and headlines on its own schedule.
3. **The Monell predicate** — *"a municipal budget is a mathematical expression of intent"*; the voucher line items (concert advances, bouncy houses, comedy-settlement) versus lead-pipe pleas are being marshaled to show a *custom and policy* of prioritizing aesthetics over health/safety. That is exactly the showing a Monell claim requires, and his April lead-pipes argument shows he's been building this file since spring.
4. **Language warfare** — "rubber stamp" (used at least four times), "autopilot," "corporate machine," "trillion-dollar developer," "ghost project," "evidentiary void." These are not arguments; they are **brand assets** — compressed, repeatable units that other commenters, reporters, and eventually candidates can deploy. Note that the press already quotes him in exactly these units ("corporate camouflage," "arbitrary and capricious act"). He is writing the movement's dictionary. [STRONG INFERENCE]

## XI. Implications — What the Goal Model Predicts for the Other Side

If this analysis is right, the city's rational counters are precise (and their current play is wrong): live engagement with him *feeds* the record — every "Nope," every "speaks for itself," every exasperated out-of-order ruling is a harvested exhibit.
