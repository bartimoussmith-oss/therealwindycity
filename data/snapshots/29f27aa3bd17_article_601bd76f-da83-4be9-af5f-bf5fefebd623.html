Confidence growing for Wyoming football going into bye week

  • Alex Taylor WyoSports

    Alex Taylor

LARAMIE — The most important game of Jay Sawvel’s tenure awaits.

The third-year University of Wyoming head coach will have a week off before facing UNLV on Nov. 7 in Las Vegas.

The Cowboys (5-3 overall, 4-1 Mountain West) are riding a three-game winning streak into their bye week following a 28-14 win over Air Force. They moved up to second in the conference standings following North Dakota State’s loss to New Mexico last weekend.

“This is not just a rest week around here,” Sawvel said. “Our players will need to know that when they come in on Wednesday. ... Our guys are really engaged and ready to go. We just need to have a really good, positive week of getting better while also getting healthier.”

Things looked bleak for the Cowboys early on. They started 1-2 following road losses to Colorado State and Central Michigan. UW took another step back with a 28-0 road loss to North Dakota State earlier this month.

The Cowboys have responded with wins over San Jose State, Northern Illinois and Air Force. UW has won four of its past five games.

“To me, I thought the low point was after the Central Michigan game. We played like crap,” Sawvel said. “To be honest, I don’t even remember what I said to the team after that game in the locker room, other than that everything was from the heart. It was like, ’We have to be much better than this.’ From that came certain steps that the players wanted to take.

“... From that point forward, our energy has been great in every game. Even in the North Dakota State game, it wasn’t like we lacked energy. We lacked execution. … We have a number of players, from a leadership perspective, that have taken really strong positions on how we’re going to practice. Our practices right now are tremendous.

“… There’s been growth within the team, too. There’s a lot of younger players that are growing and getting better. They’re gaining confidence. Confidence can be a dangerous thing when you have some guys that have talent. So, we’re using that right now.”

While there was plenty of outside noise hovering over the program following its shaky start, Sawvel said his group never wavered in the face of adversity.

“We had to go in a hole together a little bit, but there was never a time where coaches turned on players or players turned on coaches or players were turning on each other. It was never that time,” Sawvel said. “When you never have that time in any organization, then you have an opportunity to ride something out and get better at it.

“We’re not perfect. We still have games to go play, but we’re a whole lot better football team than what we were four weeks ago.”

The Cowboys have already eclipsed their most wins during Sawvel’s tenure. They’re one win away from bowl eligibility entering the home stretch.

The Cowboys still face UNLV, Texas-El Paso and New Mexico in conference play. UW ends the season with a nonconference tilt against Connecticut.

Sawvel pointed to five key areas for improvement for the Cowboys leading up to their game against Northern Illinois earlier this month. That included turnover margin, total yardage, third-down percentage on offense and defense, penalty yardage and offensive touchdown rate in the red zone.

“We’re plus-three in turnovers over the last two weeks since we talked about laying those out. Those all came (against Air Force), and that was a part of the reason we won,” Sawel said. “We’ve not turned the ball over in those last two games.

“I said we wanted to outgain opponents. We’ve done that. It’s been a slimmer margin, but here’s the big key to it. We’re averaging over eight yards a play in our last two games. Our opponents are averaging just barely over five. … When you’re averaging eight yards a play, you’re creating explosives. Well, when you do that, you’re going to score points.”

The Cowboys have converted 47% of their third downs compared to their opponents’ mark of 41% over the past two games. Much of UW’s success in that department has come from better production as an offense on first and second down.

“We’ve only had 15 third downs on offense the last two games,” Sawvel said. “... No. 1, Air Force kept the ball so much the other day that we didn’t have a lot of plays. The second thing is, over the last couple games, we’ve had a lot of explosive plays. So, when you score in a two- or three-play drive, then you’re not going to have as many plays and as many third downs.

“We’re not taking an 18-play drive like we did against San Jose where you have to go for four third downs. That’s helped us in that space. ... Drastically cutting back on penalties is obviously a piece to it, too.”

Sawvel wanted to see the Cowboys have less than 40 yards of penalties over its final six games.

“We’ve had 48 penalty yards in the last two games,” Sawvel said. “When you look at that, we’re the least-penalized team in the conference in average yards and total penalties. I want us to continue in that space.

“We had 23 yards of penalties against NIU and 25 (against Air Force), and two of those (penalties) were by our punt team.”

The Cowboys have flourished in the red zone since Sawvel challenged his team two weeks ago.

“We’re 6-of-9 (scoring touchdowns in the red zone) in our last two games,” Sawvel said. “Before that, we were 9-of-21. … We have to continue those things.”

The Cowboys return to practice Wednesday afternoon.
