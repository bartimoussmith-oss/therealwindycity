1st-round regional loss sends Laramie volleyball on detour for potential state berth

LARAMIE — Laramie volleyball coach Jill Stucky demanded a “dogfight” version of her team.

It showed at times — when athletes dove for every ball, delivered serves to nothing but hardwood and scrapped from behind.

But a first-round setback suddenly pushed a potential state tournament qualification into jeopardy.

Northeast Quadrant No. 3 seed Sheridan stopped Southeast No. 2 seed Laramie, 25-15, 25-13, 19-25, 25-22, in the first round of the Class 4A East Regional tournament Friday at Laramie High.

“There were moments we were the bigger dog in the fight,” Stucky said. “There were other times where we weren’t. This group is a work in progress. We will continue to work.”

Laramie will see Cheyenne South in an elimination match at 9 a.m. Saturday.

Laramie must win two consecutive matches, otherwise it will miss the state tournament for the first time since 2014.

“As we get into tomorrow’s matches, I think we are going to be fine, but my message to the girls is everybody needs to carry their weight,” Stucky said. “When it is their time to do their job, they need to step up and do it. They have to have a belief in each other.”

Ashlyn Stucky paced the Plainsmen (14-17 overall) with 11 kills, 11 digs and eight assists. Adelia Summers joined in the double-double category with 15 digs and 11 assists.

Sia Taufa managed 21 digs, which put her 15 shy of 1,000 career digs.

Ellie Oakey added nine kills, plus four blocks and four digs. Maggie Cornehlsen led with eight blocks.

Laramie’s comeback bid carried over from the third set when a 6-2 Laramie run in the fourth set provided a 13-12 lead. The Broncs evened things at 18, which sparked a 4-1 Broncos run.

“In the third set, I thought we had great sanity on the court,” Stucky said. “Their nerves weren’t getting the best of us. We were completely sane.”

Jill Stucky understood her team’s dynamic when she accepted a promotion from assistant coach to head coach for her third stint as Laramie’s leader.

Eight seniors — including three all-state talents — departed from Laramie’s 2025 squad that placed third at the state tournament.

It left a largely inexperienced squad, despite listing seven seniors on its regional roster.

Instilling confidence became Jill Stucky’s priority.

“For me as a coach, it is really important for me to understand what each player needs in order for them to be confident,” she said earlier in the week. “So, I have built some really nice relationships with these kids over the course of the season.

"I feel like I trust them and they trust me. That has been a huge factor for us.”

She demanded “good maturity” from her team and said it showed up in the third set.

“Tomorrow, we just need to go in with confidence,” Jill Stucky said.
