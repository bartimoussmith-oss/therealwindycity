Election 2026: Property tax proposition fails

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Wyoming voters have rejected a constitutional amendment that would have established a 50% property tax exemption for eligible primary residences.
In Tuesday’s race, there were 105,200 votes cast against the proposition to 80,029 votes in favor, with all counties reporting. Polls closed at 7 p.m.
Laramie County voters were among those to defeat the proposition. There, it received 12,832 votes in favor and 16,940 votes against.
State estimates projected the exemption would have reduced Wyoming state revenues by $92,614,266 in fiscal year 2028 and $95,855,766 in fiscal year 2029. Those losses would have recurred each year the exemption was in place.
The proposition drew debate across Wyoming.
Supporters said rising home assessments are pricing long-term residents out of their homes. In an opinion column for Oil City News,
gubernatorial candidate Rebecca Bextel wrote
that the proposition would provide lasting relief for retirees and working families living on fixed incomes. She said escalating tax bills are forcing people to consider selling properties they have owned for decades.
In a letter to the editor,
Casper resident KG Fox wrote
that state Department of Revenue figures show residential property values climbed roughly 73% between 2019 and 2024. He said officials must trim government spending instead of treating homeowners as an endless source of revenue.
Cheyenne resident Ben Hornok said
the exemption would give working families a raise by letting them keep more of their paycheck to cover basic household needs.
Opponents argued the measure would hurt essential services without offering a plan to replace the lost funding.
Gov. Mark Gordon urged
voters to reject it, saying property taxes stay local to fund roads, schools and emergency crews. He said lawmakers have already passed targeted relief bills, and cutting local revenue forces towns to depend on state handouts from Cheyenne.
Fire chiefs around the state also warned
about the impact on emergency protection. Natrona County Fire District Chief Howard Oden said the revenue drop could force him to trim staff and permanently close the county’s east-side station. That closure would add at least five minutes to emergency calls on that side of the county, Oden said.
Laramie County Fire Authority Chief Jason Caughey said rural fire districts rely almost entirely on residential property taxes and don’t receive sales tax dollars.
Casper College Board of Trustees Chair Dave Applegate estimated
the measure could create a $2 million deficit for the campus, where personnel makes up 75% of operating costs.
Other critics questioned how the tax break would be applied. In a letter to the editor,
Casper resident Ken Carpenter said
a blanket 50% cut rewards wealthy mansion owners while providing no direct relief to renters who pay property taxes through rent.

here
. Results are unofficial until certified by county and state canvassing boards.
