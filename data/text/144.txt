Advancement of urban farm ordinances spurs strong emotions from county pocket residents
by
Garrett Grochowski
October 27, 2026

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Ordinances updating the City of Cheyenne’s Unified Development Code and Municipal Code to make room for agriculture as a land use in Cheyenne made it past another round of City Council readings Monday. Fearing that the ordinances don’t do enough to protect their way of life ahead of a looming annexation, county farmers pleaded with the council to reconsider the changes ahead of a final vote in two weeks.
“Please work with us,” Tommie Kniseley, co-owner of WY Fresh Farms, said. The urban farm, located in a county pocket on Walterscheid Boulevard in south Cheyenne set for city-initiated annexation in the coming weeks, will soon have to adapt to the new rules on urban agriculture.
The annexation has been looming since spring. The city held off on the move over the summer, however, to give its Planning and Development Department time to create a series of code updates that would expand agriculture as a feasible lifestyle for Cheyenne residents. That would allow WY Fresh to continue its operations and the city to close up a gap in its emergency and utility service capabilities.
However, the Kniseleys and their allies around Cheyenne say that the ordinances do little to ensure their business’s survival. When the city and WY Fresh agreed to work together to update city code to allow the farm to continue operating in March, co-owner David Kniseley said, they asked the city to fulfill eight different requests.
“We’ve been left with roughly two and a half out of the eight things that we said we needed and have had next to no interaction with the city administration after the city administration, through a chief of staff, restricted us to communicating with city agencies only through our attorney,” David Kniseley said.
The ordinances establish definitions surrounding agriculture as a use in the city and some rules pertaining to the lifestyle. Changes include allowing electric fencing, gravel parking lots and expanded animal husbandry, all with specific conditions attached. Read more about the ordinances
here
.
Even the conditions that the ordinances do meet, such as allowing electric fencing, leave the farm owners uncertain, the Kniseleys said.
“There were talks about limiting voltage. I need to know these parameters so we can get them up before we are annexed in two weeks,” Tommie Kniseley said. “I don’t have much time, and this isn’t even going to be codified before I can do anything.”
Planning and Development Director Charles Bloom later clarified that the voltage requirements are purposefully loose to allow residents some leeway in setting up their fence; the only requirement is that the voltage be at a prudent level and be low enough to not kill anybody who touches the fence.
Other questions remain about how many languages the warning signs required to go along the fence every 100 feet must appear in, the farmers said. They have yet to set up a fence without knowing what the rules will be, an increasingly perilous decision in light of
recent mountain lion sightings in the area
and the constant threat of stray dogs.
Months of negotiations — unsuccessful ones, in the eyes of the WY Fresh farmers — have come to a head, as the ordinances and county pocket annexation are up for final consideration in two weeks on Nov. 9. Tommie Kniseley became emotional as she pleaded with the Cheyenne City Council another time before the body took a vote on whether to advance the ordinances to another reading.
“Can we just be human and try to let me continue to my way of life? I’m not asking for anything, I’m just asking to keep the status quo right now. Is there any workable solution?” she said before pausing briefly. “Please? I’m really at wit’s end. I’m willing to make compromises. Is there a workable solution? Somebody please answer me.”
Visibly emotional, she left the podium.
Unable to answer questions during public comment, the body of city councilors and Mayor Patrick Collins listened in silence as a handful of other speakers approached the dais to implore the city work with the farm.
The council voted to advance the ordinances, promising to have staff consider adding extra rules implementing a time limit for animals brought into the city for shipping or rodeo purposes and fixing a text error inadvertently reducing the amount of chickens one can keep in the city by one.
