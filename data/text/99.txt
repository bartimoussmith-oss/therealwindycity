Months after arson, Cheyenne approves emergency contract for Pumphouse roof repair

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Months after a fire destroyed the roof of Cheyenne’s Historic Pumphouse, the City of Cheyenne has approved an emergency procurement to replace it. The $1,365,402 contract will go to Legacy Construction, the contractor originally approved to provide construction manager at risk work before the fire.
Before the fire, the pumphouse was a candidate for considerable municipal development. In February,
the council approved a $198,903 contract
with TDSi to design and renovate the pumphouse to serve as the new base for the city’s Clean and Safe program.
Fire destroys the Historic Pumphouse
The project temporarily fell apart when the pumphouse caught fire. Emergency dispatch was notified of a fire inside the building the evening of May 17. Three minutes after being called to respond, Cheyenne Fire Rescue Engine 1 arrived at the scene to find the roof on fire and partially collapsed.
Days later, police arrested 35-year-old Cristophe Carabajal under suspicion of causing the incident. The building had no utility connections, ruling out an accidental start. Investigators also eliminated environmental factors, mechanical issues and nearby railroad activity, determining that the fire was either intentionally or recklessly set.
Security footage from a neighboring business captured a man, identified as Carabajal, entering the property through a gap in the fence and climbing into the building through a ground-floor window shortly before the incident. The footage showed him leaving the building minutes before visible flames appeared; no other individuals were recorded at the property.
According to Carabajal’s criminal affidavit, the fire caused $200,000–$500,000 in damages.
Cristophe Carabajal
Carabajal was charged with felony arson and criminal trespass. By late August, however, the Laramie County District Court case against him was dropped due to the state being unable to prove beyond a reasonable doubt that Carabajal, one of many individuals to have broken into the vacant property in the last 10 years, caused the fire.
The misdemeanor criminal trespass charge was refiled in Laramie County Circuit Court Aug. 31. He was found guilty and sentenced to 98 days in jail.
Cheyenne moves to replace pumphouse roof
With winter coming, the City of Cheyenne said, replacing the pumphouse’s roof is imperative. Cheyenne Mayor Patrick Collins initiated an emergency procurement to approve the contract and bypass the lengthy municipal review process, saving the city weeks of potential weather delays before winter.
“Without expedited authorization, work could extend through January 2027, increasing the risk that winter weather will prevent timely stabilization of the building,” the city document detailing the contract said.
The scope of the contract is limited exclusively to weather-proofing. Non-urgent work not required for that purpose is excluded from the contract and will be bid later.
Legacy Construction will first have to remove the ruined roof structure, fascia, soffits, parapet wall, damaged interior stairs, mezzanines, north-end second-floor addition, concrete masonry vestibule and, per Wyoming Department of Environmental Quality standards, asbestos.
The company will then design and install temporary shoring and bracing along the west wall of the original building before constructing the new interior load-bearing metal stud walls, interior concrete footings and roof structure.
The building’s metal roof and sidings will be completed down the line, when the second phase of work is performed. Final stone restoration, brick repointing, landscaping, interior finishes and utility work will be completed during that phase as well.
