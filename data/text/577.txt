Laramie County gas prices drop 29 cents alongside falling national average

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — With a
29-cent drop
in its average price to $3.54 per gallon Monday, Laramie County now offers Wyoming’s cheapest fuel,
according to AAA data
.
Wyoming’s average is down 21 cents to $3.89 per gallon. That’s down 41 cents from two weeks ago. AAA reported a 19-cent drop in the national average to $3.81, with
price-tracking service GasBuddy
reporting a 21.3-cent drop to $3.74 per gallon.
Today’s average is down 55.8 cents from a month ago, but is still up 71.3 cents from a year ago, GasBuddy said. The national average price of diesel fell 18.7 cents over the last week to $5.99 per gallon.
(AAA graphic)
Cheyenne drivers could find the cheapest fuel at $3.30 at Moody’s Rock Stop on West Pershing Boulevard, followed by $3.34 at Sam’s Club on Dell Range Boulevard,
according to GasBuddy reports
.
Laramie County has shed 56 cents from its average price over the past two weeks to take the state’s top spot for cheapest gas. Coming in No. 2 this week is Washakie County’s $3.54 per gallon average. Sweetwater County offers Wyoming’s third cheapest price at $3.61, according to AAA.
Gas prices fell in every state over the last week, with 12 seeing drops of more than 25 cents per gallon, GasBuddy data show.
“These trends may continue, as the International Energy Agency has raised its oil production forecast, putting additional pressure on oil prices as shipments ramp up through the Strait of Hormuz,” said Patrick De Haan, head of petroleum analysis at GasBuddy. “GasBuddy expects the national average price of gasoline to fall this week to its lowest level since March, dropping below $3.72 per gallon.”
Oil prices fell to start the week as expectations of a coming supply surplus outweighed Middle East security concerns. The IEA said the market should return to surplus in the fourth quarter, with supply averaging 105 million barrels per day.
Shipments through the Strait of Hormuz have largely returned to pre-war levels, and Chinese refinery throughput fell 8.1% from a year ago, GasBuddy reported. That’s added downward pressure on crude prices.
“While the oil market likely faced an oversupply in October, escalating vessel attacks in the Strait of Hormuz constrained Middle Eastern exports so far in November, tightening the oil market again and keeping prices supported,” UBS commodities analyst Giovanni Staunovo said in an email. “Conversely, softer economic data — particularly from China, the world’s second-largest oil consumer — cap the upside for crude prices.”
In early Monday trading, West Texas Intermediate crude was down 92 cents to $87.64 per barrel. Brent crude fell $1.41 to $94.43 per barrel.
U.S. commercial crude oil inventories also rose by 4.7 million barrels for the week ending Nov. 6, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. Gasoline inventories rose by 1.8 million barrels.
