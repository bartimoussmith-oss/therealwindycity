Laramie County Planning Commission to hear Chalk Bluffs Energy project permit request
by
Garrett Grochowski

November 12, 2026

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — The Laramie County Planning Commission will hear a conditional use permit request for the Chalk Bluffs Energy Project today before deciding whether to recommend granting it to the Laramie County Board of Commissioners next week. According to the project developer’s application, the 5,210-acre project would encompass 12 wind turbine generators in Laramie County, miles of power lines and more.
The bulk of the project, including most of its solar energy and battery storage infrastructure, would develop just south of the border in Weld County, Colorado. The Laramie County part of the project — 11 miles southeast of Cheyenne — would support some of the wind turbines and power connections to town. Connections include below-ground cables and
above-ground high-voltage transmission lines, the latter of which also requires a 125-foot right-of-way
.
Map of the Chalk Bluffs Energy Project scope (Chalk Bluffs Energy Project website)
Other infrastructure like meteorological monitoring stations, lay-down yards and construction roads is planned as well
Before the project, led by energy developer company Enyo, can get to work, it needs to secure a Class C Conditional Use Permit from Laramie County. Today, the Planning Commission, which hears all land use-related requests before they head to the Laramie County Board of Commissioners for a final decision, will hear the plan and make a recommendation.
County staff,
according to the commission’s Nov. 12 agenda
, are recommending approval. A Class C permit is the most stringent, requiring multiple public hearings and a concept plan. In a meeting last month, Laramie County residents made sure the commissioners were aware that they were not in support of the plan, especially in regards to the transmission lines.
“Looking at the proposed route, we wonder why the jog to our property is in place? Why not do a straight line on property to the south of us, which is pasture land and there are no trees or residences on it? It appears this is a deliberate move to affect our homes and destroy a residential area that has been in place for 100 years,” Cathy Yosten, one of the proposed routes’ neighbors, wrote to Laramie County Planning last month.
The transmission lines aren’t included in the permit, however; they must be permitted separately. The permit currently up for consideration, if granted, would allow Enyo to install the dozen turbines it has planned.
Map depicting the planned location of wind infrastructure included in the Chalk Bluffs Energy Project (Laramie County)
According to submitted comments, some residents are also opposed to that part of the plan, saying that they don’t want the turbines near their homes and that they lament the loss of wild prairie views. Enyo reported that it is trying to address these concerns, working to adjust the projected path of transmission lines and siting of the turbines. It also said negotiations are open with property owners to provide things like landscaping and berms to minimize view impacts.
Submitted agency comments are mostly positive. Wyoming Game and Fish, for example, commended Enyo for choosing a site with low wildlife impact concerns.
If the county approves the project, Enyo’s work in Laramie County would begin in 2028. More than $7.53 million in property taxes would be generated by the project in the next 30 years, Enyo said. The project would also provide 436 total full-time equivalent jobs in Laramie County during construction and 35 total after the site is fully operational.
