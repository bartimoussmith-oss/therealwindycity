1 pound of meth, 1 pound of fentanyl reportedly seized in Cheyenne traffic stop

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — A regular traffic stop for an expired registration on Interstate 25 south of Cheyenne led to the seizure of more than a pound of suspected fentanyl, around a pound of methamphetamine and smaller amounts of heroin and marijuana. The driver of the car is being hit with trafficking charges for both the fentanyl and meth.
According to the Cheyenne Police Department, Carol Blohm-Aguilar, born in 1965, is being charged with multiple felonies and misdemeanors: possession of fentanyl, possession of methamphetamine, possession of fentanyl with intent to deliver, possession of methamphetamine with intent to deliver, misdemeanor possession of heroin and misdemeanor possession of marijuana.
Her felony possession charges each come with a maximum potential penalty of seven years in prison, while the two trafficking charges could add 20 years each. Blohm-Aguilar is presumed innocent until pleading or being found guilty on those charges.
Police said in Blohm-Aguilar’s criminal affidavit that an officer was driving a patrol car in the area of
I-25 and College Drive
south of Cheyenne on Oct. 23 when they spotted a Subaru bearing temporary Colorado registration. The registration being expired, the officer conducted a traffic stop on the car and reportedly noticed the odor of marijuana emanating from the car.
A K-9 sniff test conducted outside the car lent support to the officer’s suspicion, prompting a probable cause search.
During the search, officers reportedly found 565 grams, or about 1.2 pounds, of fentanyl; 453 grams, or approximately 1 pound, of methamphetamine; two grams of heroin; and 10 grams of marijuana.
The amount of meth and fentanyl seized was determined by officers to be too much for personal use and more consistent with a trafficking amount. Blohm-Aguilar also reportedly had a digital scale in the car with suspected drug residue on it.
Due to the severity of her charges, Blohm-Aguilar was issued a $100,000 cash bond upon her arrest and entry into the Laramie County Circuit Court. Before she heads to District Court, she will have a preliminary hearing Nov. 4.
