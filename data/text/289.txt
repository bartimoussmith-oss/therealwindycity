Agriculture code changes, county pocket annexation up for last committee read today

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — The City of Cheyenne’s Public Services Committee will review 12 proposed ordinances today before sending all but one back to the Cheyenne City Council for final consideration. If they are approved, Cheyenne would create new rules for urban farming and gravel parking lots, annex a county pocket and more.
The committee is set to meet at noon today in the Council Chambers of the Municipal Building, located at 2101 O’Neil Ave. The public can also access the meeting remotely
via Zoom
or telephone.
The committee’s agenda today is entirely comprised of ordinances, which require two committee readings before the City Council can vote to adopt them. The only ordinance of the 11 not up for second reading is an amendment to the city’s Unified Development Code regarding construction projects in town.
The amendments codify property-line noise limits based on EPA and WHO guidelines. Non-residential zone thresholds are capped at 65 decibels during the daytime and 60 during the nighttime, while residential zone thresholds are capped at 45 during the daytime and 40 during the nighttime.
They would also require mandatory site plans for construction staging, staging setbacks from residential lot lines, a 125% financial bond for site reclamation and a 1,000-foot buffer between temporary workforce housing and residential areas or schools.
If the committee approves the amendments today, the City Council will review them next week. However, it will ultimately need to send them back to the committee for a second reading.
The remainder of the ordinances need only be read one time more before the City Council can vote to adopt them.
A handful of the ordinances are regular owner-initiated annexations and zoning requests. Areas considered for annexation include Christensen Road and a nearby triangular parcel and the 5-acre Havel property off College Drive set to join
the recently annexed Read Property
as a site for data center development.
Up for annexation without the consent of landowners is the County Pocket Annexation #10, which would take into the city 16 unincorporated enclave properties completely surrounded by city boundaries in western and southern Cheyenne. The purpose of the city-forced annexations is to bridge gaps in utility and emergency service limitations and make access to parts of Cheyenne bordering these neighborhoods easier to access.
The city would assign Agricultural, Medium-Density Residential, Community Business and Light Industrial zones to parts of the area to match current land uses and surrounding neighborhood designations.
One of the properties included in the county pocket is the WY Fresh farm, a local farmstand that sells locally grown agricultural products to the community. Because the city had little Unified Development Code and Municipal Code standards for agricultural enterprises, this annexation will coincide with a series of code amendments made to ensure the business’s continued operation.
The amendments establish standardized Animal Unit measurements to regulate the keeping of farm animals in the city. They also create defined terms for agritourism, farmers markets and non-commercial animal harvesting, while exempting low-impact agricultural uses from formal site plan requirements.
Gravel parking lots would also be permitted within the city for the first time. A UDC amendment would allow gravel lots in site-specific situations such as agricultural operations and areas not frequently visited or only seasonally used.
