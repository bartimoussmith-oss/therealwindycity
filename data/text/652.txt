Draft bill would create categorical grant for food service program expenditures

SHERIDAN — A draft bill would create a state categorical grant program that would reimburse Wyoming school districts for a portion of food service program expenditures.
Currently, schools struggle with food service costs, as districts are not reimbursed by state funds for food service. School districts primarily rely on subsidizing a percentage of its general fund and use federal programs such as the National School Lunch Program.
The proposed bill — 27LSO-0193 — K-12 school finance - food service — will be up for discussion during the Wyoming Legislature’s Select Committee on School Finance Recalibration’s Dec. 14 meeting in Cheyenne.
It states that subject to Wyoming Legislative appropriation, the amount provided to each school district for each school year shall be equal to 33% of the amount actually expended by the district during the immediately preceding school year for qualifying school food service expenditures.
Qualifying school district expenditures means expenditures incurred by a school district for the operation of food service programs for students enrolled in the district, according to the draft.
Sheridan County School District 2’s anticipated expenses for fiscal year 2027 is $2,325,003.87. With the proposed reimbursement rate, the total positive fiscal impact for SCSD2 would be $767,251.27.
SCSD2 is a part of the National School Lunch Program, which is a federally assisted meal program operating in public and nonprofit private schools and residential child care institutions, according to its website.
As SCSD2 is part of the national program, it receives federal reimbursements, but Food Service Director Leslie Haberkern said the school district has to pull funds from its general fund to help supplement the food service program.
“This is not something that’s just individualized to our district. It’s happening (all) over the country,” Haberkern said.
SCSD2 receives $4.76 from the United States Department of Agriculture for a student who qualifies for free meals, but for a student who does not qualify, the district only receives 45 cents.
According to the income eligibility guidelines for determining free and reduced price benefits, a family of four cannot earn more than $42,900 a year to qualify for free meals. The same family size cannot earn more than $61,050 a year to qualify for reduced meals.
Reduced-price meals for SCSD2 are 30 cents for breakfast and 40 cents for lunch, according to its free and reduced meals program page.
Haberkern said the school district is in favor of the draft bill.
“If we want kids to come and experience school every day, we should provide them lunch, too. I think more districts and directors across the state would be in favor of it and it would really benefit,” Haberkern said.
SCSD2 has also worked on its “Ranch to School” program since 2021, through which the school district has received donations of local beef.
School district 2 has also put its efforts toward its Fifth Street greenhouses, which produce about 100 pounds of lettuce and other leafy greens per week.
Sheridan County School District 1 Business Manager and Food Service Director Jeremy Smith said he believes the 33% reimbursement represents what, on average, school districts are experiencing in terms of subsidy.
“Every district is slightly different, obviously, but across the whole state, on average, school districts are having to subsidize their programs by about one-third of the cost of those programs from their general funds,” Smith said. “This bill, should it come out of committee, will be a huge help for us and others to be able to continue to provide high-quality nutritious lunches for our kids and not have to make difficult choices about what you do or don’t do to support that.”
SCSD1’s two elementary schools are part of the National School Lunch Program, while its middle and high schools are not.
According to previous Sheridan Press reporting, the school districts' secondary schools have not been part of the national lunch program since 2010. Smith said that the government imposed nutritional and operational standards on districts that participate, which the school district found to be untenable for its secondary school students.
In general, Smith said SCSD1’s secondary schools do have the larger subsidy from the general fund because they do not have the federal support of those programs, but some of that is really in recognition that it’s a larger food cost at the middle and high schools.
“Some of that’s really driven by the need of the kids and a little bit less about the federal subsidy,” Smith said.
SCSD3 does not participate in the national program, and its current meal prices are $3.50 for grades K-6 and $4.00 for grades 7-12, according to its website.
SCSD3 Business Manager and Food Service Director Greg Rohrer said he is glad to see the legislators are still looking to help with food service expenses.
SCSD3 subsidizes its school lunch program $100,000 every year, Rohrer said.
"Which doesn't help our general fund, which should be focusing on the rest of our education expenses. This year, it was even more challenging because of the increase in teacher wages which resulted in wages for all of our other staff members," Rohrer said. "That $100,000 for food services needed to stay in the general fund to meet our needs for this year."
Rohrer was hoping for more than 33%, but said given the circumstances, anything will be helpful and appreciated.
The 2027 Wyoming Legislative session is set to begin Jan. 12 in Cheyenne.
