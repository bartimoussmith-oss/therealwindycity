Runoff, accessibility concerns doom Cheyenne gravel parking lot proposal

Even in areas with little traffic and where unpaved parking is permitted, ADA-accessible parking spaces must be paved (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — The City of Cheyenne will hold off on permitting gravel parking lots after turning down an ordinance updating the city’s Unified Development Code on Monday night. The ordinance, narrowly rejected 6–4, was proposed alongside a series of other ordinances to support seasonal operations and agricultural businesses like
the WY Fresh farm
.
After initiating the forced annexation of the city’s final batch of county pockets, city staff drew up a series of code amendments to allow agricultural businesses or seasonally opened businesses like pumpkin patches to maintain gravel parking lots. Specifically, the ordinance would have amended UDC Section 6.2.8 to create exceptions to Cheyenne’s universal rule requiring all parking lots and driveways to be paved.
According to city staff, agritourism operations like WY Fresh wouldn’t be the only places in town to have benefited from the change. Single-family homeowners and owners of event and overflow parking lots could have applied to use gravel or compacted aggregate for parking instead of concrete or asphalt to save money.
However, councilmembers, concerned with runoff and accessibility issues, didn’t think the benefits outweighed the costs.
“We work really hard and have worked and invested a lot of money around trying to keep dirt and silt out of our drainage system and out of Crow Creek. We also have a lot of individuals who have accessibility issues,” Councilor Michelle Aldrich said before the council voted on the ordinance.
She acknowledged that the rule requiring businesses to maintain a paved parking space and walk for handicapped individuals is appreciated, but added that it is misleading to business owners who might think they are “off the hook” in regards to paving their lots.
Ultimately, councilmembers Tom Segrave, Kathy Emmons, Ken Esquibel, Mark Rinne and Pete Laybourn agreed and voted no, killing the ordinance 6–4. Cheyenne’s universal paving mandate for commercial and urban parking areas will remain in place.
