Republicans dominate Wyoming’s federal, state and Laramie County elections
by
Klark Byrd
November 4, 2026
November 4, 2026

Left: U.S. Rep. Harriet Hageman. Right: Wyoming Secretary of State Chuck Gray (Oil City News file photos)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Wyoming Republicans completed a sweeping victory across the ballot Tuesday, electing state Sen. Eric Barlow as governor and sending
U.S. Rep. Harriet Hageman to the Senate alongside House-bound Chuck Gray
.
Hageman defeated Democrat James Byrd
with 122,157 votes, roughly 65%, to win the Senate seat being vacated by retiring Sen. Cynthia Lummis. In Laramie County, she received 17,258 votes while Byrd netted 12,919.
Gray defeated three challengers
, picking up about 54% of the vote, to win the House seat left open by Hageman.
The Associated Press called the Senate race for Hageman at 7 p.m. Tuesday as polls closed across the state.
Hageman, an ally of President Donald Trump who first won election to the House in 2022 after defeating incumbent Rep. Liz Cheney, said she plans to continue advancing Trump’s agenda in the Senate.
“The people of Wyoming have hired me to do a new job. I am grateful, and I will not let you down,” Hageman said Tuesday night. “I will now move across the Capitol to the Senate, where I will again put my shoulder to the wheel and be the champion Wyoming wants in Washington, D.C.”
Hageman advanced to Tuesday’s general election after defeating four challengers — Jill M. Edwards, John Holtz, Sam Mead and Jimmy Skovgard — in the August Republican primary.
In the House race, the Associated Press called the contest for Gray at 9:40 p.m. Tuesday with 52% of the vote counted.
By the end of the evening, Gray earned 99,891 votes to defeat Democrat Lisa Kinney, who received 61,687 votes, according to unofficial tallies. Libertarian Shawn Johnson received 13,665 votes and Jeff Haggit of the Constitution Party received 7,960.
Laramie County swung in Gray’s favor with the Republican receiving 13,706 votes to Kinney’s 12,537, Johnson’s 2,226 and Haggit’s 1,316.
Gray, a former Casper radio host who served three terms in the Wyoming House of Representatives, won election as secretary of state in 2022 with Trump’s endorsement. During his time in statewide office, Gray enforced proof-of-citizenship rules for voter registration and banned ranked-choice voting.
Gray secured the Republican nomination for the congressional seat in August after emerging from a nine-candidate primary with 25.4% of the vote.
“I’ve run this campaign to advance real leadership for the people of Wyoming that will continue to advance our Wyoming values,” Gray said on Election Day. “I’m proud to have traveled to all 23 Wyoming counties and traveled over 60,000 miles on my vehicle campaigning across our state.”
GOP retains Wyoming’s top 5 seats
Republican state
Sen. Eric Barlow will serve as Wyoming’s next governor
, leading a sweep of statewide offices for the GOP on Tuesday.
Barlow won 128,480 votes, or 68.6%, with 97% of ballots counted Tuesday night. Constitution Party candidate Rebecca Bextel received 31,430 votes, or 16.8%, while Democrat Kenneth Casner earned 27,469 votes, or 14.7%.
Wyoming governor candidate Sen. Eric Barlow celebrates at a watch party on Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026, at the Wyo Sports Ranch in Casper.(Dan Cepeda, Oil City News and WyoFile)
Laramie County lent its support to Barlow, giving him 20,521 votes to Casner’s 5,779 and Bextel’s 3,874.
The Associated Press called the race for Barlow at 7 p.m. Tuesday as polls closed. Barlow succeeds Gov. Mark Gordon, who is stepping down due to term limits.
“Kelly and I are deeply grateful to the people of Wyoming for the trust they have placed in us,” Barlow said. “We began this campaign by traveling the state, listening to our neighbors and working to earn their support. Those conversations shaped this campaign, and they will guide the work ahead.”
A veterinarian, U.S. Marine Corps veteran and Gillette native, Barlow has served in the Legislature since 2013. He previously served as Speaker of the House before winning a seat in the state Senate.
In the race for secretary of state,
Converse County Commissioner Robert Short
secured the office with 124,924 votes, or 67.8%, with 95% of ballots counted. Democrat Bryan McCarty received 43,872 votes, or 23.8%, and Constitution Party candidate Tamara Trujillo took 15,504 votes, or 8.4%.  Short will succeed Gray in the office.
Secretary of State candidate Robert Short talks at a watch party on Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026, at the Wyo Sports Ranch in Casper.(Dan Cepeda, Oil City News and WyoFile)
In Laramie County, Short received 18,828 votes while McCarty netted 8,680 and Trujillo picked up 2,584.
Short, a Douglas resident and business owner, has served on the Converse County Commission since 2014.
Republican state Rep. Steve Harshman
won the race for superintendent of public instruction, netting 136,744 votes, or 75.1%. Harshman defeated Democrat Ana Cordova, who received 45,318 votes, or 24.9%.
Harshman found strong support in Laramie County, where he received 20,652 votes. Cordova received 9,125 votes.
Harshman has taught and coached at Natrona County High School for 40 years and has represented Casper’s House District 37 in the Legislature since 2003. He replaces Megan Degenfelder, who stepped down to mount
an unsuccessful run for governor
in the August primary.
Republican State Superintendent Steve Harshman hugs his wife Becky after speaking at a watch party on Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026, at the Wyo Sports Ranch in Casper. (Dan Cepeda, Oil City News and WyoFile)
Two other Republican incumbents won reelection after running unopposed on Tuesday’s ballot. State Auditor Kristi Racines and State Treasurer Curt Meier both secured another term in office.
Republicans maintain power in Wyoming Legislature
The GOP won all but one legislative race across Laramie County
on Tuesday.
The victories helped preserve strong Republican majorities in the state Capitol, where the party secured 55 seats in the Wyoming House of Representatives and 29 seats in the Wyoming Senate. Democrats held on to six House seats and two Senate seats.
The sole seat flipped by a Democrat was in Wyoming House District 11, where
Britney Tennant edged out Republican Dan McIntosh
1,080 votes to 1,026. Libertarian Ryan Shollenberger picked up 152 votes in the race. Tennant will succeed Republican Rep. Jacob Wasserburger.
In Senate District 5, Republican candidate Lauren Schoenfeld
, executive director of the Wyoming Innovation Partnership and a former Sweetwater County commissioner, won with 3,356 votes to Democrat challenger Ted Hanlon’s 1,389. She will be succeeding outgoing Sen. Lynn Hutchings.
Longtime Cheyenne City Councilor
Mark Rinne will succeed Sen. Stephan Pappas in Senate District 7
. Rinne received 3,276 votes to Democrat challenger Jim Lee’s 2,257. Rinne and Lee ran unopposed in the August primary elections.
In Senate District 31, incumbent Sen. Evie Brennan defeated Democrat challenger Shifa Hamid to retain her seat. Brennan received 3,172 votes to Hamid’s 1,479.
Brennan was challenged in August’s primary election
by Rep. Daniel Singh, defeating him 1,753 votes to 894.
Sen. Taft Love ran unopposed for his District 6 seat.
For the Wyoming House of Representatives,
incumbent Rep. Bob Nicholas defeated
Democrat challenger Gavin Lobmeyer for his District 7 seat. Nicholas received 1,993 votes to Lobmeyer’s 1,776. Nicholas was challenged in August’s primary election by Deb Mutter Shamley, defeating her 1,319 votes to 698.
The former WYDOT director who ousted incumbent Rep. Steve Johnson for the Republican nomination for House District 8 seat secured his victory Tuesday.
Luke Reiner received 3,120 votes
to Democrat challenger Brenda J. Lyttle’s 927.
In August, Reiner defeated Johnson
by a vote of 2,062 to 970.
Boyd O. Wiggam will be succeeding
outgoing House District 9 Rep. Landon Brown. In Tuesday’s election, Wiggam received 1,792 votes to Democrat challenger Stephen D. Latham’s 1,052.
In August, Wiggam, a Cheyenne bankruptcy attorney and small business owner, defeated Republican challenger Exie R. Brown Jr.
by a vote of 1,075 to 653.
Incumbent Rep. Justin Fornstrom defeated
Constitution Party challenger Tony Perry in Tuesday’s general election to retain his House District 10 seat. Fornstrom received 2,907 votes to Perry’s 578. Fornstrom was elected to his first term in the Wyoming House in 2024.
The Republican candidate who ousted incumbent Rep. Gary Brown will represent House District 41.
Erin Edwards received 1,602 votes
to Democrat challenger Stacy Becker’s 1,137.
In August, Edwards, a longtime volunteer firefighter and former Department of Defense contract firefighter who served during Operation Iraqi Freedom, defeated Brown
by a vote of 592 to 244.
Incumbent Rep. Rob Geringer defeated
Democrat challenger Jim VanDyke on Tuesday to retain his House District 42 seat. Geringer received 2,375 votes to VanDyke’s 695. Geringer was elected to his first term in the Wyoming House of Representatives in 2024.
Four other Laramie County Republicans won seats Tuesday without facing general election opponents.
Reps. Clarence Styvar and Lee Filer retained their District 12 and District 44 seats, respectively, while Jessica Crowder won District 43 and Andrew Server won in District 61. In August,
Crowder ousted incumbent Rep. Ann Lucas
with 1,050 votes, or about 62.9% of the total votes cast. Lucas received 619 votes. Server defeated fellow Republican Seth Bard 543 to 400 in August’s primary to succeed outgoing Rep. Daniel Singh, who
unsuccessfully challenged incumbent Sen. Evie Brennan
for the Wyoming Senate District 31 seat.
Laramie County GOP sits three on Board of Commissioners
All three Republican candidates secured seats on the Laramie County Board of Commissioners
, holding off two Democratic challengers.
Commissioners Troy Thompson and Gunnar Malm sit during the regular Board of Commissioners meeting Oct. 15 inside the Commissioners Meeting Room inside the Laramie County Government Complex in Cheyenne. (Jared Gendron/Cap City News)
The top three vote-getters were incumbent Commissioners Gunnar Malm and Troy Thompson with 18,765 votes and 19,109 votes, respectively, and Jess E. Ketcham with 19,475 votes.
Democrat challengers included Barbara Cabot, who received 10,100 votes, and Lynne K. Davis, who netted 10,955.
“I am incredibly humbled and grateful to the voters of Laramie County for once again entrusting me with the opportunity to serve as your County Commissioner,” Malm wrote in a statement Wednesday morning. “As I begin another term, my commitment remains the same: to listen, to lead with common sense, to protect private property rights, to be a responsible steward of your tax dollars, and to always put the people of Laramie County first.”
Malm, Thompson and Ketcham were the top three candidates to emerge from August’s primary.
They outpolled challengers Julie Christen, M. Lee Hasenauer, Jason MacGregor, Gregory C. Pitts, Clifford Spoonemoore, Josh Tuttle and incumbent Commissioner Linda Heath.
The only other contested county-level race was for sheriff.
Republican incumbent Sheriff Brian Kozak cruised to an easy victory
with 19,066 votes over Democrat challenger Robin Cozad’s 10,545.
In August, Kozak defeated challenger Eric Norris
in the Republican primary election. Kozak earned 11,256 votes to advance to the general election. Norris received 7,271 votes.
Uncontested Republican candidates in Tuesday’s election included Misty Tinney for clerk, Diane Sanchez for clerk of the District Court, Rebecca Reid for coroner, Todd A. Ernst for assessor, Sylvia Lee Hackle for district attorney and Trista Wood for treasurer.
All election results remain unofficial until certified by county and state canvassing boards. Those elected on Tuesday will take office in January.
