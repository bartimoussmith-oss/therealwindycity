‘The essentials budget’: Gov. Gordon introduces $500M supplemental budget

Garrett Grochowski

Wyoming Governor Mark Gordon discusses his final supplemental budget with senior policy advisor Seth Ulvestad Nov. 16, 2026 (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Totaling more than $500 million, Wyoming Gov. Mark Gordon’s final supplemental budget was released Monday. In a press conference, the governor said the budget, which addresses unforeseen and additional costs not approved in the regular budget from January, reflects his long-standing commitment to fiscal responsibility.
The budget, upon approval by the Wyoming Legislature during its session early next year, would appropriate hundreds of millions of dollars to fund property tax relief, health and human services, food security, education programs, capital construction projects and more. Gordon said he was focused on covering what he considered the necessities.
The budget in full is available at the Wyoming State Budget Department’s website
here
. The 800-plus-page document breaks down the entire multimillion-dollar supplemental budget by item and department, including Gordon’s justification for each appropriation.
“I have really talked for the last two years about this budget being the essentials budget,” Gordon said. “I grew up on a ranch and I’ve always admired families that have been able to pass on their operation year after year and that is dedication to future generations and a desire to always leave things a little bit better than we found them.”
More than $15 million will go to property tax relief for Wyoming homeowners, fully funding the state’s
Property Tax Refund Program
for the 2026 tax year. Without it, the funding set aside by the budget would run out.
A large chunk of the added budget amount — $100 million — would go directly into savings. Half would go into the state’s Permanent Wyoming Mineral Trust Fund, while the other half would go into the Common School Permanent Land Fund. Both, through active market investment, are among the state’s top revenue sources and help keep the state from dipping into taxpayer pockets, Gordon said.
“This is important because it’s something we build every year and I think people in Wyoming need to understand that, at least one year when I was treasurer and I think two or three years since
Treasurer [Curt] Meier has been in office
, the number-one funding source has been our investments,” Gordon said.
Education was also a concern of Gordon’s, and the budget guarantees the state continues to ensure good educational opportunities for its young people through a handful of programs and project funding. Tens of millions would go to various capital improvement projects for schools, including vehicle barricades and other safety features, as well as ongoing construction projects around the state.
Gordon also recommended the University of Wyoming receive $24 million, which he said will relieve increases in Higher Education Price Index inflation and meet endowment fund matches, among other things. Community colleges would benefit as well — especially Casper College, which could receive funding for its proposed Industrial Technology Building.
“It is important, I think, that people remember that our constitution requires that every child get a good education and they be equally funded. That is a state responsibility,” Gordon said. “We owe our students, families and taxpayers a clearer understanding of what our education investments accomplish and to that end we have worked on a couple of things that will be particularly valuable going forward.”
That also means feeding students in need. He highlighted one appropriation in particular: a year’s funding for the SUNbucks program, which provides grocery benefits to eligible students during the summer, when reduced-price lunches aren’t available.
The program is federally funded, but states are required to match administrative costs. A little under $500,000 would fully fund the program for the next year and an additional appropriation of $575,000 would pay for the administration costs for the next few years.
“I think legislative action is important to continue this program,” Gordon said. “It’s something that I have heard repeatedly over the course of this year, how important that program was to make sure kids were not dealing with hunger and were ready to learn, ready to do well.”
Other appropriations fund future legal expenses, forestry and wildfire suppression, tourism services and more.
As this is his final supplemental budget, Gordon said he wanted to make sure it reflects the practicality and fiscally responsible values that he built his governorship upon.
“I would hope that as people reflect on this budget and try to find what that meant for me, that they would say it was practical, it met the essential needs and it put the state on a good footing going forward,” he said. “Those would be the key elements to that.”
It will ultimately be the legislature’s responsibility to approve the budget when after convening in January. See more about the legislature’s schedule
here
.
