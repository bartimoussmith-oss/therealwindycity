Oct. 7 armed standoff defendant pleads not guilty; jury trial set for February

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Brian Kuzma, arrested Oct. 7 after a domestic dispute with his parents in which he reportedly fired a weapon into the air, pleaded not guilty to his charges last week. His trial is set to begin Feb. 9, 2027.
An east Cheyenne neighborhood was put on lockdown the afternoon of Oct. 7. Neighbors received text alerts from law enforcement telling them to stay indoors as officers worked in the area. After more than six hours, police announced that they had made an arrest and there was no longer any need to be alarmed.
They arrested 42-year-old Brian Kuzma on charges of aggravated assault with a deadly weapon, interference and stalking. He was transported to the Laramie County Detention Center via SWAT vehicle.
Kuzma is now pleading not guilty to those charges, kicking off a court process set to begin early next year. He will have scheduling and pretrial conferences Dec. 14 and Jan. 26 before his jury trial begins Feb. 9.
Kuzma is presumed innocent unless he is found guilty on his charges.
Kuzma’s affidavit, made available shortly after his arrest, reports that the Oct. 7 incident began with a confrontation between him and his parents.
Police were called to the neighborhood at around 3:30 p.m. Oct. 7 but were originally called to Kuzma’s parents’ house a few blocks away from where the standoff occurred. There, they spoke to Kuzma’s father, who reported that he had barricaded himself inside his house while Kuzma went to the home and shot a firearm at a tree. The father was described as being “distraught” over what occurred.
A spent .45 ACP casing was found on the porch, but police were unable to find anywhere nearby where a bullet might have struck.
The father told police that Kuzma struggles with anger issues and can act irrationally. Kuzma had pointed the gun at his own head before eventually shooting it elsewhere, the father said. Kuzma also reportedly stole their yellow Labrador retriever before fleeing from his parents’ residence.
Officers contacted Kuzma via phone. Initially, he did not share where he was, only that he could see officers outside his residence. Law enforcement had at that point been able to discover his approximate location via tracking his cellphone, which the Laramie County Sheriff’s Department justified as an exigent circumstance because Kuzma appeared to be experiencing suicidal thoughts.
Kuzma’s parents had also told law enforcement about a prior incident in which their son threatened them. On Sept. 5, Kuzma reportedly called his parents every 10 minutes over the course of several hours, telling them that he would come over to their home and kill them. He reportedly drove over to the house and entered using spare keys. His father reportedly barricaded himself until Kuzma fled and deputies arrived. They tracked Kuzma back to his residence — the same in which he would barricade himself the following month — using a drone, but eventually left after he refused commands to exit.
Using this prior incident as probable cause, officers charged Kuzma with aggravated assault, stalking and interference. The assault charge is a felony and carries a maximum potential penalty of 10 years in prison. Kuzma is again presumed innocent until pleading or being found guilty.
Kuzma was issued a $25,000 cash bond, which will combine with another $1,000 bond issued specifically for the interference charge.
