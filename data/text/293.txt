PODER Academy charters renewed by LCSD1

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — The Laramie County School District 1 Board of Trustees voted Monday to renew its charter with PODER Academy, ensuring the charter schools will receive district operational resources for another five years.
The trustees met Monday evening in the Storey Gymnasium in Cheyenne. Before holding their regular meeting, the trustees heard reports from district administration on the upcoming reorganization of school boundaries as Jessup Elementary prepares to close and options for the early retirement health insurance subsidy offered to qualifying district staff.
Regular meeting business included purchasing new school buses, renewing the district’s memorandum of understanding with Laramie County Community College, approving the early retirement plan and more. See the recording of the meeting
here
for more information.
The board voted on two contracts related to PODER Academy, a local charter school program. As a charter school, PODER Academy offers a unique curriculum and operates independently from the district, but is still publicly funded and benefits from operating as a partner with its local school district.
As a charter of LCSD1, PODER Academy Elementary School, the academy’s K-6 school, and PODER Academy Secondary School — also known as P.A.S.S. — get access to the district’s nutritional services, coordinated transportation and the Infinite Campus student information system.
In return, the district gets a high-performing public educational partner that offers unique public education choices to district parents. According to PODER COO Nick Vaughn, PODER’s two schools boast some of the best statewide test results in Wyoming. PODER Elementary, he said to the district in an Oct. 19 information session, consistently scores 90%–100% proficiency in state WY-TOPP ELA and math tests, while PODER Secondary recently received state recognition for being the highest-performing high school program in Wyoming for WY-TOPP ELA, math and science.
Both schools serve an economically disadvantaged student population and, due to their unique class structures, emphasize college readiness in their curricula. At P.A.S.S., accelerated classes early on give students time to take classes for college credit in their junior and senior years so they have an early advantage when they graduate, Vaughn said.
Both votes passed 6–0, ensuring that the PODER Academy schools are LCSD1 charters for another five years. The district will soon begin the process of drafting the terms of the charter agreement.
