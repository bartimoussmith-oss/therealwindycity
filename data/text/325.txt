CASPER, Wyo. — Republican State Rep. Steve Harshman has been elected to serve as Wyoming’s next superintendent of public instruction.
He succeeds Megan Degenfelder, who lost her Republican gubernatorial bid in August to Sen. Eric Barlow.
Barlow ultimately won the seat Tuesday.
In Tuesday’s race, called by the Associated Press at 9:26 p.m., Harshman received 79,578 votes compared to Democrat challenger Ana Cordova’s 23,674.
The final tally is subject to change as the race was called with 52% of the total votes cast. Polls closed at 7 p.m.
Harshman advanced to the general election as the Republican nominee after defeating Chad Auer and state Rep. Tom Kelly in August’s primary election.
A Casper native who taught and coached at Natrona County High School for 40 years, Harshman has represented House District 37 in the Legislature since 2003. During his tenure in the House, he authored legislation establishing the Hathaway Scholarship and the Wyoming’s Tomorrow Scholarship programs. Harshman is running on a platform focused on supporting teachers, protecting local school board decision-making and prioritizing reading instruction and career readiness across public, private and home education options.
