(The Center Square) – More than half a million Americans are without power nationwide as winter storms continue to batter the Northeast.
States across the northern U.S. are expected to experience continued severe weather through the weekend.
“A large storm will shift across the Northeast U.S. today and tonight with widespread gusty winds, rain, and coastal flooding,” the National Weather Service
said
Friday morning. “Freezing rain over Maine and accumulating snow in the Tennessee Valley and the Appalachians may create hazardous travel. Heavy snow and high winds are expected across portions of the Rocky Mountains and southern High Plains through Saturday.”
This comes as millions of Americans take to the roads and skies to return home from Thanksgiving celebrations, with AAA
projecting
81.5 million people will travel at least 50 miles from home by Monday.
Across the nation, approximately 525,000 households are without power already. The outages are largely
concentrated
in Pennsylvania, New Jersey, Missouri and Maryland.
The winter storm that battered the East Coast forced the postponement of the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade for the first time in its 100-year history.
“With severe weather expected on Thanksgiving Day, and out of an abundance of caution, in partnership with the City, the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade will be postponed,” Macy’s said.
The parade is now scheduled for Saturday at 8:30 a.m. The delay is expected to add nearly $2 million in helium costs alone.
This comes as thousands of flights have been
canceled
since Wednesday, with tens of thousands delayed and more delays expected going into the weekend.
“Heading home after Thanksgiving? Nearly 100,000 flights are planned this weekend,”
said
the National Weather Service Aviation Weather Center. “A Central U.S. low-pressure system will bring aviation hazards Saturday morning into Sunday morning.”
