Cheyenne father charged with child abuse after baby rushed to hospital with brain trauma

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — A young Cheyenne father is being charged with aggravated child abuse after reportedly being “too rough” handling his infant son, causing severe head trauma.
The man, Benyen Glade, born in 2004, faces one count of aggravated child abuse causing physical injury. The charge threatens a potential maximum prison sentence of 25 years in jail. He is presumed innocent until convicted in a court of law.
According to police, Glade’s son, born in 2026, was rushed to the hospital Oct. 5 after he went limp and stopped breathing. Glade and his wife called emergency services after the infant became unresponsive, but managed to resuscitate him with the help of the wife’s mother, a registered nurse, before services arrived.
Once at Cheyenne Regional Medical Center, the baby underwent medical testing, none of which showed any indication of physical abuse. However, suffering seizures, he was flown to the Children’s Hospital of Colorado. There, additional testing found subdural hemorrhages, multiple retinal hemorrhages and significant leg injuries, police reported.
Hospital staff determined the infant had likely suffered “abusive head trauma” and refuted Cheyenne Regional Medical Center’s assessment that CT results had shown benign buildups of cerebrospinal fluid in the brain. The Colorado hospital reportedly said that was a misread of results, that the baby was suffering hemorrhaging and that additional MRI results suggested a history of prior brain injury.
Glade’s wife told police that the baby had been lying on the bed before getting a bath, and that air from a nearby fan hitting his face shocked him, causing him to go limp. He appeared normal before the air blew on him, she said.
Police said Glade told the same story, adding that he was performing “bicycle kicks” with the baby’s legs to help relieve gas before changing his diaper.
The court document states that both parents denied any history of accidental trauma, including drops, rolls, falls or “bonks” to the head, which police said added to clinician’s concerns of abuse.
