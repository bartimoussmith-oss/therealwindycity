Tough choices ahead for LCSD1, Jessup Elementary parents as school closure looms
by
Garrett Grochowski
November 6, 2026
November 6, 2026

Laramie County School District 1 Superintendent Stephen Newton talks to parents about the different potential school boundary map options the district may choose from ahead of Jessup Elementary School's closure at the end of the 2026-2027 school year (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Jessup Elementary School on the west side of Cheyenne is one of a handful of area elementary schools planned for closure after a
2024 districtwide Most Cost-Effective Remedy study determined that 10 local schools should be closed to save money
. As the school next up for shuttering, the school’s parents and the district itself are faced with a tough decision: Where should the students go?
The 2024 study determined that three local schools will be replaced, two will receive additions and two brand-new schools will be built. It also found that, due to facility conditions and capacity imbalances, eight local elementary schools would be systematically shut down.
Read more about the MCER study and parent responses to it
here
.
In 2025, Miller Elementary waved goodbye to its last class of students. Deming Elementary did the same next year. At the end of this current school year, the last class of Jessup Jaguars will leave for summer break.
While the decision to close the school is already made up, Laramie County School District 1 faces a few additional questions about the Jessup community’s future. By the end of summer 2027, the LCSD1 Board of Trustees will need to decide if they want to sell, demolish or mothball the Jessup building itself. In the next month, they’ll have to decide where to send the school’s current students.
As a neighborhood school district, LCSD1 students are required to attend the school nearest to where they live as determined by school boundaries the district sets. In every scenario, Jessup’s boundaries are consumed by Prairie Wind Elementary and Davis Elementary, while Hobbs Elementary’s boundaries change to make up for Davis’s expansion.
From the district administration’s perspective, the decision will hopefully come down to making the cleanest possible boundaries, regardless of ever-fluctuating capacity.
“It helps make busing a lot easier and more efficient with the routes. It also keeps it more of a ‘neighborhood’ school when they’re a bit more squared-up,” Andy Knapp, LCSD1 executive director of support operations, told the district board of trustees Nov. 2.
But the different options take other factors into consideration. While the Brittney Road option presents the most “squared-up” boundaries, the Interstate 25 boundary would present a clearer separation between the two areas.
Some, however, mean students might have to travel a longer way to Prairie Wind to the north. In every scenario, some neighborhood previously connected will soon see itself sending students on the north side of the street to one elementary and students on the south side to another.
As emotional a consideration as it is, the district hosted a parent meeting at Jessup Elementary on Nov. 5. There, parents heard directly from administration about its rationale, learned about the ultimate decision-making process and got to add their own input.
One mom told Superintendent Stephen Newton, who led the meeting, that Jessup classes have already gotten so small in recent years that classmates and, by extension, their families, have gotten very close.
“My son will be in fourth grade next year and he’s been with the same kids since kindergarten,” she said. “I think it’ll be really good for them to have more kids, but I think it would be so helpful if they had a couple friendly faces in their classroom. As you’re deciding that, just kind of keep that in mind.”
Other parent concerns included class sizes: Would sending Jessup’s kids to David and Prairie Wind inhibit their learning because there are so many more additional students? The district clarified that capacity is usually never an issue, as both Davis and Prairie Wind have vacant classrooms that would accommodate the additional students without significantly changing class sizes.
Newton also told parents that if they are redistricted into a neighborhood school that they don’t want their children attending, they can apply for a boundary waiver that, if approved and if that school is not at full capacity, would allow them to send their kid elsewhere.
“We will honor the Jessup boundary waivers first,” Newton said.
However, students attending schools outside their area with a boundary waiver can’t use district buses; their parents would have to drive them. Even if a boundary waiver is approved, a transition from Jessup isn’t guaranteed to be a seamless transition for parents.
Parent input will be taken into consideration over the next two weeks, but the boundaries will ultimately be decided by the LCSD1 Board of Trustees on Nov. 16.
As for the fate of the Jessup building, which will be decided on sometime next summer, Newton said there isn’t really anything in particular that district administration is pulling for. If the building is in good shape and the district finds a need, it could be used for some administrative purpose. Miller Elementary, for example, is now used as a storage facility. Deming Elementary, which the district couldn’t find a use for but was added to the
National Register of Historic Places in 2005
and could therefore not easily be demolished, was put up for sale.
“I will tell you this: There is no leading theory on what we’re probably going to do and we just haven’t said it out loud yet,” Newton said. “It wasn’t that way for the other two, it’s not that way for this one. We are really, really trying to focus on what is the issue in front of us right now, and what issue is in front of us right now is making sure your kids have a great experience with where they go next.”
The Board of Trustees’ decision on the new school boundaries will be made Nov. 16 alongside other items in its regular meeting agenda. See the LCSD1 website
here
for access to the agenda.
