DEA: Over 22,000 lbs of drugs destroyed in Take Back Day, curbing abuse and contamination

CASPER, Wyo. —  The Drug Enforcement Administration announced that 22,596 pounds of unneeded, unwanted and expired medications were destroyed across four states in the Rocky Mountain region during the most recent drug Take Back Day on October 24.
That included 1,364 pounds from 15 collection sites in Wyoming, the release said.
Since 2010, the DEA has collected and incinerated more than 20 million pounds of medications nationwide with the goal of curbing abuse and keeping the drugs from ending up in landfills or water supplies.
The DEA’s Rocky Mountain Field Division intiative covered 199 collection sites across four states: Colorado, Utah, Montana and Wyoming.
“We extend our thanks to people in the Rocky Mountain Division who got rid of more than ten tons of medications the right way,” said DEA RMFD Special Agent in Charge Mark Putnam.
“We’re also appreciative of our local and state partners who assist in the collection, preventing potential misuse or abuse of prescription medication.”
The breakdown by state for collection October 24 is as follows:
Utah: 62 departments with 95 sites – collected 10,975 lbs.
Colorado: 50 departments with 72 sites – collected 9,242 lbs.
Montana: 16 departments with 17 sites – collected 1,015 lbs.
Wyoming: 13 departments with 15 sites – collected 1,364 lbs.
Division totals: 141 departments with 199 sites – collected 22,596 lbs.
The DEA’s Take Back Day is always the final Saturday in April and October, the release said.
