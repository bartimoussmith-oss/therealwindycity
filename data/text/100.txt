Cheyenne finalizes deal to use old F.E. Warren Golf Course for CFD parking

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Mary Kay Hill, Board of Directors chair for Cheyenne Frontier Days, has called the Park N’ Ride experience the least attractive part of the annual event. However, the ordeal is slated to become a lot easier after the City of Cheyenne and F.E. Warren Air Force Base agreed to an easement allowing the base’s defunct golf course to be used as a new parking lot.
The lot, much closer to the CFD grounds than the current Park N’ Ride location near Happy Jack Road, will eventually connect to Frontier Park via a pedestrian bridge over Interstate 25.
According to the terms of the easement, a 36-acre section of what used to be the base’s golf course would be handed over to the city to be turned into a 2,868-spot parking lot and CFD would assume responsibility for the site’s long-term maintenance. The easement officially runs for one year from Nov. 1, 2026, through Nov. 1, 2027, and is conveyed to the city at no cost.
Conceptual image of the proposed Park N’ Ride lot that would take over the defunct AFB golf course (City of Cheyenne)
Access to the lot would come from Gate 1 of the Air Force base near Randall and Pershing. A future pedestrian bridge over Interstate 25, which runs directly between the area and Frontier Park, would let Frontier Days attendees go directly from their cars to the festivities by foot.
For the immediate future, however, the lot will be served exclusively by the Park N’ Ride system the same way that visitors are used to: pay to park in a lot, then board shuttle buses that will continuously run between the lot and Frontier Park.
The contract is good for one year, after which the city can extend it. According to City Engineer Tom Cobb, spaces will be prepared with a gravel surface, save for 39 ADA-compliant spaces paved with concrete.
Crews will also need to expand entry and exit lanes off Patriot Drive, the road leading to the lot from the Randall Avenue gate, and conduct other work around the base’s entrance to accommodate more traffic during event days.
The city would assume no financial responsibility in developing the lot itself, nor would it for the development of the pedestrian bridge, which will come in a future phase of development. Both the lot and the bridge would, Hill told the Cheyenne Public Services Committee last week, fulfill CFD’s long-term goals of making the event less of a burden on Frontier Park’s neighbors and safer for visitors.
As an active military installation, F.E. Warren retains entry control, security oversight and the right to end or restrict access on 120 days’ notice in the event of national defense needs or emergencies on base grounds.
