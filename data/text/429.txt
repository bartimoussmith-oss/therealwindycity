The Cheyenne City Council meets Sept. 28, 2026 (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — The Cheyenne City Council will take its final round of votes tonight on a series of ordinances to annex and zone pockets of unincorporated county land that, acting as barriers for utilities and emergency services, the city wishes to incorporate. One of those pockets, housing a local farm operation, will be subject to sweeping agriculture rule changes the city is also poised to pass.
The council has more than 10 ordinances up for final reading tonight. A handful are related to small, owner-initiated annexations. Others would, upon passing, complete the city’s long-term goal of fully incorporating all surrounded county pockets.
The County Pocket #10 annexation — the last round of county pocket annexations — incorporates about 50 acres of land across 16 properties.
One of those properties belongs to local farmstand WY Fresh. As a agricultural producer, extant city code would not have allowed it to continue its operations upon annexation. That is why the city originally postponed the final county pocket annexation and began drafting a series of code amendments specifically addressing agriculture.
The ordinances establish definitions surrounding agriculture as a use in the city and some rules pertaining to the lifestyle. Changes include allowing electric fencing, gravel parking lots and expanded animal husbandry, all with specific conditions attached. Read more about the ordinances
here
.
The proposed changes have reportedly failed to satisfy the owners of WY Fresh,
who announced Sunday their intentions to sell their land and relocate their business after the annexation of their land is approved
.
Other City Council business includes the approval of two local liquor licenses, modifications of the city’s contracts with the National Forest Foundation and for
the Reed Avenue Rail Corridor
, the purchase of 26 sets of SWAT ballistic body armor and more.
The council will also introduce other items not yet up for final consideration, including another liquor license, a funding increase for the
Laramie County Comprehensive Climate Action Plan
, a resolution authorizing the city treasurer to discharge uncollectible debts, a resolution approving salary adjustments for non-uniformed City employees, two final plats and more.
