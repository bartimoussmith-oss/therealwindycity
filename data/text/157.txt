Jenny Lou Christman
Jenny Lou Christman:
1948 – 2026
Jenny Lou Christman (Houghton) passed away on October 16, 2026, in Cheyenne, Wyoming. She was born on February 14, 1948, in Kirksville, Missouri, and lived a life marked by strength, kindness, and deep devotion to her family.
Jenny graduated from East High School in 1966 and went on to build a long and steady career, working for the State of Wyoming for 36 years. Her commitment and perseverance were reflected not only in her work, but in the way she lived every part of her life. She was known as a super kind woman, and also as someone wonderfully stubborn and strong. That strength carried her through life and was especially evident in the love and determination with which she raised her children by herself, an accomplishment that remained among the things she was most proud of. Her children were, above all, the center of her heart.
In her younger years, Jenny was an avid runner, enjoyed making crafts, and volunteering for Safe House. She also loved watching Western movies, a simple pleasure that brought her enjoyment over the years. Whether in motion, meeting life head-on, or relaxing with a favorite film, she brought the same resilient spirit to all she did.
She is survived by her sons, Mathew Rusk and Jeffrey Rusk, who were her greatest pride and joy. She is also survived by her sister, Winnie Talkington. Jenny had five grandkids: Michael Wester (Susie), Kristina Madden (Charlie), Austin Rusk (Alyssa), Cortland Kazol (Wyatt), Rebecca Fryman (Wyatt); and 11 great-grand-kids. She is also survived by nieces and a nephew and by all who loved her and were shaped by her strength, care, and enduring example.
Jenny was preceded in death by her first husband, Ted Rusk; her second husband, Ed Christman; her father, Virgil Houghton; her mother, Rosalie Houghton; her brothers, Steven, who died as a baby, and Tom; and her nephew, Casey Talkington.
Jenny will be remembered for her strength, her determination, her kindness, and the fierce love she gave to her family. Her life was one of perseverance and heart, and her memory will remain a lasting comfort to those who knew and loved her.
Elvin Lue Steward:
1942 – 2026
Elvin Lue Steward, 83, died on October 23, 2026. Born on December 19, 1942, he lived a life marked by strong loyalties, vivid stories, and a spirit that left a lasting impression on those who knew him.
Glendo was home to Elvin, and so much of what he loved grew out of that place and the life he built around it. He found joy in the open air and in motion—in RVs, snow mobiles, motorcycles, fishing, boating, and 4-wheeling. He loved the Broncos and the Wyoming Cowboys, and when the game was on, he expected the world to know it. He was a storyteller by nature, the kind of man whose presence could fill a room, and whose tales and memories were shared with unmistakable personality.
Elvin served in the Air Force, a chapter of his life that stood among the experiences that shaped his character. He was also proud to serve as president of The Oil, Chemical and Atomic Workers Union, a role that reflected both his strength and his commitment. Among his proudest accomplishments were his band, his family, and his union leadership. Music was not just an interest to him—he was a rockstar in his own right, and literally had a band called “The Kountdowns.”
He was remembered as a family man, even as those closest to him lovingly described him as a grump, a crotchety old man with a style all his own. He loved hard, lived boldly, and carried a rough-edged charm that made him unforgettable. He was known for beating people up and getting into bar fights, a reflection of a fierce and unvarnished streak that was simply part of who he was. Through it all, he remained deeply himself—colorful, stubborn, spirited, and impossible to ignore.
Those who carry his memory forward include his daughter, Brenda, her husband Mark, their children, Michael and Alyssa and their families; his daughter, Faith and Faith’s daughter, Melissa; his son, Sonny and his family; and his granddaughter, Kylie. Also remembered with affection is his beloved dog, Sadie, who was part of the household and part of the love that surrounded him.
Elvin was preceded in death by his wives, Ruth and Elaine; and daughter, Wendy.
He leaves behind a memory that is lively and unmistakable: a man of music, motion, stories, grit, and family. He will be remembered in the roar of a motorcycle, in the pull of the water, in the excitement of game day, and in the stories retold by those who knew him best.
The family asks you to join a celebration of life that will be announced at a later date.
