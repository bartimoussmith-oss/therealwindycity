Laramie County seeks $250K federal grant to inspect rural bridges for resiliency

Garrett Grochowski

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — The Laramie County Board of Commissioners agreed Tuesday to submit a grant application to the Federal Highway Administration, asking for $250,000 to test the resiliency of 43 bridges in the county against tornadoes, floods and earthquakes.
The board unanimously approved a resolution to submit the application, which lays out the county’s case for wanting to test how area bridges would endure natural disasters and regular wear and tear, as well as where those bridges specifically are.
(Laramie County)
The majority of the bridges are either around Cheyenne or dispersed around eastern Laramie County. They range from as small as a one-lane bridge spanning the skinny headwaters of Chugwater Creek near Farthing to highway bridges hopping over drainages north of Cheyenne. They all place a similar concern on the minds of Laramie County Public Works: flood damage.
“Of the 43 bridges proposed for assessment in this project, 29 are located in a FEMA 100-year floodplain, two are located in a FEMA 500-year floodplain, and five are located in a FEMA floodway,” the grant application notes. “The assessment will allow the county to identify and, if appropriate, repair, retrofit or replace bridges at highest risk for failure in 100-year flood level and or are located in floodways.”
The application noted that the county had already fallen victim to fatal flooding events in years past, including the 1985 event that left 12 dead and caused $65 million in property damage. Extreme precipitation events causing localized flooding are, the county added, on the rise since 2009.
Eleven of the bridges are also in places known to pond up regularly, sometimes ending up with water depths of over 2 feet. Water isn’t the county’s only cause of concern for local bridges, however.
Laramie County is the area of the state most susceptible to tornadoes, with six tornado days per year.
“All 43 bridges will be assessed for their vulnerability to tornado event damage, and design for those that are most at risk will include infrastructure hardening design to mitigate damage and avoid bridge failure during and after tornado events,” the grant application read.
Earthquakes, as well as the increase in yearly average temperature that has been linked to more extreme weather events, were considered as well.
If the Federal Highway Administration grants the county the $250,000 it is asking for, Public Works staff would tour the county, conducting field work to assess the bridges for scouring, asphalt damage, infrastructure vulnerabilities, other potential deficiencies and the condition of the bridge overall. The grant covers 100% of the project cost.
The Wyoming Department of Transportation currently conducts routine inspections every two years and alerts the county when issues arise. This assessment, however, would move Laramie County from a reactive repair model to a proactive, system-wide risk management plan.
That would help the county avoid suffering from transportation cutoffs in the case of an emergency, which is especially important, it said, around places like F.E. Warren Air Force Base.
Each bridge would cost around $5,814. That figure includes the cost of field work, data analysis, design work, project management and grant administration.
If the grant is approved, field work and public engagement would kick off in spring 2027, data organization would run through the summer, cost estimates and concept designs for high-risk bridges would take up the fall and the assessment would conclude in the last three months of the year.
The commissioners also approved a memorandum of understanding to provide up to $98,664 in matching funds for public transportation services, ratified a $39,178 agreement with Tovar Concrete for work outside the courthouse, accepted $51,917 in federal funds to pay for Cheyenne police overtime and training, approved an emergency wildfire suppression agreement with the Wyoming Forestry Division, approved an MOU with Laramie County School District 1 for juvenile center schooling, ratified a $5,017.20 change order for hydromulching steep slopes on Chalk Bluffs Road and authorized removing four 2016 Ford Taurus cars from the sheriff’s office’s fixed assets for auction.
