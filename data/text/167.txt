Cheyenne Happenings (10/30, 10/31): Trick or Treat Street, Stephen King’s ‘Misery,’ more
by
Nick Perkins
October 29, 2026
October 29, 2026

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — The weekend is almost upon us, which means you made it through another week. You should be proud of yourself, and you should treat yourself to the myriad of activities happening throughout Cheyenne this weekend. From a Trick or Treat Street at the Wyoming State Capitol, to a production of Stephen King’s “Misery,” a Halloween pub crawl and more, this is what’s happening this weekend in Cheyenne.
Story Time at Cheyenne Botanic Gardens
Paul Smith Children’s Village is hosting a Story Time event at the Cheyenne Botanic Gardens on Friday, beginning at 11 a.m.
“Join us Fridays at 11 a.m. for a story, a fun project, and shenanigans,”
the event page states
. “Meet under the blue dome during the summer. During the winter, meet us in the classroom in the Children’s Village.”
Happening Friday at 11 a.m.
Book Babies
On Friday, the Laramie County Library is hosting Book Babies, beginning at 10:15 a.m.
“Book Babies is a shorter, less-structured Early Literacy Class just for babies (best for ages 0–24 months)!”
the event page states
. “It’s a great time to bond with your baby and inspire a love of reading. You will learn lap bounces, tickle rhymes, movement songs, and fingerplays that you can do with your baby at home, too! We will typically read 1 developmentally appropriate book and explore 1 flannel story each week. It’s also a great time for babies to start learning how to socialize with other children. After the stories, we have Stay and Play where babies can play with developmentally appropriate toys while caregivers visit.”
Happening Friday at 10:15 a.m.
Dia De Los Muertos
On Friday and Saturday, Cheyenne Botanical Gardens is hosting a Dia De Los Muertos celebration, beginning at 10 a.m.
“Celebrate those that have passed on and learn about the culture, traditions, and history of the Day of the Dead,”
the event page states
. “You can find events across town throughout the month of October and into the first week of November! Día de los Muertos is a 2-day holiday that began in Mexico and is traditionally celebrated across Latin America and beyond. During these two days, it is believed that the passage between the real world and the spirit world opens, allowing deceased loved ones to come back and visit their living relatives. Traditions and customs vary in different places, but the base beliefs remain the same. This holiday offers a unique and colorful perspective on death that turns a sad fact of life into something beautiful. In partnership with a committee of community members, the Cheyenne Botanic Gardens, Laramie County Public Library, Wyoming State Museum, and Cheyenne Depot Museum are hosting community celebrations to share the significance of this holiday and provide welcoming spaces for people to celebrate their culture. Everyone is welcome to enjoy these community celebrations and open their eyes to cultures and traditions that they might have never known about.”
Happening Friday and Saturday at 10 a.m.
(Shutterstock)
State of Wyoming Trick-or-Treat Street
On Friday, the Wyoming State Capitol Complex is hosting a trick or treat event for Cheyenne residents from 1 to 5 p.m.
“Bring your little ghosts, goblins, superheroes, and princesses for an afternoon of Halloween fun!”
the event page states
. “On October 30 from 1 to 5 p.m., Wyoming state agencies are teaming up to host a free, family-friendly trick-or-treat event across multiple Capitol Complex locations. Costumes are encouraged, treats will be plentiful, and there’s fun waiting around every corner. Gather your family and enjoy a spooky afternoon exploring participating state offices while collecting candy along the way!”
Participating locations include the Wyoming State Capitol, Herschler Building (West & East), Pioneer Building (first floor), Idelman Mansion, Kendrick Building, Barrett Building, Retirement System, DEQ Building, and Department of Audit (9 a.m.–12 p.m. only).
Happening Friday at 1 p.m.
Dark Victorian Tales
On Friday, the Laramie County Library is hosting an evening of “Dark Victorian Tales,” beginning at 5 p.m.
“An evening of haunting historical literary entertainment in a Victorian parlor setting, presented by a miniatures museum,”
the event page states
. “Costumes encouraged.”
Happening Friday at 5 p.m.
Downtown Halloween Pub Crawl
On Friday, Downtown Cheyenne bars are hosting a Pub Crawl, beginning at 6 p.m.
“Downtown bars and restaurants will have spooky specials as you walk your way through downtown enjoying all the great places downtown has to offer,”
the event page states
.
Happening Friday at 6 p.m.
Nightmare on 17th: Dinner & A Haunt
On Friday, The Metropolitan Downtown is hosting Nightmare on 17th: Dinner & A Haunt, beginning at 6 p.m.
“Planning a night out at the Nightmare on 17th Street Haunted House?”
the event page asks
. “Start your evening with us at The Metropolitan Downtown! The Deal: Receive 1 FREE Haunted House ticket for that night with every entrée purchased! How to Claim: Reserve your table at The Metropolitan Downtown for any event date. Add promo code Haunted Dinner in the reservation notes. Enjoy your meal and get ready to get spooked! Reserve your spot now — if you dare!”
Happening Friday and Saturday at 6 p.m.
Stephen King’s ‘Misery’
On Friday and Saturday, the Cheyenne Little Theatre Players are hosting a production of Stephen King’s “Misery,” beginning at 7 p.m.
“This psychological thriller based on Stephen King’s novel ‘Misery’ follows successful romance novelist Paul Sheldon, who is rescued from a car crash and wakes up captive in a secluded home owned by his self-identified ‘number one fan,’ Annie Wilkes,”
the event page states
. “While Paul is convalescing, Annie reads the manuscript of his latest book and becomes enraged when she discovers the author has killed off her favorite character, Misery Chastain. Annie forces Paul to write a new Misery novel and he quickly realizes Annie has no intention of letting him go. The irate Annie has Paul writing as if his life depends on it — and it does.”
Happening Friday and Saturday at 7 p.m.
