Cheyenne elects 3 City Council members; incumbents remain in Pine Bluffs and on school boards
by
Klark Byrd

November 4, 2026

CHEYENNE, Wyo. —
Nonpartisan races were decided
during Tuesday’s general election, including seating two new members of Cheyenne City Council and new trustees on county school boards.
In Cheyenne, voters elected three councilmembers across the city’s three wards, returning one incumbent to City Hall.
Ward I voters chose Maren Kallas, who earned 2,623 votes compared to challenger Rhianna Brand’s 1,932.
Ward II voters chose Janelle Rose with 4,466 votes over challenger Lynn A. Storey-Huylar, who received 2,875.
Ward III voters selected incumbent Councilor Ken A. Esquibel to retain his seat. He received 2,698 votes while challenger Bill J. Bryant netted 2,639.
Pine Bluffs
Pine Bluffs voters have chosen familiar faces
for three open government seats this election.
Incumbent Mayor Dennis Michaud was challenged by Councilor Janna Kestner. Michaud earned 263 votes, around 59%, compared to Kestner’s 165 votes in the primary. Michaud also led Kestner in August’s primary election with 222 votes to 100.
Incumbent Councilors Amy Currie and David Crouse also sought reelection. They received 380 votes and 375 votes, respectively. They were the sole candidates for the two seats in August’s primary election.
School boards
Incumbent Laramie County School District 1 Trustees Rene Hinkle, Marilyn Burden and Brooke Humphrey will be joined by a new face
on the board in January.
Burden ran unopposed for her Area 1 seat. Hinkle defeated challenger Tanya Barcelona 14,797 to 8,832 for her at-large seat. Humphrey defeated two challengers, John Weber and Diane Wedl, for her Area 2 seat. Humphrey received 3,033 votes to Wedl’s 2,847 and Weber’s 2,277.
Hank Bailey will be joining the board in January to represent Area 3. With 4,059 votes, he defeated challengers Paul Bankes and Casi Crites. Bankes received 2,804 votes while Crites picked up 2,604.
The Laramie County School District 2 race isn’t quite settled as Laramie County Clerk Debra Lee announced
a recount on Wednesday
for two candidates who were separated by a single vote.
In Tuesday’s race
, incumbent Trustees Ashley Jessen, Michael Lerwick and Kendra Roeder retained seats on the board. They will be joined by newcomers Kelly Greonweg and, if the recount holds up, Ashley Jessen, both of whom upended incumbents Tansie Derner and Mikki Munson.
Wilson ran unchallenged for her Area D seat. Groeneweg secured 841 votes to defeat Munson with 712 votes and Arthur Botelha with 299 votes for the Area F seat.
Three at-large seats were up for election. Lerwick, Roeder and Jessen were the top vote-getters from a pool of five. Lerwick received 1,345 votes, Roeder got 1,175 votes and Jessen netted 1,003 votes. Challenger Lisa Heinert and incumbent Tansie Derner received 1,002 votes and 844 votes, respectively.
In the Laramie County Community College Board of Trustees race
, incumbent Kathy Emmons retained her seat.
Three trustee seats were up for election, and there were six candidates vying for the roles. Voters elected to send Joe Kelly and Kenneth Guille to the board alongside Emmons.
Emmons received 15,900 votes, Kelly netted 12,295 votes and Guille got 11,042 votes.
Challengers for the seats included Troy Rumpf with 9,402 votes, R. Larry Meuli with 6,963 votes and Michael Bechtel with 9,770 votes.
Special districts and judicial retentions
Voters also decided
several special district board races
and
voted to retain every judge
on the ballot.
Two full-term seats on the Conservation District Rural Board were up for election. Melissa Harding and Jason Crowder were the top vote-getters with 11,589 votes and 9,505 votes, respectively.
They were challenged by Kiel W. Harding, Monte Lerwick Harding and Brent Wickham. Kiel Harding received 6,782 votes while Lerwick netted 6,387. Wickham got 9,325 votes.
Ford Rundell was the sole candidate to fill a two-year term on the Conservation District Rural Board. He received 24,321 votes.
Tim Ash was the sole candidate for the Chugwater Fire District director seat. He received 29 votes.
On the judicial ballot, Laramie County voters favored retaining First District Court Judges Robin S. Cooley and Nathaniel S. Hibben. Cooley received 20,827 votes in favor, with 7,286 votes against. Hibben netted 20,843 votes in favor, with 7,074 votes against.
Voters also supported retaining Wyoming Supreme Court justices Bridget Hill and Robert C. Jarosh, as well as Chancery Court Judge Ben Burningham. Hill received 122,303 votes statewide in favor with 38,528 against, while Jarosh netted 119,928 in favor to 38,898 against. Burningham had 121,273 votes in favor and 35,451 against.
In Laramie County, Hill received 20,751 votes statewide in favor with 7,507 against, while Jarosh netted 20,647 in favor to 7,602 against. Burningham had 20,858 votes in favor with 6,960 against.
First District Circuit Court Judges Timothy “TJ” Forwood and Sean C. Chambers were retained. Forwood had 21,318 votes in favor and 6,649 votes against while Chambers had 20,969 votes in favor and 6,938 votes against.
All election totals remain unofficial until county and state canvassing boards certify the vote.
