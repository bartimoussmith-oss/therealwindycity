WY Fresh says it will sell its land and move elsewhere upon city annexation

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Hours away from the City of Cheyenne annexing their land into the city and adopting new ordinances regarding urban farming, the owners of local farm WY Fresh are now welcoming changes. As the most “financially advantageous” solution to their year-long dispute with the city, annexation would allow them to sell their land and reestablish elsewhere.
In the Cheyenne City Council’s meeting at 6 p.m. tonight, WY Fresh’s farmstand, located in a pocket of unincorporated county land, will likely be annexed into the city. Consequently, the business would have to start following city ordinance regarding farm operations, which WY Fresh has for almost a year fought against.
“This will put us out of business. Mark my words, it absolutely will put us out of business,” David Kniseley, a co-owner of WY Fresh, told the Cheyenne Public Services Committee ahead of its vote to advance a package of proposed farm ordinances to the council. “We’re not even sure we can continue if we remain in the county.”
A week later, the owners have reportedly made up their minds. When the land is annexed, the business posted on Facebook on Sunday, it will be sold and the business will be moved.
“The damage has been done and we will move as soon as practically possible,”
WY Fresh said
. “Consequently, we want our land to be annexed into the city tomorrow night, as this is the most financially advantageous way for us to resolve this issue. We need this money to relocate and reestablish our lost dreams and farm. Thank you for understanding.”
The business added that, if Cheyenne residents want to support their continued operation, they could petition the state legislature to pass legislation establishing a recall process for public officials, term limits for local officials and boundaries on how many different offices local leaders can hold.
