Warm holiday weekend across Cheyenne to bring afternoon storm threats, gusty winds

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — A deepening low-pressure system moving inland from the Pacific Northwest will drive unsettled weather across southeastern Wyoming this weekend, bringing chances for strong afternoon thunderstorms, gusty winds and large hail to Laramie County and surrounding areas, according to the National Weather Service in Cheyenne.
Temperatures throughout the weekend are forecast to reach into the 80s and 90s across Cheyenne, accompanied by comfortable overnight lows in the 50s and 60s. However, atmospheric instability will set the stage for two potential rounds of storm activity on Saturday.
Meteorologists note low confidence in an early-day round of light showers near the Nebraska border due to morning atmospheric cap conditions. The primary threat for severe weather is projected to begin mid-afternoon, around 3 p.m., as daytime heating erodes capping inversion layers. Regional high-resolution models indicate storm initiation could concentrate across neighboring counties in western Nebraska and northeastern Colorado before sweeping east, though atmospheric disturbances moving across Laramie and Goshen counties could trigger localized activity earlier in the process.
The Storm Prediction Center has highlighted areas just east of Laramie County into the Nebraska Panhandle under a marginal risk for severe weather on Saturday. Primary threats associated with any sustained storms include strong wind gusts reaching up to 60 mph alongside hail ranging from 1 to 1.5 inches in diameter. Synoptic forcing is expected to wane late Saturday night, with the most intense activity pushing east out of the forecast area.
Similar weather patterns are forecast to persist on Sunday, which will feature afternoon breezes as the upper-level system advances against a ridge to the east. Enhanced atmospheric moisture will increase storm efficiency across southeastern Wyoming. Convection initiation on Sunday is expected to occur further west than Saturday’s storms, developing directly across Laramie, Goshen and Platte counties during the afternoon hours.
Severe risks on Sunday remain categorized as marginal for eastern Laramie County and neighboring eastern areas, with damaging wind gusts and variable hail sizes remaining the primary hazards.
Looking into next week, the breakdown of the upper-level ridge will keep storm chances in place for Monday and Tuesday afternoons. A drying trend is anticipated to settle across Laramie County by Wednesday and Thursday as westerly downsloping winds take hold across the region.

Partly cloudy, with a low around 59. East wind at 5–10 mph becoming light and variable.

A slight chance of showers between noon and 3 p.m., then a slight chance of showers and thunderstorms. Sunny, with a high near 85. South-southeast wind at 5–15 mph shifting to the north-northwest in the morning. Chance of precipitation is 20%.
Sunday Night:
A chance of showers and thunderstorms before 9 p.m., then a chance of showers until midnight. Partly cloudy, with a low around 60. South-southeast wind at 5–15 mph shifting to the west after midnight. Chance of precipitation is 30%.

A 20% chance of showers and thunderstorms after noon. Partly sunny, with a high near 84. West-southwest wind at 5–15 mph.
