Mark Klaassen chosen for vacant Laramie County District Court judge position

Wyoming’s U.S. Attorney Mark Klaassen. (Department of Justice)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Wyoming Deputy Attorney General Mark Klaassen was appointed by Wyoming Gov. Mark Gordon as the next district court judge for the Laramie County 1st Judicial District, replacing retiring judge Peter Froelicher.
According to a release by Gordon’s office, Klaassen graduated from Campbell County High School before earning a degree in finance from Oral Roberts University. He received his law degree from the University of Notre Dame in 1998.
Before serving as deputy attorney general, Klaassen previously served as U.S. attorney for the District of Wyoming from 2017 to 2021 and as assistant U.S. attorney from 2009 to 2017. Prior to that, he served as chief of staff to the general counsel for the U.S. Department of Homeland Security.
“Mark Klaassen has spent decades proving his commitment to justice, but what impresses me most is his character,” Gordon said. “Whether serving as our U.S. attorney or leading in the Attorney General’s Office, Mark has always been guided by a deep respect for the rule of law and the people it protects. From his roots in Campbell County to this bench in Cheyenne, Mark has earned the trust of the legal community and the people of Wyoming. I couldn’t be more pleased to make this appointment.”
Klaassen, also quoted in Gordon’s release, said that he was honored and humbled by the confidence placed in him by Gordon and the Judicial Nominating Commission.
“I’d like to commend Judge Froelicher for his years of service,” Klaassen said. “I look forward to serving the people of Laramie County and Wyoming.”
