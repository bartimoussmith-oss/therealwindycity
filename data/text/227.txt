LARAMIE — University of Wyoming women’s basketball never held a lead in regulation during its exhibition against South Dakota Mines on Friday night.
The Cowgirls outscored the Hardrockers 10-5 in overtime to escape with a 65-60 win inside the Arena-Auditorium. UW outscored Mines 18-7 in the fourth quarter to force overtime.
Both teams failed to score in the final 2 minutes, 53 seconds of regulation.
“Obviously, not a pretty win, but the important thing is we pulled it out,” said freshman Taylor Donaldson, who led the Cowgirls with 13 points. “We figured out how to pull it out together as a team. We went through different lineups with different people. We went smaller, and we went big.
“Just knowing that we can do it (is important). Obviously, we’d like to (play better) at the beginning, but if it gets down to it, we can get it done.”
The Cowgirls finished just 8-of-31 (25.8%) from the field and 2-of-15 (13.3%) from 3 in the first half. They responded by making a conscious effort of attacking the basket.
UW shot 52% from the field in the second half.
“It started on the defensive end. We started getting stops,” Cowgirls coach Heather Ezell said. “That allowed us to get out and push and get into our offense a little quicker. It got them into mismatches they didn’t want to be in, which creates different closeouts. Then, we were able to attack.
“(In the fourth quarter), we were really executing what we had been trying to execute the whole game of getting to our actions and looking for those slips. I thought Taylor really took advantage of that.”
The Cowgirls had 12 players log minutes in their final exhibition. Preseason All-Mountain West selection Jane Rumpf and guard Payton Muma did not check in against the Hardrockers.
Eight of the Cowgirls that logged minutes are underclassmen.
“We obviously put a lot of different people and were trying to find different people to step up,” Ezell said. “Katie (Harrington) really gave us some good minutes. ... She really stepped up on the defensive end for us.
“(Jersey Berg) gave us some really good things. Jersey came in there in that fourth quarter and in overtime, and we didn’t take her out. She played the whole time there and just gave us a lot of good energy and really executed what we wanted to do with getting stops and getting rebounds. She scored some points there, but she did all the dirty work.”
The Hardrockers took a 17-11 lead going into the second quarter after a cold-shooting start for the Cowgirls. Mines stretched its lead to 11 midway through the second period and maintained a 29-22 advantage going into halftime.
The Hardrockers built their lead back to double digits in the third quarter, including a 46-32 advantage with 1:50 left in the period. That led to an 11-point lead for Mines going into the final 10 minutes.
The Cowgirls got back within five with 6:30 left to play, and an and-1 layup by Zoey Blackwell cut Mines’ lead to 53-51 two minutes later. Donaldson tied it at 55 with a layup with 2:53 left.
Neither team scored for the rest of the fourth quarter, pushing the game to overtime.
The Cowgirls went 1-1 in their exhibitions. They beat Colorado Christian 75-50 earlier this week.
“Our last two games, we haven’t come out hard,” Donaldson said. “That’s going to be a really big goal. Just playing our game. We’ve come out a little frantic, and we’ve talked about that. We need to have energy, but it needs to be good energy, not frantic energy.”
The Cowgirls open the regular season against Santa Clara at 6:30 p.m. Monday in the Arena-Auditorium.
