Gillette man gets 10 years in federal prison after chase in Natrona County, discovery of meth in impounded truck

Mugshot: Christopher Gore. Background: Joseph Mahoney Federal Building  in Cheyenne (NCSO;  Cap City File)
CASPER, Wyo. — A Gillette man who was reportedly sleeping in his SUV on private property before leading Natrona County deputies and a Wyoming Highway Patrol trooper on
a high-speed chase
in January got 10 years in federal prison for the 1.2 pounds of methamphetamine found in the impounded vehicle over a month later.
Christopher Gore, 42, got the sentence from U.S. District Judge Alan B. Johnson for possession of 500 grams of methamphetamine with intent to distribute, according to a U.S. Justice Department release.
According to a Wyoming Division of Criminal Investigation affidavit, Gore was suspected of running drugs in multiple counties.
The case began shortly after 10 a.m. Monday, Jan. 19, when a Natrona County Sheriff’s Office deputy responded to a report of a suspicious vehicle parked on private property near the Ranch Road exit of Interstate 25 north of Casper, according to the NCSO report.
The driver, identified as Gore, was asleep in the driver’s seat when the deputy contacted him and learned that he had warrants out of Campbell County.
Gore reportedly fled south onto Interstate 25 before exiting onto a gravel road and into a pasture. The deputy said the SUV went airborne after going through a wash. The vehicle then entered Wyoming Highway 259 and headed north toward Midwest. Case records say Gore went at least 110 mph during the chase.
Gore reportedly dodged spike strips placed by WHP Trooper Kolton Carbon and passed a Bud Light semi on the right-hand shoulder going 95 mph, the deputy wrote.
Carbon was able to perform a tactical vehicle intervention on the SUV, allowing deputies to box Gore in, but the trooper himself went off the roadway and into a steep embankment. Deputy Tyler Ludwig said that Carbon limped up the hill and that he could see the unit’s airbags had deployed through the open door.
Gore reportedly complemented the trooper’s driving. The NCSO affidavit indicates that supplemental “use of force” reports were filed.
After his arrest on the chase, deputies found documents with Gore’s name on them, including hotel and “sex store” receipts, in the SUV, as well as a Springfield 9mm handgun under the driver’s seat, the affidavit said.
The Nissan Rogue was towed to a yard secured by barbed wire fence and keypad access.
In late February, DCI agents and NCSO Investigations Sergeant Ken Jividen followed up on the tip from DCI agents in Gillette who’d been told that Gore was suspected of running drugs through multiple counties in the state.
On Feb. 26, Jividen and DCI got a warrant and visited the yard where the Nissan Rogue had sat undisturbed and unvisited since the impound date of Jan. 19, the report said. They reportedly found a void in the side of the glove compartment and a black smell-proof pouch underneath the stereo deck.
1.2 pounds of suspected methamphetamine was found in two large bags, along with a digital scale in a red Tupperware container, the report said.
After the discovery, investigators went to talk with Gore in jail. He reportedly said he was aware they might find something and had been planning on telling authorities about it “to get ahead of the issue,” the special agent wrote in the affidavit.
Gore will serve seven years of supervised release after serving the sentence, the release said.
