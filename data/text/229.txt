LARAMIE — A second-half surge spoiled a historic night in Laramie.
Relentless rushing steered No. 5 Cheyenne Central to a 27-20 win over No. 4 Laramie in a Class 4A first-round playoff tilt on Friday at Deti Stadium.
The Indians avenged a regular-season loss in what was Laramie’s first home playoff game since 2000.
The Plainsmen also sought their first playoff win since the turn of the century, despite three consecutive playoff appearances.
"Bunch of special players on this team," Laramie coach Jake Chick said. "In particular, the senior class. Going to miss those guys a bunch because not only did they live through the grind, they embraced it. Wish we could've been able to celebrate these guys a little bit differently tonight."
Central’s 14 unanswered points provided the cushion, thanks to a rushing attack that totaled 247 yards.
"We needed to be able to control the ball and score when we got in the red zone," Central coach Mike Apodaca said. "For the most part, we did that, outside of the interception that we had. We know (Laramie) was an explosive offense, so when we got down there, we had to take advantage, and we did."
Despite a 6-6 deadlock at the half, Central’s Aaron Gonzales-Alvarado ripped off a 35-yard rushing touchdown less than five minutes into the third quarter. Cody Witt bulldozed in for a 1-yard score with 35 seconds left in the frame — his second touchdown of the game.
"Our (offensive) line is such a big deal with (our rushing attack)," Gonzales-Alvarado said. "We knew that was our strong suit, and we just wanted to pursue that in our rushing game. We knew our running backs could do it."
That pushed the Indians ahead 20-6 with 35 seconds left in the third quarter. Central quarterback Owen Woods punctuated the win with a 4-yard keeper with 1:50 left in the game.
Laramie’s Dax Parvin hit a 37-yard dart to Issac Kirsch — with 39 seconds left — which sliced the deficit to seven.
It concluded a similar path Central traveled in 2025 — when it lost a regular-season battle to Natrona County, settled for the No. 5 seed, then defeated the Mustangs on the road in the first round.
"I would be lying to you to say we didn't talk about that," Apodaca said. "We had the experience of going on the road as a five seed in a tough environment and winning. It hasn't been a perfect season for us, but that was one thing that we could bring to the table. I thought our kids embraced that."
Gonzales-Alvarado totaled 126 yards on 10 carries, while David Sack added 81 on 16 attempts.
Woods completed 2-of-7 passes for 41 yards, plus an interception.
Parvin completed 12-of-20 attempts for 123 yards, plus that score to Kirsch.
Laramie running back Boone Frude found extra mileage in his gas tank. He trucked two Central defenders amid a 28-yard touchdown run less than two minutes into the fourth quarter. The extra point pushed Laramie within seven — 20-13.
It marked Frude’s second rushing score of the game, though he entered with just one on his ledger. He led Laramie with 156 yards on 12 runs.
"Love to see (Frude) get in the end zone," Chick said. "Wish we could have got a lot more of those for him this year, but we knew what he was to us in the run and pass. Again, he is our ultimate leader, and the guys know who they can lean on."
Frude’s 99-yard scamper evened things at 6-6 with 2 minutes, 29 seconds left in the first half.
Witt opened the scoring with a 2-yard rush with 73 seconds left in the first quarter, though the ensuing two-point conversion failed.
It capped an eight-play drive (all runs) that featured a touchdown-saving tackle from Laramie’s Noah Reese on Gonzales-Alvarado’s 33-yard stampede, which put the Indians on Laramie’s 7-yard line.
Laramie’s offense opened the game with two consecutive turnovers on downs.
Kirsch intercepted Woods with seven seconds left in second quarter. It marked Kirsch’s team-leading sixth interception for a guy who leads the state with 82.6 receiving yards a game.
Kirsch finished with 45 receiving yards on two grabs.
Laramie will graduate 18 seniors.
"We have seen the growth of the program and what that looks like in terms of all three programs," Chick said. "This last year, had a really good buy-in from the underclassmen. A lot of those guys really stepped up and filled their roles."
Central will visit Natrona County in a semifinal at a time to be determined Friday.
"We got another chance to redeem ourselves from an earlier season loss," Apodaca said.
