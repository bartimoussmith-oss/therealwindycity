Mild temps return to Cheyenne before late-week cold front
by
Nick Perkins
November 14, 2026
November 14, 2026

(Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Low clouds and patchy morning fog across Laramie County on Saturday will give way to sunshine and a warming trend early next week before a pair of cold fronts brings gusty winds and increasing chances for measurable snow by Friday.
According to the National Weather Service in Cheyenne, low clouds and fog developed early Saturday along the Interstate 25 corridor on the lee side of the Laramie Range and across the Cheyenne Ridge into Kimball, Nebraska. While dense fog remained brief and largely confined to lower valleys, lingering cloud cover and south-to-southeast upslope winds are expected to keep Saturday afternoon temperatures cooler, with highs topping out in the mid-40s to mid-50s.
A Pacific storm system moving eastward over the Great Basin is tracking south of the Wyoming–Colorado border, keeping precipitation chances minimal for lower elevations. Forecasters said only spotty showers are possible along and south of the Interstate 80 corridor, while mountain areas above 9,000 feet could see an inch or two of snow.
Patchy fog and low clouds may briefly return to the high plains late Saturday night alongside breezy surface winds. By Sunday morning, the storm system will push southeast toward Texas and Oklahoma, allowing lingering morning clouds in the Cheyenne area to clear by midday. Mostly sunny skies will return Sunday afternoon, pushing highs back into the 50s to low 60s.
Unseasonably mild and dry weather will continue into the start of the workweek. Downslope winds on Monday will boost afternoon highs into the mid-50s to mid-60s across southeast Wyoming, followed by slightly cooler but still mild and dry conditions on Tuesday.
A more active weather pattern arrives Wednesday as a Pacific cold front sweeps across the region. While the front is expected to bring scattered rain and snow showers west of a Douglas–Laramie line and accumulating snow in the Laramie, Snowy and Sierra Madre ranges, downslope winds east of the Laramie Range will likely create a rain shadow effect, keeping Cheyenne and eastern Laramie County mostly dry.
Strong winds will return Thursday ahead of a second, stronger cold front, particularly in wind-prone areas such as Arlington, Bordeaux and the Interstate 80 summit between Cheyenne and Laramie.
Forecasters are closely watching Friday, Nov. 20, which marks the latest date on record for Cheyenne’s first measurable snowfall of at least one-tenth of an inch. A progressive shortwave trough and cold front moving through the region Friday will bring cold, blustery conditions with highs in the upper 30s to upper 40s and an increasing likelihood of accumulating snow in lower elevations, depending on the storm’s exact track.
