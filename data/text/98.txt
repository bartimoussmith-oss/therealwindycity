Laramie County gas average falls to $4.10 as crude markets pull back
by
Klark Byrd

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Laramie County’s average gas price
shed 19 cents from last week
to $4.10 per gallon Monday,
according to AAA data
.
Meanwhile, Wyoming’s statewide average dropped 9 cents to $4.38 per gallon. AAA reported a 1-cent drop in the national average to $4.28 per gallon, while
price-tracking service GasBuddy
reported an unchanged national average of $4.24 per gallon national average.
That average is down 22.3 cents from a month ago, but remains up $1.24 from a year ago, GasBuddy said. The national average price of diesel rose 4 cents over the last week to $6.34 per gallon.
(AAA graphic)
Cheyenne drivers could find the cheapest fuel of $3.97 at Loaf ‘N Jug on Yellowstone Road, followed by $3.99 at Moody’s Rock Stop on West Pershing Boulevard,
according to GasBuddy reports
.
After a two-week run offering the state’s cheapest fuel, Natrona County was bumped into the No. 3 spot by Albany County’s average of $4.09 and Laramie County’s $4.10, down 19 cents from last week, according to AAA.
Patrick De Haan, head of petroleum analysis at GasBuddy, said pump prices dropped across most states, but a regional refinery outage in the Great Lakes caused a price jump that erased declines. That refinery issue is now starting to clear up, he said.
“Diesel edged modestly higher nationally, and with the national average for gasoline likely to remain above $4 per gallon and diesel above $6 heading into the U.S. election, the relief motorists and policymakers have been hoping for remains elusive,” De Haan said.
Crude oil markets showed a slight retreat Monday morning following last week’s rally. West Texas Intermediate crude fell $2.05 to $101.22 per barrel, while Brent crude dropped $1.51 to $115.21 per barrel.
Traders took profits after reports surfaced of Qatari diplomatic efforts to reduce tensions between the United States and Iran. Even with Monday’s dip, crude prices remain elevated amid ongoing conflict in the Middle East. Shipping attacks in the Strait of Hormuz kept supply lanes unsettled, with the waterway logging just four ship transits on Oct. 18 compared to a normal baseline of 85 vessels per day.
“While Middle Eastern oil exports have partially recovered, they remain below pre-conflict levels, leaving the oil market undersupplied and vulnerable to renewed regional tensions,” UBS commodities analyst Giovanni Staunovo said.
Domestic petroleum inventories also remain tight. The Energy Information Administration reported commercial crude stockpiles dropped by 3.1 million barrels for the week ending Oct. 16, leaving inventories roughly 3% below the five-year average. Gas stockpiles dropped by 3 million barrels to sit 5% below seasonal norms, while domestic refinery utilization slipped to 93.4%.
