Man arrested for Halloween assault and shooting at Cheyenne bar

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — A man has been arrested following a reported shooting outside the Peppermill Bar in Cheyenne on Halloween. According to the Cheyenne Police Department, the suspect, identified as 26-year-old Latrell Grayson, is facing aggravated assault, reckless endangerment and open container charges.
Police were called to the bar at around 11:15 p.m. Saturday, Oct. 31 to reports of a disturbance. Callers reported to dispatch that someone had been run over by a car and several shots had been fired.
Upon arrival, officers were shown videos taken by witnesses of a vehicle accelerating out of the parking lot and hitting a man, then attempting to drive off. The man struck by the car was not injured, refused medical assistance and denied any involvement.
The video depicted bystanders chasing the car for a moment before the car stopped, then the driver got out, fired several rounds from a handgun and drove off.
“Footage showed what appeared to be several bullet strikes on the ground in the area,” Cheyenne police said. “Further investigation by officers located five spent 9mm shell casings on the roadway, but, thankfully, no injuries to any of the bystanders.”
As more officers drove to the scene, some spotted what appeared to be the wanted vehicle. As they attempted to engage a traffic stop, the driver reportedly threw what appeared to be a bottle of vodka out of the window. Officers conducted a high-risk traffic stop; arrested the driver, Grayson; and found a handgun in the car.
Grayson is presumed innocent until pleading or being found guilty in a court of law.
This case remains under investigation, the department said.
“The Cheyenne Police Department is grateful for the continued cooperation and support of the Cheyenne community. The prompt reporting of suspicious activity and the timely descriptions provided by citizens played a critical role in the swift apprehension of a dangerous suspect,” the department said. “This partnership between law enforcement and the community helped mitigate further risk and enhance public safety.”
