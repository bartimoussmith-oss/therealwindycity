American Legion gathers in Burns Cemetery to dedicate new flagpole on Veterans Day

American Legion Post 21 members raise the flag in Burns to dedicate the local cemetery's new pole on Veterans Day 2026 (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
BURNS, Wyo. — Laramie County veterans gathered in the Burns Cemetery on Veterans Day 2026. The group, consisting of members of the local American Legion Post 21, elected to spend the day honoring the spirits of the cemetery by dedicating a new flagpole, which now proudly flies the banner of the United States.
“The cemetery is a place of profound memory, quiet reflection and eternal peace,” Jim Merryfield, the American Legion member leading the dedication ceremony, said in a speech. “By raising this flagpole here, we create a towering symbol of honor that stands watch over those who rest below.”
He later explained that the flagpole that has long stood at the gates of the cemetery was in rough shape. The Burns American Legion chapter was dissolved for years due to lack of membership, and when it got back together, the vets began looking around the area for ways to help the community. They saw the flagpole and thought a restoration was in order.
The old flagpole at the Burns Cemetery is replaced by a new telescopic one, dedicated on Veterans Day 2026 (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
“Originally we were just going to redo the flagpole and put a new flag up there,” Merryfield explained, “but once we got the flagpole down, we decided that it doesn’t look that good and we think it’s seen better days. We decided to put up a new flagpole and new flag just to give the cemetery the the look that it deserves.”
Besides being shiny and new, the replacement pole is telescopic. Unlike the more common sectional flagpoles, which are cheaper at the expense of a more difficult install, telescopic poles are collapsible, making them easier to install and much more adjustable. Instead of raising or lowering the flag, the entire pole can be raised and lowered.
Jim Merryfield joins the American Legion Post 21 in Burns to dedicate the local cemetery’s new flagpole on Veterans Day 2026 (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
It’s a fitting tribute to the cemetery and a great project for the area’s veterans, Merryfield said.
“We wanted to do this for the veterans that are interned here and for people going by. Seeing the American flag kind of gives you a good sense of pride and unity in the community,” he said.
Burns is a special place for veterans, the American Legion members agreed. Merryfield pointed to Liberty Park as an example. The park is the centerpiece of the town, and
at its center is a memorial to the victims of the Sept. 11, 2001, terrorist attacks in New York, Pennsylvania and Washington, D.C., featuring a steel beam recovered from the World Trade Center site.
Veterans coming out on a chilly, windy Veterans Day to the sleepy cemetery outside of town shows just how much respect the community has poured into them and how much they care about the community, Department of Wyoming American Legion Adjutant Vanessa Stuart said.
“It just shows the tight-knit community and how much they care about each other and helping all the other vets,” Stuart said.
The cemetery is a step closer to the look it deserves, Merryfield said. Future plans for the cemetery include a paved road to enhance accessibility.
(Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News
CORRECTION, Nov. 12, 2026: Vanessa Stuart is the adjutant for the Department of Wyoming American Legion. The story is corrected.
