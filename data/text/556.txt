Slime store, opening in Frontier Mall Saturday, benefits Colorado animal rescue

The Steinmark family poses in front of a banner in their new store, Slimeology, in the Frontier Mall in Cheyenne Nov. 13, 2026 (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Tomorrow, Nov. 13, Slimeology will open its doors to customers in Cheyenne’s Frontier Mall. The brand-new store lets visitors custom-make their own slimes and is built to host birthday parties and other fun kids events.
The store, located near the interior entrance to Dillard’s, has an assembly line of slime ingredients à la Subway. Customers can pick out their slime types, additions like glitter and beads and even a scent.
More important than the slime itself, store co-owner Krysta Steinmark said, is the experience it creates for kids.
“Kids don’t have much to do anymore except for be on an iPad or their phone,” Steinmark said. “They don’t get out much. If you can find something to do that’s fun, we want to provide that.”
The store is her and her husband’s first brick-and-mortar location, but Slimeology is not new. They created the business as a way to bring in a little extra income to support their other passion, their animal rescue.
Located in Pierce, Colorado,
Chris P’s Rescue and Rehabilitation is a sanctuary for pigs
. According to the organization’s website, the Steinmarks founded the rescue in 2021 after one of their pigs, Christopher, became seriously ill. Since then, they’ve grown to provide shelter to pigs facing all sorts of difficult situations, including abuse. They’ve even begun accepting other kinds of animals.
“We were looking for a little extra income to come in for our special-needs animals. You know, they have ongoing bills and medical that they have to go through — a lot of rehab, a lot of medicine, a lot of therapy,” Steinmark said. “We were looking for something on the side to raise some money, so we started this back in June.”
Before taking on a permanent location, the Steinmarks sold their slime at festivals and craft shows. It’s been a bit of a messy learning experience — literally — learning the world of slime when they didn’t even let their own kids have the messy substance growing up, but they figured it out. It was just two weeks ago when Frontier Mall administration asked if they wanted a permanent location.
“We said, ‘All right, let’s do this.’ Getting all of the permits and licenses and everything, that was kind of hard to squeeze into two weeks, but we did it,” Steinmark said.
They open tomorrow, Saturday, Nov. 14. Kids can immediately come in and make their slime. The Steinmarks have plans to organize events like birthdays in the next few weeks as well. They’ll be here, they said, at least through the holidays to raise some money for their rescue. If the community really responds to their work, they said they will extend their stay even further.
It would be nice, Steinmark said, as the mall is a great place to be. Being able to find an experience and connect with people directly has been something the Steinmarks considered when they committed to opening the store.
