Cheyenne approves WY Fresh annexation after owners concede
by
Garrett Grochowski
22 hours ago
November 9, 2026

Supporters of WY Fresh crowd a Cheyenne City Council meeting before the business's land is annexed into the city Nov. 9, 2026 (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — An emotional round of public testimony preceded the annexation of the WY Fresh farm and other south Cheyenne county pockets Monday night. Expecting that result, the farm’s owners and their supporters used the time to memorialize the farmstand, plan its future and ultimately ponder how the decision will impact Cheyenne city councilors’ legacies.
WY Fresh has been the target of annexation since early this year, but the motivations behind the decision to incorporate the parcel of county land into the city began much earlier.
Since 2022
, the City of Cheyenne has been strategically planning the annexation of “islands” of county land surrounded by city territory.
WY Fresh was one of the last of those parcels, which its owners reportedly enjoyed due to more lenient regulations regarding agriculture. Since March, however, the city has been attempting to incorporate the farm’s land and plug up utility and service gaps in town.
The Cheyenne City Council postponed the annexation until November to give city staff time to draw up Unified Development Code changes that would satisfy the farm’s agricultural business needs.
Ultimately, the farm’s owners say that the
new changes
, one of which was postponed another two weeks and a second rejected outright, wouldn’t have been enough. The farm’s very presence in the city threatens its survival, they state.
“The damage has been done,” Tommie Kniseley, one of the owners, told the Cheyenne City Council ahead of its vote to annex the land. “There’s no recovering what we’ve lost this year through this forced annexation process. You may have allowed me to continue to farm, but you stripped me of any legal protections to farm. You are all culpable.”
She cited nuisance ordinances as the farm’s greatest threat.
“What you’ve created is a situation where technically I can continue to farm, but I’m completely vulnerable to anyone who wishes to complain about a rooster crowing, a manure that may stink, or a tractor that is noisy,” she continued.
However, Tommie and co-owner David Kniseley came into the council’s Nov. 9 meeting with a much different attitude than they had the last year — they welcomed the annexation.
In a now-deleted Facebook post, the farm said that the damage had already been done and a quick decision to annex by the city would just encourage the farm to close and move.
“Consequently, we want our land to be annexed into the city tomorrow night, as this is the most financially advantageous way for us to resolve this issue,” the farm wrote. “We need this money to relocate and reestablish our lost dreams and farm. Thank you for understanding.”
The farm’s last day of business was Nov. 7.
Cheyenne Mayor Patrick Collins asked the Kniseleys if that was still their desire.
“My intention tonight is that people give you hell for the way you treated us,” David said, pointing to months of stalled and ultimately unfriendly negotiations. “Then I’m expecting that you will free us from the burdens of living in this city because city officials have told us, county officials have told us and state officials have told us that you will hound us and hound us and hound us out of existence anyway. So please vote to annex us and then please listen to the people and understand that they’re not going to take it much longer. You all are a threat to liberty. Not all of you, but some of you are. And it needs to stop. Your administration is corrupt.”
WY Fresh co-owner David Kniseley speaks at a Cheyenne City Council meeting Nov. 9, 2026 (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
A group of Cheyenne community members then approached the dais and shared their frustrations with the city, calling councilors bullies, corrupt, unprincipled and backstabbers.
“What we have watched over the last several months is not governing, it is steamrolling. I’ve always believed in the principles of servant leadership. However, looking at the forced annexation of a hardworking family in our community, I do not see leadership. I see a usurping of authority and a blatant form of bureaucratic bullying,” Tommy Perry, a Pine Bluffs resident, told the council.
The Cheyenne City Council, which ultimately voted 8–2 in favor of the annexation, took the Kniseley family’s request that their farm be annexed so they can move on seriously. Councilors Michelle Aldrich and Jeff White said ahead of the vote that they, believing in the principle that those who want to be annexed should be annexed and those don’t shouldn’t be, were prepared to vote against the annexation before they read the Kniseleyses’ Facebook post. Aldrich had even drafted an amendment to remove the farm from the annexation ordinance.
“I was fully prepared tonight to present a substitute that had been drawn up and had been prepared to withdraw this property from this annexation,” Aldrich said. “However, in light of the social media post today and in light of the Kniseleyses’ request — because they really are feeling the need to be able to put this behind them and to move on — I will not be bringing that substitution forward.
“I will support this annexation at their request. I know that many of you have asked for us to vote no on this, but I think historically, as I’ve served on council over the last six years, I have never voted for an annexation when there was a land owner opposed to it. However, I’ve never failed to vote for one where the land owner was asking for it. So, in this instance, I will be supporting this annexation tonight.”
Michelle Aldrich at a Cheyenne City Council meeting Nov. 9, 2026 (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
Councilor Ken Esquibel, one of the two “no” votes, said he was honoring a promise he made earlier in the year when he thought about how he would vote.
“What I said to you back then,” Esquibel said, looking at Tommie Kniseley, “was that I would vote for the annexation when you were comfortable with the annexation. You don’t seem like you’re comfortable with the annexation, so I am going to stick by what I said back in March.”
However, the majority of the councilors reiterated Aldrich’s words and voted in favor of the annexation so that the city could provide some closure for the farm. They ultimately postponed one of the farm code updates to give councilors more time to consider a provision added late to grandfather in legal nonconforming agricultural uses upon city annexation. Another to allow gravel parking lots at certain properties failed outright.
