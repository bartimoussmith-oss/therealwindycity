Laramie County Library’s dreams of a makerspace supported by $120K Microsoft donation

Laramie County Library in the West Edge of Cheyenne (Lisa Hushbeck/Cap City News)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Microsoft has donated $120,000 to the Laramie County Library Foundation to help establish a makerspace, the library announced Monday. The funds will help the library buy the equipment and other materials to furnish the operation at its Cheyenne location.
Establishing a makerspace — a shared workshop providing access to high-end tools for community members’ projects — has been a longtime goal of the foundation. The foundation set creating a makerspace as a priority in its 2025–2030 strategic plan, which it made using insights shared by nearly 500 people across Laramie County.
Planning for the area is already underway, with space plans, equipment needs and furnishings identified. Equipment planned for the space, the library foundation said, include a longarm quilting system, 3D printers, an AI lab workstation, a laser cutter and engraving machine and a sublimation printing machine.
The makerspace will be only partially supported by Microsoft’s donation. Repurposed operational funds, foundation contributions and Laramie County Sixth Penny Tax funds will also help support the idea.
Besides providing access to tools and technologies, the dedicated space also gives residents access to experienced staff who know how to use them.
“Library makerspaces give people access to tools and opportunities they may not otherwise have a chance to explore,” Antonia Gaona, Laramie County Library System executive director and county librarian, said. “They are places where ideas hatch, prototypes take shape, self-sufficiency is built and people learn from one another. We’re delighted that Microsoft is helping bring these possibilities to Cheyenne.”
For Microsoft, the donation is aligned with the company’s goal of supporting creativity and getting involved with the community.
“Microsoft is committed to supporting the communities where we live and work,” Lucas Downey, senior community affairs manager at Microsoft, said. “We want this investment to help more people in Laramie County access opportunities to learn, create and develop new skills. We’re proud to support the Laramie County Library Foundation in creating a space where residents can explore technology and bring their ideas to life.”
Opening details and information about using the makerspace will be announced as the project progresses.
