CHEYENNE — It took Cheyenne East’s offense a quarter to find its footing Friday.
Once it did, the Thunderbirds never slowed down during a 34-14 victory over visiting Thunder Basin during the first round of the Class 4A playoffs at Okie Blanchard Stadium in Cheyenne.
“We talked a lot this week about stacking good with good,” East coach Chad Goff said. “We didn’t come out great our first series offensively, but our defense was great the first few series until we got going offensively.
“We started to figure some things out on our second drive because (Thunder Basin) was doing some new things on defense compared to the first time we saw them. Once we got a feel for it, we started doing a lot of good stuff.”
East (8-2) plays at Sheridan (8-1) in the semifinals.
The T-Birds went three-and-out on their first drive. Thunder Basin took over at East’s 49-yard line after a short punt. The Bolts got down to the 1-yard line and went for it on fourth-and-goal, but seniors Tyler Stephenson and Trace Eldridge stuffed running back Zander Kumm short of the end zone for a turnover on downs.
East got three first downs on the ensuing drive, but stalled out on Thunder Basin’s 43. Three plays later, Sadell Johnson recovered a fumble by Bolts quarterback Ty Eisenhauer.
The T-Birds needed eight plays to cover the 39 yards and get their first touchdown on the night. Senior Javon Register bulled in from a yard out for a 7-0 lead with 10 minutes, 45 seconds remaining in the second quarter.
The Bolts responded almost immediately.
Senior Gracin Peak caught an Eisenhauer pass on a drag route over the middle and beat East’s defense down the left sideline for an 80-yard touchdown that knotted the score 7-7.
East regained the lead on its next drive. Ty’ree McCord hauled in a 33-yard pass on third-and-10 from the T-Birds’ 44. Register knifed through Thunder Basin’s defense for a 23-yard touchdown on the next play.
Junior defensive lineman Will Shaw jumped up and intercepted an Eisenhauer pass on Thunder Basin’s next drive. Register capped the 24-yard drive with a 7-yard run for his third touchdown of the night.
“That was something we saw on film,” Shaw said of his interception. “I usually watch film once I’m done with all my assignments in school. We’ve been talking about the screen (pass) a lot, and practicing how to defend it.
“I’ve gotten a lot of picks in practice. I’ve also been close to an interception for a few weeks now, and I finally got one.”
East senior quarterback Eldridge scrambled for a 19-yard touchdown with 49 seconds remaining in the first half. He also had a 40-yard touchdown run late in the third quarter to five East a 34-14 lead.
Register finished with 109 rushing yards and three touchdowns. Eldridge ran for 230 yards and two scores.
“We needed to be more tenacious,” Shaw said. “Coach Goff is always says we have to want to hit every play. We can’t wait to see what their defensive line is doing, we have to go after them and make them decide what they’re going to do.
“We were kind of angry after we didn’t really move the ball on our first two drives, so we got more tenacious and went for it.”
McCord also snared an interception in the second half.
Senior linebacker Kason Kinstler posted 11 tackles (five solo) and two pass breakups. Reid Horton notched seven tackles (two solo and two for loss) and a quarterback sack.
“Those turnovers we forced are part of playing complementary football,” Goff said. “If the defense can go out and get you the ball back like that and give you good field position, you have to do something with it.
“Our kids executed well.”
