Jon Ott
Jon Ott:
1944 – 2026
In Loving Memory of Jon Ott
Jon Ott was a beloved husband, father, grandfather, and friend to many. He touched countless lives throughout his years and leaves behind a lifetime of memories, laughter, and love that will never be forgotten.
Jon proudly served 20 years in the United States Air Force before beginning his second career working for Holiday Inn. Throughout his life, he demonstrated dedication, hard work, and a love for the things that mattered most to him.
Jon deeply loved his wife, Mary, and cherished the life they shared. Mary passed away on October 24, 2020, and Jon carried the love they shared throughout his life. Their love and the memories they created together will forever remain a treasured part of his family’s story.
Jon’s children and grandchildren were among his greatest joys, and some of his happiest moments were spent making memories with them, especially at the racetrack.
Jon spent much of his life building and racing cars, a passion that began in the 1960s when he first raced at Julesburg Drag Strip. Many weekends were spent at Douglas International Raceway, but Julesburg Drag Strip always held a special place in his heart. It was his favorite place on earth, a place filled with memories, friendships, and a lifetime of racing adventures.
Known by many as “Triple Ott,” Jon earned his nickname through his many perfect lights at the track. His love for racing extended far beyond the driver’s seat. He spent years serving as head tech at the racetrack, sharing his knowledge and helping keep the racing community going.
Some of the most treasured memories his grandchildren will carry with them are the times Grandpa spent running junior dragsters with them. He passed down his love for racing while creating special memories that will last for generations.
Jon’s legacy is one of family, friendship, dedication, and a lifelong passion for racing. He was a loving husband, a devoted father, a proud grandfather, and a friend who will be remembered by many.
Jon is survived by his son, Brian Ott (Christina West); his daughter, Tammy Reorda (Jared Reorda); and his beloved grandchildren: Josh (Heather), Kayleena (Trystan), Katelyn, Allen, Samantha (Will), and Elizabeth (Trevor). He also leaves behind many dear friends whose lives were enriched by knowing him.
Though Jon and Mary are no longer here with their family, the love they shared and the memories they created will live on in the hearts of those who knew them. Jon will be deeply missed, but his spirit will live on in the stories shared at family gatherings, the friendships he built, and the roar of engines at the racetrack.
Every trip down the strip, every perfect light, and every memory made with his children and grandchildren will serve as a reminder of the incredible man he was.
Rest easy, Jon. Your race here may be finished, but the memories, love, and legacy you leave behind will continue for generations. May you and Mary be reunited again. You will forever be loved, remembered, and missed by all who were fortunate enough to know you.
The Funeral Service at the Cathedral of St. Mary’s on Tuesday October 27, 2026 at 11 a.m. MST will be livestreamed and viewable here:
https://stmarycathedral.com/livestream
.
Jay W. Smith:
1929 – 2026
Jay Will Smith, 96, of Cheyenne died Wednesday, October 21, 2026 at Davis Hospice Center.
He was born November 28, 1929 in Tulsa, Oklahoma. Jay grew up in Red Oak, Iowa, before moving to Casper, Wyoming in his teens. He graduated from NCHS in 1947.
He met Barbara Attebery at the Junior Follies in 1949. They were married on Easter Sunday, April 9, 1950 in Casper.
He served in the Wyoming National Guard during the Korean War.
In 1950, Jay began his bricklaying apprenticeship, eventually forming C.W. Smith Masonry with his father, Bill. His masonry work can be seen all over Cheyenne and the region.
After retirement in 1985, Jay stayed busy with yardwork, garage sales and gambling trips with his family. He enjoyed working with his hands and could fix anything.
In 2025 Jay recieved a 75 year service award from the International Union of Bricklayers Local 7 Colorado/Wyoming.
He is survived by three sons: Jim Smith and Bill Smith, both of Cheyenne, and Ken Smith of Gilbert, Arizona.
Jay was preceded in death by his parents, Bill and Etha Smith; a brother, Jean; two sisters, Hazel and Dorthy; and his beloved wife of 64 years, Barbara Jean.
