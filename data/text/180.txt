Patrol cars by day, decks by night: Cheyenne police officer gets extra work DJ’ing

Cheyenne Police Officer David Inman DJ'ing during the CPD's Halloween Trunk or Treat event Oct. 29, 2026 (Alex Farkas, Cheyenne Police Department)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Cheyenne Police Officer David Inman has a hobby many might not associate with law enforcement. Going back to his days as a music-loving kid in the American South and eventually his time performing during his college days at Louisiana State University, Inman loves to DJ.
Even after joining the military and eventually becoming a police officer in Cheyenne, the passion stuck around.
“Before I was even able to pick up equipment and do weddings and everything, I would pretend I was a radio DJ in my room and stuff like that,” Inman said. “I was always really into it and the music and everything. I always wanted to do it.  Every school dance I went to and everything else, I really wanted to be a part of it. I wanted to be that guy to do the music and all that. It just stayed with me.”
He learned a lot about the trade and what it took to operate a DJ’ing business from his time working for an events company during college, but getting a footing in Cheyenne was a bit of a grind at first. Besides now having to get his own equipment, he had to establish himself through word of mouth as a good events DJ.
He first got hired to DJ an Oktoberfest party after mentioning his passion to a coworker in passing. That led to him DJ’ing a wedding. The bride at that wedding was a nurse.
“That was a great way in,” Inman said. “Nurses are always getting engaged. She threw my name around at the hospital and it just kind of — once you do that, you start building weddings and people recognize you and that’s how the department knew about it, too. That’s when they started coming to me and asking me to do events.”
When Cheyenne Police Department Public Information Officer Alexandra Farkas found out about his work, she wanted him at every event.
“I thought it was a great idea,” she said. “I think it kind of humanizes some of our officers, too, the fact that you get to see that, yes, they’re officers, but also everyone has special interests. I thought that was a great blend. Immediately I was like, ‘Yes, this is perfect.’ I think it’s a really great way to show off all the personalities at our police department.”
Now Inman is a regular sight at Cheyenne Police Department events. At each one, he wears his CPD-issued clothing with pride. While David Inman: Professional Disc Jockey is his professional brand, his role as a police officer is a core part of his identity.
Cheyenne Police Officer David Inman DJ’ing during the CPD’s Neighborhood Night event (Alex Farkas, Cheyenne Police Department)
“I wear the uniform when I’m doing this because it represents the police department and that officers also DJ. People like that; they like seeing that. It’s been great to do that and kind of blend both jobs together and do it for the community. It’s just awesome,” Inman said Oct. 29 before officers opened the Cheyenne Public Safety Center to trunk-or-treaters. “I think people enjoy seeing it when they come down the ramp and they see a police officer just helping add to that element of fun.”
Inman said he believes demonstrating that two distinct passions — law enforcement and disc jockeying — can intersect sends a positive message to local youth. That’s why he loves doing CPD events so much.
“If you can have a part of making those memories — they remember those, you know — it starts off giving them a great impression of the police department doing events like this and that’s why they’re important. It’s stuff they don’t forget; especially when they’re this young, their minds are little sponges,” Inman said. “It’s great to see that you’re having a positive impact on them.”
