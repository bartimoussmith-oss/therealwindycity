WY Fresh a vote away from city annexation after passing final committee read

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — The City of Cheyenne’s Public Service Committee has voted to recommend ordinances to forcibly annex and zone a handful of properties in south Cheyenne completely surrounded by city territory, including the WY Fresh farm. Also recommending local code changes to allow the farm to continue operating, the decision is set up for a final City Council vote next week.
After nearly months in the municipal approval process — the city first initiated the annexation in early spring — the annexation is a week and a vote away from approval. The annexations would incorporate more than 15 properties in south Cheyenne that are completely surrounded by the city, but aren’t in the city themselves.
Because WY Fresh is included in the final of
10 packages of county pocket annexations the city has approved in the last few years
, city staff were prompted to draft updates to the Unified Development Code and Municipal Code that would allow the farmstand to continue growing food and operating its business in city limits.
The ordinances to make those changes were also voted on and ultimately approved by the committee. They make changes to how the city manages volume of livestock allowed to be kept, add exemptions from use approval for certain agricultural operations and allow things like gravel parking lots and electric fencing — all with conditions applied. Read more about proposed changes
here
.
Still, WY Fresh co-owners Tommie and David Kniseley told the committee that, if the annexation is approved, their business is doomed.
“This will put us out of business. Mark my words, it absolutely will put us out of business,” David said. “We’re not even sure we can continue if we remain in the county.”
City planner Connor White noted during the meeting that multiple businesses have been subject to changes across the 333 annexations the city has conducted to clean up county pockets in the last few years. To his knowledge, he said, none have gone out of business as a result.
However, the code amendments tailor-made to allow WY Fresh to continue operating are flawed, the Kniseleys said, and address only parts of what new challenges they claim their business will face upon annexation. Neighbor complaint enforcement regarding farm odors, dust, livestock guardian dogs and barn cats, David gave as an example, will be their “death by a thousand cuts.”
David said negotiations with the city have broken down before, claiming during the city’s Oct. 27 council meeting that the city had restricted him to talk to administration solely through lawyers. In the nearly one year that the farm and the city have discussed the annexation, the Kniseley family’s needs have reportedly not been met.
Upon annexation, the county pocket properties would be immediately zoned to fit the use most similar to when it was in the county. In the case of WY Fresh, it would be given an Agricultural designation.
According to city planning staff, as long as the property continues to be used that way, if it switches hands — which David Kniseley said was possible and reported to have already heard purchase offers — it could continue as a farm.
The annexation received favorable votes from all on the committee except Councilmember Pete Laybourn, who said he was unhappy with how both the Kniseleys and the city handled the issue. He sees failure from both sides: the city for not being fair to the Kniseleys and the Kniseleys for making the process difficult for the city.
“On both sides of this issue I see failure. I see a failure of the WY Fresh farm to be able to articulate and work with the city. I especially see a failure of the city to reasonably and responsibly respond to their concerns,” Laybourn said. “That’s a tough place to be.”
The annexation, zoning and agriculture code updates will all go to the Cheyenne City Council with the committee’s recommendation of approval. The committee also advanced ordinances to annex and zone Christensen Road and a nearby parcel as well as another agricultural property near
the recently annexed Read land off College Drive that
, although originally included in the final county pocket annexation, is now being considered as an owner-initiated annexation. Those two annexations will also be voted on for a final time next week.
For more information on that upcoming meeting, see the City of Cheyenne’s web page on the Cheyenne City Council
here
.
