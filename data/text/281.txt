As statewide gas prices ease, Laramie County sees 1-cent drop

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Laramie County’s average gas price
shed 1 cent from last week
to $4.09 per gallon Monday,
according to AAA data
.
Wyoming’s statewide average also dropped 8 cents to $4.30 per gallon. AAA reported a 12-cent drop in the national average to $4.16 per gallon, a drop also reported by
price-tracking service GasBuddy
, which put the national average at $4.12 per gallon.
That average is down 24.7 cents from a month ago, but remains up $1.13 from a year ago, GasBuddy said. The national average price of diesel fell 5.9 cents over the last week to $6.28 per gallon.
(AAA graphic)
Cheyenne drivers could find the cheapest fuel at $3.94 at Sam’s Club on Dell Range Boulevard, followed by $3.96 at Loaf ‘N Jug on Vandehei Avenue and Maverik on Gardenia Drive,
according to GasBuddy reports
.
After being pushed to the No. 3 spot last week, Natrona County once again offers the state’s lowest fuel average at $4.03 per gallon. That’s followed by Converse County’s $4.08 average and Laramie County’s $4.09, according to AAA.
“Average gasoline prices fell in nearly every state over the last week, while diesel declined in most, as refinery maintenance wraps up and seasonal demand continues to fade with the onset of cooler weather,” said Patrick De Haan, head of petroleum analysis at GasBuddy. “That combination has given gasoline and diesel prices some much-needed breathing room, and motorists are finally seeing relief at the pump.”
He added that market volatility could return depending on developments abroad and the general election.
“Whether new attacks occur or a truce takes hold around the Strait of Hormuz, along with continued Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries, will likely dictate where prices head as we move closer to the holidays,” De Haan said.
Crude oil prices fell to open the week, giving back much of October’s rally as diplomatic talks in Doha between Iranian and Qatari officials eased worries over Middle East supply disruptions. In early Monday trading, West Texas Intermediate crude fell $4.77 to $99.35 per barrel, while Brent crude dropped $5.03 to $108.40 per barrel.
“The oil market is still reacting with extreme sensitivity to every headline out of the Middle East,” said Giovanni Staunovo, UBS commodities analyst. “While fears of an imminent escalation pushed prices up last week, they have dropped sharply at the start of this week as diplomatic talks between the U.S. and Iran continue.”
On the supply side, the Energy Information Administration reported commercial crude oil inventories dropped by 1.9 million barrels for the week ending Oct. 23, sitting 4% below the five-year average. Gasoline stockpiles climbed by 3.9 million barrels as domestic demand dropped by 625,000 barrels per day to roughly 8.36 million barrels daily.
