(OPINION) The Mayor’s Minute with Patrick Collins (10/30/26)

Cheyenne Mayor Patrick Collins (City of Cheyenne)
Cap City News publishes letters, cartoons and opinions as a public service. The content does not necessarily reflect the opinions of Cap City News or its employees. Letters to the editor can be submitted by emailing
editor@oilcity.news
.
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Two valuable members of our community announced that they are leaving their positions, and I want to thank them for their contributions to our city. First, Dale Steenbergen has accepted the position as the new chamber director in Wichita, Kansas, and Tom Hirsig announced he is retiring from Cheyenne Frontier Days after next year’s rodeo.
Dale has spent almost 20 years leading the Chamber, and our city is better for his service. I served on his board of directors for three years and witnessed his passion for the military and business community. Wichita’s metro area is 10 times bigger than Cheyenne and has a larger population than the state. It will be a fun challenge, and I wish him much success.
The Hirsig family has been an important part of Cheyenne Frontier Days since the very first days, and Tom has carried on that family tradition of service to the rodeo and our community for years.
He announced his retirement this week and I am sure it was bittersweet for someone who started as a volunteer, won a championship buckle as a competitor, and ended up serving as the CEO of the whole organization. I appreciate how he has been an incredible ambassador for Cheyenne and the rodeo. I am hoping the next chapter is long and rewarding.
The Planning Commission serves an important function for our city. They are a citizen board that advises City Council on land use, development, zoning and long-range planning. We will have a few openings on the board at the end of the year, and I have been working to find people willing to serve on this very important board.
The city has hired a consultant to update our comprehensive plan, so it is an exciting time to be on the Planning Commission to help guide what Cheyenne’s future will look like. The city has many volunteer boards and commissions, and I want to thank members for their service to their community and hope others will step up and serve.
Our Critical Traffic Control Area Committee met again this week. The committee is charged with reviewing petitions from neighborhoods that are concerned with speeding and safety on roads in their area. Once we receive a petition, a speed study for the area is done and the committee reviews that data with input from the police department to determine if additional police patrols are warranted or other traffic calming strategies should be deployed. This week, we discussed 8th Avenue and the committee is looking at signalizing the intersection with Carey Avenue along with additional crosswalk controls to make it safer for pedestrians walking to Lions Park. The committee will revisit this area next month.
Dan Peters is the city’s new Chief Building Official. He is charged with the safety of buildings in our community. The CBO is the top authority for building code administration, permitting, plan review, inspections and enforcement. It is sometimes a challenge for people who are not in the construction industry to understand the development process. This week, Dan and I visited a house that the owner is trying to convert to a medical office. It was interesting to see the building owner share their vision for the building, and the CBO working to find a way to help them navigate building regulations to make it happen. I am excited for the owner’s vision and wish her much success.
American Fertilizer held a networking event this week to give elected officials a chance to meet their team and to share a bit about their proposed fertilizer manufacturing facility. I learned that they have 600 acres in the Swan Ranch Business Park under contract with rail access to handle the proposed 500 rail cars a week of product they will ship out. Their plan is to take Wyoming natural gas and convert it to products that help American farmers grow crops. Around 300 jobs and over $4 billion invested will result in the state’s economy growing by 1%. To meet the 2031 opening date, they will have to get through the permitting process very soon. We have a lot to learn about their proposal, and I appreciate a chance to meet the founders.
President Schaffer from LCCC hosted the first of four meetings with legislators and candidates for legislative office to discuss their legislative priorities and to hear priorities from economic development, education, healthcare and local governments in the community. I appreciate the effort to improve communication between the community and our legislative delegation. I am excited to learn more over the next three meetings. I have a lot of priorities from Cheyenne and the municipalities around the state to share.
Bechtel is one of the world’s largest engineering, procurement and construction companies. They are currently working in the Cheyenne area, constructing the Cheyenne Power Hub, and in Kemmerer building TerraPower’s Natrium nuclear plant. They are famous for building Hoover Dam and other projects in the world that many people think are impossible to construct. Walter Hudson is Bechtel’s head of construction, and he shared the opportunities that the construction industry and skilled trades have for today’s youth and workforce looking for a new career at a luncheon at LCCC.
Bechtel pledged $1 million to LCCC and WWC to help more students interested in skilled trades begin their education. It is private–public partnerships like these that will help us meet the challenging workforce issues our nation faces.
Last summer, I traveled to Colorado to tour Village Cooperative, a senior living project where owners of the condos share the building expenses and a board of owners run the facility. I loved the idea as I see so many seniors suffering loneliness and the social aspect of this concept brings seniors together. Megan Stetter, the sales manager, gave us an update this week with their plans to break ground next summer and open in 2028. I know there are a few more homes still available in the development and I am so appreciative that so many are working in Cheyenne to meet our housing challenge. Senior-specific housing is one of our many areas of housing need.
This weekend, Daylight Saving Time ends and we change our clocks by falling back an hour. Our cell phones automatically change, but don’t forget your traditional alarm clocks. The time change is also a good reminder to check your smoke alarm batteries. I love getting an extra hour of sleep on the following morning.
Tuesday is Election Day and Wyoming could do better in the percentage of eligible voters who cast ballots. I hope everyone reading this week’s Minute will make a plan to vote. We are so blessed with the right to freely cast a vote for the candidate of our choice in our country. Please exercise that right!
Mayor Patrick Collins,
Cheyenne
