Election 2026: Laramie County voters approve 2% lodging, Fifth Penny taxes

Jim Walter, CEO and President of Visit Cheyenne, speaks to the Laramie County Board of Commissioners June 2, 2026 to request the reinstatement of the optional 2% lodging tax on the 2026 general election ballot (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Laramie County voters have lent their support to retaining a 2% lodging tax that applies to hotels and other short-stay operations and the Fifth Penny optional retail sales tax.
In Tuesday’s election, the 2% lodging tax received 22,232 votes in favor to 7,985 votes against.
The
tax takes advantage of the tourism
that is already coming into Cheyenne from visitors seeking short-term lodging. It’s applied to the cost of their stay and reinvested mostly back into Visit Cheyenne, allowing the organization to further pursue improving the tourism economy of the city. Laramie County residents do not pay the lodging tax unless they stay in those establishments. Tax-exempt entities and school groups don’t pay the tax.
The Fifth Penny tax, officially known as the Laramie County 1% Sales and Use Tax and first enacted by voters in 1977, will continue for another four years after getting 19,212 votes in favor and 11,109 against.
The countywide general-purpose tax has served for 49 years as the primary mechanism for funding public assets, capital projects and core services in Laramie County, Cheyenne, and area cities and towns.
The breakdown of how Cheyenne would spend
its portion of the funds is as follows:
$40 million: Street and pavement maintenance and improvements, including slurries, overlays, crack seals, installation of signage, new road construction, road widening, drainage repairs, etc.
$4 million: Traffic safety improvements such as replacing or upgrading traffic signals, signal controllers, new painting and striping, etc.
$8 million: Snow removal, street cleaning, street maintenance materials (asphalt, sand, gravel, ice melt), equipment, etc.
$8 million: Construction, maintenance and repair of storm sewers, culverts, drainage channels, detention ponds, etc.
The remaining 20% of the collections — about $16 million — would help fund different city departments and their projects. Cheyenne Fire Rescue, for example, would get $3.2 million for “fire protection and training facilities maintenance and development, equipment, supplies, communications equipment” and more.
Per the Laramie County government’s web page: “Laramie County distributes its 50% share among several service areas. Public Works receives 70% of the County’s share, the Library receives 10%, Health and Welfare and the Sheriff’s Office each receive 5.5%, General Government receives 3%, Fire District Equipment and Conservation and Development each receive 2.5%, and the Recreation Board receives 1%.”
