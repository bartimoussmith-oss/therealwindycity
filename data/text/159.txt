Federal election monitors return to Laramie County Nov. 3, must keep 100 feet from polls
by
Garrett Grochowski
October 28, 2026

(Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Like the Aug. 18 primary election in Laramie County, federal election monitors will be deployed near Laramie County polling places. This time,
County Clerk Debra Lee confirmed Tuesday
, they won’t be allowed inside the polling places and must keep 100 feet or more away from the polls themselves.
Local and state officials were concerned by the intrusive presence of federal officials at polling places during the primary election, raising concerns from Wyoming Gov. Mark Gordon. Having Department of Justice officials stationed around the country during election season isn’t anything new, but,
their presence this time was described by Gordon as “aggressive” and “irregular.”
It is also a slightly unfamiliar experience for Laramie County in particular. According to Lee, former senior staff of the Wyoming Secretary of State’s Office whom she spoke with had no recollection of such an experience in their 40 years of service.
The DOJ later confirmed it would continue to monitor elections around the country, including in Laramie County, during the general election Nov. 3. This time, Lee confirmed, they will have to follow stricter rules.
In accordance with state law and the Oct. 16 opinion of
Wyoming Attorney General Keith Kautz
clarifying that state law regarding elections trumps federal law, only officials “specifically authorized by state statute are permitted in polling places during an election.” Those individuals include county election staff and poll watchers, as well as the voters themselves.
According to Lee, an Oct. 26 meeting between Wyoming county clerks and DOJ officials established that the federal government understood the opinion and would conduct observations and interviews outside the 100-foot boundary and only with consenting individuals. Monitors will have to identify themselves and wear badges issued by Lee’s office.
“We appreciate the DOJ’s advance communication and the opportunity to discuss its plans before Election Day. That coordination will help us prepare our staff and provide voters with a safe and smooth voting experience,” Lee said in a Laramie County release. “It was welcomed news that the monitors dispatched to Laramie County during the Primary Election did not identify any concerns regarding our compliance with federal statutes.”
With prior notice of their presence, Lee said, the county is better prepared for federal monitors’ presence. Poll workers, local election staff and sheriff’s deputies have been briefed and will follow the law as they always do.
2026 is set to be one of the biggest voting years in recent Laramie County history. On Oct. 16, just 10 days into early voting, the county had already succeeded 2022’s impressive numbers by 194%. About 7% of the total Laramie County electorate had already cast their ballots at that point, but early voting is sure to represent even more votes before it ends Nov. 2.
See
Lee’s web page on the Laramie County website
for more information on elections in Laramie County.
