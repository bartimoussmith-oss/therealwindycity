Richard Charles Chambers
Richard Charles Chambers
: 1948 – 2026
Richard Charles Chambers was born August 20, 1948, in Rock Springs, Wyoming to Charles and Lois Chambers. He died peacefully on September 3, in Cheyenne, Wyoming, at the age of 78.
In his childhood, Rick lived in Rock Springs and Lander, before his family moved to Cheyenne in 1963. Some of Rick’s fondest childhood memories were spending summers with his Grandma Mary in Ogden, Utah. As the eldest grandchild, Rick was the only one to have met his great-grandfather, Charles Hendrick, an early homesteader of Jackson Hole, Wyoming. Rick was proud of his family’s Wyoming heritage. His grandchildren are the sixth generation Wyoming natives.
While living in Cheyenne and attending East High School, Rick met the love of his life, Susan Kepley. Rick and Sue’s first date was to a James Bond-themed party. After graduating from East in 1966, Rick attended the University of Wyoming for one year before enlisting in the United States Navy. After naval boot camp, Rick returned to Cheyenne to marry Sue in December 1968.
Rick and Sue lived in Denver for a short time where Rick underwent training as an electrical technician. Rick was stationed at Naval Station Norfolk, in Norfolk, Virginia. Rick’s naval service saw him sail to the Mediterranean and the Caribbean Seas. While in Virginia, Rick and Sue welcomed their oldest daughter, Tia, in 1970.
Upon his discharge from the Navy, Rick’s young family returned to Cheyenne where Rick embarked on a nearly 40-year career with the phone company, working for Mountain Bell, US West, Qwest, and CenturyLink. In Cheyenne, Rick and Sue welcomed another daughter, Cara, and a son, Sean. Together, the family spent many summers visiting Rick’s parents in Rock Springs and his aunts and uncles in Jackson.
Rick retired from CenturyLink in 2011 after 36 years with the phone company. He and Sue purchased a fifth-wheel trailer and spent summers traveling around Wyoming. Rick was an avid fly fisherman and spent winters tying flies and sorting gear to hit his favorite Wyoming fishing holes each summer. Rick and Sue especially enjoyed visiting Pinedale, Jackson, Yellowstone, Cody, Thermopolis, Buffalo, and Sheridan. For several of those summers, Rick and Sue took their grandchildren along. The grandkids have fond memories of watching Grandpa jumping from the high dive in Thermopolis and going to the 4th of July parade in Dubois.
In recent years, Rick was a regular at his local gym and worked out every morning. He maintained a healthy diet and was strong and active until the moment of his recent illness. To any of his caregivers, Rick was quick to show off how many squats he could do. Only weeks before his illness, Rick could cross the Laramie River in thigh-deep water with little effort. His strength and courage were proven in the face of his illness.
When Rick joined the Navy, Sue gave him a St. Christopher medal that he wore around his neck until the day of his passing. The patron saint of travel accompanied Rick on his earthly travels and will surely guide him as he searches for the great fishing hole in the sky.
Rick was predeceased by his parents, Charles and Lois; and his brother, Terry. He is survived by his wife of 57 years, Susan; their three children: Tia (Troy) of Mesa, Arizona, Cara (Jeremy) of Cheyenne, and Sean (Cara) of Cheyenne; and their three grandchildren: Thayne, Charlotte, and Maxwell.
Honoring Rick’s wishes, the family will hold a private gathering as Rick is interred at the Cheyenne National Cemetery. The family wishes that you honor Rick’s memory by thanking a veteran for their service to your country, by spending time with your loved ones, or by catching a fish from your favorite fishing hole.
Jacqueline Romena Price:
1931 – 2026
Jacquelyn (Smith) Price peacefully passed from this life to the next on September 1, 2026, at home with her family in Cheyenne, Wyoming. She was 95 years old.
Jackie was born in Knobel, Arkansas, to John and Elsie Smith on August 2, 1931. After her family moved to Centerville, Missouri, she met the love of her life, Arlon (Al) Price as he got off the school bus his first day at Centerville High School when she was 16 years old. They became high school sweethearts and married three years later in September 1950. They set up housekeeping in St. Louis, Missouri, and added daughter, Hope, to their little family. Their family life was interrupted while Al served in the Korean War. Jackie spent that year caring for their infant daughter and keeping track of where his letters said he was in Korea with pins she put in the large map of Korea she kept on her wall. They resumed their family life when he returned and settled down once again in St. Louis, adding to their family two sons, Jim and Alan. Her devotion to her family and care of them never failed. Their love and loyalty to each other never faded. She and Al raised their three children, took full advantage of their many years of retirement, and enjoyed 68 years of marriage until his death in March of 2018.
Jackie enjoyed her life to the fullest until the very end. She recognized the blessings she had been given in every situation and was grateful for each one. She was a true people person, made friends easily, and loved to talk and laugh. Her infamous sense of humor could pop up at any time. She loved to travel and see and experience new places. For her, every trip was the best trip ever; every vacation was the best vacation ever; every family visit was the best visit ever. She was an enthusiastic, energetic woman who rarely said no to any invitation to do something or go somewhere. Although her body grew old, her lively, happy spirit never did.
She will be greatly missed by her three children: Hope Peckham (Kelly), Jim Price, and Alan Price (Heather); grandchildren: Mike Davis (Katherine), James P. Davis, Aiden Price and Abel Price; and two great-grandchildren, Mairwen Davis and James W. Davis.
A celebration of life memorial service will take place on September 25 at 11 a.m. at her church home of many, many years — Arlington United Methodist Church in Bridgeton, Missouri.
