Reuben Algazy
Reuben Algazy:
1959 – 2026
Reuben Algazy, 67, passed away on October 28, 2026, in Cheyenne. He was born on May 23, 1959, to Michael Algazy and Rachel Anders Woss. Reuben served in the United States Air Force from 1979–1992, and in the Air National Guard from 1998-2007. He moved to Cheyenne with the Air Force in 1989.
Reuben is survived by his wife, Carolann Algazy; daughter, Angel Algazy; sons, Christoper and Nathan Algazy; brothers, Arye and Aaron Algazy; sisters, Ronit Navarro and Bethany Patstone; and grandsons, Dominic Abrahamson and Theodore Algazy.
No services have been scheduled. Interment will be at Cheyenne National Cemetery.
Constance K. Anderson:
1941 – 2026
Constance K. Anderson was born in Wymore, Nebraska, in October, 1941 to Richard L. and Doris L. (Swiler) Cameron. Connie retired from the Wyoming Real Estate Commission and the Certified Real Estate Appraiser Board and was an ex-Officio Member of the National Association of Real Estate License Law Officials.
She is survived by her sons, Victor Nation and Robert Anderson; her beloved grandsons, Jesse Nation JD and Cameron Anderson; nephew, Richard Cameron; niece, Rachel Cameron Bayne; and friend and co-worker, Barbara Blaker.
Preceded in death by her parents; brother, Richard E. Cameron.
Cremation has taken place.
In lieu of flowers, please make donations to Cheyenne Botanical Gardens.
Celebration at later date.
Dr. Donald Lee Bryngelson
: 1946 – 2026
Dr. Donald Lee Bryngelson, 80, of Cheyenne, Wyoming, passed away peacefully, surrounded by loved ones, on October 27, 2026. He was born on June 24, 1946, in Bismarck, North Dakota, to Richard Bryngelson and Jean Bryngelson (Mc Fadgen). He was one of four children, along with Rich, Alice, and Doug. Their family bond was strong, and they always placed the care and love of family first. He married Jody Bryngelson, and together they had two daughters, Erin and Shauna, whom he loved deeply.
He dedicated his life to educating children. He began his career as a math teacher in Jeffrey City and later served as Superintendent of Schools in Jeffrey City, Pinedale, and Wheatland, Wyoming, as well as Raton, New Mexico. He earned his bachelor’s degree from Chadron State College and went on to earn two master’s degrees and a Ph.D. in education from the University of Wyoming.
He was passionate about giving children a strong start in their education and helping them build life-long skills that would improve their lives. He also served as head of the Wyoming School Facilities Commission, where he helped build schools for children across the state.
Donald loved Wyoming and enjoyed every aspect of the outdoors, including hunting, fishing, hiking, and small-town life. As a young man, he served in the U.S. Army and later worked as a surveyor, hiking through Wyoming’s beautiful terrain. When we once asked whether he would ever leave Wyoming, he replied, “Why would I?” He truly loved his life in Wyoming.
He loved spending time with family and friends. He never said no to a new adventure and always had a gleam in his eye when fun was on the horizon. He had a wonderful sense of humor and preferred to keep life on the lighter side. Everyone who knew him appreciated his quick wit and joy for life.
He never asked for much and was always the first to help, whether it was advice, a listening ear, or boots on the ground to fix something. His family was deeply blessed to share his life as a husband and father. He will be greatly missed and never forgotten.
Donald is survived by his best friend, Jody, of Cheyenne; his daughters, Erin Bryngelson of Sheridan, Wyoming, and Shauna Bryngelson of Broomfield, Colorado; Joe Winter of Colorado; his sister, Alice Feser of Kennewick, Washington, and her children and their spouses, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren; and his nephew and niece, Cory and Kim Bryngelson, and their spouses and children.
He was preceded in death by his father, Richard Bryngelson; his mother, Jean Bryngelson; his brothers, Richard and Doug Bryngelson; and his brother-in-law, Rick Feser, whom he loved like a brother.
A private service will be held graveside with the family in the spring.
He was a lifetime member of the Elks and strongly supported the organization’s mission of giving back to those in need. In lieu of flowers, the family asks that donations be made in his memory to Friends of Don Bryngelson – 660, Cheyenne Elks Lodge, 100 E. 17th Street, Cheyenne, Wyoming 82001.
Donna Jo Best Epler:
1930 – 2026
Donna Jo Best Epler was born on May 22, 1930 to Arthur and Gertrude Ullstrom in Wahoo, Nebraska. The Ullstrom family moved to Scottsbluff, Nebraska in early 1935 and on to Alamosa, Colorado shortly thereafter. An opportunity presented itself and the Ullstroms moved to Wood River, Nebraska, establishing a family farm on the Platte River that still exists today as Luehr Farms.
Donna had two siblings, Elaine Luehr Hannon and brothe,r Harold Ullstrom, deceased. Also, a grandson Joel, deceased.
Donna’s entire family are still in awe of their mother, grandmother and great-grandmother. Donna rode her horse to a rural school through 8th grade in Wood River. She graduated in the spring of 1948 and had met her first husband in high school, Clyde Aubrey “Boze” Best. They were married on May 28, 1949 in Wood River. They had three children: Kent, Patti and Mark, all born in Nebraska.
Our father was seriously injured in a well drilling accident in the summer of 1959. Mom ran our small farm where we raised cows, chickens, ducks, geese and had an elaborate garden. We milked six to nine cows, separated skim, and sold cream and lived a very frugal life. Mom also cleaned the Bank of Wood River, the insurance building two doors down and worked at Maxon’s Drug Store. The family sold their farm and moved to Cheyenne, Wyoming Jan. 19, 1961, a new beginning.
When someone lives a long abundant life, grew a loving family, worked, played, entertained, baked, traveled and touched as many people as she did and never wanted to be noticed, this truly is the passing of an era.
After moving to Wyoming, Donna attended night school to enhance her accounting skills to help secure her job at the Wyoming State Library, working 27 years and retiring in 1988. She absolutely loved the many associates, from the library to the Supreme Court and the Capitol, so many seemed like family.
Mom loved every ounce of Mark, Patti and Kent’s school activities, which had her scrambling all the time in the 60’s, 70’s: sports, rodeos, picnics, hunting, all of her favorite things. The late 70’s, 80’s brought weddings and grandchildren: Joel, Trevor, Courtney, Matthew and bonus Greta, Michael and Brian. All the miles between didn’t deter Donna from driving or flying and to be there. Clyde passed away in June, 1982.
Donna married Orval D. Epler on Oct. 7, 1989, in a wedding fit for a Hallmark movie! This was as authentic as you could imagine i.e., horse drawn wagon, Wyoming Hereford Ranch, barn dance, growing mixed family that had known each other for years through the Shetland pony rodeos, Saddle Tramps, Frontier Days everything, brandings, you name it.
Who could have predicted that dad and ,om meeting Orval and Maxine in late 1961 and our families still together today? Dad and ,om were “Frontier Days Volunteer Heels” and Grandpa Orval was elected into the CFD HOF two years ago.
There were many extended families from Texas, Oklahoma, New Mexico, Montana, Nebraska that worked all the parades from Frontier Days Park. Donna never met a cream can dinner, serving 10 or to 100, that she couldn’t cook for or manage.
Picked up a Wyoming Tribune-Eagle and on the front page is Orval, Donna Jo and sister Patti moving cattle for a weekend chore along I-80, behind the Harriman mines, over by Vedavoo and some near Happy Jack Road, cattle being rounded up.
Donna embraced our outdoor Wyoming culture like no other; she was an enthusiastic sports fan, including drives over the Snowies, visiting our cabins in Fox Park, steer ropings, rodeos, football, hauling hay from Walden, you name it. One tiny burr under her saddle is how Josh ended up in Buffalo when her Broncos were right here? She would take Patrick if she couldn’t have Josh, however, no one could replace her Wyoming Cowboys, all sports, “Go Pokes!”
Donna loved to dance, country music and especially George Strait, we have all of his CD’s.
For all of these wonderful memories, entertaining friends, family, cooking for 2 or 100, she also was a deacon in the Cheyenne and Riverton Presbyterian Churches, a testament to her faith.
Donna was devoted to her children, grandchildren, great-grandchildren and to our families, the Best, the Luehrs, and Eplers. Whether it was birthdays, weddings, reunions, sports of all kinds, she did not do anything half-hearted. She was always there and all in! Almost anyone who wanted an afghan, had one or two that she crocheted!
Thank you for allowing us to share a passing of an era, here are some quotes:
“We were the first amongst our friends to acquire a black and white television. Our friends came over to watch the 1956 World Series, Ed Sullivan, big time wrestling, the test pattern and play cards.”
“I have outlived two cowboy husbands, 5 card playing groups and been retired longer than I worked!”
Donna Jo Best Epler could be a pistol:
A few years ago my sister called wondering if I knew where “my” mother was? “What about your mother?” She is not home and not answering her phone! Patti had checked with Mom’s friends, my church activities, befuddled.
You know Mom mentioned last week the Eplers may be having a function at Gary and Cheryl’s in Douglas this weekend? Surely in these winds she didn’t take off for Douglas! Mom, why did you take off? “Because I wanted to!”
“The little old lady of Cheyenne or Pasadena may have had a lead foot…. or not!”
“A few years ago, Donna Jo, 80 and our Aunt Sherry, 82, loved volunteering at Meals on Wheels, delivering to the older people who needed help.”
“Many of my friends and family are gone now, but I have been so fortunate to have lived such an abundant life!”
By the way, we had a 70th birthday party in 2000 for Donna and Matthew’s graduation, where it struck him ‘that graduation must be very important! Grandma never misses any of our graduations whether in Kansas City or Longmont.
Surviving members in Donna’s family are nephews, Loyd and Bill Luehr and their families: Mark (Toni), Patti (Ken), Kent (Gayle); grandchildren: Trevor (Heather), Courtney, Matthew (Kelly), Greta, Michael, Brian (Jessica), Jenny (Shawn); nine great-grandchildren: Zoe, JJ, Bailey, Elektra, Kalel, Layla, Joey, Davis, Aubrey, Braxton.
Epler Family — daughters, Wanda and Cheryl (Gary), grandchildren: Brenda, Tammy (Pat), Krystal, Nicole (Larry); great-grandchildren: Josh, Hailee, Caleb (Emma); great-great grandchild Audrey.
Our families would like to thank the staff at Point Frontier, all of Donna’s church friends who visited her, and all of our family and extended family who have stayed in touch all of these years.
Lastly, a heartfelt thank you to sister Patti for keeping Mark and I “always in the loop” while super caring for mom.
If so inclined, memorials will gladly be accepted by Meals on Wheels – Cheyenne or the St. Joseph’s Pantry – Cheyenne.
It would be a very short trip for mom to heaven as she sang ‘The Cowboy Rides Away?’”
Viewing will be Tuesday at 9:30 a.m., at First Presbyterian Church, followed by a funeral service at 11 a.m., with a reception to follow. The funeral will be live streamed through First Presbyterian’s YouTube channel.
Rita J. Forbes:
1939 – 2026
Rita J. Forbes passed away on October 28, 2026, at the age of 87. Rita was able to stay home until her passing, receiving care from Inhabit Hospice Care and caregivers Sandy (daughter), Megan and Lynnette.
Born on February 28, 1939, Rita lived a long life that will be remembered with love, respect, and gratitude by those who knew her. Her passing marks the close of a life that spanned many years and touched the hearts of family and friends. Rita loved the outdoors, and enjoyed gardening, hiking and camping. Rita also enjoyed bible study, carrying her purple bible and strong faith in God.
Rita is survived by her three children, including two daughters, Sandy Anton of Cheyenne, Wyoming and Cyndy Forbes of Kearney, Nebraska; and one son, Bud Forbes, of Tulsa, Oklahoma; grandchildren: Amber, Stephanie, Brittany and their families.
She is preceded in death by her parents, L.B. and Frances Sweet (Sappenfield); daughter, Peggy Herren of Kearney, Nebraskal granddaughter, Felicia Renae Blevins; and her ex-husband Rodney Forbes.
A viewing will be held on November 5, 2026, from 11 a.m. to 3 p.m. at Wiederspahn-Radomsky Chapel of the Chimes, 1900 E 19th St, Cheyenne, Wyoming 82001.
Rita will be remembered with affection and honor, and her memory will remain with all who had the privilege of knowing her.
Woodrow L. “Woody” Nestor:
1946 – 2026
Woody Nestor, 80, of Cheyenne died October 26, 2026, at Davis Hospice Center.
Woody was born, September 11,1946, in Kingwood, West Virginia, the son of the late Samuel Nestor and the late Grace Nestor. He was the third of five children who were raised in a small house on Coal Lick Road. His mother Grace, who put faith and family first, was a strong and loving guide for her children.
He attended his first eight years of school in a one-room schoolhouse. The family moved to Kingwood where he graduated from Kingwood High School in 1964. Upon graduation, he joined the Marines, served in Vietnam and was recruited by the Metropolitan Police Department in Washington DC. He worked many areas of the job before being accepted into the K-9 division. He worked his last 13 years in the Special Operations Division bomb squad where he partnered with police dogs, Shep, Joey, and Haywire, protecting five Presidents of the United States. After retirement, he owned a landscaping business in Virginia. The lyrics of “God Bless the USA” described Woody. He was “proud to be an American.” Wherever he lived, he was active in the American Legion and the VFW.
Woody met his wife, Judy Winiarz, in 1991 when she briefly worked in DC. His pick-up line was, “Can I mow your lawn?” And he did, at their homes in Chicago, Cheyenne and Riverside. They were married on April 21, 1994. When Judy retired in 2005, they moved to Wyoming, where Judy’s mother’s family homesteaded. While his home would always be West by God Virginia, his second home was Wyoming. He enjoyed the beauty of the Snowy Range, Sierra Madre Range and Battle Mountain. He never tired of driving to Riverside via 130 or 230 spending the time hiking, fishing, hunting and enjoying all of God’s creations.
He is survived by his wife, Judy; son, Mark; sisters: Martha, Shirley and Jackie; sister-in-law, Debby; and many nieces and nephews. He was preceded in death by brother, Dave; brothers-in-law: Bobby, Bud, and Rick; and nephew, Greg.
Cremation has taken place and there will be a celebration of life in Riverside this summer. His ashes will be spread with the ashes of Haywire and his five rescue dogs. If so inclined, memorials can be sent to Davis Hospice Center in Cheyenne or a dog shelter of your choice.
The family would like to give hugs to those who supported and cared for him in his battle with renal failure: his support group, friends, neighbors, Dr. Fischer, CRMC vascular and podiatry doctors, Fresenius Kidney Care, VA, Davis Hospice Center, and three special ladies, RN Jane, CCHT Tammie, LPN Judy. All were angels and a blessing from God.
Orlando Felix Olague:
1998 – 2026
Orlando Felix Olague, nacido en Cheyenne, Wyoming, fue un querido hijo, hermano, primo y amigo que vivirá por siempre en los corazones de quienes lo amaron.
Fue el amado hijo de Jaime y Juanita Olague, y querido hermano de Mario Olague. Orlando se graduó de Cheyenne South High School, donde disfrutaba jugar fútbol y crear recuerdos que durarán toda la vida.
Orlando falleció tranquilamente en su hogar, rodeado del amor de sus padres y su hermano.
Le precedieron en la muerte sus queridos abuelos, Manuel Olague Chaírez, Felix Castro y Andrea Rodriguez.
Le sobreviven sus queridos padres, Jaime y Juanita Olague; su hermano, Mario Olague; tías, tíos, primas, primos, demás familiares y amigos que lo extrañarán más de lo que las palabras pueden expresar.
Orlando, nunca imaginamos tener que despedirnos de ti tan pronto. Daríamos lo que fuera por un abrazo más, una conversación más y un momento más contigo. Las reuniones familiares nunca volverán a sentirse iguales, pero tu recuerdo vivirá en cada historia, cada fotografía y cada corazón que te ama. Si hubiéramos sabido que algunos momentos serían los últimos, te habríamos abrazado un poquito más fuerte.
Que el cielo te reciba con los brazos abiertos. Hasta que volvamos a encontrarnos, serás amado por siempre, profundamente extrañado y jamás olvidado.
Que la bendición de Dios te alcance hoy donde tú no puedes llegar, y te sorprenda por caminos que no esperabas.
Dios te bendiga.
Orlando Felix Olague, born in Cheyenne, Wyoming, was a beloved son, brother, cousin, and friend who will forever be remembered by those who loved him.
He was the cherished son of Jaime and Juanita Olague and the beloved brother of Mario Olague. Orlando graduated from Cheyenne South High School, where he enjoyed playing soccer and making memories that will last a lifetime.
Orlando passed away peacefully at home, surrounded by the love of his parents and brother.
He was preceded in death by his beloved grandparents, Manuel Olague Chaírez, Felix Castro, and Andrea Rodriguez.
He leaves behind his loving parents, Jaime and Juanita Olague; his brother, Mario Olague; aunts, uncles, cousins, extended family, and friends who will miss him more than words can express.
Orlando, we never imagined having to say goodbye so soon. We would give anything for one more hug, one more conversation, and one more moment with you. Family gatherings will never feel the same, but your memory will live on in every story, every photograph, and every heart that loves you. If we had known some moments would be our last, we would have held on a little tighter.
May heaven welcome you with open arms. Until we meet again, you will forever be loved, deeply missed, and never forgotten.
May God’s blessings reach you today in places you cannot reach on your own, and may they surprise you along paths you never expected.
May god bless you.
David W. Smallwood:
1952 – 2026
David W. Smallwood, age 74, passed away on October 29, 2026, in Cheyenne, Wyoming. He was born on September 24, 1952, in Greeley, Colorado. He moved to Cheyenne as a baby with his parents.
He worked for Union Pacific for 34 years first as a brakeman and then as a conductor. He enjoyed fishing, photography, lapidary, and travel.
He is survived by wife, Cathy Smallwood; son, Jason (Irma) Smallwood; daughters, Aimee Sorensen and Heather Schaffer; granddaughters, Noel and Skye Sorensen, and Taylor Schaffer; step-grandchildren, Jenny (Braden) Johnson and Aaron (Robin) Smith.
He was preceded in death by his parents, Jack and Marian Winslow Smallwood.
No services will be scheduled.
William “Bill” James Stone:
1941 – 2026
William “Bill” James Stone, a cherished father, grandfather, great-grandfather, community leader, athlete, historian, and devoted volunteer, passed away peacefully on October 24, 2026, at Davis Hospice Center in Cheyenne, Wyoming. He leaves behind a legacy of service, friendship, cultural preservation, and community involvement that deeply touched many families in Wyoming.
Born to William James Stone and Katherine Coale Stone, Bill settled in Cheyenne in 1949 and remained devoted to the city throughout his life. During his childhood, Bill and his sister lived at the historic Plains Hotel while their father worked as a federal representative for FDR Insurance. At fourteen, Bill joined the Ki-Ann Club, fostering a lifelong appreciation for American Indian culture, particularly the Sioux Nation.
Bill served as a medic in the National Guard, displaying the same spirit of service and compassion that defined his life. He was preceded in death by his former wife, Linda Stone. He is survived by his daughter, Shaunna Stone; son, William Markley; grandchildren Danika Stone, Tiana Stone, Chloe Stone, Jessica Markley, Cameron Markley, and Sebastian Markley; several great-grandchildren; and numerous friends, colleagues, and extended family members who were blessed to know him.
A talented athlete and advocate for healthy living, Bill dedicated years to the Senior Olympics movement, earning induction into its Hall of Fame. He played a pivotal role in introducing and promoting pickleball in Cheyenne, helping it become a widely popular sport in the region.
His dedication to excellence and community service also earned him places in the Laramie County Community College Hall of Fame and the Cheyenne Frontier Days Hall of Fame. Bill’s association with Cheyenne Frontier Days began in 1956, evolving from a concessionaire role to over sixty years of service to this cherished tradition.
In 1965, Bill joined his father-in-law, Richard “Dick” Hutchison, at the Cheyenne Frontier Days Pancake Breakfast, where he met members of the Indians Committee, leading to a lifelong commitment to honoring and preserving Native American traditions.
Appointed Chairman of the Indians Committee in 1979, Bill sought to enhance the Indian Village at Frontier Days, transforming it into a vibrant cultural center that attracted a wide array of Native participants. His friendship with Billy Evans Hunting Horse, a Kiowa chief, expanded Native involvement and enhanced the cultural richness of the event.
Bill’s community involvement extended beyond Frontier Days. He volunteered tirelessly for organizations like the Elks, Kiwanis, and the Ki-Ann Indian Club, always aiming to improve his community and support others.
Those who knew Bill remember his boundless energy, unwavering integrity, and genuine kindness. He was a storyteller, mentor, volunteer, athlete, and friend who believed in the importance of relationships, preservation of traditions, and service to others.
Over more than six decades of volunteerism, leadership, and cultural advocacy, Bill helped shape Cheyenne Frontier Days into a more welcoming and meaningful celebration. His influence endures in the traditions he strengthened and the people whose lives he touched.
In leu of a service, Bill’s family asks that Bill be remembered not only for his many honors but for the respect he showed, the friendships he built, and the bridges he created between cultures and generations. His legacy will continue to inspire those who follow his path of service, respect, and community dedication.
Maria De Jesus Stutzman:
1939 – 2026
Maria De Jesus Stutzman passed away on October 23, 2026, in Cheyenne. She was born on August 21, 1939 in Teptitlan Jalisco, Mexico.
Maria worked in the hospitality industry and was a member of Centro de Alabanza Jesús Es La Vida.
Maria loved beautiful flowers and especially enjoyed being surrounded by her family. Her children, grandchildren, and great-grandchildren brought her great joy, and family remained an important part of her life.
Maria is survived by her three sons: Miguel Peña of Greeley, Colorado, Hector De La Paz Sr., and Jose J. De La Paz of Cheyenne, Wyoming; and her three daughters: Susie Taylor of North Carolina, Maria Leticia Gonzalez of Riverside, California, and Angelica De La Paz of Mexico.
She is also survived by 10 grandchildren, 24 great-grandchildren, and four great-great-great-grandchildren.
Funeral Services will be held on Thursday, November 5, 2026, at 10 a.m. at Restoration Fellowship International Church. Interment will be at Fort Logan National Cemetery in Denver, Colorado at 2 p.m. on Thursday November 5, 2026.
