Historic Pumphouse restoration begins this week after Cheyenne approves emergency repairs

Construction workers begin repairs to the Historic Pumphouse's roof Nov. 3, 2026 (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Work to repair the fire-damaged Historic Pumphouse in downtown Cheyenne began this week after the Cheyenne City Council approved an emergency funding procurement on Oct. 26. With winter weather posing an additional threat to the exposed building interior, work is underway.
In may, a suspected arson attack destroyed the roof of the 130-year-old pumphouse. The city-owned building sat vacant for years before the incident, but had just been chosen for restoration to become the future home of the Community Recreation and Events Department’s Clean and Safe Division. In February,
the council approved a $198,903 contract
with TDSi to design and renovate the pumphouse.
The project temporarily fell apart when the pumphouse caught fire. Emergency dispatch was notified of a fire inside the building the evening of May 17. Three minutes after being called to respond, Cheyenne Fire Rescue Engine 1 arrived at the scene to find the roof on fire and partially collapsed.
Construction workers begin repairs to the Historic Pumphouse’s roof Nov. 3, 2026 (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
Damage to the Historic Pumphouse’s roof Nov. 3, 2026 (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
Arson was quickly suspected, as the building had no utility connections that would have helped an accidental fire start. Security camera footage from a nearby building caught a man enter and exit the building before the fire started, but his charges were dropped months later due to the state’s failure to meet the necessary burden of proof.
On Oct. 26, with winter fast approaching and the planned work on the pumphouse delayed, the city knew it needed to act fast to get the roof repaired. A $1,365,402 emergency contract was given to Legacy Construction Group, which began repair work this week.
The project will later include lead and asbestos abatement, removal of a non-historic addition and construction of interior bearing walls to help stabilize and protect the building.
According to a release by the city, work to facilitate the rest of the improvements will appear before the City Council in the spring. The building is still planned to host the Clean and Safe Division.
