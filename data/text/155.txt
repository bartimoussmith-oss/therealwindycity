Wyoming trades education gets $1M boost from engineering company Bechtel
by
Garrett Grochowski
October 28, 2026
October 28, 2026

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — During a panel featuring Wyoming Gov. Mark Gordon, Laramie County Community College President Joe Schaffer and company Senior Vice President and Head of Construction Walker Kimball, global engineering company Bechtel announced a $1 million investment into Wyoming community colleges, as well as an education campaign, to support skilled trades education.
The workforce landscape of Wyoming is changing, Schaffer said at an LCCC panel exploring the expansion of skilled trades jobs coming to Wyoming students Wednesday. As large industrial projects continue moving into Wyoming, more students, perhaps influenced by their college debt-ridden Gen X or Millennial parents, are looking beyond the traditional college route toward their future.
“There’s a shifting sentiment towards ‘What I should do after high school?’ What we’re seeing is the public being more concerned about the value of postsecondary education, the ROI and the affordability of it, which has started to shift the perspective on trades — which I think is a positive thing,” Schaffer said. “The challenge that we have right now is that we don’t have enough capacity to introduce young people to it in the high schools.”
Those students, if they saw the trades as a potential next step for them, might consider a career with Bechtel.
The global engineering company
has its hands in megaprojects all over the world, from airport metro systems and copper mines in South America to Fukushima disaster cleanup in Japan to the
Natrium Project in Wyoming
.
Representatives traveled to Cheyenne on Wednesday to give insight to, and gain insight from, local leaders on how the public and private sectors can work together to foster a greater interest in trades from high school and college students. The company also announced that, to help support that goal, it would donate a combined $1 million between LCCC’s and Western Wyoming College’s trades education programs and launch Build For More, the statewide education campaign to raise awareness for the trades.
Bechtel Senior Vice President and Head of Construction Walker Kimball and LCCC President Joe Schaffer at a panel discussing new trades opportunities for Wyoming students Oct. 28, 2026 (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
Kimball said that Bechtel has found that students know about the trades generally, but don’t know about how great its industries are for making global contributions and honing ambitions.
“You can lead a horse to water, but you can’t make them drink. By educating and getting out in the community and helping people understand why it’s important, that it’s well-paying, that it’s a community of very talented people — I promise you, after you spend several years in that you’ll feel privileged to be a part of it,” Kimball said. “You will want to drink the water. You will want to go on, fix things, help build America, be part of things that sustain your community.”
Schaffer called the donation a “catalyst investment,” saying that it would be stretched to allow LCCC to expand its capacity for trades education and give students access to some of the best engineers on the planet.
The demand for education is already there, he added: All of LCCC’s trade classes are at capacity this semester and even Laramie County School District 1 is struggling to find space for all of its trade students.
“We hope we can build upon that to continue to scale the number of program slots and opportunities we provide to many of the young men and women who are in this room who are saying, ‘Hey, this is for me,'” Schaffer said.
LCCC President Joe Schaffer and Wyoming Gov. Mark Gordon at a panel discussing new trades opportunities for Wyoming students Oct. 28, 2026 (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
The recent boom of interest in the trades by young people is encouraging to Gordon, who said he made it a priority of his eight-year-long administration. Technology like artificial intelligence has shaken up the world of skilled jobs, even driving heavy machinery in the mines and job sites that have powered Wyoming for decades. Access to these resources and Bechtel’s experience, he said, keeps Wyoming students ahead of the curve.
“Hopefully this education system — and I would say the government behind that — has equipped each of you with the opportunity to go out and and and be part of the solution,” he said to the room of LCCC trades students. “More importantly, my gosh, the introduction to what Bechtel can provide.”
There are great jobs out there for students who want them, and Bechtel’s presence puts them even closer within reach, he said.
“To think about how you have the opportunity as a student in Wyoming to get the training that makes you really relevant for any of the trades you decide to take up and then the introduction to Bechtel to be able to think about ‘Where I can take those skills and what I can do with them?’ — that’s, to me, the whole recipe right there,” he said.
LCCC President Joe Schaffer and Wyoming Gov. Mark Gordon at a panel discussing new trades opportunities for Wyoming students Oct. 28, 2026 (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
The partnership between the company, the colleges and the state government also means they can tailor-make a pipeline for students to go directly from trades exposure in high school to skilled trades programs in their postsecondary education to their careers on jobs in Wyoming.
“Being available and understanding how to move through this system, how to be able to make your job continually more relevant, that’s the excitement,” Gordon said. “I believe our students are capable of doing the best things for America.”
According to a release published by Bechtel immediately after the conclusion of the panel, the funding will be used to purchase hands-on training equipment and tools, as well as create dedicated training spaces, fund instructors and develop curricula.
At LCCC specifically, the donation funds programs geared toward connecting graduates directly with local Cheyenne contractors and industry partners.
