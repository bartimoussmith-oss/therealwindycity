Cheyenne OK’s armored vehicle, vest purchases for police

Garrett Grochowski

(City of Cheyenne)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — The Cheyenne City Council approved the purchase of two armored vehicles and 26 armored vests for the Cheyenne Police Department on Monday night, investing nearly $1 million into the department in one evening.
During its Oct. 26 meeting, the council approved two purchases for the CPD. The councilors approved an $853,980 sole-source purchase from Lenco Industries for two BearCat G3 Armored Rescue Vehicles and a $142,800 sole-source purchase from AARDVARK Tactical for 26 sets of PROJECT 7 ballistic armor systems.
The BearCat is an American-made, heavy-duty armored vehicle that the CPD said it plans to use primarily for responding to hostage and barricade situations, active threats and high-risk tactical incidents. A department representative told the committee that the vehicles can also be employed in severe weather when other emergency response vehicles like ambulances can’t get to those in need.
The machines would replace two of the department’s MRAPs — Mine-Resistant Ambush Protected vehicles — that have been suffering from mechanical failures and rendered unusable in law enforcement operations in the past year.
The BearCats, which are built on a standard Ford F-550 commercial chassis and are easier to repair, are expected to function for at least 10–15 years. Because Cheyenne is safer than most other American communities and the vehicles may not be used as much, the police said, they could survive for even longer.
The $853,980 secured for the purchase comes from two sources: half from Federal Forfeiture Funds and half from General Fund reserves.
AARDVARK Tactical ballistic armor systems are heavy-duty vests and are set to replace aging gear for the Cheyenne Police Department SWAT unit. The $142,800, secured through federal grants, will pay for 26 sets.
According to the City of Cheyenne, the vests’s plates are modular, allowing officers to scale them up or down depending on what the situation requires.
In total, the two purchases cost the city $996,780.
