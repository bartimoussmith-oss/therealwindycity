Alleging debilitating injury after arrest, Cheyenne man suing pair of police officers
by
Garrett Grochowski
November 9, 2026
November 9, 2026

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Last week, a Cheyenne man filed a lawsuit against two Cheyenne Police Department officers after they reportedly broke his leg and severed a critical artery during a blood draw, requiring five surgeries in the span of one year and placing him under significant physical and financial stress.
Brandon Moyte, a 35-year-old Cheyenne man, was arrested after a DUI in November 2024. Failing on-site sobriety tests and refusing chemical testing, Moyte was taken by the Wyoming Highway Patrol trooper conducting the stop to the Cheyenne Regional Medical Center for a blood draw.
At the medical center, he refused to give consent for the draw after a search warrant was remotely granted, telling two Cheyenne Police Department officers who were there to take the blood that they would have to pin him to the floor and take his blood by force.
Still handcuffed and without any aggression, according to the suit’s plaintiff complaint, Moyte was reportedly placed face-down on the floor, with his torso and head pulled up for the draw and his legs locked into place. According to the complaint, the force and angle the officers used for the draw bent his right leg “completely in half in the wrong direction at the knee joint,” shattering the leg’s tibia, tearing its knee ligaments and severing the popliteal artery.
All of this, the complaint says, was captured on video.
The pain and distress caused by that incident and the reported fallout of the incident on Moyte’s life has been significant, the complaint said. Now he is suing Cheyenne police officers Zabriskie and Lecaire, who he says are responsible for the injury by using excessive force that he is constitutionally protected from, for an amount to be determined in court.
After the blood draw, he was immediately transported to the hospital’s emergency department and had a “life-saving” surgery the same night, the complaint states. He reportedly had no pulse in his right foot and was screaming in agony immediately after the break occurred. Doctors first conducted a CT scan of his leg, confirming a severed popliteal artery, a dislocated femur and tibia, a shattered tibial plateau, bone fragments floating and trapped in the center of the knee, and severely torn and disrupted major knee ligaments.
The six-hour surgery included an arterial bypass to reconnect his arteries, which was reportedly complicated by significant hematoma in the knee area.
Over the next 10 months, Moyte would have four more surgeries. One, performed a week after the incident to repair Moyte’s knee fracture and ligaments, revealed irreparable nerve damage, ruptures of the ACL and PCL, a meniscus tear and more.
“The surgeries of Nov. 6, 2024 and Nov. 11, 2024, while life-saving and largely successful in stabilizing Mr. Moyte’s leg and knee, were unable to repair the peroneal nerve, which controls lifting the foot and gives feeling to the top of the foot and outer lower leg,” Moyte’s complaint read.
The third surgery was a skin graft to cover more than 400 square centimeters of wounds. The remaining two were performed throughout 2025 and culminated in a total knee replacement. All of them, Moyte’s complaint read, left him with extensive scarring and debilitating, life-altering pain.
The complaint noted that receiving a total knee replacement at the age of 34 would likely lead to major complications later in life.
Unable to work because of his inability to properly walk, Moyte’s plumbing business failed. His family reportedly suffered multiple outages and utility shutoffs as a result of Moyte failing to pay his bills, and his wife eventually left him.
Cheyenne police and court documents show he was arrested in April 2026 for domestic battery, a charge filed after he slapped his wife at a local bar. He was arrested again in June on charges of assault and battery and interference with a peace officer causing injury, and in July for failure to comply, later pleading guilty to two of the three charges filed against him.
The pain itself has also caused Moyte significant distress, he said. Physical therapy was long and painful and he often loses sleep from having to wake up in the middle of the night to submerge his entire leg in ice water.
Both Zabriskie and Lecaire, the suit charges, acted against the Fourth Amendment on the basis of
42 U.S.C. § 1983
and are responsible for the injury and subsequent damages. Moyte will be represented by Devon W. Petersen of
Fleener Petersen LLC
.
The Cheyenne Police Department declined to provide a comment to Cap City News regarding the lawsuit.
CORRECTION, Nov. 9, 2026: The timeline of Moyte’s 2026 arrests was corrected and expanded.
