Prometheus Hyperscale drops plans for $500M data center campus in Natrona County
by
Klark Byrd
October 28, 2026

Eric Schlidt with Prometheus Hyperscale's Build Wyoming, left, and Prometheus founder Trenton Thornock sit among the Natrona County Board of Commissioners audience just prior to their workshop discussion about plans to build a1.5-gigawatt data center campus to the southeast of Casper. (Greg Hirst, Oil City News)
CASPER, Wyo. — Prometheus Hyperscale will not build its planned $500 million data center campus in Natrona County.
The decision, first reported by the Casper Star-Tribune on Wednesday and confirmed by
WyoFile
via an email with Prometheus President Trevor Neilson, came as a surprise to local officials who said they had no heads up.
Justin Farley, CEO of economic development organization Advance Casper, confirmed the company’s exit to Oil City News
. He said local economic recruitment continues
.
“Advance Casper is disappointed in their decision to pull out of there,” Farley said. “Fortunately, we have other developers that have properties under option in Natrona County. So, we’ll continue to work with them.”
He said those developers holding land options remain anonymous and have not bought property, but efforts are underway to keep courting tech investments.
“Natrona County is still open for business and we’re still going to pursue any leads that we get in data centers,” Farley said. “But this one, of course, was the first in, and we’re disappointed to see them pull out of there.”
Natrona County Commissioner Dave North said the withdrawal came as a surprise, first hearing the news during the phone call with Oil City News. County officials received no advance notice or explanation from company leaders, he said.
“I have absolutely no clue of why they’ve decided to pull out or anything else at this point in time,” North said. “Obviously, that’s a business decision, but I don’t know where we’re at or what happened on that.”
North said the board took no votes or regulatory actions that would have hindered the project or forced Prometheus to abandon its plans, a stance Commissioner Jim Milne echoed.
“I’m extremely disappointed that they weren’t able to come into our county and chose to go somewhere else,” Milne added. “It would have been a heck of an opportunity for our county. I wish them well. I hope everything works out for them, and I hope they look to Natrona County in the future potentially if they do try to do another project.”
Milne pointed to aggressive recruitment efforts by economic development organizations across county and state lines that may have successfully lured Prometheus away from central Wyoming.
Prometheus is the second industrial project to fall through in the county recently, following the
exit of nuclear energy developer Radiant Industries
. North said Radiant left because state lawmakers failed to act on energy regulations.
Farley said state energy rules and power grid limits remain hurdles for heavy industrial projects
.
“I think there is a thread of commonality between the Radiant side of things and the data center and that the state government has some legislation that they’re looking at behind the meter and things like that that I think will open that up,” he said. “That was needed to get into Natrona County due to who our utility provider is. They don’t have capacity.”
Prometheus pitched the 1.5-gigawatt computing campus
last spring. The company planned to build data halls and on-site power generation across 940 acres of the Falls Ranch property east of Casper, south of Interstate 25. The plan used closed-loop liquid cooling to prevent heavy water use.
Prometheus had recently announced
a partnership with Evansville-based Mesa Power Solutions
to supply natural gas generators.
Company founder Trenton Thornock and Build Wyoming Director Eric Schlidt
presented the proposal to county commissioners in September
. They asked the board to establish an industrial park zoning designation, which would have let the project bypass review by the Wyoming Industrial Siting Council, speeding up construction while keeping permitting at the county level.
The company estimated the project would have created 3,000 temporary construction jobs, 150 permanent jobs and tens of millions of dollars in annual property tax revenue.
Landowners in the nearby Hat Six Road and Meadow Acres areas
pushed back against the plan
. During public meetings, neighbors raised concerns about round-the-clock generator noise, light pollution, air emissions, truck traffic and falling property values. Residents also questioned the actual tax benefits because the state exempts data center equipment from sales taxes.
Milne acknowledged that neighborhood opposition and widespread public skepticism surrounding AI data center infrastructure likely played a role in driving Prometheus to seek an alternative location.
Farley said vocal critics of growth represent a minority within the community.
“I think there is a small group of people that are resistant to it, but I think overall our county and the citizenry understand that we need to look at every opportunity and work with them because we need to grow,” he said. “When it really comes down to it, I think you have the majority of people that are behind expansion, diversification, growth, all of that.”
Prometheus had not submitted a permit application to the Natrona County Planning and Zoning Commission before withdrawing. A similar project in Uinta County remains underway.
