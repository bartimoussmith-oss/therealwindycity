Gerald "Jerry" Blessing
Gerald “Jerry” Blessing:
1940 – 2026
Gerald “Jerry” Leslie Blessing died Wednesday, October 21, 2026. Jerry will be remembered for his devotion to family, his service to others, his strong work ethic, and his lifelong love of motorcycles and the outdoors.
Jerry proudly served in the United States Navy. After his military service, he built a long and accomplished career with the State of Wyoming, working as an Office Machine Technician for more than 30 years before retiring. Dedicated to his profession, he earned numerous certifications throughout his career.
Many years ago in Sterling, Colorado, Jerry demonstrated remarkable courage when he helped rescue two men from drowning after their boat capsized. His actions reflected the bravery and willingness to help others that remained an important part of who he was.
An avid motorcycle enthusiast and rider, Jerry spent many years as a member of the Harley Owners Group (H.O.G.) and enjoyed attending motorcycle rallies. He also loved the outdoors and treasured the many summers he spent camping.
Family held a special place in Jerry’s heart. With his first wife, Evie JoAnn Blessing, he adopted his beloved daughter, Anna Marie Blessing. He was immensely proud to become her father and lovingly wrote each of her birth announcements by hand. Jerry was later married to Mary Blessing and shared his later years with his wife, Dorothy Blessing.
Jerry leaves a legacy of service, courage, adventure, and love. His stories, accomplishments, and devotion to those he loved will be remembered by his family and all who knew him.
Left to Cherish His Memory
Jerry is survived by his wife, Dorothy Blessing; his daughter, Anna Marie Blessing; his stepsons: Rohan (Sarita) Robinson, Rashawn Robinson, and Renaldo Robinson; his grandchildren: Reghan Blessing, Riley Blessing, Malachi Robinson, and Safia Robinson; his brothers: James Thompson, Donald Thompson, David Thompson, and John Thompson; and many nieces and nephews.
Preceded in Death
Jerry was preceded in death by his mother, Iris Thompson; his grandmother, Anna Blessing; his father, Orange Nunn; his first wife, Evie JoAnn Blessing; his second wife, Mary Blessing; and his brothers, Ronald Thompson and Richard Thompson.
Services
A celebration of his life will be held at Weiderspahn Rodomsky funeral home on Thursday, October 29, 2026 at 10 a.m.
Richard August Person:
1935 – 2026
Richard A Person age 91 passed away Sunday, October 18, 2026, with his family by his side. Rich was born March 8, 1935, to David and Doris Person. He lived his entire life on the family homestead in Banner County until his declining health forced him to move into Scottsbluff, Nebraska.
Richard’s funeral services will be held at 11 a.m. on Saturday, October 24, 2026, at the Albin Community Center in Albin, Wyoming. Interment will follow at Albin Cemetery. A reception will follow with a light lunch at the Albin Community Center. Schrader Aragon & Jacoby Funeral Home in Cheyenne, Wyoming is in charge of the services. It is respectfully requested that in lieu of any flowers, memorials be made in Rich’s honor to the Albin Community food service program or Grace Lutheran Church in Pine Bluffs WY.
Rich rode his horse to a country school until the 7th grade when he transferred to the Albin Schools. He met the love of his life, Louise E Clark, his senior year. They were married in May of 1956, 70 years ago. Rich was a dry land farmer and raised cattle his entire life. In his later years he drove school buses for the Albin, and Banner County schools. He later worked full time for the Banner County Road Department until he retired in 2005.
Rich has five daughters: Karen (Jim) Larson, Janet (Lon) Johnson, Gayle Person, Cheryl Dady, Lesli (Ken) Fitzgerald.
In 1960 Rich was baptized and confirmed into the Grace Lutheran Church where he remained as an active member. He served as the Sunday School Superintendent, and Church Elder for many years.
Rich worked hard seven days a week, 365 days a year, rarely taking a day off for leisure. He was a faithful husband, dedicated dad and a loving grandpa.
Rich is survived by his wife Louise; five daughters; 12 grandchildren; 28 great-grandchildren; and sister, JoAnn Taylor; brother-in-law, Richard Clark; and numerous nieces and nephews.
He was preceded in death by his parents; infant daughter, Susan; an infant brother; and brother, Charles; numerous in-laws, aunts and uncles and cousins.
Rich was a man with a huge heart and fun sense of humor. His love for his family can’t ever be replaced.
We would like to thank everyone for their prayers. Also, to the staff at Emerald Court for being so amazing with the care and compassion you provided to him, we are forever grateful.
Gerald Milton “Gary” Ricley:
1946 – 2026
Gerald Milton “Gary” Ricley, 79, of Slater, Wyoming, passed away peacefully in his sleep on October 17, 2026, leaving behind a lifetime of love, laughter, adventure, and stories that will be told for generations.
Born November 14, 1946, in Denver, Colorado, to Arthur Morgan and Pauline Rose Ricley, Gary was a man of remarkable ingenuity, unwavering determination, deep faith, and an unforgettable sense of humor. He knew how to get things done but never believed life should be taken too seriously.
If asked, Gary would say his greatest accomplishment was the family and life he built with his beloved wife, Karen Sue. They met in 1964, and married after two years of courtship, going on to spend 50 years together. They raised four children and created a life rich in love, laughter, and unforgettable memories. Summers meant weeks of boating, winters brought family ski trips, and he continually prioritized creating memories through family vacations, hunting adventures, late-night card games, and time spent together filled the years. Gary especially loved deer and elk hunting and believed life’s greatest adventures were even better when shared with family and friends, even including his mom on a trip to Hawaii.
Gary’s professional accomplishments were extraordinary. Beginning his career as a mechanic, he worked his way through the ranks of General Motors to become an executive, retiring at just 52 years old. He possessed the mind of an engineer, a natural gift for problem solving, and a work ethic that he used to be successful across several endeavors, both personally and professionally.
Retirement was hardly the end of Gary’s working years. For more than 30 years, he enjoyed a successful ranching and cattle operation on his rolling Wyoming ranch. Alongside his beloved pet horses, chickens, and cattle, Gary and Karen took great pride in caring for the land and creating a haven for wildlife. Their dedication to wildlife habitat improvements earned them the honor of 2011 Landowners of the Year, a recognition of their commitment to conservation and stewardship. They also welcomed walk-in pheasant hunting on their property, sharing their appreciation for the land and outdoors with others. The ranch became another chapter in a life defined by hard work, ingenuity, and a deep appreciation for nature.
Wonderfully nostalgic, he loved sharing stories from his colorful life, often telling the same favorites again and again. His family could frequently recite them right along with him, but his enthusiasm and familiar punchlines were part of what made him so endearing. Those stories, and the laughter that accompanied them, became treasured family traditions.
His faith in and love for the lord were central to his life, providing strength and comfort throughout every season. The passing of Karen eleven years ago profoundly changed Gary’s world. After a lifetime together, navigating life without her was never quite the same.
In his later years, Gary faced the challenges of dementia, particularly difficult for a man who had always proudly described his memory as a “steel trap.” Though the disease gradually changed his world, it never erased the man his family loved. When comfortable and at ease, his familiar humor and quick wit still shone through, offering precious glimpses of the storyteller and jokester everyone knew.
Gary lived a full and vibrant life. He worked hard, loved deeply, laughed often, and filled his years with experiences worth remembering. His greatest legacy isn’t measured by professional titles, cattle raised, or places traveled, but by the family he cherished, and the memories he leaves behind.
Gary was preceded in death by his beloved wife, Karen Sue; cherished granddaughter, Elizabeth Rose Dimzoff; parents, Arthur Morgan and Pauline Rose Ricley; and brother, Ronald Arthur Ricley.
He is survived by his four children: Ronda Dimzoff of Frederick, Colorado; Terry Ricley of Burlingame, Kansas; and David Ricley and Sheila Ricley, both of Cheyenne, Wyoming; nine grandchildren; seven great-grandchildren; and many extended family members and friends.
And though Gary may have told the same stories a hundred times, his family would give anything to hear him tell them just once more.
Rest peacefully, Gary. A life well lived, a job well done, and a lifetime of stories left for us to tell.
A celebration of Gary’s life will be held Friday, November 6, 2026, at 1 p.m. at Lakeview Chapel at Schrader, Aragon and Jacoby Funeral Home, 2222 Russell Avenue, Cheyenne, Wyoming. [Reception details to be added.]
In lieu of flowers, the family requests memorial contributions to Davis Hospice Center in Cheyenne, Wyoming.
