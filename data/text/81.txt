Planning Commission endorses new construction rules, postpones data center regulations
by
Garrett Grochowski
October 21, 2026

(City of Cheyenne)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — A series of updates to the City of Cheyenne’s Unified Development Code that would enforce stricter quiet hours for construction projects and mandate a buffer between temporary labor housing and buildings like schools, among other changes, will go to the Cheyenne City Council for review after earning Planning Commission support Monday. New standards specific to data centers, meanwhile, were delayed another two weeks.
The Cheyenne Planning Commission, the volunteer-led body that reviews planning-related proposals before they go to the City Council for voting, met Monday, Oct. 19 to review a handful of proposed amendments to the city’s Unified Development Code. The UDC establishes zoning rules and building codes for the entire city to follow.
The new batch of amendments to the code applies mostly to large construction projects. Ultimately, the goal of the amendments is to help the city’s development standards catch up to the pace of development of large industrial projects like data centers, which weren’t considered until recently.
Data centers specifically differ from other construction projects in that they are typically multi-year efforts, prolonging the effects development has on traffic, noise and lights.
The amendments address just that. One, affecting UDC Section 1.4.3, expands the window of time that stricter nighttime noise decibel limits are enforced from 7 a.m. to 10 p.m. daily to 7 a.m. to 9 p.m. daily. It also requires there be a 1,000-foot buffer between the temporary workforce housing projects that occasionally pop up alongside the large data center projects and neighborhoods, churches and schools.
A change to Section 4.5.3 would add a requirement that site plans explicitly disclose estimated monthly water consumption measured in gallons. Before, the section only required developers to provide a general “demonstration of adequate water and sanitary sewer utility systems” and “adequate water and sanitary sewer capacity,” without any specific volumetric data.
Amendments to Section 4.5.4 establish that developers are responsible for paying all construction costs for required system expansion and utility improvements. Section 5.8.9 amendments would require that construction sites over 5 acres in size submit site layout plans to the city, implement downward-facing shielded lighting at property lines, provide 125% financial security for site reclamation and submit traffic impact studies for sites anticipating over 100 workers at any given time.
Section 5.7.1 changes, which would have established codes specific to data center developments, were postponed for another two weeks to give staff time to organize and incorporate a handful of amendments recommended and approved on the floor during discussion. Those amendments include 1- to 2-mile setbacks for data centers featuring on-site power generation facilities and 2,000-foot setbacks for data centers running round-the-clock fuel cells or backup diesel generators.
Besides the postponed changes to Section 5.7.1, the batch of amendments passed with the Planning Commission’s recommendation. They’ll move on to the Cheyenne City Council for review. If they are eventually approved by the council, they’ll establish clearer and stricter rules for long-term construction projects within Cheyenne city limits.
