Wyoming Highway Patrol reminds drivers shoulder parking is illegal after 70 crashes

A car sits parked on the shoulder of a highway. (Wyoming Highway Patrol photo)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — The Wyoming Highway Patrol is urging drivers to stay off highway shoulders after 70 crashes involving parked vehicles left six people dead and 21 injured across the state.
Between February 2025 and April 2026, collisions involving vehicles stopped on the shoulders of state highways and county roads also resulted in 51 instances of property damage, according to a patrol news release.
State law prohibits parking on the shoulders of Wyoming highways except during an emergency. Troopers define emergencies as mechanical breakdowns, medical emergencies, traffic stops or sudden maneuvers to avoid a collision.
Stopping on the shoulder to check a GPS, take a phone call or rest from driver fatigue is not considered an emergency and can result in a citation. Troopers advise drivers needing to pull over for non-emergencies to take the nearest exit and park at a rest stop or service station.
Drivers experiencing an actual emergency should pull as far right onto the shoulder as possible and resolve the issue quickly. Troopers advise calling for roadside assistance during a mechanical failure and dialing 911 immediately in a medical emergency.
Many shoulder crashes involve commercial vehicles traveling at highway speeds, which increases the danger and likelihood of fatal injuries, the patrol said.
