GREEN RIVER -- The Sweetwater County Historical Museum recently researched a uniquely-modified Old West lever-action rifle.
The museum’s Vintage Firearms Research Program identified the gun as a Model 1873 Winchester in .38-40 caliber. Dubbed “The Gun That Won the West” by many, the ‘73 was hugely popular; over 720,000 were manufactured from 1873 to 1923.
In the last quarter of the 19th century and well into the 20th, exhibition shooters, also called “trick shooters,” were in great demand.
Annie Oakley could split a playing card held edge-on from 30 paces, shoot the ash from a cigarette held between the lips of her husband, Frank Butler, himself an accomplished exhibition shooter, and hit targets behind her, aiming over her shoulder while sighting with a mirror. Other exhibition shooters of the time included William F. “Doc” Carver, Dr. H.A. Ruth, Elizabeth “Plinky” Topperwein, and William F. “Buffalo Bill” Cody.
Shooting glass balls stuffed with feathers thrown in the air was a reliable crowd pleaser. When it came this event, however, some shooters saw nothing wrong with creating a special advantage for themselves by using rifles that fired not bullets, but bird shot, with the audience none the wiser. A healthy measure of skill was still called for, as anyone who has shot skeet or trap for the first time can attest, according to the museum.
Among these was Buffalo Bill himself, or one of the shooters in his traveling show. Official records confirm that on Sept. 23, 1909, a custom-made smoothbore Model 1873 in .44-40 caliber was shipped from the Winchester factory to “Buffalo Bill’s Wild West in Shawnee, Oklahoma.” This rifle brought over $150,00 at auction not long ago.
The Model 1873 researched by the museum was similarly modified to handle bird shot cartridges. The bore of the round barrel was reamed out to create a shotgun. Its full-length tubular magazine was shortened, a shotgun bead front sight was mounted, and an unusual front sight was installed as a large-aperture rear sight.
It was determined that the ‘73 researched was made between 1873 and 1882, based on certain receiver characteristics. Unfortunately, additional details were unobtainable because the rifle lacks a serial number. Model 1873 serial numbers were stamped on the lower tang, a detachable part that was often replaced during repairs without the number being re-stamped--  such was apparently the case here. There is little doubt that the rifle was modified for a serious, though unidentified, exhibition shooter; the modifications were extensive and professional.
Those with a vintage firearm or firearms who would like to learn more about them are encouraged to contact the museum at (307) 872-6435 or via email at
blustd@sweetwatercountywy.gov
. There is no charge for the service.
