$100K bond set for suspect in deadly July DUI crash

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — A Colorado man charged with the July 23 killing of one passenger and injury of another after a violent car crash while eluding law enforcement was processed in Laramie County court this week. Being severely injured himself, he was life-flighted to Colorado, where he was also on probation, after the crash to seek treatment.
The man, identified as James Moore, is back in Laramie County and was just issued a $100,000 cash-only bond. He was also assessed a new charge: aggravated assault and battery causing bodily injury. That felony — punishable by up to 10 years in prison — joins his extant aggravated vehicular homicide by DUI, felony property destruction, aggravated eluding causing bodily injury, aggravated eluding causing property damage, reckless driving and driving without taillights charges.
Moore is presumed innocent until pleading or being found guilty on those charges.
According to Moore’s criminal affidavit, the incident leading up to his Laramie County arrest began July 23 at around 11 p.m. The Wyoming Highway Patrol trooper who would come to arrest him was sitting in a median on Interstate 25 when he reportedly saw a white Hyundai sedan from Colorado pass without any taillights.
The trooper pulled out of the median and turned on his lights to signal the car to pull over. It pulled off to the side as if pulling over, but continued to the nearby exit onto Clear Creek Parkway and quickly sped up to flee, the affidavit states.
The trooper gave chase and turned on his sirens, but the car reportedly continued, accelerating to as fast as 100 mph in a 45 mph zone.
After just one minute of chasing the car, the trooper saw what looked like “a small explosion” from the car and watched it  speed off of the roadway in the direction of a distant parking lot, the affidavit states.
When the trooper caught up and properly entered the lot three minutes later, he approached the car on foot. He reportedly saw the driver, Moore, unconscious and unresponsive. His passenger — identified as a South Carolina resident by his driver’s license — was partially unconscious but slowly became responsive.
The trooper tried talking to the passenger, who reportedly said he and the driver were the only people in the car. That would turn out to be false, the affidavit states.
A few minutes after approaching the two in the car, the trooper searched the debris field where Moore had exited the road, finding a third, unidentified person who was fatally injured. Laying on his stomach face-down, he had reportedly sustained injuries “not compatible with life.”
The trooper reported a strong smell of alcohol coming from the car and found a beverage in the driver’s-door cupholder. Beer cans and shooter bottles also littered the nearby debris field. The trooper also found a backpack that contained a sex toy and some fake urine, according to the affidavit.
Moore, who was severely injured in the crash, was life flighted to Colorado after a blood test was performed. The surviving passenger was transported to the Cheyenne Regional Medical Center.
While investigating the crash, the grandparents of the dead passenger called and said they received a crash notification from their grandson’s phone, helping identify the ID-less victim.
Moore was not permitted to leave Larimer County, Colorado, without permission, as he was on probation there. He was transported back there to seek treatment to his injuries. An arrest warrant was issued shortly after, but was not executed until October.
Now back in the Laramie County court system, Moore will begin the trial process. He first has a preliminary hearing Nov. 4.
