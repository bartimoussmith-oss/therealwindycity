Morning showers clear out of Cheyenne as breezy, dry weather settles in

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Lingering morning rain and snow showers will clear out across Cheyenne by mid-morning today, paving the way for breezy winds, sunshine and cool seasonal temperatures.
Drivers heading out early should watch for slick spots and patchy fog during the morning commute, according to National Weather Service in Cheyenne. The slickest road conditions are expected where snow fell overnight, including U.S. Highway 85 in northern Laramie County, Interstate 80 between Arlington and Elk Mountain, and the I-80 summit.
Skies will turn sunny this afternoon as temperatures climb to a high near 51 degrees in Cheyenne for
Election Day
. Winds from the north-northwest are expected at 15–20 mph, with gusts reaching 35 mph before tapering off around sunset.
Mostly clear skies will settle in Tuesday night as the low drops to around 27. Temps across southeast Wyoming will drop close to or below freezing, with areas holding snow cover dipping into the low 20s.
Clouds will return Wednesday as a system passes south across Colorado. Meteorologists expect most of that moisture to remain south of the state line, keeping rain chances in Cheyenne below 15%. Wednesday’s high will reach near 53 under light winds, with an overnight low around 31.
Dry conditions and typical November temperatures will continue through the end of the workweek. Cheyenne will see sunshine Thursday with a high near 57, followed by a mostly sunny Friday and a high near 56.
A warmup is set to arrive in time for the weekend. High pressure will build across the region Sunday, pushing highs into the 60s across Cheyenne and east of the Laramie Range. That will run roughly 10 degrees above normal for early November.
Because snow did not stick in Cheyenne this morning, meteorologists said the dry stretch will place the city in the top 10 latest dates on record for its first measurable snowfall.
October was yet another warm month for the Cheyenne area. Cheyenne had its warmest October on record, while Laramie, Rawlins, Scottsbluff and Sidney all ended second warmest. Precipitation was a mixed bag, but generally close to average. (National Weather Service graphic)
