Cheyenne updates investment policy to add stocks and ETFs to portfolio

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — The City of Cheyenne has updated its investment policy, allowing the city to invest in equities like stocks. It is now one of multiple Wyoming cities to expand its portfolio since the State of Wyoming Legislature moved to allow the policy in 2025.
Despite state law allowing investment into stocks and bonds since last year, Cheyenne has only invested in low-risk securities like bonds and government investment pools to this point. Before it could diversify into money-generating sources like index mutual funds and exchange-traded funds, the city was legally required to update its policy on how staff could invest.
A resolution to update the policy hit the Cheyenne Finance Committee for consideration last week, where councilors unanimously supported it. There, City Treasurer Robin Lockman said using small portions of the city’s core funds to invest would help yield higher investment returns for the city and beat inflation.
The city’s operating money and other short-term cash will not be used for trading, according to the resolution. Instead, a $47,300,000 pool made up from portions of long-term core funds like the General Fund reserve and the Solid Waste Landfill Post-Closure Fund would be used. The city can only pull up to 20% of what each eligible fund has stored. That equals a maximum initial investment of around $9,500,000 — only around 3.6% of the city’s total $260,000,000 investment portfolio.
“Our goal is not to take unnecessary risks with taxpayer dollars, particularly funds needed for daily operations,” Lockman said to the committee.
The new policy also establishes a clear rulebook for how the city will invest. Importantly, the policy notes, the city cannot pick and choose single stocks for its portfolio. It would instead be limited to investing in index mutual funds or exchange-traded funds. Other riskier investments like leveraged funds would be prohibited.
The slow but sure appreciation of those funds is still leagues faster than the annual growth of the safer investments Cheyenne has made in years past.
It wouldn’t be the city managing the funds day-to-day. Now that the city has approved the resolution, it will select a firm to manage its financial assets, while the city’s investment committee — composed of the mayor, treasurer, City Council members and finance professionals — provides oversight.
