Converse County Commissioner Robert Short speaks during an Election Night watch party at Ford Wyoming Center on Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026. (Dan Cepeda, Oil City News and WyoFile)
CASPER, Wyo. — Converse County Commissioner Robert Short will be Wyoming’s next secretary of state.
In Tuesday’s race, called by the Associated Press at 9:28 p.m., Short received 72,523 votes. The Republican nominee was challenged by Democrat Bryan McCarty and Rep. Tamara Trujillo of the Constitution Party. McCarty received 23,000 votes while Trujillo got 9,231.
The final tally is subject to change as the race was called with 52% of the total votes cast. Polls closed at 7 p.m.
Short will succeed Secretary of State Chuck Gray, who was elected to fill Wyoming’s lone U.S. House of Representatives seat, vacated by Rep. Harriet Hageman,
who won her bid to fill retiring Sen. Cynthia Lummis’s U.S. Senate seat
.
Short advanced to the general election as the Republican nominee after defeating Qwenton Eagle Oviatt, Rep. Rachel Williams and CJ Young in August’s primary election.
A Douglas resident, Glenrock High School graduate and former election judge, Short has served on the commission since 2014 and serves as vice president of the Wyoming County Commissioners Association. He owns businesses across the energy, agriculture, retail and tourism industries. Running on a conservative platform of limited government and lower taxes, Short aims to protect business privacy laws, strengthen safeguards against fraudulent filings and support county clerks in running fair and accurate elections.
