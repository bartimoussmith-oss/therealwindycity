Laramie County gas prices drop 27 cents as national average tiptoes $4

A man prepares to pump gasoline. (Shutterstock)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Laramie County’s average gas price
plummeted 27 cents from last week
to $3.82 per gallon Monday,
according to AAA data
.
Wyoming’s statewide average shed 20 cents to $4.10 per gallon. AAA reported a 16-cent drop in the national average to $4 per gallon, with
price-tracking service GasBuddy
reporting a 15.9-cent drop in the national average to $3.96 per gallon.
That average is down 39.3 cents from a month ago, but remains up 92.2 cents from a year ago, GasBuddy said. The national average price of diesel fell 9.5 cents over the last week to $6.18 per gallon.
(AAA graphic)
Cheyenne drivers could find the cheapest fuel at $3.55 at Moody’s Rock Stop on West Pershing Boulevard, followed by $3.63 at Sam’s Club on Dell Range Boulevard,
according to GasBuddy reports
.
At $3.821, Natrona County continued to offer Wyoming’s cheapest fuel for a second week, but only by two-tenths of a cent after dropping 21 cents. Laramie County’s $3.823 is the state’s second cheapest average. Converse County’s average dropped 22 cents to the state’s third cheapest average of $3.86 per gallon, according to AAA.
Gas prices fell across every state over the past week. While drivers often tie price shifts to politics, seasonal refinery operations and crude supply are the primary forces behind the relief, according to GasBuddy.
“Many will assume the election had something to do with it, but that’s a myth,” said Patrick De Haan, head of petroleum analysis at GasBuddy
. “Gas prices have fallen in the 30 days leading up to the election every year since 2006 except 2010, and it’s typically seasonal: refiners finish maintenance and ramp up output just as demand fades with cooler weather.”
He said those seasonal adjustments, combined with more crude moving through the Strait of Hormuz, could keep pump prices below $4 in the coming weeks.
“That said, the election’s outcome may send a message to Washington to sharpen its focus on affordability, and I’d expect even more attention on bringing prices down,” De Haan said
. “But for now, it’s the seasonal shift, not politics, driving the relief at the pump.”
Crude oil prices extended their slide to open the week after Brent crude fell roughly 16% last week, marking its steepest weekly drop since the start of the pandemic
. In early Monday trading, West Texas Intermediate crude fell $3.62 to $87.21 per barrel, down from $99.35 the previous Monday. Brent crude dropped $4.18 to $94.14 per barrel, settling below $100 for the first time since early October.
Oil markets responded after President Donald Trump said extensive new strikes against Iran were unlikely during ongoing talks, though he conditioned that on Tehran signing an agreement
. Eased shipping demands along the Strait of Hormuz added downward pressure on prices, though tanker travel disruptions over the weekend kept shipping risks present.
Domestic commercial crude inventories fell by 900,000 barrels for the week ending Oct. 30, sitting about 4% below the five-year seasonal average, according to the U.S. Energy Information Administration. The Strategic Petroleum Reserve dropped 400,000 barrels to 282.2 million barrels.
