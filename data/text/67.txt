Capital City Athletics pushes for board appeals in new LCSD1 facility rental policy
by
Garrett Grochowski
October 22, 2026

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — As Laramie County School District 1 considers revisions to its policies on lending property to outside organizations for use, representatives from those organizations are asking that the district make the proposed policy changes fairer and allow a more streamlined appeals process. If the policy passes as proposed, they said, the superintendent’s word is final and leaves local clubs with no possible recourse if denied a request.
To better align with state statute, LCSD1 is considering revising district policy regarding how it lends its facilities to non-district organizations, specifically athletic teams.
According to the district, amendments to this policy both establish that the district’s educational, operational, safety, staffing and maintenance needs are the first priority of any use of district facilities and create clearer rules for outside organizations that wish to utilize those facilities.
New rules handed down from the legislature were made to protect the pockets of non-school youth organizations like local youth athletic clubs. Proposed protections include at-cost fee limits, capping how much the district can charge for use of a space at how much it incurred to maintain the space for that specific rental period.
Other changes establish published scheduling guidelines, rules prohibiting employees and outside users from taking district property for unauthorized personal use, enforcement for overdue payments and rule violations and rules regarding what types of animals are allowed on district property.
Before the district could approve the revisions, however, it needed to open a 45-day public comment period. The period began Sept. 29, which means the district is accepting input until Nov. 12 before a final vote Nov. 19. Local athletic club Capital City Athletics is taking advantage of that opportunity, appearing at a public hearing before the district’s Oct. 19 Board of Trustees meeting to implore the trustees to add a clearer appeals process for organizations barred from using district property.
If a group like Capital City Athletics, which specializes in swimming, is told it can’t use East High School’s pool for an evening, the only person CCA can appeal to is District Superintendent Stephen Newton, according to local attorney Sam Tortorich. If he rejects the appeal, there is nothing CCA can do.
“We are grateful in the policy for the right to appeal to the superintendent and I believe that that is going to be utilized to solve any differences in between competing uses of third-party users,” Tortorich said. “I don’t think that’s going to be adequate to resolve decisions that are being made from the superintendent’s direct line of command. My only recourse if we can’t resolve the issues here locally is to go to court.”
Going to court would be expensive for both parties. They could also lobby the state legislature, but that would place a heavy burden on the financially hamstrung CCA as well.
The Board of Trustees itself should have the right to consider an appeal, he said.
He also had a series of other concerns about the draft policy that he said could unintentionally harm the local athletic organizations the district partners with. To prevent organizations like CCA from being arbitrarily banned from facilities over disputed bills, Tortorich suggested adding a “good faith” protection clause. That, he said, would ensure groups disputing unreasonable fees with district administration aren’t locked out of facilities while the dispute is pending.

He also shared his concern about a clause in the draft policy preventing outside organizations from using “district equipment,” which he said was overly vague.
“By ‘equipment,’ do you mean wrestling mats? Do you mean lane lines? You’re going to give us facilities without wrestling mats and lane lines? Now, of course, that’s not what you intend, but if a district wants to look at this and say, ‘How can we exclude these children?’ I’m not going to give you all of them, but these are just the easy ones that are clearly available within the statute,” Tortorich said.
He and CCA coach Sarah Delay recommended the board also consider a distinct priority tier for nonprofit youth organizations, giving groups like CCA preference for using district property over random commercial users, as well as less strict requirements for custodians at facilities during use. The policy requires that custodians be present at every facility being used at any time, which limits when facilities are open because the district doesn’t have enough custodians to keep every facility open at all times. Tortorich said that one custodian could work between multiple areas because all they have to do in the evenings is close the place up and handle emergencies.
The district couldn’t respond to any of those requests during the meeting, but they’ll be taken into consideration before the trustees officially vote on the policy changes next month. See the
