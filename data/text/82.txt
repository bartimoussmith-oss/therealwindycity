Most Wyoming lawyers recommend retention of Laramie County’s judges, poll suggests

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — The majority of Wyoming lawyers, polled for a University of Wyoming–conducted survey, recommend retaining Laramie County’s circuit and district court judges. The four judges up for retainment were positively graded based on their knowledge of the law, impartiality and other qualities relevant to the profession.
Two Laramie County District Court and two Laramie County Circuit Court judges are up for retention this year. County residents have the opportunity on Election Day, Nov. 3, to vote to retain the judges, allowing them to serve another term, or to remove them from office. This election cycle, District Court Judges Nathaniel Hibben and Robin Cooley are up for six-year terms, while Circuit Court Judges Sean Chambers and Timothy Forwood can be retained for new four-year terms.
To help the public make more informed decisions when it comes to judicial retentions, the Wyoming State Bar Association commissions the Wyoming Survey & Analysis Center at the University of Wyoming to conduct a Wyoming Judicial Performance Assessment every election cycle.
The assessment is a survey of all of the state’s Supreme Court justices, district court judges, circuit court judges and chancery court judges administered to the state’s lawyers and Bar Association members. While only two justices, eight district court judges and 10 circuit court judges are up for retention around Wyoming, every single judge in the state is subject to rating.
Unlike some state bar associations, Wyoming State Bar Association membership is exclusive to those practicing law in the state. That means every member must have a professional-level understanding of the law.
Lawyers tend to agree that Laramie County residents would be doing the right thing retaining Hibben, Cooley, Chambers and Forwood. They each received majority support from the Bar Association members; Chambers’s 70.9% approval was the lowest rating of the bunch.
The judges were rated from a scale of one to five on 11 criteria:
Knowledge of the law
Open-mindedness and impartiality in judicial matters
Attentiveness to arguments of counsel
Courteousness and politeness
How they address and answer issues squarely posed
Industriousness/promptness in performing judicial duties
Participation in law-related professional activities
Integrity and ethics to carry out the duties of the judicial office
Application of rules of evidence and procedures
How well their decisions are reasoned and how clearly they are expressed
Preparedness for court proceedings
Forwood and Chambers were rated by a group of 45–55 Bar Association members, all of whom rated the two circuit court judges favorably. Forwood received no lower than an average score of four out of five on all 11 criteria and 94% of the polled lawyers voted in favor of retaining him. Chambers, meanwhile, averages some scores in the three to four range. He was rated slightly lower than his peer on his knowledge of the law, open-mindedness, attentiveness, applications of rules of evidence and addressing of issues in the courtroom. Regardless, 70.9% of participants recommended that he be retained.
Results table depicting how Wyoming Bar Association members rated the job performance of Laramie County Circuit Court Judge Timothy Forwood (Wyoming State Bar Association)
Results table depicting how Wyoming Bar Association members rated the job performance of Laramie County Circuit Court Judge Sean Chambers (Wyoming State Bar Association)
Laramie County’s two district court judges up for retention were similarly rated, with one performing slightly better than the other. Wyoming lawyers’ average score for each criterion for judge Hibben dipped no lower than 4.45; 95.4% of participants recommended voters retain him. 72.2% of participants agreed that Cooley should be retained, but were slightly more critical of the judge’s knowledge of the law, application of rules of evidence and some other qualities.

Results table depicting how Wyoming Bar Association members rated the job performance of Laramie County District Court Judge Robin Cooley (Wyoming State Bar Association)
While not currently being considered for new terms, Laramie County’s other judges were also rated by Wyoming lawyers. Their results, as well as those of Wyoming’s Supreme Court justices and other judges around the state, can be reviewed at the Wyoming State Bar Association website
