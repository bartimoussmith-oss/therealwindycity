Mark Bradley Klein
Mark Bradley Klein:
1958 – 2026
Mark Bradley Klein, 67, of Casper, Wyoming passed away unexpectedly on October 23, 2026.
Mark Klein was born on December 22, 1958, in Milwaukee, Wisconsin. He was a beloved son, brother, husband, father, uncle, and friend whose life was defined by adventure, hard work, generosity, and an unwavering love for his family.
Mark moved to Wyoming around 1970, where he would eventually make Casper his home. His life was full of adventure from an early age. After high school, he traveled to Australia with little more than a tool bag, finding work along the way as he explored the world. From Australia, he continued to Hawaii, where he lived with his uncle for a short time before returning to the mainland. Mark’s heart had long belonged to Cindy, whom he began dating in high school. Their love grew through the years, and they married in 1983, building a life together filled with memories, challenges, dreams, and a love that endured. After they married, Mark and Cindy went to Colorado, where he studied solar panel installation before returning to Casper and beginning the next chapter of his life.
Cindy was his partner through every chapter, and together they raised their sons, Matt and Jakob. They also carried the memory of their daughter, Kesha, who passed away shortly after birth and remained forever part of their family.
In 1987, Mark and Cindy moved to Las Vegas, where he put his construction skills to work. In 1996, they moved to Utah to be closer to home, before returning to Casper in 2000. There, Mark started his own business, MC Construction, turning his talent, determination, and strong work ethic into something of his own.
In 2004, Mark and Cindy began building their dream home. For two years, they poured their hearts into creating a place where they could enjoy the life they had worked so hard to build together. Just weeks before they were scheduled to move in, the unimaginable happened: the house burned down. It was a heartbreaking loss, but it was only one chapter in a life that was defined by perseverance, love, and the ability to keep moving forward.
Mark’s greatest accomplishments, however, could never be measured in buildings or projects. They were measured in the lives he touched and the people he loved. He never had grandchildren of his own, but that never stopped him from being a grandfather figure to many. He loved showing up for the children in his family and his friends’ families, cheering them on and supporting them in all their sporting endeavors. Whether he was in the stands or on the sidelines, his presence was a reminder that they were loved, encouraged, and never alone. Mark never needed a title to love a child as his own, and the many young lives he supported were a testament to how big his heart truly was.
Mark had a boisterous laugh that could fill a room and a generous spirit that will stay with those who knew him forever. He was the kind of person who would give you the shirt off his back, but his generosity went far beyond that. He helped people regardless of where they stood in life, never judging someone by whether they were up or down. He didn’t wait for people to ask for help. If he saw a need, he found a way to fill it before the request was ever made. That was simply who Mark was: someone who cared deeply and showed that care through his actions.
Mark loved the outdoors, especially hunting and camping in the Shirley Mountains with friends and family. He treasured the time spent in the mountains, enjoying the beauty of Wyoming, sharing stories, laughing, and making memories with the people he loved. Those outdoor adventures were another reflection of Mark’s adventurous spirit and his appreciation for the simple things in life. The memories made in the Shirley Mountains will live on in the hearts of those who shared those special times with him.
Mark also loved spending time at the lake, making many wonderful memories during summers at Pathfinder. Those summers were a special part of his life, filled with the kind of moments that make lasting memories with family and friends. Whether he was enjoying time at the lake, hunting and camping in the mountains, cheering on the kids he loved, traveling to new places, or simply taking in a beautiful view, Mark knew how to appreciate the people and experiences that made life meaningful.
His love of adventure never faded. Even while working and building his business, Mark always made room to appreciate the world around him. If he was driving somewhere and came across a scenic lookout, he would stop to take in the view. He understood that life was about more than reaching a destination; it was also about appreciating the moments along the way.
At the time of his passing, Mark was working on what he considered his last project: building a house for his brother-in-law and wife. He and Cindy were looking forward to retirement, with plans to travel together and explore more of the world. Though those plans remained unfinished, Mark’s adventurous spirit, love of travel, and appreciation for life’s simple moments will continue to inspire everyone who knew him.
Mark is survived by his beloved wife of 43 years, Cindy; his sons, Matt and Jakob; and his siblings: Todd, Cindy, Patrick, Brian and his wife, Lesa, and Jennifer. He also leaves behind nieces, nephews, extended family, and the many children he embraced as his own through love and support.
He was preceded in death by Gail Brown; Tim Klein; and his beloved daughter, Kesha.
Mark will be remembered for his adventurous heart, his dedication to his craft, his boundless generosity, his love for his family, and the boisterous laugh that will forever echo in the memories of those who loved him. He showed us that family is not only who we are born to, but also who we choose to love, support, and stand beside. His legacy lives on in the homes he built, the lives he helped, the children he cheered for, the summers spent at Pathfinder, the hunting and camping trips in the Shirley Mountains, the places he explored, and the countless memories he leaves behind.
Though he and Cindy may not have made it to the retirement adventures they dreamed of sharing, Mark’s spirit will continue to travel with everyone whose life he touched.
He was deeply loved, he will be profoundly missed, and he will never be forgotten.
In lieu of flowers, the family asks that donations be made in Mark’s memory to Special Olympics Wyoming or I-Reach. Your generosity will honor Mark’s loving spirit and his lifelong commitment to supporting and encouraging others. The family appreciates your kindness and support as we celebrate Mark’s life and the lasting impact he made on so many.
An open-house celebration of life will be held on Saturday, October 31 at 10:30 a.m. at The Hangar in Bar Nunn.
Please join us for food, drinks, laughter, and memories as we come together to celebrate Mark’s life, share stories, and remember the incredible person he was. We invite everyone whose life Mark touched to gather with us, honor his memory, and celebrate the love, laughter, and lasting memories he leaves behind.
We look forward to sharing this time together as we remember Mark and the many ways he made our lives better.
Jerry David Sieler:
1944 – 2026
Jerry “Dave” Sieler, affectionately known as Dave, Davey, Dad, Papa, Grandpa, and Great-Grandpa was born on January 17, 1944, in Philip, South Dakota, to Harold and Eda Jane “Maxine” Sieler. Dave was a kind, funny, generous, and big-hearted man who touched the lives of everyone fortunate enough to know him.
Dave spent much of his life enjoying the outdoors, fishing/hunting, or as he liked to say, “shooting the sky full of holes.”
He especially treasured every opportunity to hunt and fish alongside his grandchildren, creating memories they will carry with them for a lifetime. When the weather kept him indoors, Dave could usually be found watching football, cheering on his beloved Green Bay Packers, or enjoying bull riding and the Cowboy Channel. Dave held a variety of jobs before establishing himself as an outstanding dozer operator. He earned the respect of many through his knowledge, experience, and work ethic. It was not uncommon to hear someone say, “I learned a lot from the Ole Man!”
Dave retired in 2008 after 20 years of service at Cordero Rojo Mine. Some of Dave’s most cherished moments were the simple, everyday ones. For more than 20 years, he enjoyed nearly every morning having coffee with his chosen daughter, Victoria “Vicky” Rivera, in Gillette, Wyoming. Their mornings were filled with stories about everything under the sun, plenty of laughter, and friendly football bets. Above all else, Dave was proud of his grandchildren and great-grandchildren. They were his greatest joys of his life. He never missed an opportunity to spend time with them and was always eager to share their accomplishments. When his great-grandchildren began arriving, he could hardly wait to hold each new baby. During his courageous fight with cancer, Dave had one more goal: to live long enough to meet his newest great-granddaughter, who is due to arrive on his birthday, January 17 2027. He told his doctors they had to keep him going until she was born.
Dave is survived by his sons, Jason Sieler and Bryan Sieler, both of Gillette, Wyoming; his chosen daughter, Victoria “Vicky” Rivera of Gillette, Wyoming; and his grandchildren: Kiley Sieler, Taegan Sieler, Steele Burrow, and Radley Sieler, (Jason); Nathan Rivera “Tarzan”, (Vicky); MacKensie Sieler, Bailey Sieler, Aidan Sieler, Andrew Sieler, Finnley Sieler, and Raglan Sieler, (Bryan). He is also survived by his great-grandchildren: Charlotte and a great-granddaughter expected in January, (MacKensie); Haesel, Penelope “Lace”, Harlyn, (Bailey). He is further survived by his brothers: Gary, Terry, and Steve; and his sisters, Dianne and Sally.  Dave will be remembered for his generous heart, quick wit, and the love he gave will live on through the generations of family who were blessed to call him Dad, Papa, Grandpa, and friend.
Forever the proudest Papa, Dave will never forgotten.
A Celebration of Life is planned for Dave on November 28, 2026 at Prairie Sky Venue 1120 Wy-50 Gillette Wyoming, from 11 a.m. to 3 p.m.
Vernon Alton Testerman JR:
1975 – 2026
Vernon Alton Testerman Jr., 50, of Rozet, Wyoming, went to heaven in his sleep, on October 20, 2026, at Monument Health Hospital in Rapid City, South Dakota; his wife and youngest son by his side.
He was born on December 15, 1975,  in York, Pennsylvania, to Vernon Alton and Cynthia Testerman Sr.
Vernon was homeschooled in his high school years, and graduated in 1993. He attended Pensacola Christian College,  where he graduated with a Bachelor’s Degree in Accounting in 1998, and acquired his Masters in accounting from Western Governors University in 2020.
He was mayor of Burns, Wyoming, from 2007 through 2009.
Vernon was married in 2000 through 2019; six children were born to this union. Vernon married Melissa Lane on March 11, 2022, at the Campbell County Courthouse; one daughter was born to this union.
Vernon was a Managerial Accounting Manager for Ambler Metals of Alaska.
He enjoyed camping and fishing with his wife and children and anything involving nature. He loved playing and singing hymns on his guitar. He was also a member of the NRA.
He was a Christian, of the Baptist persuasion, who loved God and his family above everything else. He was loyal, and determined to give his wife and children the best life he could. He did not concern himself with what others thought or said about him, choosing instead to follow God and the Bible. One of his greatest desires was for his children to know and love Jesus Christ as their savior.\
He is survived by his wife, Melissa Testerman; his children: Vernon Alton III, Priscilla, Reagan, Jordan, Melody, Montana, and Odessa all of Rozet, Wyoming; his mother, Cynthia Testerman (Burns, Wyoming); and his siblings, Angela (Rustin) Sundstrom  (Moorefield, Nebraska), Gabriel Testerman (Cheyenne, Wyoming), Anna (Jessie) Tribby (Burns, Wyoming); as well as many nieces, nephews, aunts, uncles, and cousins.
He was preceded in death by his grandparents, Edd and Josephine Testerman and Paul and Nellie Moser; his father, Vernon Alton Testerman Sr., and a beloved baby he and his wife lost before birth.
Funeral services for Vernon Testerman Jr. will be held in the  Burns Town Hall (north gym meeting room) at 315 S Main Street, Burns, WY 82053 on Thursday, October 29 at 2 p.m.
Bobbie Anne Huylar:
1946 – 2026
Bobbie Anne Lee Huylar was born on July 19, 1946, in Washington, D.C., to Edward Robert Lee and Gladys Bedell Wadsworth.
She had three siblings: Robert Lee, Charles Lee, and Pat Mistretta, who are all still living.  Bobbie had fond memories of vacationing at the family cabin on Cobb Island in Maryland.  She remembers catching frogs and snakes and fishing.
Bobbie graduated from Surrattsville Senior High School in Maryland on June 8, 1964.  During high school, she worked at the Clinton dry cleaner’s, where she met her future husband, Kenneth Allan Huylar.  He was serving in the United States Navy.
After graduation on June 20, 1964, they were married at the Clinton, Maryland, Courthouse. After a short time in Maryland, Kenneth left service, and Bobbie and Ken returned to his home state of California.  They lived in Recida, California; Canoga Park, California; Westlake Village, California; and Thousand Oaks, California.
They were blessed with two children, Robert Lee Huylar and Leanne Huylar Bland.  After 20 years of marriage, Bobbie and Ken divorced. In the years that followed, Bobbie went back to school and pursued a career in cosmology.  After her son, Robert, joined the Air Force and was stationed at F.E. Warren AFB, Bobbie moved to Cheyenne to be with him and his family.
She worked for her beloved Hitching Post, where she was a staple at the front desk and was the front desk manager.  She especially loved legislative season and made friends with many of the legislators who came year after year.
After 20 years of being divorced from Kenneth, they reconnected; he came to Cheyenne, and they became a couple again but never remarried.  He was her faithful companion for life.
Bobbie fell in love with the cabin owned by her daughter-in-law’s family in Red Feather Lakes, Colorado.  She spent many days fishing and on the lake as it reminded her of her beloved Cobb Island.
Bobbie had seven grandchildren and two great-grandchildren when she passed.  At the end of her life, she was surrounded and cared for in-home hospice by her son, Robert Lee Huylar and her daughter-in-law, Lynn Storey-Huylar, as it was her wish to die at home.
Bobbie is survived by her companion and ex-husband, Kenneth Allan Huylar; son, Robert Lee Huylar, and wife, Lynn Storey-Huylar; and daughter, Leanne Bland, and her husband, Leland Bland. She was grandmother to Brad, Megan, Ryan, Katie, Valerie, Steven Lee, and Dillon.  She had three great-grandchildren as well.
She will be missed by all.
Diane “Dee” Melander:
1932 – 2026
Diane “Dee” Melander, passed away on October 19, 2026 in Cheyenne, Wyoming at the age of 94. She was born May 30, 1932 in Detroit, Michigan to Theodore and Allah Lutz.
At first glance, Dee was the sweet old lady who was a social butterfly with a zest for life. Spending more than just a few minutes with Dee, one would see Dee was genuine, had a heart of gold, and wished happiness for everyone. She would quickly find a home in the heart of anyone that walked into her life and became part of the family for many. She truly cared about the people who crossed her path and she would find ways to provide encouragement, support, and care by sending little cards, leaving random Post-it notes, sending emails, making phone calls, and reaching out on Facebook.
She also cared deeply about many causes and showed this by donating and volunteering in the community with organizations like Relay for Life and joined the Red Hat Society. She was a faithful Christian and attended Grace United Methodist Church from the time she moved to Cheyenne. In lieu of flowers, Dee has asked that donations be made to Grace United Methodist Church.
She is survived by her kids, grandkids, extended family and will be greatly missed by them, her “bonus” family, and many friends. There will be a celebration of life at Grace United Methodist Church on May 29, 2027. The time is yet to be determined.
Damon G. Shaw:
1993 – 2026
Damon Shaw, born on April 13, 1993, passed away on October 22, 2026.
Damon will be remembered with love by those who knew him. His life, though far too short, held meaning in the connections he made and the memories he leaves behind. The loss of Damon is deeply felt, and he will remain in the hearts of all who cared for him.
May his memory bring comfort and peace to those who mourn him.
The family will present a longer, more in depth obituary at a later date.
