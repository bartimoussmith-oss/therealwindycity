D. Harry Romango
D. Harry Romango:
1941 – 2026
Born twice, died once, nearly died a dozen times.
Words cannot encapsulate the giant that was Harry Romango. He was born on May 4, 1941, in Thermopolis, Wyoming, to Mildred and Paul Romango. He attended elementary school at Hamilton Dome, junior high at Dean Morgan in Casper, and finally Natrona County High School. He spent his working years operating heavy machinery and cranes, ranching, and trucking. He married the love of his life, Dorothy Mae Slane, in 1958. The couple welcomed a daughter and a son shortly thereafter.
His work moved the family throughout Wyoming and Colorado before settling in Cody, Wyoming. He was a faithful servant of Christ and a devout member of the Cody Bible Baptist Church. His love for Christ was reflected in his perseverance, compassion, and willingness to extend a helping hand. Those who knew him well knew he could fix almost everything, feared little, and possessed a wild streak that made just about anything worth trying. When he wasn’t breaking horses, square dancing with his bride, or restoring tractors, he could be found enjoying the company of his loved ones, entertaining them with stories of his younger, wilder days. Among his greatest earthly blessings were his family and friends.
He was preceded in death by his parents, Mildred and Paul Romango; his older brother, David Oscar Romango; and many close relatives. Surviving family members include his wife of sixty-eight years, Dorothy Romango; oldest brother, Paul Duane (Mary) Romango; daughter, Michelle (Mike) Boland; son, Vance (Karen) Romango; granddaughters: Brooke (Charles) Bowkley, Kaitlyn (Arthur) Steward, Courtney Boland, and Amber Faris. His great-grandchildren include Callah, Piper, and Will Bowkley; Pake and Zenna Steward; and Charlotte-Ann Boland. He also leaves behind numerous cousins, nieces, nephews, great-nieces, and great-nephews.
A celebration of life will be held at First Baptist Church, 310 S. 6th Street, Thermopolis, WY 82443, on November 6 at 11 a.m., followed by a short graveside committal at Monument Hill Cemetery and a reception at First Baptist Church Fellowship Hall.
In lieu of flowers, please make a donation to Samaritan’s Purse International Relief at
samaritanspurse.org
. Those wishing to honor Harry’s memory are encouraged to carry forward his greatest legacy: sharing the message of Christ’s sacrifice through faith, joy, kindness, and service to others.
