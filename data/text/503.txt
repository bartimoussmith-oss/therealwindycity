Men stopped near Colorado border, arrested after K-9 reportedly finds large amount of drugs

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Two men are facing felony drug charges after a routine traffic stop on Interstate 25 yielded 168 grams of methamphetamine and smaller amounts of fentanyl and heroin, which sheriff deputies suspect were intended for sale.
Laramie County Sheriff’s Office deputies recommended that Dustin Brummet and Kenneth Callaway — born in 1980 and 1982, respectively — be charged with possession of methamphetamine, possession of fentanyl, possession of heroin, possession of methamphetamine with intent to deliver and conspiracy to manufacture and deliver meth. The three possession charges are punishable with up to 10 years of prison each, while the two trafficking charges each carry maximum potential penalties of 20 years.
Both Brummett and Callaway are presumed innocent until pleading or being found guilty.
Shortly before 9 p.m. Nov. 7, deputies reported in the pair’s criminal affidavits, Brummett and Callaway were pulled over on Interstate 25 near High Plains Road south of Cheyenne. Deputies reportedly observed the truck, driven by Brummett, going over the road’s fog line and rumble strips. Its license plate was also obscured.
While completing paperwork during the stop, an LCSO drug detection K-9 was deployed for an open-air sniff test of the vehicle. After receiving a positive indication, officers searched the vehicle and found 168 grams of meth, 4.5 grams of heroin and 5 grams of fentanyl in a hidden compartment under the passenger-side floorboard. On the driver’s side, deputies also reportedly found tinfoil with burnt drug residue.
Having come from Denver with a volume of drugs that couldn’t reasonably be used by one person, deputies concluded that the two were planning on distributing the substances and tacked on trafficking charges.
