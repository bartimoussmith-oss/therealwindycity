The continuing legacy of Cheyenne’s Gunslingers: Bringing western gunfights to life
by
Halle Sheppard
September 4, 2026

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Cheyenne is a city that is immensely proud of its western heritage, and perhaps nothing indicates this spirit better than the performances put on by the Gunslingers, an enduring tradition spanning over 40 years.
A volunteer, nonprofit troupe with a small-town charm reminiscent of shows like “The Gilmore Girls,” the Gunslingers perform free Old West gunfight reenactments, comedies and melodramas in downtown Cheyenne during the summer months surrounding Cheyenne Frontier Days.
Gunslinger shootout signs beckon in visitors (Halle Sheppard, Cap City News).
The troupe performs almost every day during the June and July months, but during CFD itself, it performs twice a day.
The show takes place near downtown, and features a number of miniature frontier-style houses, jails, businesses, as well as a graveyard labeled “Boothill” — a reference to Dodge City rather than the Johnny Cash song.
Boothill graveyard is one of the many western decorations that make up the Gunslingers’ performance areas (Halle Sheppard, Cap City News).
Roger, also known as Lucky by his fellow performers, explained that the troupe was established in summer 1983 by businessmen to both entertain visitors and encourage them to stay in the downtown area after the parades and pancake breakfasts of CFD.
“They did a gunfight at noon to try to keep people here during the lunch hour, and then they turned around and did another gunfight at 6 to try and keep people down here for dinner” rather than having them run off to the exciting CFD fairgrounds, Lucky explained.
The shows later extended to the summer months beyond CFD; with the opening of a new mall, the businesses needed an attraction to keep tourists in the downtown area.
“That’s our main mission,” said Lucky, “to keep people downtown.”
The Gunslingers have long been a fixture of downtown Cheyenne and its western heritage (Halle Sheppard, Cap City News).
Originally on Kerry Avenue, the Gunslingers were relocated in 2009 to Gunslinger Square, which, while slightly farther from downtown, also changed the demographic from inebriated adults leaving the town bar to larger crowds of families and children.
“They love the gunfire,” Lucky said, referring to the middle-grade children that make up their audience. With the location change, the Gunslingers had to change their script to become more family friendly; “then, it just kept getting bigger and bigger.”
Supported by local businesses like Boot Barn, the Gunslingers also sell bullet necklaces, keychains, posters and even yearly belt buckles to support their performances, alongside donations and sponsorships.
The 2026 Gunslingers poster. The Gunslingers sell posters that change yearly. (Halle Sheppard, Cap City News).
As for its use of firearms, the troupe prides itself on a multi-decade safety record without a single firearm-related accident, and each show opens with a strict safety inspection.
The performers use real firearms, and while they only shoot blanks, those can still pack a punch at close range, as demonstrated on unfortunate soda cans.
The Gunslingers also advocate for gun safety, distributing gun safety and storage education pamphlets — provided by the National Shooting Sports Foundation — to the audience.
Pamela, a gunslinger from Cheyenne who also goes by the name of Trixie, has been performing and volunteering with the Gunslingers for 12 years and can speak to their dedication.
“We try not to do it in the rain, but we’ll still do it if it is raining,” she explained under an awning after a show that finished in a downpour.
When asked why she and the other Gunslingers keep coming out to perform, rain or shine, Trixie explained, “It’s just the love of the show.”
“We try to keep everything Western, you know; that’s kind of what Wyoming’s about,” Trixie said.
