Cheyenne Halloween Guide 2026: Family-friendly Halloween events taking over the capital city
by
Garrett Grochowski
October 28, 2026
October 29, 2026

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — Halloween is just around the corner, and there is no shortage of fun and seasonal events in Cheyenne for the whole family to enjoy. For those hoping to get more mileage out of their costume, look no further. See below Cap City News’s Halloween in Cheyenne events guide.
This guide is not comprehensive and may exclude certain events. See Cap City News’s events calendar
here
for more information about all of the events taking place in Cheyenne over the course of the year.
All events are free to attend unless stated otherwise.
Día de los Muertos at the Cheyenne Botanic Gardens: Oct. 28 – Nov. 7
Día de los Muertos, Spanish for “Day of the Dead,” is the Mexican multi-day celebration honoring deceased relatives. The holiday is typically celebrated in the two days immediately following Halloween on Oct. 31, but is sometimes extended to as many as five days depending on where it is celebrated. In Cheyenne, the Cheyenne Botanic Gardens is opting to make the celebration last as long as possible.
“Celebrate those that have passed on and learn about the culture, traditions and history of the Day of the Dead,” the garden said on
Facebook
.
Just like the Day of the Dead itself, the botanic garden is using its event to put a positive and beautiful spin on dying and the afterlife.
In partnership with a committee of community members, the Cheyenne Botanic Gardens, Laramie County Public Library, Wyoming State Museum and Cheyenne Depot Museum are hosting community celebrations to share the significance of this holiday and provide welcoming spaces for people to celebrate their culture.
“Everyone is welcome to enjoy these community celebrations and open their eyes to cultures and traditions that they might have never known about,” the garden said.
See the garden’s Day of the Dead event schedule below:
Oct. 28-29: Take a self-guided tour and add a butterfly in memory of a loved one to the memorial wall.
Oct. 29: A light to remember family class. Make a luminary bag for loved one who has passed. Registration is required. See
botanic.org
for all classes.
Oct. 30–31: Take a self-guided tour and add a butterfly in memory of a loved one to the memorial wall.
Oct. 31, 1 p.m.: Nature’s Paintbrush kids class. Kids will make their own paintbrush from a corn husk and paint a picture to take home. Drop-in class.
Nov. 3: Take a self-guided tour and add a butterfly in memory of a loved one to the memorial wall. Memory Candles adult class: Create and decorate a special candle to honor and remember a loved one. Your candle can become a meaningful addition to your family’s ofrenda. Registration is required. See
botanic.org
for all classes.
Nov. 4–6: Take a self-guided tour and add a butterfly in memory of a loved one to the memorial wall.
Nov. 6: Story time with CHISPA at the Paul Smith Children’s Village.
Nov. 7: Community Event at the garden from 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. Bring in items to add to the community ofrenda. Attendees are asked to only bring copies of photos as the garden is not responsible for misplaced originals. Take in the beautiful flower display and learn about the history of the celebration. Enjoy Dance performance by Mosaíco Folklórico dance group at 11 a.m. and by Las Angelitas Unidas dance group at 1:30 p.m. Get delicious treats and food from local food vendor Kiss My Asada Tacos & Desserts. Take-home kids activity kits will be given out. Make you own maracas in the Gathering Rooms while supplies last. Take home a marigold grown at the Cheyenne Botanic Gardens while supplies last.
See the Cheyenne Botanic Gardens’
Facebook event posting
for more information.
Cheyenne Police Department Trunk or Treat: Oct. 29
Have your costumes ready by Oct. 29, when the Cheyenne Police Department is planning to host Trunk-or-Treat at the Cheyenne Public Safety Center, 415 W. 18th St., from 5 to 7 p.m.
Residents are invited to the department to talk to officers, listen to tunes DJ’d by one of the department’s officers, enjoy food and drinks from trucks and, most importantly, participate in trunk or treat. Trunk or treat is a safe alternative to trick-or-treating where kids collect candy from decorated car trunks parked together instead of going door-to-door.
Kids also have the opportunity to meet specialty units like the SWAT team, K-9 Unit and Motor Unit.
Pumpkins and Providers Trick or Treat at College Park Business Plaza: Oct. 29
Join
College Park Business Plaza off College Drive in east Cheyenne
for a “Pumpkins and Providers” trick-or-treat event.
Crafts, balloons, candy and community are all up for grabs at this free-to-attend event.
See the events posting
here
for more information.
Haunted Happy Hour with Mike Kassel at the CFD Old West Museum: Oct. 29
On Oct. 29 at 5:30 p.m., CFD Old West Museum Curator Mike Kassel will share eerie stories and strange history from a Wyoming museum at a Halloween happy hour — a seasonal evening combining local lore with drinks.
Kassel will share some of the darker, stranger and downright spooky stories from Cheyenne’s past. Hear tales of local legends, mysterious happenings and the history that might just give you chills.
People can RSVP
here
.
Health Works Trunk or Treat: Oct. 29
“Come dressed in your favorite costume and get a jumpstart — or jumpscare — filling your candy bucket with a spook-tacular evening of treats, fun, and festive trunk decorations,” Healthworks said.
According to
Healthworks
, this event takes place from 5 to 7 p.m. at
its building, 506 W. 17th St. in downtown Cheyenne
.
Nightmare on 17th Street: Oct. 29–31
Nightmare on 17th Street
is the premier haunted house experience in Cheyenne. Don’t want to face your fears on an empty stomach? Cheyenne residents can bundle the experience with a meal at the Metropolitan Downtown.
The deal: Receive one free Haunted House ticket for that night with every entrée purchased at the restaurant. Tickets can be claimed by reserving a table at the Metropolitan Downtown for any event date and adding promo code “Haunted Dinner” in the reservation notes.
“Enjoy your meal and get ready to get spooked,” the restaurant said. “Reserve your spot now — if you dare!”
State of Wyoming Trick-or-Treat Street: Oct. 30
Bring your little ghosts, goblins, superheroes and princesses for an afternoon of Halloween fun at the State of Wyoming buildings in downtown Cheyenne. On Oct. 30 from 1 to 5 p.m., Wyoming state agencies are teaming up to host a free, family-friendly trick-or-treat event across multiple Capitol Complex locations. Costumes are encouraged, treats will be plentiful and there’s fun waiting around every corner. Gather your family and enjoy a spooky afternoon exploring participating state offices while collecting candy along the way.
Participating locations include the Wyoming State Capitol, Herschler Building (West and East), Pioneer Building (first floor), Idelman Mansion, Kendrick Building, Barrett Building, Retirement System, DEQ Building and Department of Audit (9 a.m. to noon only).
Cheyenne Little Theatre Players – Stephen King’s ‘Misery’: Oct. 30 – Nov. 15
For $19–$29, see a stage adaptation of Stephen King’s thriller “Misery,” the story of a writer held captive by his self-proclaimed number-one fan.
Stephen King’s “Misery” will be showing at the
Mary Godfrey Playhouse
from Friday, Oct. 30 through Sunday, Nov. 15th. Tickets are on sale now and can be purchased
here
.
“This psychological thriller based on Stephen King’s novel ‘Misery’ follows successful romance novelist Paul Sheldon, who is rescued from a car crash and wakes up captive in a secluded home owned by his self-identified ‘number one fan,’ Annie Wilkes,” the theater said of the production. “While Paul is convalescing, Annie reads the manuscript of his latest book and becomes enraged when she discovers the author has killed off her favorite character, Misery Chastain. Annie forces Paul to write a new Misery novel and he quickly realizes Annie has no intention of letting him go. The irate Annie has Paul writing as if his life depends on it — and it does.”
Dark Victorian Tales at the Laramie County Library: Oct. 30
Step into the Laramie County Library’s “parlor” for an evening of haunting historical literary entertainment presented by The Denver Museum of Miniatures, Dolls & Toys. Costumes are encouraged but not required.
This event runs from 5 to 7 p.m. See the
Visit Cheyenne website
for more information.
Autumn Pumpkin Teen Jewelry Making Class at the Cheyenne Children’s Museum: Oct. 30
Join teen artist Grace Lyles for a hands-on workshop where you’ll learn to work with wire, jewelry-making tools and more. No experience is needed; just bring a willingness to try something new. You’ll leave with your very own handmade earrings.
This 4–6 p.m. event costs $21. Find more information
here
.
Trick or Treat Street at Cheyenne South High School: Oct. 30
Wear your costume and trick or treat at Cheyenne South High School Friday afternoon from 3 to 5 p.m.
Trunk or Treat and Community Coat Drive with Laramie County Fire District #1: Oct. 30
Candy, games, vendors and Halloween fun at the
Town and Country Liquor Store
parking lot from 5 to 8 p.m. New or gently used coat donations are welcome.
Wyoming Supreme Court Haunted House and Halloween Carnival: Oct. 30
The Wyoming Supreme Court is inviting the public to its haunted house and Halloween carnival on Friday, Oct. 30 from 3 to 5 p.m. at the
Supreme Court building in Cheyenne
.
Games, treats and prizes will all be available for a scary good time for all ages. Costumes are welcome and encouraged.
Hauntings & History Tours with the Cheyenne Street Railway: Oct. 30–31
Climb aboard the Cheyenne Street Railway for a journey into Cheyenne’s darker side. Hear tales of ghostly encounters, mysterious happenings and the people and places that have left their mark on the Magic City. As darkness settles over downtown, you’ll discover some of the chilling stories hiding behind Cheyenne’s historic streets.
Tours depart from the Cheyenne Depot Plaza at 6 and 8 p.m. The cost of admittance is $25 for adults and $15 for children. Learn more
here
.
Flea Market Trick or Treat: Oct. 31
Come hang out with the
Christine and Co. Flea Market
on Oct. 31 for a fun trick-or-treating walk through the flea market! Bring your little ghouls, superheroes and princesses for some candy, the market said. They’ll be ready to hand out treats no matter what the weather’s like and will also have a free photo booth!
This event is free to attend from 11 a.m. to 1 p.m. Children 16 and under must be accompanied by an adult.
Halloween Toddler Party: Oct. 31
“Big kids shouldn’t have all the Halloween fun,” local yoga studio GROW Movement and Mindfulness said. “This year, bring your littlest monsters over to GROW for a special Halloween party just for them.”
The event will have glow sticks, glow-in-the-dark tattoos and spooky music. Families can dance together, play fun games, create a simple keepsake craft and take Halloween pictures at their photo booth.
“We’re hosting 45-minute parties throughout the afternoon, and space is limited so that we don’t become overcrowded.
We currently have space available to book at 11 a.m. and 12 p.m. if both times fill, we’ll be adding more
,” the studio said.
While this event is geared toward families and children under 4, older siblings are welcome.
Fallin’ for Jesus Trunk or Treat: Oct. 31
The
Ridge Road Calvary Chapel Cheyenne
is hosting a “Fallin’ for Jesus” trunk or treat from 4 to 6:30 p.m. Halloween day.
“Come out and enjoy an evening of fall fun! Enjoy trunk-or-treating, games, bounce houses, food trucks and a hay ride! The whole night is free! Please invite your family and friends and join us,” the chapel said.
Cheyenne Cruise Nights’ Season Finale Trunk or Treat: Oct. 31
Saturday, Oct. 31st is the last Cruise Night of the 2026 season, so they’re going out with a bang. The event will host a trunk or treat event sponsored by the Cheyenne Classic Chevy Club from 5-9 p.m. at the State Lot at
24th Street and Central Avenue
.
They will also be accepting donations for Toys for Tots of Laramie County. The Ranch Eats food truck will be present.
“Bring the kids for safe trick-or-treating, bring an unwrapped toy to donate for kids this Christmas, and bring your car if you want to deck out your trunk,” Cruise Nights said on
Facebook
.
Schrader, Aragon, and Jacoby Funeral Home Nonprofit Trunk or Treat: Oct. 31
Join in the trunk-or-treating fun at the
Schrader, Aragon and Jacoby Funeral Home
on Oct. 31 from 2 to 5 p.m. Attendees can participate in trick-or-treating, face painting, balloons and games.
Visitors can also help vote on the best-decorated trunk and support local nonprofits.
Trunk or Treat Community Event Presented by F.E. Warren AFB ALS Class 27-A: Oct. 31
Another trunk-or-treat event will take place in south Cheyenne at
206 Van Lennen Ave.
, hosted by F.E. Warren Air Force Base’s ALS Class 27-A.
“We’ll have candy and pumpkins,” the class said. “Bring the family, wear your costumes and come enjoy a little Halloween magic.”
The event listing on the
Visit Cheyenne website
did not give a time for the event.
