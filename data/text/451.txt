See Freedom Elementary students thank veterans, service members in song at Veterans Day performance

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — The meaning of Veterans Day is not lost on the youth of Cheyenne, especially at Freedom Elementary. The Eagles, from kindergarten to fifth grade, made sure to let Cheyenne’s veterans and service members — many of whom are their own parents at F.E. Warren Air Force Base — know how much they appreciate them through song.
The school’s students gathered in Cheyenne Central High School’s auditorium Wednesday morning. Their parents filed into the room and listened to their kids sing each military branch’s anthem and other songs dedicated to veterans and the nation’s thankfulness for their service.
They also got to hear from service members themselves. F.E. Warren 90th Missile Wing Commander Colonel Terrence Holmes and Chief Master Sergeant Cherise Mosley were there to tell the young students what it actually means to serve — what service members have to sacrifice for the common good.
“Those men and women that have come before us to create a great nation, they did so sometimes while being asked, but most of the time without being asked,” Holmes told the students. “So, take this opportunity to reflect and do something for someone else today and let them know how much they’re loved and appreciated.”
An act of service could, for the young students, be as simple as cleaning their room. That got a few head shakes out of the crowd.
However, both Holmes and Mosley said, it is a civilian’s duty to show appreciation. Service members have to miss a lot — birthdays, holidays and all the other special moments in life. Mosley asked the kids to reflect on what they can do to help make the world a better and safer place.
“You can serve right now by practicing those core values, by helping your teacher out, helping your classmates out in school — if somebody maybe is being bullied, standing up for them when they can’t stand up for themselves. It’s doing your homework before you’re asked or putting a little more effort into it. Walking the dog, helping out with your siblings, taking care of mom and dad,” Mosley said. “And saying thank you, very much like you did here today.”
