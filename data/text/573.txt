INSET: Aili Wang, 32. OUTSET: The University of Wyoming Agriculture Building (Garrett Grochowski, Cap City News)
LARAMIE, Wyo. — Aili Wang,
a University of Wyoming graduate student arrested earlier this fall for reportedly attempting to poison and kill a fellow student researcher
, entered a “not guilty” plea in her Albany County District Court arraignment Monday. Her trial date has been set for May 3–5.
Wang faces two criminal charges, second-degree attempted murder and aggravated assault and battery. Her attempted murder charge, if she is convicted, would put her in prison for 20 years to life. Her aggravated assault and battery charge could add an additional 10 years.
In her arraignment, assisted by a remote translator, she pleaded not guilty. She is presumed innocent until found otherwise in court.
Albany County District Court Judge Misha Westby also assigned Wang a trial date. Her case will be heard May 3–5.
Wang will likely not be free to travel around Laramie in the meantime. Her bond, set at $1.5 million, was not reduced. Arguing that her actions leading up to her arrest demonstrated a considerable flight risk, and considering that alleged victims still roamed in Laramie, the State of Wyoming successfully convinced Westby to retain Wang’s current bond. The state also argued that, by being trespassed from the University of Wyoming, Wang no longer holds any ties to the Laramie community.
Her bond was also left as is when
she attended her preliminary hearing in Albany County Circuit Court earlier this month
.
