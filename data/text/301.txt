Cheyenne Finance Committee to talk Reed Avenue Rail Corridor project expansion

A design concept depicting the Reed Avenue Corridor. (Photo courtesy of the City of Cheyenne)
CHEYENNE, Wyo. — The Cheyenne Finance Committee is set to meet today. On the committee’s short agenda is a plan to increase the contract amount of the Reed Avenue Rail Corridor Design Project by more than $1 million, bringing the total project cost to $2,487,566.
The Finance Committee meets biweekly to discuss financial matters that will go back to the City Council for consideration. While the committee cannot approve a resolution or ordinance, a proposal needs to get the committee’s support to advance.
Tuesday, the committee meets at noon at the Council Chambers of the Municipal Building, 2101 O’Neil Ave. The public can also access the meeting remotely
via Zoom
or telephone.
The committee members will discuss expanding the Reed Avenue Rail Corridor Design Project, a city project to comprehensively restore and reimagine the Reed Avenue area in Cheyenne’s West Edge neighborhood. The project would convert the area into a public area fit to serve as a gathering and event space that celebrates Cheyenne’s rail history.
The railroad plays a significant role in the area’s vision.
Earlier this summer, the city placed historic rail cars along 15th Street nearby.
Learn more about what the project will include
here
.
Now, the city is considering expanding the project, requiring another $1,166,647 to be set aside for that purpose. It would also extend the completion date for the project to April 30, 2028.
If the committee supports the expansion and the Cheyenne City Council approves it next week, the project would grow to include street improvements to Snyder and Bent Avenues as they follow Reed and the design of a parking lot and pedestrian connectivity between 20th and 21st streets.
It would also pay for more coordination between the city and the BNSF Railway to enable development in the project area, which follows an old railroad track.
Funding for the new costs would consist of $466,620 from the Fifth Penny Tax, $422,527 from the Sixth Penny Tax and $277,500 from FRA Grant funds.
In other action, the committee will discuss the purchase of new tactical armor for the Cheyenne Police Department SWAT team for $142,800. Additionally, the members will discuss a potential funding increase and contract extension for the city’s agreement with the National Forest Foundation for capacity building and feasibility studies for forest restoration in the Medicine Bow National Forest.
