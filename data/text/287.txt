LCSD1 to vote on new charter with PODER Academy Monday night

CHEYENNE, Wyo. — The Laramie County School District 1 Board of Trustees will vote tonight to reapprove its charter with PODER Academy, accepting five-year contracts with the PODER Academy elementary and secondary schools to provide them with district services.
The LCSD1 board meeting begins at 6 p.m. and will take place at Cheyenne’s historic Storey Gymnasium. Interested residents can also attend virtually via
Zoom
. Prior to that, however, the district will have a work session, starting at 5 p.m., that it will use to hear from district staff on the school assignment boundary for
Jessup Elementary School
and proposed early retirement incentives.
Trustees will consider renewing their five-year charters with PODER Academy Elementary School and PODER Academy Secondary School, two local charter schools operating in Cheyenne. By approving their charter, the district would agree to provide important operational services like school meals services, coordinated transportation and the Infinite Campus student information system.
In return, the district gets a high-performing public educational partner that offers unique public education choices to district parents. According to PODER COO Nick Vaughn, PODER’s two schools boast some of the best statewide test results in Wyoming. PODER Elementary, he said to the district in an Oct. 19 information session, consistently scores 90%–100% proficiency in state WY-TOPP ELA and math tests, while PODER Secondary recently received state recognition for being the highest-performing high school program in Wyoming for WY-TOPP ELA, math and science.
Both schools serve an economically disadvantaged student population and, due to their unique class structures, emphasize college readiness in their curricula. In PODER Secondary, also known as P.A.S.S., accelerated classes early on give students time to take classes for college credit in their junior and senior years so they have an early advantage when they graduate, Vaughn said.
The district will also vote to purchase seven school buses for an amount not to exceed $1,452,095 — declaring seven aging buses as surplus — and to approve a memorandum of understanding with LCCC for concurrent and dual enrollment programming for a maximum of $2,500,000.
Trustees will hear from Executive Director of Student Services Jeff Lewis for an update on district assessment data as well.
Finally, students and staff recently recognized by the district and the state for their achievements will also be celebrated in person by trustees. For more information on any of these topics, see the district’s Nov. 2 meeting agenda below:
