-<p class="wp-block-paragraph">CHEYENNE, Wyo. — During a panel featuring Wyoming Governor Mark Gordon, Laramie County Community College President Joe Schaffer and company Senior Vice President and Head of Construction Walker Kimball, global engineering company Bechtel announced a $1 million investment into Wyoming community colleges, as well as an education campaign, to support skilled trades education.</p>
-
-
-
-<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The workforce landscape of Wyoming is changing, Schaffer said at an LCCC panel exploring the expansion of skilled trades jobs coming to Wyoming students Wednesday. As large industrial projects continue moving into Wyoming, more students, perhaps influenced by their college debt-ridden Gen X or Millennial parents, are looking beyond the traditional college route towards their future.</p>
-
-
-
-<p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;There&#8217;s a shifting sentiment towards &#8216;What I should do after high school.&#8217; What we&#8217;re seeing is the public being more concerned about the value of postsecondary education, the ROI, and the affordability of it, which has started to shift the perspective on trades — which I think is a positive thing,&#8221; Schaffer said. &#8220;The challenge that we have right now is that we don&#8217;t have enough capacity to introduce young people to it in the high schools.&#8221;</p>
-
-
-
-<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Those students, if they saw the trades as a potential next step for them, might consider a career with Bechtel. <a href="https://www.bechtel.com/">The global engineering company</a> has its hands in megaprojects all over the world, from airport metro systems and copper mines in South America, Fukushima disaster cleanup in Japan to the <a href="https://www.bechtel.com/projects/natrium-demonstration-project/">Natrium Project in Wyoming</a>.</p>
-
-
-
-<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Representatives traveled to Cheyenne Wednesday to give insight to and gain insight from local leaders on how the public and private sectors can work together to foster a greater interest in trades from high school and college students. The company also announced that, to help support that goal, it would donate a combined $1 million between LCCC&#8217;s and Western Wyoming College&#8217;s trades education programs and launch Build For More, the statewide education campaign to raise awareness for the trades.</p>
+<p class="wp-block-paragraph">CHEYENNE, Wyo. — During a panel featuring Wyoming Gov. Mark Gordon, Laramie County Community College President Joe Schaffer and company Senior Vice President and Head of Construction Walker Kimball, global engineering company Bechtel announced a $1 million investment into Wyoming community colleges, as well as an education campaign, to support skilled trades education.</p>
+
+
+
+<p class="wp-block-paragraph">The workforce landscape of Wyoming is changing, Schaffer said at an LCCC panel exploring the expansion of skilled trades jobs coming to Wyoming students Wednesday. As large industrial projects continue moving into Wyoming, more students, perhaps influenced by their college debt-ridden Gen X or Millennial parents, are looking beyond the traditional college route toward their future.</p>
+
+
+
+<p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;There&#8217;s a shifting sentiment towards &#8216;What I should do after high school?&#8217; What we&#8217;re seeing is the public being more concerned about the value of postsecondary education, the ROI and the affordability of it, which has started to shift the perspective on trades — which I think is a positive thing,&#8221; Schaffer said. &#8220;The challenge that we have right now is that we don&#8217;t have enough capacity to introduce young people to it in the high schools.&#8221;</p>
+
+
+
+<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Those students, if they saw the trades as a potential next step for them, might consider a career with Bechtel. <a href="https://www.bechtel.com/">The global engineering company</a> has its hands in megaprojects all over the world, from airport metro systems and copper mines in South America to Fukushima disaster cleanup in Japan to the <a href="https://www.bechtel.com/projects/natrium-demonstration-project/">Natrium Project in Wyoming</a>.</p>
+
+
+
+<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Representatives traveled to Cheyenne on Wednesday to give insight to, and gain insight from, local leaders on how the public and private sectors can work together to foster a greater interest in trades from high school and college students. The company also announced that, to help support that goal, it would donate a combined $1 million between LCCC&#8217;s and Western Wyoming College&#8217;s trades education programs and launch Build For More, the statewide education campaign to raise awareness for the trades.</p>
