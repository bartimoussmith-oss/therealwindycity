## THE BIG ONE — Finding 1: The 45-day number was in their own code the whole time — for SWINE

The OLD General Agriculture definition (strikethrough text) excluded concentrated feeding operations **"where swine are confined, fed and maintained for 45 consecutive days or more in any 12-month period."**

The NEW standalone definition — "Concentrated Feeding Operations and Slaughterhouses" — applies the identical threshold to **ALL livestock**: *"livestock are confined, fed and maintained for 45 consecutive days or more in any 12-month period."*

**Against the Aug. 21 record:** staff answered the origin question (23:42–25:05) with *"just what we found through our different searches of different codes. It seemed to be a standard number."* The draft's own markup shows the number was not found in other cities' codes — it **already existed in Cheyenne's own UDC, where it applied only to swine**, and the draft silently generalizes it to every kind of livestock. The work-session answer was at minimum incomplete; the change is a substantive expansion of the CFO definition presented as a cleanup.

**Use:** This is the strongest single fact in the document — verifiable in the city's own markup, no interpretation needed. It reframes the package's most contested number from "industry standard we found" to "our own swine rule, extended to your animals without saying so." Mic line: *"Staff told us the 45 days came from searches of other codes. Your own draft shows it came from your own code — where it only ever applied to pigs."*

## Finding 2: The discretion standard is now IN WRITING, unchanged

§2.2.3.b.5(c), site-plan exemption list: **"Agricultural uses which are not expected to have significant customer impacts, including Nurseries, Landscaping (if no on-site sales are proposed); Gardens and Homemade Foods; and General Agriculture."**

On Aug. 21 (27:58–31:02) this standard was described on the record as "subjective," "based on the determination of the director at that time," and confirmed as "solely at your discretion," with Councilwoman Aldrich responding: "I find that really problematic." **The draft adopts the subjective standard verbatim and adds no objective criteria, no threshold, no appeal path.** The exemption now expressly covers General Agriculture — broadened, still ungated. The Aldrich problem was identified at the work session and the draft does not touch it.

## Finding 3: The "producer bookmark" is visible in the draft — exactly as described

Gardens and Homemade Foods = *"Any property producing food for sale whose usage meets the definition of a 'producer' according to state statute 11-49-102"* — and the draft carries a margin note: **"For this draft (not to be included in the ordinance)"** quoting the statute in full ($250,000 gross revenue / 250,000 products annually). This is the Aug. 21 "we reference the state statute we don't spell it out… keep the bookmark there" exchange, confirmed in the text. Net effect: the permissive tier is defined by reference to a state law the ordinance won't print — any future state amendment silently rewrites Cheyenne's most permissive ag tier.

## Finding 4: The package is, on net, MORE permissive than the work session implied — and that's the strongest position available

- **Gardens and Homemade Foods: "P" (permitted by right) in ALL 21 zoning districts** (Table 5-1, both panels). The old "Limited Agriculture" category (≤10 acres definition) is deleted and replaced by the state-threshold definition.
- **Home occupation exemption** (§5.7.2.k.3): Gardens/Homemade Foods exempt from home-occupation approval **if no defined business entity and no on-site sales**; either one triggers approval (not prohibition).
- **General Agriculture gains** "small-scale, intermittent harvesting and processing of livestock raised primarily on the premises" — on-farm processing of your own animals, permitted by right in AG.
- **Personal slaughter carve-out:** CFO/Slaughterhouse definition excludes "customary, non-commercial animal harvest as an accessory use to a primary residential or standard agricultural operation."
- **The grazing protection is real and permanent** — §6.08.020.F.7: exempting **"areas where agriculture or grazing was lawful within the year prior to annexation or change in zoning."** The WY Fresh pre-annexation-use protection, in the text, no sunset.

**Strategic re-read:** the correct posture for the comment and the mic is **support with named fixes** — "I support this package; here are the four lines that need objective criteria before introduction." That position is unattackable and forces engagement with the specifics.

## Finding 5: The hard edges that remain

1. **CFO/Slaughterhouses: "S" (special exception) only in AG, LI, HI, BP, AD — banned outright in AR and every residential district.** County-pocket parcels zoned AR (most rural-residential land) can never host a mid-size feeding operation; AG parcels need governing-body approval. This is where the 45-day generalization (Finding 1) bites: it defines what triggers "S" — and in AR there is no "S" to trigger.
2. **Two Director-discretion gates, same structure:** the site-plan exemption (Finding 2) and the new gravel-surfacing exception (§6.2.8.c: "subject to approval by the Director. … The Director may not approve gravel surfacing if deemed inconsistent with the development standards or character of the surrounding area" — "character of the surrounding area" is a second subjective standard, word-for-word ungated).
3. **New arbitrary numbers without published analysis:** 45 consecutive days (Finding 1) and **55 times per year** for "low-usage or overflow parking" — same genre, no derivation offered for either.
4. **Agritourism** new use (pumpkin patches, corn mazes, petting zoos): P in AG/AR/RR, C in LR/MR/NR-1/CB — genuinely new economic permission.
5. **Farmers markets / Agriculture Product Sales:** new cap — "less than 2,000 square feet of gross leasable area"; sales from trucks/trailers expressly allowed. P in AG, AR, NB, CB, CBD, MUR, MUB, BP, SS.

## Finding 6: The city's own summary ties the package to the pocket fight

Top of document: **"During the County Pocket Annexation process many people had questions about the rules for farms and agriculture… City staff took these questions and found some old rules that needed to be updated."** The city's own plain-language summary states the package originated in the annexation fight — the questions were the public's, the review was reactive, and the old rules were admittedly out of alignment the whole time the county pockets lived under them.

## Finding 7: The electric-fence change (§9.12.080)

Existing night-only/12-volt goat-fence rule retained; NEW subsection B allows electric fencing for livestock containment **outside public ROW/sidewalks/trails, with warning signs every ≤100 feet**, maintained per "standard agricultural practices." A real legalization with reasonable conditions.

## THE FOUR FIXES (the ask, before introduction — 2nd September meeting)
1. **45-day trigger:** disclose the swine origin in the staff report, publish any comparative analysis, or restore "swine" / adopt a Wyoming-context threshold. (Finding 1)
2. **"Significant customer impacts":** written measurable criteria + appeal path. (Finding 2 — this was Aldrich's own flagged problem; she owns the follow-up)
3. **"Character of the surrounding area"** in the gravel exception: same treatment — objective standard or administrative adjustment with published criteria. (Finding 5.2)
4. **Publish the comments:** all comments received before introduction enter the introduction packet, as stated Aug. 21 — and BOPU's written position on the slaughter/processing distinction (Emmons' question) before introduction.
