## V-1. First Amendment — Viewpoint Discrimination in the Public-Comment Forum
**U.S. Const. amend. I** *(incorporated against municipalities via amend. XIV)*

**Rule.** A city that opens a comment period — live or Zoom — is bound by the designated-forum rules of *Rosenberger v. Rector of Univ. of Va.*, 517 U.S. 819 (1995): restrictions may be reasonable and viewpoint-neutral, never viewpoint-based. **Controlling circuit authority:** *Make Salt Lake v. Salt Lake City Corp.*, 875 F.3d 1312 (10th Cir. 2017) [C — Vol. VI verification] — officials may not silence a comment period speaker because of hostility to his viewpoint; this is the case Miller never cites and the one that governs him. Forum status is fixed by objective characteristics, not the government's label: *First Unitarian Church v. Salt Lake City Corp.*, 308 F.3d 1114 (10th Cir. 2002) [C].

**Evidence of viewpoint-based treatment [R unless noted]:**
1. The dais slur — a councilwoman's "ChatGPT robots" remark directed at commenters of Miller's cohort; replayed into the 16K-view reel [R; canon §5/§5a].
2. **May 26, 2026:** one-item comment restriction on the fast-tracked rezoning while the moratorium item remained pending; the mayor's "chat GBT" exchange [R]; the pre-moratorium gag item surfaced in press only as CSD's "Before the Discussion" segment [P-10].
3. **Apr. 27, 2026 GB:** three separate sustained points of order (Moody/White; Moody/Laybourn; multiple members) cutting Miller's comments as "not germane," while other commenters on the same items completed their turns — enforcement asymmetry is the classic *Make Salt Lake* fact pattern [R — Granicus record of proceedings, Apr. 27, 2026].
4. **Jul. 27, 2026:** podium threat from a councilman after Miller's turn — "So, let's go" [R].
5. Councilwoman Sigliano's floor statement "I am not an AI chatbot" [R] — cumulative animus evidence.
6. The press's own neutral descriptor — "a frequent critic of the city's planning process" [P-13] — establishes he was *known and identifiable* as the critic viewpoint when treated.

**City's defense (register-grade candor):** germaneness and three-minute rules are lawful time/place/manner content-neutral restrictions; the city will argue even-handed enforcement; the residue-of-hostility evidence (bot slurs) is speech by officials, which *Snyder*/*Garcetti* line cases treat as government speech. The count survives on the *pattern* (items 2–4), not the insults.

**Grade: B+** (Vol. VI, unchanged). **Exposure:** declaratory relief; nominal damages against the City; forward-looking injunction on comment administration. Punitive damages barred against a municipality, *Newport v. Fact Concerts, Inc.*, 453 U.S. 247 (1981) [C].

## V-2. First Amendment — Retaliation for Protected Speech
**U.S. Const. amend. I** *(speech and petition)* · **42 U.S.C. § 1983**

**Rule.** *Mt. Healthy City Sch. Dist. Bd. of Educ. v. Doyle*, 429 U.S. 274 (1977): once protected conduct motivated the adverse action, the burden shifts to the government to prove it would have acted anyway. Social-media conduct by officials is state action under the *Lindke v. Freed*, 601 U.S. 187 (2024) [C] test when the account deploys governmental authority.

**Adverse actions [R]:**
1. **The mutes.** Repeated muting/silencing of Miller's remote comment turns across meetings [R] — the founding entry of Vol. V's own-goal ledger (mutes → pleaded as Monell "custom").
2. **Comment disablement.** After the June 19–21 video factory crested (16K reel; 20-share canon drop), the city disabled livestream comments outright [R; canon §5/§6]. Disabling a comment channel *after* virality is timing evidence of retaliatory motive under *Mt. Healthy* step one.
3. **Jul. 27 podium threat** [R] — express conditional adverse treatment.
4. **Escalating procedural targeting:** Apr. 27 points-of-order cascade [R] contemporaneous with his referendum-adjacent commentary.

**Monell layer [C — Monell v. Dep't of Soc. Servs.*, 436 U.S. 658 (1978)]: the mutes/disablement pattern pleaded as persistent custom; a single official's ad hoc act will not reach the entity absent custom-or-policy.

**Individual-capacity problem, stated plainly:** *Bogan v. Scott-Harris*, 523 U.S. 44 (1998) [C] gives local legislators absolute immunity for legislative acts (introducing, debating, voting). **What Bogan does not cover:** administrative or executive acts — muting a feed, disabling a channel, threatening a speaker are operational, not legislative. The retaliation count is therefore pleaded against the City (entity) and, for the operational acts, against officials in individual capacity, with Bogan conceded as to votes and speech.

**Grade: B− as entity claim; C+ against individuals (discovery-dependent).** **Exposure:** nominal + compensatory (minimal — his damages are dignitary and access-based); attorney's fees under 42 U.S.C. § 1988 [C] — *this is the fee engine; compare V-13 (WPRA has none)*.

## V-3. First Amendment — Petition Clause (Referendum Interference)
**U.S. Const. amend. I** (right of petition) · paralleled by **Wyo. Const. art. 1, § 6** and the art. 13 § 1(b) referendum text (V-8)

**Rule.** *Borough of Duryea v. Guarnieri*, 564 U.S. 374 (2011) [C]: the Petition Clause protects petitions to all branches of government; retaliation for petitioning is actionable. Petitioning is not absolutely protected: *McDonald v. Smith*, 472 U.S. 479 (1985) (no immunity for libel in petitions); *BE&K Constr. Co. v. NLRB*, 536 U.S. 516 (2002) (protected petitioning includes reasonable litigation).

**The act.** On Aug. 10, 2026, after an executive session, the council voted 8–1 (Moody dissenting) to direct the city attorney to bring a declaratory-judgment action asking a court to hold that the referendum process does not apply to the annexation ordinance [P — Vol. VIII §4; Cap City Aug. 10–11; WTE Aug. 11–13]. Petitioners had already reported 3,200–3,275 signatures against a 2,739 bar [P]. The Secretary of State publicly cautioned the city against "undermining a clear statutory process" [P — Cap City Aug. 14].

**Register status: THREATENED, NOT COMPLETE.** Stated with discipline: (1) seeking a DJ is itself petitioning — the city's filing enjoys the same Clause it confronts (Vol. V's mirror-image lawfare finding; the movement's own *Noerr* analysis runs in both directions); (2) no violation exists until the city takes an act *beyond* adjudication — e.g., refusing certification, adding preconditions, or naming and deposing petitioners as adversaries. **Trigger registered:** if the DJ action names Miller (Vol. I prediction #1 — "a defendant in his own trap"), a Guarnieri retaliation count attaches on filing, with the Aug.–Sept. chronology as motive evidence.

**Grade: B− (contingent).** **Exposure:** injunction; fee exposure via §1988 if prevailed.

# PART II — WYOMING CONSTITUTIONAL VIOLATIONS

## V-8. Wyo. Const. art. 13, § 1(b) — The Municipal Ordinance Referendum Right
**Verified constitutional text [S — Wyo. Secretary of State, Constitution of the State of Wyoming]:**
> All cities and towns are hereby empowered to determine their local affairs and government as established by ordinance... **subject to referendum when prescribed by the legislature**... If prior thereto, a petition, signed by a number of qualified electors of the city or town, **equaling at least ten per cent (10%) of the number of votes cast at the last general municipal election**, shall be filed in the office of the clerk of such city or town, demanding that such ordinance be submitted to referendum, then **the ordinance shall not take effect unless approved by a majority of the electors voting thereon**. Such referendum election shall be called within thirty (30) days and held within ninety (90) days after the [petition's filing]...

**Register finding (new to the canon):** the 10% municipal referendum is not a bare statutory gratuity — it is constitutionalized home-rule architecture. The live numbers match the text exactly: 27,397 voters in the last municipal-cycle general election; 2,739-signature bar; petition reported at 3,200–3,275 [P — Vol. VIII §4, §6.1].

**The constitutional question the city's Aug. 10 DJ action now poses:** whether the legislature's exclusive annexation-appeal channel (W.S. 15-1-409) "prescribes" the referendum away for boundary ordinances, or whether art. 13 § 1(b) guarantees the petition path for *ordinances* generally, with W.S. 22-23-1005 merely implementing. Two canon-scale observations for whichever brief is written: (1) the constitutional text speaks of "ordinance" without categorical carve-outs [S]; (2) the state-level referendum clause, art. 3 § 52(g), *expressly excludes* "local or special legislation" from referendum — the drafters' demonstrated habit of stating exclusions, absent here, is a textual argument that no annexation exclusion was intended in art. 13 [S; argument flag].

**Violation status: UNRIPE.** No deprivation has occurred; certification was proceeding as of Aug. 18 [P — Epoch Times]. **Registered triggers, each converting this entry to a completed constitutional violation:** (a) refusal of certification absent statutory ground; (b) reliance on the DJ action to suspend the 30/90-day election clock of art. 13 § 1(b); (c) naming petitioners to deter (overlaps V-3). **Grade: incomplete pending events; text verified [S].**

## V-12. Wyoming Public Meetings Act — W.S. 16-4-401 through 16-4-408
**Framework [S]:** 16-4-402: all governing-body meetings public; "no action... except during a public meeting"; action not in conformity is **"null and void and not merely voidable"** (statutory text as quoted in CSD's open-government primer [S]) — though the 2025 LSO survey describes Wyoming actions as void *or* voidable by court order [S], and Vol. VI grades the family B− because re-vote in cure is available.
**Anti-circumvention, 16-4-403** [C — Vol. VI verbatim verification]: serial communications by which a quorum deliberates outside a public meeting are a violation — the Evanston 2025 disposition (four councilmembers sanctioned $750 each for sequentially deliberating a no-confidence letter; no sanction recommended; City Atty. Mark Harris) [C — Vol. VI] is the state's freshest precedent.

**Candidate episodes (each registered with its exact defect):**

3. **Recitals-as-findings, July 13** [R — Vol. VI entry]. Not an OMA violation; folds into V-14.
4. **The record's "quorum-artifact override"** [R — Vol. I]: procedural pathway by which a committee non-recommendation reached adoption without a fresh deliberative record — an OMA-adjacent artifact; discovery target, not a completed violation.

**Penalty math [S/C]:** W.S. 16-4-408: civil penalty up to **$750 per member** + misdemeanor exposure; safe harbor for any member whose objection is entered in the minutes [C — Vol. VI verification].

**Grade: B−** (curable by re-vote; the nullity language is the leverage). **Exposure if proved:** voidance of the tainted action — the only remedy in this register that reaches Ord. 4687 directly.
