### II. WYOMING CASE LAW — ARBITRARY/CAPRICIOUS + CONTIGUITY + NOTICE

**A. BJ Hough, LLC v. City of Cheyenne — Wyoming Supreme Court — annexation arbitrary/capricious standard:**
- Court: **"If the court determines that the action taken was capricious or arbitrary, or if it appears from the evidence that the landowner's right in his property is being un-warrantedly invaded or that the governing body abused its discretion, the court shall declare the annexing ordinance void."** [CourtListener BJ Hough]
- Review under 15-1-409(b) is for **abuse of discretion or arbitrariness or capriciousness** — considers merits of governing body's actions and findings, not just validity [BJ Hough]
- Appellants burden to show district court clearly erroneous in affirming council decision — must demonstrate findings **"wholly unsupported by evidence and were only remotely related to the standards required in the statute"** — record devoid of evidence supporting 15-1-402(a)(i)-(iv) [BJ Hough]
- Contiguity: legislature intended to **limit annexation to lands sharing common boundary or touching at some point**, with exception for natural/artificial barriers listed in 15-1-402(b) — **"where statutory provisions have made no attempt, as in Wyoming, to define 'contiguous' and 'adjacent,' courts have generally held such terms to require touching or actual contact"** — AG Opinion 83-021 [BJ Hough Footnote 8]
- **"If the City's interpretation... that lands need only be 'nearby' is correct, there would be no reason for exceptions... If 'nearby' lands that do not touch may be annexed, there is no reason specially to permit annexation of lands separated by roads, rivers"** [BJ Hough] — rejects crazy quilt/leap frog annexation — 5.26% contiguity admission [150413 Staff p2-3] fails substantial contact test
- **Application to rule change:** Changing time limits mid-ordinance is arbitrary — findings wholly unsupported because public not allowed to develop record — Rome not built in a day argument rejected — **"It is not necessary for the City to show an immediate impact... Just as Rome was not built in a day, it takes time to fully integrate"** [BJ Hough] — but city must still show reasonable evidence before annexation, not after

**B. Cox v. City of Cheyenne, 79 P.3d 500 (2003) — notice + declaratory judgment + standing:**
- Annexation case focused on notice and use of declaratory judgment actions — City annexed Village West 1.5 miles from nearest city limit Aug 31 2001 — Ten months later persons owning property within 1/2 mile brought DJ action to void ordinance — District court dismissed: plaintiffs lacked standing under 15-1-409(a) (limits challenges to city residents and owners of properties being annexed) and even if standing, declaratory judgment inappropriate [wyoleg.gov Annexation Issues PDF summarizing Cox]
- Court looked at definitions of contiguous/adjacent from Webster's, examined how other states looked at issue — concluded legislature **"intended to limit annexation to lands sharing a common boundary"** — phrase **"contiguous with or adjacent to"** in 15-1-402(a)(iv) legislatively intended to require boundaries touch to substantial degree, although need not be lengthy shared border [Annexation Issues PDF]
- **"limiting to ten days the time in which aggrieved persons could pursue some sort of civil action for improper annexation, the Court noted that the challengers were not included in other provisions — specifically the notice"** statutes prohibited DJ action — effect: four-year statute of limitation applied since action brought under declaratory judgment statutes — Supreme Court remanded [Annexation Issues PDF]
- **Application:** Changing speaking limits mid-ordinance limits aggrieved persons' ability to create record for 60-day forever barred appeal under 15-1-409(c) — violates due process — notice statutes must be strictly construed in favor of openness (RCFP Guide)

**C. In re West Laramie, 457 P.2d 498, 501 (Wyo. 1969):**
- **"under our statute the only requirement with respect to the scope and extent of the area to be annexed is that it must be contiguous to the annexing city or town"** [BJ Hough quoting] — contiguity jurisdictional

**D. Topanga Assn. for a Scenic Community v. County of Los Angeles, 11 Cal.3d 506 (1974) — findings requirement — adopted in Wyoming per City Attorney Brody:**
- **"An administrative grant of a variance must be accompanied by administrative findings. A court reviewing that grant must determine whether substantial evidence supports the findings of the administrative board and whether the findings support the board's action."** [Justia Topanga]
- **"regardless of whether the local ordinance commands that the variance board set forth findings, that body must render findings sufficient both to enable the parties to determine whether and on what basis they should seek review and, in the event of review, to apprise a reviewing court of the basis for the board's action"** [Topanga]
- **"implicit... is a requirement that the agency which renders the challenged decision must set forth findings to bridge the analytic gap between the raw evidence and ultimate decision"** [Topanga]
- **"by requiring that administrative findings must support a variance, we emphasize the need for orderly legal process and the desirability of forcing administrative agencies to express their grounds for decision so that reviewing courts can intelligently examine the validity"** [Topanga]
- Brody July13 2:59:58: **"The principle... that the Topanga Doctrine stands for... is a broad principle that many states follow — Wyoming... has adopted that general principle years before Topanga."** [Miller record]
- **Application:** Changing speaking limits mid-ordinance without findings bridging analytic gap — no finding why 3-min on first reading becomes 2-min or 1-min on second reading, why developers get multiple 3-min slots — fails Topanga — Bloom admission **"very loose, very open interpretation"** [Highlands] proves no findings

**E. Decker v. Wyoming Medical Comm'n, 2005 WY 160, ¶24 — arbitrary/capricious safety net:**
- **"Even if sufficient evidence is found to support the agency's decision under the substantial evidence test, this [Board] is also required to apply the arbitrary-and-capricious standard as a 'safety net' to catch other agency action which might have violated the Wyoming Administrative Procedures Act. Under the umbrella of arbitrary and capricious actions would fall potential mistakes such as inconsistent or incomplete findings of fact or any violation of due process."** [Tax Appeals citing Decker]
- Changing rules mid-ordinance = inconsistent findings + due process violation = arbitrary/capricious

### III. FEDERAL FIRST AMENDMENT — LIMITED PUBLIC FORUM — VIEWPOINT DISCRIMINATION + TIME LIMITS

**A. White v. City of Norwalk, 900 F.2d 1421 (9th Cir. 1990) — city council meeting is limited public forum:**

- **"Citizens have an enormous first amendment interest in directing speech about public issues to those who govern their city. It is doubtless partly for this reason that such meetings, once opened, have been regarded as public forums, albeit limited ones."** [White 900 F.2d 1421, 1425 — Justia, CourtListener, OpenJurist]
- **"On the other hand, a City Council meeting is still just that, a governmental process with a governmental purpose. The Council has an agenda to be addressed and dealt with. Public forum or not, the usual first amendment antipathy to content-oriented control of speech cannot be imported into the Council chambers intact."** [White]
- **"In the first place, in dealing with agenda items, the Council does not violate the first amendment when it restricts public speakers to the subject at hand."** [White citing Madison School Dist 429 U.S. 175 n]
- **"Of course the point at which speech becomes unduly repetitious or largely irrelevant is not mathematically determinable. The role of a moderator involves a great deal of discretion. Undoubtedly, abuses can occur, as when a moderator rules speech out of order simply because he disagrees with it, or because it employs words he does not like. But no such abuses are written into Norwalk's ordinance, as the City and we interpret it."** [White]
- **"Speakers are subject to restriction only when their speech 'disrupts, disturbs or otherwise impedes the orderly conduct of the Council meeting.' So limited, we cannot say that the ordinance on its face is substantially and fatally overbroad."** [White]
- **"A speaker may disrupt a Council meeting by speaking too long, by being unduly repetitious, or by extended discussion of irrelevancies. The meeting is disrupted because the Council is prevented from accomplishing its business in a reasonably efficient manner. Indeed, such conduct may interfere with the rights of other speakers"** [White, OpenJurist]
- **Application:** Discretion exists, but **abuses can occur when moderator rules speech out of order simply because he disagrees with viewpoint or words he does not like** — Emmons gavel **"This issue is not about data centers"** while allowing developer closed-loop pitch is abuse — White allows subject-matter restriction (must be relevant to annexation), but once developer introduces data center to prove benefit, public must be allowed to discuss negative impacts of same subject — otherwise viewpoint discrimination

**B. Rosenberger v. Rector and Visitors of Univ. of Virginia, 515 U.S. 819 (1995) — viewpoint discrimination egregious:**

- **"Viewpoint discrimination is thus an egregious form of content discrimination. The government must abstain from regulating speech when the specific motivating ideology or the opinion or perspective of the speaker is the rationale for the restriction."** [UMKC]
- **"Once it has opened a limited forum, however, the State must respect the lawful boundaries it has itself set. The State may not exclude speech where its distinction is not 'reasonable in light of the purpose served by the forum,' nor may it discriminate against speech on the basis of its viewpoint."** [Rosenberger]
- **"The government cannot justify viewpoint discrimination among private speakers on the economic fact of scarcity."** [Rosenberger] — cannot justify giving developers more time because more speakers want to speak — scarcity does not justify viewpoint discrimination
- **"It is as objectionable to exclude both a theistic and an atheistic perspective on the debate as it is to exclude one, the other, or yet another political, economic, or social viewpoint. The dissent's declaration that debate is not skewed so long as multiple voices are silenced is simply wrong; the debate is skewed in multiple ways"** [Rosenberger] — excluding both pro and anti data center? No, here city allowed pro data center, excluded anti — even more egregious

**C. Good News Club v. Milford Central School, 533 U.S. 98 (2001) — limited public forum viewpoint discrimination:**

- **"Restrictions on speech that take place in a limited public forum must not discriminate on the basis of the speaker's viewpoint and be reasonable in light of the forum's purpose. Because the school's exclusion of the Good News Club violated this principle, the school violated the Club's free speech rights"** [Wikipedia Good News Club]
- **"A 'limited public forum' may 'reserve its forum for certain groups or for the discussion of certain topics' but because the exclusion... discriminated against a viewpoint, the Court did not rule on whether the restriction was 'reasonable in light of the purpose served by the forum'"** — viewpoint discrimination alone violates, even if reasonable
- Applying Lamb's Chapel and Rosenberger: exclusion of religious viewpoint was impermissible viewpoint discrimination

**D. Norse v. City of Santa Cruz, 629 F.3d 966 (9th Cir. 2010) — cited in First Amendment Coalition:**

- Public comment portion of city council meeting can be considered limited public forum, restrictions upheld if reasonable and viewpoint neutral [First Amendment Coalition — Can a city council reduce time?]
- Rules imposing time limits or restricting comments to relevant subjects might be deemed reasonable if applied **equally to all speakers** [First Amendment Coalition]

**E. Chaffee v. — Brown Act time limit reduction — reasonable if announced at beginning and applied equally:**

- **"It did not violate the Brown Act when the chair... announced at the beginning of the meeting that public comment on each agenda item would be limited to two minutes per speaker, instead of the three minutes normally allotted... because the chair anticipated that four items would be lengthy, and Commission would not be able to complete meeting in reasonable period unless comments shortened."** [First Amendment Coalition — Can a city council reduce time?]
- **"If a rule indicates that each speaker during a public comment period may speak for 'up to three minutes,' then legislative body may 'exercise their reasonable discretion in departing from the normal time limits.'"** [Chaffee]
- **"The Brown Act does not specify a three-minute time period... does not prohibit public entities from limiting the comment period in the reasonable exercise of their discretion."** [Chaffee]
- **BUT:** Must be announced at beginning, must be based on reasonable anticipation meeting cannot complete in reasonable period, must be applied equally — **changing mid-ordinance on second reading, different enforcement than first reading, without announcement at beginning, and allowing developers multiple 3-min slots while holding public to single slot, violates Chaffee's equal application requirement**
- **"A speaker may not be stopped from speaking because the moderator disagrees with the viewpoint he is expressing."** [White v. Norwalk cited in First Amendment Coalition]

**F. ACLU-WA — Know Your Rights — public comments at local government meetings:**

- **"Certain local government meetings, such as city council meetings, are considered 'limited public forums.' This means that a city council can enact viewpoint-neutral restrictions on speech ('place, time, and manner'), if there is a legitimate and compelling government interest."** [ACLU-WA]
- **"Local government bodies can limit speech to certain topics (e.g., agenda items) and timeframes, as long as such restrictions are not unreasonable and the restriction is not based on disagreement with a speaker's viewpoint."** [ACLU-WA]
- **"It is unconstitutional for a governing body to restrict a member of the public from speaking because it disagrees with their specific viewpoint."** [ACLU-WA]
- **"A government body may impose a time limit on a speaker during a public comment period so long as it does not discriminate based on the content of the speaker's expression. If the time limit is content-neutral, is viewpoint-neutral, and serves a compelling government interest (such as conserving time and allowing others to speak), then it is likely permissible. For example, a city council may provide a total public comment period of 30 minutes and restrict speakers to 3-5 minutes each, depending on time available and number of speakers. However, a city council may not give one speaker in support of a certain legislative decision 5 minutes, and another against the same decision 3 minutes"** [ACLU-WA] — **directly prohibits giving developer 5 min and opponent 3 min — allowing developer multiple 3-min slots (total 15-20 min) vs public single 3-min is same violation**

**G. First Amendment Coalition — public comment sessions are limited public forums:**

- **"Public comment sessions have been found to be limited public forums, limited by the content restrictions set forth in the Brown Act. Courts recognize that... 'Citizens have an enormous first amendment interest in directing speech about public issues to those who govern their city... such meetings, once opened, have been regarded as public forums, albeit limited ones.' White v. City of Norwalk, 900 F.2d 1421, 1425 (9th Cir. 1990)"** [First Amendment Coalition — New rules restrict free speech]
- **"While the governmental body may not stop a speaker from speaking because the moderator disagrees with the viewpoint expressed, it may stop the speaker if his or her speech becomes irrelevant or repetitious."** [First Amendment Coalition]
- **"In a limited public forum, such as the City Council meeting, reasonable time, place and manner regulations are permissible, but restrictions must be 'content neutral' and narrowly tailored to serve a significant government interest, and must allow ample alternative channels. Where content-based restrictions are involved, very high standard must be met. Regulations must be neutrally administered."** [First Amendment Coalition]

**H. Viewpoint-Discriminatory Closure of Designated Public Forum — Michigan Law Review:**

- Note argues closing designated public forum for viewpoint-discriminatory purpose is unconstitutional — government must act in good faith when closing forum — purpose may not be viewpoint-discriminatory [Michigan Law Repository]
- **"The closure of a public forum will typically be a facially neutral action... Nonetheless, closing a forum with a discriminatory purpose can have numerous adverse effects"** — changing time limits mid-ordinance to close forum for disfavored viewpoint (anti-data-center) while keeping open for favored (pro-data-center) is viewpoint-discriminatory closure

**I. Recent case law — 11th Circuit En Banc — city council meetings as limited public forum:**

- **"City council meeting was deemed... limited public forum (rather than designated) as forum limited to speech on matters 'pertinent to the city'. On remand, city will need to show that its decision to ban... was reasonable in light of purpose of meeting and was not on basis of viewpoint"** [Reddit r/supremecourt summary of 11th Circuit En Banc]
- Limited public forums: government may impose restrictions so long as **viewpoint-neutral and reasonable in light of purpose served by forum** [Reddit]

**J. Judge: City's arrest of critic is quintessential viewpoint discrimination — Newton, Iowa 2026:**

- Federal judge ruled City of Newton violated rights of critic by twice arresting at council meetings — rule prohibited **"derogatory comments"** — Mayor interrupted, directed police chief to remove — **"Petersen was neither boisterous nor disruptive and used no abusive language or gestures... sole offense was insisting on right to complete allotted speaking time after being improperly silenced based on content of protected speech"** [Times Republican Feb 2026]
- **"To permit one side of a debatable public question to have a monopoly in expressing its views to the government is the antithesis of constitutional guarantees. Whatever its duties are as an employer, when the board sits in public meetings to conduct public business and hear the views of citizens, it may not be required to discriminate between speakers on the basis of their employment, or the content of their speech."** [Times Republican quoting case law]
