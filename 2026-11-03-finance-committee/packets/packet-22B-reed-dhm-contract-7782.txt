Purpose and Need 
The purpose of this memorandum is to request Council approval of Contract 
Modification No. 2 to the Professional Services Agreement with DHM Design 
Corporation for the Reed Avenue Rail Corridor Design Project. This modification 
incorporates additional professional services needed to address newly identified 
project requirements, ensure continuity of ongoing design work, and meet federal 
and railroad coordination obligations. The added services are necessary to maintain 
project momentum, resolve scope gaps not addressed under the original agreement, 
and support adjacent corridor needs. 
 
Summary of Additional Professional Services 
Contract Modification No. 2 expands the consultant’s scope to include four 
components, as detailed in Exhibits 1–3 of the agreement. 
 
Add-Service Request #2a – Exhibit 1:  Expanded Street Improvements 
(Bent Ave to Snyder Ave) 
This component provides design services for full street reconstruction and 
pedestrian improvements beyond the core Reed Avenue Corridor, specifically 
from Bent Avenue to Snyder Avenue. This includes sections of 17th, 18th, 
20th, 21st, 22nd, and 23rd Streets.  Due to incomplete field data, “worst‑case” 
design assumptions were used; any cost savings resulting from reduced 
actual conditions will remain with the City. 
 
Add-Service Request #2b – Exhibit 2:  Design of Parking Lot & Pedestrian 
Access (20th Street to 21st Street) 
This work includes design of a parking lot and associated pedestrian access 
improvements to support circulation and safety at the southern end of the 
corridor.

Add Service #2c – Exhibit 3: Railroad Coordination, Environmental 
Compliance, and Additional Corridor Design required to complete the

To avoid project delays, a portion of the funding originally allocated to 
corridor design was used for additional railroad-related work. This work

preparation of materials required for railroad approval, including the 30% 
Preliminary Engineering submittal, the BNSF Railway 22905 Agreement, 
additional National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA) documentation, and 
Class I railroad engineering services. 
 
Add Service #2d – Exhibit 3: FRA Design Elements, Environmental 
Compliance, and Railroad Coordination 
This request funds additional FRA‑eligible tasks required to meet federal 
expectations for the Reed Avenue Rail Crossing Elimination Grant including 
expanded environmental NEPA documentation.  Of the $370,000 total for this 
component, $277,500 is eligible for reimbursement through FRA Grant No. 
69A365242140RCEWY, with the remaining $92,500 representing the City’s 
25% match. 
 
Justification for Contract Modification 
1. Project Continuity and Efficiency.   
The expanded scope is directly tied to ongoing corridor design work. 
Completing these tasks with the existing consultant avoids fragmentation, 
duplication of effort, and potential rework. 
 
2. Timely Project Delivery.   
Retaining the current project team for adjacent and interrelated design 
elements avoids delays associated with procuring a new consultant for similar 
services. 
 
3. Improve non-motorized, motorized connectivity, and drainage.  
The modification supports roadway rehabilitation, non‑motorized and 
motorized connectivity, and drainage improvements across adjacent street 
corridors, enhancing safety and overall corridor function. 
 
Available Funding Status and Anticipated Costs 
The primary funding for the project is the 6th Penny Specific Purpose Tax with 
additional funding sources available from the 2023-2026 5th Penny Optional Tax and 
a portion of the U.S. Department of Transportation Federal Railroad Administration 
(FRA) Grant Agreement No. 69A365242140RCEWY.

The above-mentioned contract is amended or modified as set forth in the following and these 
amendments and modifications of the plans and specifications are agreed to by the parties. All other 
aspects of the original agreement remain unchanged and in full force and effect. 
 
I. Description of Changes 
 
This Contract Modification No. 2 expands the scope of the Professional Services Agreement for the 
Reed Avenue Rail Corridor Design Project to include four additional work components. These 
additions are necessary to complete the project design and address needs identified through 
coordination with the consultant team and BNSF Railway, as well as federal grant requirements. 
 
The scope of work, deliverables, design assumptions, exclusions, hourly rates, and maximum fees 
for these additional services are detailed in the following DHM Design Corporation proposals, which 
are attached and incorporated into the Agreement by reference: (1) Exhibit 1: Add Service Request 
#2a, dated September 9, 2026; (2) Exhibit 2: Add Service Request #2b, dated September 9, 2026; 
and (3) Exhibit 3: Add Service Requests #2c and #2d, dated January 14, 2026, and transmitted 
September 4, 2026. 
 
II. New Contract Items 
 
Add Service Request #2a: Expanded Street Improvements (Bent Avenue to Snyder Avenue) - 
Not-to-Exceed Fee: $466,620 (see Exhibit 1) 
This component provides design services for the reconstruction and  improvement of 17th, 18th, 
20th, 21st, 22nd, and 23rd Streets from Snyder Avenue to Bent Avenue. Work includes full roadway 
reconstruction from back‑of‑sidewalk to back‑of‑sidewalk on 17th, 18th, 20th (east side only), 21st, 
and 22nd Streets, as well as improvements along the west side of 20th Street and on 23rd Street, 
including Snyder Avenue. Because complete existing‑condition data is not available within the 
expanded limits, the consultant has made “worst‑case” design assumptions, and any resulting cost 
savings from reduced field conditions will remain with the City.  
 
Add Service Request #2b: Design of Parking Lot and Pedestrian Access (20th Street to 21st 
Street) - Not-to-Exceed Fee: $79,600 (see Exhibit 2) 
This component includes design of a parking area located between 20th and 21st Streets and 
associated pedestrian connectivity improvements to support multimodal circulation and safety at the 
south end of the Reed Avenue corridor.

===== PACKET PAGE 6 =====
Add Service Request #2c: Additional Railroad Coordination and Corridor Design Services - 
Not-to-Exceed Fee: $250,427 (see Exhibit 3) 
To avoid project delays, a portion of the funding originally allocated to corridor design was used for 
additional railroad-related work. This work included expanded coordination with BNSF Railway, 
meetings, and preparation of materials required for railroad approval, including the 30% Preliminary 
Engineering submittal, the BNSF Railway 22905 Agreement, additional National Environmental 
Policy Act (NEPA) documentation, and Class I railroad engineering services. 
 
Add Service Request #2d: Federal Railroad Administration (FRA) Design Elements, Railroad 
Coordination, and Environmental Compliance - Not-to-Exceed Fee: $370,000 (see Exhibit 3) 
This item provides for additional FRA‑eligible design tasks, environmental compliance support, and 
railroad coordination required to meet federal funding expectations and ensure the corridor design 
sufficiently supports future rail crossing elimination imp rovements. Of the $370,000 fee  for this 
work component, $277,500 is FRA-eligible and will be funded through the FRA Grant Agreement 
No. 69A365242140RCEWY, and the remaining $92,500 represents the City's 25% grant match 
funded through the 6th Penny Specific Purpose Tax. 
 
The not-to-exceed fees stated above, for Add Service Requests #2a through #2d, total $1,166,647.00, 
which is the amount of this Contract Modification. If a fee stated in an attached exhibit differs from 
the corresponding not-to-exceed fee in this Contract Modification, the amount stated in this Contract 
Modification controls.

 The contract completion date for design is extended to April 30, 2028.

SCOPE OF WORK 
Roadway Design –   
The DHM Design Team will work to design the reconstruction of and/or street improvements to 18th Street, 
20th Street, 21st Street, 22nd Street, and 23rd Street from Snyder Avenue to Bent Avenue.  This will  
include the reconstruction of the entire roadway section from back of sidewalk to back of sidewalk for  
17th, 18th, 20th (east side only), 21st, and 22nd Street.  Improvements will be made to the west side of 
20th Street, and to 23rd Street (including Snyder Avenue improvements).  See attached exhibit for full 
extents of proposed work. 
Our team is anticipating that we will need to provide a maximum of 2 conceptual options for improvements 
for the City and/or adjacent property owners to evaluate.  This will be done during the 60% Plan Design 
phase only.  Our scope and fee assume that once we have started preparing the 90% plans, there will be 
no significant changes to our design approach (i.e., all design decisions will be finalized prior to preparing 
the 90% plans).  Any changes to the design approach during the 90% or Final Plan design phases may 
result in additional fees.    
Our team will prepare the necessary Plan & Profile sheets, grading sheets, stormwater improvement 
sheets and reports, landscape sheets and details sheets as needed, notes sheets, etc.  In pedestrian 
areas, grading will entail meeting ADA requirements (if possible/practical).  Our goal for these additional 
improvements adjacent to Reed Avenue is to tie into existing to the east and west of Reed Avenue at 
Snyder Avenue and Bent Avenue as well as compliment the design of Reed Avenue. 
• We anticipate providing 60% plans, 90% plans, and 100% plan sets, separate from the Reed
Avenue Project
• We will establish bid items and quantities for all work along with a cost estimate.
• As part of the grading plans, we will prepare a SWPPP plan showing proposed BMPs to be used
during construction to control sediment.

additional areas of design.

public engagement, environmental review, railroad coordination, and into the next stages of design. Since our 
original agreement, the project's complexity, coordination requirements, and overall level of effort have grown 
significantly. 
A significant factor in that growth has been the ongoing coordination with BNSF Railway. As the design 
advanced, additional technical reviews, coordination meetings, design refinements, documentation, and 
specialized railroad consultant involvement became necessary to address BNSF requirements and maintain 
forward progress. Working collaboratively with the City Engineer's office and Reed Avenue Rail Corridor 
stakeholders, DHM Design elected to continue responding to these evolving project needs as they arose rather 
than interrupting the design process or delaying critical coordination. 
One example is the level of development required for the 30% BNSF review. Under our original agreement with 
the City, the 30% design phase was anticipated to consist primarily of hand-drawn conceptual graphics 
establishing the proposed project direction and Master Plan. BNSF's review process, however, required 
developed CAD drawings at this stage. The project team was also required to respond to comments on the 30% 
plans through BNSF's third-party review consultant before receiving authorization to advance the project. In 
addition, DHM and the consultant team have participated in numerous railroad coordination meetings over the 
past several years to address review requirements, resolve issues, and position the project to move forward. 
The additional effort has not been limited to railroad coordination. The City has also requested continued Class 1 
railroad consultant assistance, expanded environmental clearance review and compliance, and support 
associated with the City's FRA grant. These efforts include specialized coordination and documentation that 
were not contemplated at the level now required when the original scope and fee were established. 
As we have discussed over the past year, these additional professional services extended well beyond the 
assumptions of our original scope of work. In order to protect the City's investment, avoid unnecessary delays, 
and maintain project momentum, resources that had originally been allocated for later Design Development and 
Construction Document phases were utilized to address these evolving project requirements. The purpose of 
this additional services request is therefore to replenish those design-phase resources so that the project team 
has the budget necessary to complete the remaining work at the level of coordination and quality required.
