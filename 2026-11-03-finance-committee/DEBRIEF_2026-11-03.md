## 2. What each item actually was

### 22A — National Forest Foundation / Encampment wildfire-funding pilot
Cheyenne holds a $250,000 U.S. Endowment for Forestry and Communities grant
(#24-00799, awarded 2024) to pilot using Wyoming's **Clean Water State
Revolving Loan Fund** to pay for wildfire fuels reduction near the Town of
Encampment. The loan application succeeded: **$1,227,176** awarded, up to 75%
principal forgiveness (= $920,382 forgiven); the National Forest Foundation
covers the remaining 25% (= $306,794) plus the origination fee. The
modification raises NFF's management contract by $39,800 and extends it a
year; staff stressed the money is a **reallocation inside the existing grant**
— "no additional financial commitment from the City of Cheyenne or the U.S.
Endowment."

Why Cheyenne, 100+ miles away? White asked exactly that. Answer on record:
Cheyenne's water comes from **Rob Roy Reservoir** "up the hill" from the
project area; beetle-killed, drought-stressed timber threatens the city's
water infrastructure around Rob Roy (and, per a follow-up answer, the project
sits south/west of Hog Park Reservoir too). The city is "just… the holder of
the grant" and gets watershed protection at no city cost. White was
satisfied ("Perfect, thanks"), nobody else pushed, unanimous in about five
minutes. **You did not speak on this item** (at least not in the paste).

Two loose threads nobody raised, worth a one-line question at Governing Body
if you want them on record:
- The old performance period **expired Oct 31, 2026 — three days before this
  Nov 3 vote**. The modification is therefore retroactive on its face. Common
  and usually harmless, but the minutes should say so.
- "Increase of funding" + "will not impact the awarded grant amount" is only
  coherent as a within-grant reallocation; the packet should show which budget
  category lost the $39,800. Staff said "reallocated among budget categories"
  without naming one.

### 22B — Reed Avenue Rail Corridor design, DHM Design (+$1,166,647)
This was the meeting. City Engineer Tom Cobb presented four add-services:

- **2A** — Expanded street/pedestrian improvements on the side streets one
  block each side of the corridor, Bent Ave to Snyder Ave [ASR: "Bent to
  Schneider"], covering 17th, 18th, 20th, 21st, 22nd, 23rd. Scoped at
  worst-case full reconstruction. Cobb is candid that this scope "just wasn't
  ever conceived" in the original concept — he added it because the corridor
  needs neighborhood connections (St. Mary's school named).
- **2B — $79,600** (per Miller; Cobb did not price it on mic) — Design of a
  parking lot + pedestrian access at 20th/21st on land **currently owned by
  the Wyoming Lottery**. Cobb, verbatim substance: "I'm under the process of
  negotiating that." The city does not own the land yet.
- **2C — $250,427** (per Miller) — Additional railroad coordination /
  environmental compliance / design. Cobb's own framing: BNSF required much
  more design work during the concept phase, "so we're basically then having
  to **fill that back in** on the services that we used at that time" — i.e.,
  budget allocated to later phases was consumed early and is now being
  replenished.
- **2D** — FRA components: added federal environmental (NEPA) compliance not
  in the original scope.

Funding: $277,500 FRA-eligible + $466,620 fifth-penny (2023–26, side-street
connections) + $422,527 sixth-penny (Reed itself) = **$1,166,647** ✔ (sums
exactly).

Then Cobb gave a long project-status briefing (most of the paste). The
substance, stripped of repetition:

1. Two parallel efforts: the **FRA at-grade crossing-elimination** project
   and the **corridor revitalization** (east-side pedestrian promenade,
   ~34 ft) — plus a BNSF **maintenance road on the west side** (Cobb
   initially said "east," then corrected himself on a direct question; use
   the corrected version: promenade east, maintenance road west).
2. **Four street closures** are BNSF's non-negotiable price: 17th, 18th, and
   21st on the corridor, plus Dillon [street name as transcribed] off to the
   side. [Zoom rendered "four closures" as "foreclosure(s)" throughout.]
3. **Mar 3, 2026 meeting** (Mayor, Cobb, chief of staff, "Libby," BNSF's
   Rich Scott and Mary [surname garbled: "Amanaldi/Aminaldi"]): the mayor
   fought the 21st St closure; BNSF would not move — "safety and profit…
   those are their only two major factors." In exchange the city won an
   **8-foot pedestrian corridor on the BNSF maintenance road** so east-side
   pedestrians can reach an open crossing instead of fence-hopping. Cobb's
   word for the deal: "a compromise. Both sides lost a little bit."
4. **Jul 14, 2026 meeting** (Mayor + Cobb with BNSF north-region director
   Rich Scott, Cobb's counterpart, and French Thompson III [name unverified],
   the official whose letter had made the four closures a hard condition, now
   a BNSF VP): BNSF **volunteered** its grants and PR staff to help the city

   now opened."
5. **Agreement BNSF22905 is executed by both sides.** It commits the city to
   the four closures, records BNSF's written concurrence in the concept plan

6. **The city will have to pay BNSF for the right-of-way** — a one-time
   lease-back payment estimated at ~10% of appraised corridor value,

## 3. How *you* did — honest assessment

You were the **only dissenting voice in the meeting**, on both money items,
calling in on Zoom while the room (especially on 22B) was stacked with
supporters. Both of your comments were cut at the 3-minute mark mid-sentence.
Neither changed a motion. But the two comments were not equal — one drew blood
on the record, the other drew none, and the difference is instructive.

### 22B (12:29:52 → cut 12:32:57) — your strongest point landed; most of the speech never aired
**What landed:**
- **The 2B sequencing point is airtight because Cobb made it for you.** He
  said on mic, minutes earlier, that the $79,600 parking-lot design is for
  Wyoming Lottery land he is "still… negotiating." You then showed the
  written packet (memo p.1, contract p.5, Exhibit 2 p.15) nowhere discloses
  the city doesn't own it. Designing to 100% construction documents on land
  you don't own, before the Governing Body has approved a purchase, with
  restricted sixth-penny money — that is a clean, quotable, non-rhetorical
  defect. **Wolf's reply proves it registered:** he "assured" you that "Mr.
  Cobb and his team will properly sequence the undertaking so that we'll have
  the agreement… and then we will make sure we get the design work done."
  That is a concession the sequencing concern is legitimate. It is also
  **only an assurance** — the motion that passed was a straight
  approve-NTE-$1,166,647 with **no sequencing condition attached**. The gap
  between Wolf's assurance and the unconditioned motion is your best
  Governing Body hook (see §6).
- **The Exhibit 2 arithmetic is real but small.** Three totals for one task:
  $79,600 (contract), $79,796 (29,300+31,500+18,800+196), $79,800
  (30,216+30,506+18,882+196). Spread: $200 / $4. As proof the packet wasn't
  internally reconciled, it works; as a fiscal scandal it doesn't, and nobody
  in the room treated it as one. Best used as a 10-second "the packet doesn't
  even agree with itself" beat, not as a headline defect.
- You **front-loaded the ask** ("require you to hold or sever this
  modification today") in the first 20 seconds. Good discipline — when the
  cutoff came, the ask was already on record.

**What didn't land / never aired:**
- You promised **four** defects; the clock killed you inside #2 (the 2C
  backfill), at "a fee table dated January 14th, 2026, 9 months ago, and
  now—". Defects #3 and #4 were never spoken, never answered, and as far as
  this transcript shows, never filed in writing. Whatever they were, they
  currently exist nowhere in the administrative record.
- The **2C point got no traction** even though it's arguably the bigger
  governance issue (out-of-scope work performed, later-phase budget consumed,
  council asked to replenish after the fact — again, Cobb's own "fill that
  back in" language corroborates the mechanism). It needs documents the
  committee can see (Exhibit 3, packet p.20) and a plain-English frame;
  delivered at speed at minute 2:45, it read as process noise next to the
  BNSF-breakthrough narrative.
- **Moody voted no — do not claim him.** He gave no reason in the paste,
  and he *moved* the armor item's second and moved 22A, so he's not a blanket
  dissenter. His no is a genuine crack in an otherwise wall-to-wall support
  item, but nothing in this record ties it to your comment. If you want to
  know why, he's the first phone call — not a talking point.

### Armor (12:38:44 → cut 12:41:40) — technically your cleanest speech, institutionally ignored
- **Defect 1 (sole vs single source)** — Form #3 (packet p.2, signed Oct 16,
  2026) instructs the department to circle one; neither is circled; the Oct 21
  purchasing memo and legal notice call it both. If the packet is as you
  describe, this is a facial defect in the no-bid justification — the two
  classifications carry different showings.
- **Defect 2 (copy-pasted justification)** — your strongest armor point:
  the "fusion of proprietary and/or patented technologies… not available
  from any other source" language (typo "ROM" included) tracks Aardvark's own
  Jan 9, 2026 sales letter (packet p.4) word-for-word, while Aardvark's
  letter (packet p.5) admits third-party commercial components (Blue Force
  Gear UltraComp, FirstSpear Tubes, standard SAPI plates). Staff pasting a
  vendor's brochure as its independent justification is exactly the kind of
  thing a grant audit cares about — and SHSP money has federal procurement
  standards behind it.
- **Defect 3 (sizing/spec)** mostly aired before the cut: quote SO2435
  (Oct 16, 2026), $5,492.31/set; 7 of 11 line items totaling $94,290 still say
  "specify color / specify color/size"; all 52 ceramic plates ($31,980)
  ordered in **one size (SAPI Large)** despite Project 7's marketed
  "size-to-the-operator" no-guess sizing; fine print: 150-day delivery,
  non-returnable, 25% restocking fee. Ordering non-returnable armor in a
  single plate size for 26 different bodies is the most *concrete* (and most
  officer-safety-relevant) point in either speech. You were cut off
  immediately after delivering it.
- **Defect 4 never aired.** Same 4-point structure, same result.
- **Response from the committee: none.** No question to the sergeant, no
  question to purchasing, no discussion — motion, second, unanimous ayes.
  For the record that's actually useful: the objections are preserved with
  zero rebuttal on mic. Nobody stood up and said the Form 3 was correctly
  classified or that one plate size fits the team.

### The pattern across both speeches
The **"four defects" architecture is too big for 3 minutes** at the density
you speak. Twice today: point 1 clean, point 2 complete, point 3 rushed,
point 4 nonexistent, cut mid-sentence. The committee has also clearly
learned it can simply wait you out — no member asked staff a single follow-up
question on either item after you spoke. The counter-move isn't speaking
faster; it's (a) filing the full text in writing so points 3–4 enter the
record whether or not they're heard, and (b) making the *oral* version a
90-second, one-ask speech that finishes with time to spare and forces a
yes/no on a specific condition. Tightened scripts are in §6.

**Net for the day:** 0-for-3 on outcomes, 1 live hook (Wolf's sequencing
assurance) for next week, 1 unrebutted procurement record (armor), 1 possible
ally to identify (Moody's unexplained no). The BNSF goodwill narrative was
always going to carry 22B out of committee; the realistic fight was never
"kill it," it's "sever or condition 2B" — and that fight is still available
at Governing Body.
