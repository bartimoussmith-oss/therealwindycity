## Item 22A — Contract Modification #1 to Contract #8081, City of Cheyenne ↔ National Forest Foundation [12:01]

**CLERK:** Item number 22A, contract modification number 1 to contract number
8081 between the City of Cheyenne and the National Forest Foundation to

**CHAIR ESQUIBEL:** Okay, who do we have doing the staff report?

**ELIZABETH FINNECARO** [spelling unverified], Economic Resource
Administrator: Hello. Before you today, I have contract modification number
one for city contract 8081 between the City of Cheyenne and the National
Forest Foundation. Unfortunately the National Forest Foundation is in a
conference today, but they did send some information with me and their thanks
for considering this extension today. This modification is being requested to
reflect an extension in the performance period from October 31st, 2026 to
October 31st, 2027, and an increase of funding to the contract from $110,000
to $149,800. This contract modification will result in no additional
financial commitment from the City of Cheyenne or the U.S. Endowment. The
funding has been reallocated among budget categories and will not impact the
awarded grant amount. This request is a result of an approved budget
modification between the City of Cheyenne and the U.S. Endowment for
Forestry and Communities, Innovative Finance for National Forests, Grant
number 24-00799. The City of Cheyenne was awarded $250,000 in 2024 to pilot
the potential use of the Wyoming Clean Water State Revolving Loan Fund
Program to fund wildfire risk reduction activities with the town of
Encampment. Representatives from the Board of Public Utilities, the City of
Cheyenne, the Town of Encampment, the National Forest Foundation, and World
Resources Institute have utilized the U.S. Endowment funding to develop the
fuels reduction project and apply for the Clean Water State Revolving Loan
Fund program. The loan application was successful. The total loan amount
awarded was $1,227,176, with a maximum 75% principal loan forgiveness.
National Forest Foundation is going to be covering the remaining 25% of the
principal and the loan origination fee. This contract modification will
ensure that the National Forest Foundation is able to continue to provide
management to the project, and this will serve as a model for other
communities looking for innovative funding opportunities to reduce wildfire
risk. And I stand if you have any questions for me today.

**CHAIR ESQUIBEL:** Questions for staff? Councilman White.

**COUNCILMAN WHITE:** Elizabeth, I certainly support the grant and the
purpose, but when you start talking about the distance between us and
Encampment, that seems a little far. I'm not sure why we're partnering with
Encampment on this and not the City of Laramie, which is much closer, or
even [Rawlins]. Can you clarify some of that for me?

**FINNECARO:** Through you, Mr. Chairman. The City of Cheyenne is receiving
our water from Rob Roy, which just so happens to be up the hill from this
entire program. Unfortunately, Wyoming knows too well that fires don't
understand boundaries or water districts — fires move as they will. The area
that this project is going to take place in has a significant amount of dead
and dying trees due to beetle infestation and drought. It has made the City
of Cheyenne's water infrastructure around Rob Roy vulnerable to damage from
a large [wildfire]. So this is really advantageous to the City of Cheyenne.
This is not costing us anything. We just serve as the holder of the grant,
and they are doing all the work, and we get the benefit of our water
infrastructure being protected by the work that they're doing.

**WHITE:** Perfect, thanks.
