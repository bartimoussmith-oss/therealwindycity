## SPEECH 1 — ITEM 10a, DDA AMENDMENT (pulled — use now)

My name is [NAME], [ADDRESS], for the record.

The amendment before you moves the Downtown Development Authority's staff out of Planning and Development and into Community Recreation and Events — on the recommendation of something called the Cost-Benefit Task Force, acting at a special board meeting on August 7.

Before you approve it, some history this body should have in front of it. The DDA's contracts run 1991, number 2891 — then 2023, number 7704 — then 2025, number 8188 — and now the FY27 memorandum, number 8374, which tonight's Mod #1 amends. Four instruments in thirty-five years, three of them in the last three years. An entity that restructures itself this often is either solving a problem or hiding one.

Here is what we know, from the city's own January 2025 audit session: the Downtown Development Authority's financial statements drew a **qualified opinion** — and I quote the record — the auditors *"weren't able to get adequate documentation and information"* from the previous board. The city itself passed clean. The DDA could not produce its paperwork. That is not my characterization. That is the auditor's.

Three asks, numbered. One: produce Contract 8374 itself — the underlying FY27 memorandum this amendment modifies — before you vote on its amendment. You are being asked to amend an instrument that is not in your packet. Two: produce the Cost-Benefit Task Force's roster and its report. A recommendation that reorganizes a board arrived from a body this public cannot name. Three: state for the record what corrective action was taken on the qualified audit — because restructuring the org chart is not corrective action on missing documentation.

I'll add the history honestly: this council once had a member serving, in the city's own words at budget, as *"the representative and essentially the executive director"* of this same authority. That arrangement is why the documentation culture failed. Tonight's amendment moves the failure to a new department.

The principle is simple and it is not personal: an entity that cannot pass an audit for paperwork does not get reorganized in the dark on a consent designation. Produce the contract. Produce the roster. Produce the corrective action. Then amend.

For the record — every quote I've given tonight is from the city's own sessions and the city's own agenda documents.

Thank you.

## SPEECH 2 — ITEM 9f, CHEYENNENEXT COMPREHENSIVE PLAN ($351,790)

My name is [NAME], [ADDRESS], for the record.

Item 9f awards $351,790 to Houseal Lavigne Associates of Chicago to update the comprehensive plan — the document that guides where this city grows, what it annexes, and what it zones. On the merits of having a plan, I have no complaint. My complaint is the funding line, and it is on the city's own memo, dated today.

The contract is paid for with $240,000 of one-time budget — plus a **$112,900 rollover of unspent County Pocket annexation budget**. Read that again. The annexation program did not spend its money — and the surplus was rolled into the contract for the plan that will guide the next annexations. The engine funds its own map. That is a loop, and it should bother this body more than it bothers me.

Four asks, numbered. One: which ordinance or resolution made that re-appropriation? Money does not move between purposes in this city without an instrument. Name it, and its agenda line. Two: who is the **local developer who sat on the selection committee**? The memo describes the committee; it does not name it. Eleven firms responded, three were disqualified, four were interviewed. The public gets the count and not the name. Three: release the scoring sheets, and explain why Lotus — the consultant already working for the county — was not shortlisted. Four: disclose in writing the **Placer.ai** arrangement — this contract buys mobile-device location data to analyze commuting. The scope and the privacy terms of purchasing phone-location data about this city's residents belong on the record before Finance meets Wednesday, not after adoption in November of 2027.

And note the calendar: the timeline was compressed from eighteen months to fourteen — final adoption November 2027, which falls after this November's referendum and after the November 9 third reading of the agriculture package. The plan that rationalizes growth lands after the votes that constrain it.

The comprehensive plan will outlive every member of this body. It deserves clean money and a named committee. Producing a contract number is not a burden. It is the minimum.

For the record — the $112,900 figure, the committee count, and the Placer.ai scope are all from the city's own posted documents.

Thank you.

## SPEECH 5 — ITEM 5, THE AUGUST 10 MINUTES

My name is [NAME], [ADDRESS], for the record.

I rise on the minutes, and I want to be precise about what I am and am not asking.

The minutes before you memorialize, at length and near verbatim, the motion authorizing the City Attorney to seek a declaratory judgment — the action asking a court to decide whether the people's referendum right applies to annexation ordinances. The motion, the mover, the seconder, the eight-to-one vote. All of it is there. As chronology, the minutes are accurate.

What the minutes omit is provenance. That authorization was not an agenda item. It arrived as an **unagendized walk-on**, moved immediately after an executive session closed under 16-4-405(a)(iii) — a session the public was excluded from — and it was voted the same night it first appeared. A member of this body moved it; the minutes record the motion and not the manner.

That omission matters more than the item itself. Minutes are the city's memory. A record that captures what was decided while erasing how it arrived is a record that teaches the public to check the recording instead of trusting the clerk — and this city's own clerk operation describes itself, at budget, as the bottleneck for records and streaming. The fix is cheap.

Two asks. One: amend the minutes to reflect that the declaratory-judgment authorization was moved as an unagendized item following executive session — one sentence, no editorializing. Two: adopt the practice going forward that walk-on items are minuted as walk-ons. If the manner of a motion is not fit for the minutes, that is an argument against the manner, not the minutes.

I am submitting a written correction to the Clerk tonight requesting exactly this language, and I ask that its receipt be noted.

Understand the principle plainly: I am not asking you to un-vote anything. I am asking the record to admit what happened in the order it happened. A body confident in its votes should want that too.

For the record — the vote count, the executive-session citation, and the motion's text are all in the minutes themselves. The provenance is on the recording.

Thank you.

## SPEECH 6 — ITEM 8, TRUCK WEIGHTS (support, with the pivot)

My name is [NAME], [ADDRESS], for the record.

I support this resolution, and I want to say why on the record, because the why is the part this body should keep.

The resolution opposes raising the federal gross-vehicle-weight limit from 80,000 to 91,000 pounds on the National Highway System. It cites the Department of Transportation's crash data for the heavier trucks. Its logic is infrastructure and risk: heavier vehicles, more damage, more danger, and the municipality left holding the road and the liability. That logic is correct. I adopt it as my own this evening — and then I ask this body to follow it one step further, because it stops at the city limits of its own courage.

**The same logic applies to hazardous-material trains through this city.** Freight that would require a permit, a fee, and a vote to move by truck moves through Cheyenne by rail with no municipal permit, no fee, and no vote — while this city bears the consequence if anything goes wrong. The fire department's own budget sessions describe the wildland apparatus and the federal grants that paper over that exposure. The risk is municipal. The revenue is not. The vote never happened.

So here is the ask, and it is one sentence: direct staff to prepare the same kind of resolution — the same infrastructure-and-risk logic this body is about to vote yes on — addressing hazardous-material rail movement through the municipal boundary, for referral to the same committee this resolution goes to. You have the template in your hands tonight. You are voting on the trucks. Take the trains with you.

If the answer is that the city has no power over the rail — then say it on the record, because that answer belongs in the minutes next to the vote. A city that can object to a 91,000-pound truck can at least state for the record what it thinks of a hundred-car unit train through its streets.

Support, with the pivot. The logic is yours. I'm just asking you to use all of it.

For the record.

Thank you.
