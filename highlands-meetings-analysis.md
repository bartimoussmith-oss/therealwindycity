# MEETING ① — Planning Commission · Monday, June 1, 2026

### Setting
A five-hour marathon with a "particularly lengthy agenda." Eight commissioners at roll call; by the Highlands items (hour 3+), only **five of seven** remained (Connor, Matile, Hernandez, Lair, plus Chair Wiggum voting only to break ties) — the attendance fact that decides everything downstream. ### Key exchanges (the non-Miller record)
- **Rachel Irwing's presentation** (with Brad Emmens/AVI as agent; owners named: *Arpan Hammond Hardware Co., Old Horse Pasture Inc., Lumus Livestock Co.*): *"Given the early stage of this effort, **Microsoft has not yet advanced detailed design for the campus.**"* — the exact sentence Miller will quote for six straight weeks as the "confession." Under questioning she adds: no building count exists ("we do not have a campus of this size to pull from"), the plan is **15–20+ years**, and — asked whether it's land-banking — *"overall, yes, we do plan to land bank."* Microsoft's rep also gives the jobs figure: **~50 operational technicians per data center building.**
- **The infrasound exchange (the origin of Miller's count #4):** Commissioner Connor puts *infrasound* on the record — citing Boardman OR, Newton County GA, Canton MS, Mesa AZ — and asks if the city has remedies. **Bloom: audible-noise limits are 60 dB day / 55 dB night at a residential property line, but infrasound "is something that we have not addressed… we don't have any data… we're still evaluating investigating that."** Seth Lloyd counters that the one scientific study he found measured machine noise five miles out — *"but they also picked up a refrigerator and a dishwasher that were louder."* Connor's follow-up — *"that would also mean you haven't put a data center this close to a residential property. Is that true?"* — is answered only with the HR Ranch exception.
- **The protest-petition bombshell:** Lloyd apologizes for omitting that a **valid protest petition** is likely on the Southwest Drive item — triggering a **supermajority requirement at council third reading**. That tripwire is why the item dies outright at the June 22 council meeting (no motion offered).
- **Commissioner Connor's cross-examination** — the sharpest questioning of the entire circuit: *"Cheyenne's about 20,700 acres. This is about 18% of the current city size… how in the world can you even begin to understand the impact?"*; *"How do we know long-term impacts if we don't even have one going?"*; *"If one of them's polluting the aquifer… how do you even begin to determine which one it is?"* Bloom repeatedly defers to the applicant — an answer Miller logs as an evidentiary void. On Reed, the chair himself: the parcel borders Laramie County Community College — *"their pride and joy is a rodeo team… animals are even more sensitive than humans… I don't see how those two things are compatible."* And Lloyd's candor: staff has *"heartburn"* — *"there are reasons to both approve and deny these applications. They're not cut and dried."*
- **Heather Madrid** on the AG holding zone: *"Why would anybody rezone it to agricultural when it's already been publicly shared what the intent of this land will be?… a very intentional misrepresentation of the intent."* — the citizen version of Miller's later "two-step laundering maneuver."
- **Jim Malcolm, IT professional** (Reed items, ~4:30): *"These newest data centers… are orders of magnitude larger… we're putting space heaters in these areas… I ask that we postpone making decisions on these until we understand them better."*
- **Councilman Wolf's cameo** as a public commenter — praising the Commission's questions, warning the annexation fight is coming to council, citing getting home at **1:00 a.m. after 67 witnesses** the previous Tuesday; a commissioner *enforces the three-minute limit on a sitting councilman*.
- **Betsy Hale (Cheyenne LEADS):** the "14 years, we've kind of done it right" defense — CCRs, BOPU regulation, Black Hills' three-year large-load tariff study — and the Magpole/Safeco "once they put a stake in the ground, that building is really ours" economic-development speech.
- **A commissioner's closing lament** before the FLUM vote: *"I don't think any of those people grew up here. This is my hometown. This isn't what I want to see happen to it."*

### Strategic read
This is the foundational night of Miller's case — and it cost him nothing. The **only formal defeats the Highlands package suffers anywhere in the process happen here**, via a quorum artifact and two denial recommendations the majority commission itself didn't even want. Irwing's "no detailed design," the land-banking admission, Bloom's infrasound concession, and the BOPU lift-station detail are all now on a certified record Miller didn't have to create. Every later "overrule" vote (June 22, July 13 items 19–20) is an exhibit he can describe as *the council overriding its own experts over an admitted evidentiary void.*

Two further June 1 findings sharpen the goal model:
- **The ISC bypass theory is born here, in question form** (2:17) — complete with his own statement of the exemption rule and the first articulation of his fallback demand: if the state process is skirted, sign a *binding development agreement* voluntarily replicating it. Everything he says about the "business park loophole" on July 13 is this 2:17 question, pre-scripted.
- **The Southwest Drive item he touched at 1:23 is the only item in the entire circuit that dies** — FLUM for lack of a second at PC, then no motion at council June 22, with the protest-petition supermajority as the proximate cause. His ask that night ("continue this item or deny it") is, technically, the record's only fully-granted request. Note also his **register-switching**: plain-English neighbor advocate at 1:23, cross-examiner at 2:17, statutory auditor at 2:34/3:50/4:35 — one speaker calibrating voice to audience, which is the behavior of an operator, not a monomaniac.

# MEETING ③ — Public Services Committee · Monday, June 15, 2026 (noon)
**Video:** [Public Services Committee - 06-15-26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e8pByyWP7RE) (4:24:48) · **Presiding:** Chairwoman Dr. Michelle Aldrich · **Present:** Dr. Emmons, Mr. Laybourn, Dr. Rinne (Moody and Wolfe are NOT on PSC) · **Agenda mechanics announced up front:** the five Microsoft/Highlands items (22, 23, 24, 34, 35) would be heard as **one combined report** (Rachel Irwing for Microsoft + Connor White for staff), then acted on individually; the three Southwest Drive items (25, 36, 37) were **pre-scripted to die by arranged no-second** — "we will still take a motion on that, but there will be no second."

# MEETING ④ — Governing Body · Monday, June 22, 2026 (second readings)
**Video:** [City Council - 06-22-26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RjSGlhh4q9s) (5:30:05) · **Presiding:** Council President Tom Segrave (Mayor Collins via Zoom 7:11–9:18 p.m.; brief recess 9:35) · **Official minutes:** Granicus Record of Proceedings

### Setting
The opposition's best shot: a 5½-hour meeting, **21 registered public commenters** on the annexation alone, and a real motion on the floor.

### The standing showdown (item 10 · 13:52–17:52)
The most legally significant non-Miller exchange of the circuit — the city puts *his standing* at issue, on the record. After cutting off his first notice, **Wolf** (an attorney) rules from the floor: *"There is no job in the city job descriptions… that describes a grand overseer of our planning and zoning undertakings. Planning and zoning is a site-specific issue. We— unless Mr. Miller actually owns the land or is within the zone of interest in which he would be entitled to receive notice — he has no standing to make these comments."* **Aldrich** counters: *"This is a citizen, and every citizen in our community has a right to make a comment on any item on our agenda. That's why we have open meeting laws."* Miller then does the unscripted thing — he asks that Wolf, *"an attorney,"* explain where he was coming from. Wolf obliges with doctrine: *"If you look at the cases that have interpreted who can challenge zoning decisions, it's limited to the people who are directly impacted… the neighboring residents or the land owner themselves… [he] has absolutely no standing."* Aldrich again: *"If we were to follow that logic, no one in this room tonight that wants to speak about data center annexation would be allowed to speak."* And Miller's reply is the tell — offered the perfect setup to claim land, a ward, or residency, he claims nothing: *"I was just wondering what gave you the idea that I have no standing."* Wolf: *"I don't have to [debate] with Mr. Miller."* The president gives Miller 40 seconds to finish; he spends them reconciling his notes and yields.

### The motion
**Wolfe–Laybourn: refer the annexation ordinance to committee with a report at the November 9, 2026 meeting** (press framing: a four-month delay to force a community-benefits agreement under which Microsoft would pay **$50 million over five years**). Debate highlights:
- **Dr. Emmons' "dirty laundry" speech:** the amendment takes the city "right back to where we were at the beginning of the spring" on the failed moratorium.
- **Jonathan Noble (Microsoft) — the second "confession":** *"While we fully anticipate this development to take 10 to 20 years to build out in full, **we don't plan on waiting 10 to 20 years to start the construction**… our hope is to start building well before that 10-year mark. **We can't commit to that. We don't know that**… that's how early we are."* (Also: Microsoft declined to consider a Sweetgrass-style annexation agreement because it "wasn't asking for any special consideration.")
- **Public testimony arc:** Don Taylor reads national stats (Q1-2026: 833 active opposition groups in 49 states; 300+ state bills; moratoria in 14 states) and calls the dais "a bunch of little oligarchs" (gaveled for language); a resident relays his wife's friend's exchange with a "Microsoft big honcho" — *"we're going to own Cheyenne pretty soon"* (dais: "Microsoft doesn't have any wells"); Gregory Barrow proposes a 15–20 person citizen oversight coalition; Janet Tolman invokes the 1920s; Penny Hollenbach: "It's just not the Wyoming way"; Rob Turse (Plumbers/Pipefitters Local 192) carries labor's yes.
- **The vote: referral FAILS 3–6** (yes: Wolfe, Moody, Laybourn).

### Wolf's Socratic cross of Bloom
After the motion dies, Wolfe returns to the dais with prepared hypotheticals for the planning director — base case: a 3,500-acre cattle ranch; then a conservation-easement-forever variant. **Bloom's answers are the night's record-gift:** annexation findings are *"very loose, very open interpretation,"* and staff simply confirm criteria and send it up. (Bloom, charmingly, opens by complimenting the work product: *"I really appreciate all the work you put into this."*)

### The ISC verification (item 11 — the city fact-checks him)
**Emmons, same evening:** *"Today, I called the Industrial Siting Commission because I was unclear on several things… I asked specifically, do data centers have to go through the industrial siting act? And what they told me is that when there is an expansion of an existing business park such as this, the industrial siting act… is waived. So I just want to let Charles Miller know that **there wasn't a violation**. This was done appropriately, per the industrial siting folks."* **Wolf, putting a finer point on it:** *"Mr. Miller's comments are off base completely. **The legislature created this scheme.** So this is a legislative action over which we have no authority to disagree with… we're actually following the law, contrary to Mr. Miller's objections."* — In one exchange the city confirms the loophole exists, disclaims ownership of it, and attributes it to state law. All three statements are now Miller's to use.

### Miller's turns (11 items per the official ROP · 16 turns on the sync index: 13:52, 19:26, 25:35, 28:59, 33:38, 1:24:23, 3:21:47, 3:38:48, 4:01:38, 4:15:47, 4:21:44, 4:29:24, 4:36:19, 4:40:16, 4:44:52, 5:26:41)
