## Who he is in this record

- **Charles Miller** participated **by phone** all evening (the clerk's desk — "Jennifer" — patched him in each time; he opened nearly every turn with *"Mayor Collins, members of the city council, Charles Miller, for the permanent administrative record"*).
- He **declined to identify his ward** when asked twice (*"I don't need to actually give you guys that information"*).
- A councilmember told the record that when asked months earlier, **Miller stated he is not an attorney** — and that the council relies on its own licensed counsel's advice.
- His style is that of a self-styled litigant building an appellate record: repeated use of "**constructive notice**," "**the evidentiary void is sealed,**" "**this is now logged,**" and closing refrains like *"I demand a no vote"* and *"Govern yourselves accordingly."*
- Official minutes from other 2026 meetings show he is a regular commenter whose remarks frequently draw points of order for being off-topic — which also happened several times tonight.

## His recurring legal framework (the short version)

Nearly every comment rested on the same set of assertions:

1. **Site-specific rezonings/annexations are "quasi-judicial," not legislative.** He claims Wyoming case law ("a P.3d 158 standard," "McGann v. Laramie," and what he calls the "**Tonga doctrine**") requires quasi-judicial treatment — separately stated **findings of fact and conclusions of law**, decisions on **substantial evidence**, and **de novo review** under W.S. **16-3-114(c)** (the Wyoming Administrative Procedure Act).
2. **The city has an "evidentiary void."** No independent utility/infrastructure studies, no acoustic telemetry for data centers — so approvals are "structurally void," "void *ab initio*," and "arbitrary and capricious."
3. **The "business park loophole."** He argues BP zoning is being used to bypass the **Wyoming Industrial Siting Act**'s environmental review and impact fees for massive data-center campuses (he repeatedly calls Microsoft a "trillion-dollar developer" building a "ghost project").
4. **Statutory hooks he cites:** W.S. **15-1-402** (annexation conditions), **15-1-506** (overruling the planning commission), **16-3-114(c)**, the city's **Unified Development Code (UDC)** and its **August 2025 data-center noise standards**.

*Note: citations are rendered as he spoke them (auto-captions garble several); city staff and the city attorney pushed back on the substance repeatedly (see "Pushback" below).*

## Turn-by-turn detail

### 1 · 33:44 — Public hearing, 34.4-acre annexation (North Range Business Park area)
Noting the hearing exists to determine whether annexation conditions under Wyoming statute 15-1-402 are satisfied — and that the parcel is for *"a laydown yard and parking"* — Miller asks whether the city attorney can **state for the record the difference between a legislative act and a quasi-judicial act.*** City Attorney **John Brody** answers: *"Case law pretty clearly shows that annexation[s], zonings are legislative decisions made by the city council."* A councilmember adds the body is following its attorney's advice that it's legislative. Miller says his research suggests annexation *may* be legislative, but the *rezoning* standard is where he has "an issue," runs out of time looking for his notes, and promises to *"catch you guys on the rezoning for this one."*

### 2 · 45:12 — Item 9 (AR zoning, 2.69 acres, south of Charles St.)
Establishes on Q&A that the zoning is for **one particular owner** with a specific legal description — *"the textbook definition of a quasi-judicial act."* Cites W.S. 16-3-114(c), de novo judicial scrutiny, a Wyoming Supreme Court standard (rendered as "P3D158"), and a claimed holding that **Cheyenne municipal bodies making site-specific determinations must issue separately stated findings of fact and conclusions of law.** *"If this council fails to explicitly state its findings of fact … it is acting outside its legal authority."* — Brody's reply: *"The item on the agenda speaks for itself, and Mr. Miller can take what he wants from it."*

### 3 · 1:01 — Item 9 again, before the vote
Called back after an objection that he'd already spoken (*"Mr. Miller, you can go ahead. [He] has already spoken on this issue"*). The strategic point becomes explicit: *"I'm stepping to the microphone to lock in a critical legal precedent before you vote…. The standard you apply to this 2.6-acre vote will be cited in the appellate record for the 30,000-acre votes. Govern yourselves accordingly."* A councilwoman responds that Miller **is not an attorney** and the council will follow its licensed counsel; Councilman Esquibel counters with a property-rights defense of owner-initiated annexation. **Approved unanimously.**

### 4 · 1:21 — Item 10 (annex 26.29 acres, Clear Creek Pkwy./Granite Peak Dr. — Swan Ranch, petitioned by Two R Investments)
Quotes the evening's earlier exchange (*"we need more than a feeling"*) and argues the city is **substituting feelings for findings of fact**: no utility-load data, no engineering study — just the applicant's request. *"You're confirming that the city of Cheyenne is running its entire annexation pipeline on autopilot…. I demand a no vote."* Then the ward exchange — the mayor asks him to identify his ward; he's already hung up. ( *"I'm guessing we'll get to hear from Mr. Miller yet again tonight."* ) **Approved 8–1, Moody dissenting.**

### 5 · 1:37 — Item 11 (LI Light Industrial zoning, same parcel)
Concedes item 10 was "legislative," but says the legal standard *"fundamentally changes"* for zoning: a site-specific map amendment for a named applicant is quasi-judicial and requires substantial evidence and findings — *"Where is the specific infrastructure load data to justify light industrial zoning…? They do not exist in this procedural packet…. The contradiction is logged. I demand a no vote."* **Approved 8–1, Moody dissenting.**

### 6 · 1:46 — Item 12 (rezone ~400 acres, north & south of West Wall Rd., west of I-25: LI/CB/PUD → BP Business Park)
Asked first for his ward: *"I don't need to actually give you guys that information."* Then asks if there's a staff report ("Nope" — it was in the packet), and delivers the night's first **"business park loophole"** argument: the staff report itself says BP *"is intended for data center uses and allows development with flexibility and without further zone changes,"* and (he claims) Councilman **Rinne had "already admitted on the record"** that BP lets developers *"skirt around the environmental requirements of the Industrial Siting Act."* Staff rebut: the staff report answers the criteria on pages 2–5, and **no one said BP is a loophole** — an industrial *park* designation triggers Industrial Siting Council review, and **DEQ permits still apply either way**. When Miller asks for someone else to corroborate, a voice quips: *"You could ask ChatGPT. I'm sure it could tell you."* Dr. Rinne later defends herself in detail (she'd checked with **DEQ's deputy director**: business parks escape the siting council's impact-fee process, not DEQ air/water/hazmat permitting). **Approved 8–1, Moody dissenting.**

### 7 · 4:48 — Item 13 (the big one: Highlands annexation, 3,459.99 acres east of S. Greeley Hwy./south of Sweetgrass — "future data center development")
His longest prepared statement, framed as a "**formal constructive notice of statutory invalidity**":
- The city's own **UDC** defines industrial uses to include data centers *"which consistently produce noise discernable at the property line"* — so classifying the tract BP instead of heavy industrial requires **proof the multi-gigawatt campus will be silent at its perimeter**.
- **Microsoft's land manager "openly confessed"** there are no detailed design or engineering plans — so the required acoustic measurements under the **August 2025 noise standards** cannot exist: *"a mathematical and legal impossibility."*
- Cites "**McGann v. Laramie**" (Wyo. Supreme Court) for the claim that site-specific map changes are quasi-judicial acts requiring rigorous factual support.
- *"By voting yes over this data void, you are consciously creating a regulatory blind spot to help a trillion-dollar developer evade the socio-economic—"* — **time called mid-sentence.**

### 8 · 4:53 — Moody's amendment (move effective date to Jan 25, 2027)
A rare plain Q&A mode: he asks Moody the purpose (*"A lot of people have been asking for us to slow down and pause. This is six months. That's why I chose that"*), confirms it changes nothing but the effective date, then tries to quiz Moody about his time on the Board of Adjustment and the legislative/quasi-judicial distinction — *"That has nothing to do with what's before us today…. All we're talking about is moving the effective date."* Tries to pivot to the Meta water-contamination issue; *"You're out of order."* Settles for: *"I just urge you guys to vote no on the entire thing until you can get more information."* (Councilwoman Emmons called the pause a "workaround" after five failed pause votes; Wolf argued six months could fund a data-center master plan, UDC fixes, and a community-benefits agreement — and warned *"a competent lawyer can get this annexation overturned in court without much trouble."*) **The amendment failed**, as did the follow-on postponement/recommittal effort.

### 9 · 5:29 — The "praise speech" (postponement debate)
The evening's twist — Miller announces he's there *"to do something that might surprise some of you"*:
- **To Councilman Larry Wolf:** despite past clashes (Wolf once sought to restrict Miller to one comment per meeting and *"questioned on the record if I was a ChatGPT robot"*), Miller praises him for dismantling *"the false narrative that developers have an inherent right to force the city's hand"* — *"a property owner's bundle of sticks does not include the right to be annexed."*
- **To Councilman Pete Laybourn:** despite being called *"completely out of control"* by him before, praises his pushback against the "rubber-stamp process": *"Do not give up."*
- **To Councilman Mark Moody:** respects his *"lonely no votes,"* but *"you need to step up your game… you must aggressively challenge the procedural and statutory defects… as they happen on this floor."*
- To the rest: *"Doing the right thing is always harder than doing the wrong thing…. It is easy to rubber-stamp a 3,500-acre corporate ghost project…. Retain your leverage. Do the hard thing today. Vote no on this annexation."*

Laybourn responds, reluctantly, that the discussion has become *"personality-driven… I think we can do a lot better than this."*

### 10 · 6:37 — Item 14 (AG Agricultural zoning on the full Highlands tract)
The "**two-step laundering maneuver**" argument: the staff report (p. 2) says AG is *"merely a holding zone which will allow the land to be rezoned more easily in the future"* — and item 15, minutes away, flips 3,340.7 acres of it to BP. *"You are using a temporary agricultural assignment to sanitize the annexation… only to immediately trigger the business park loophole… to bypass the state-mandated environmental oversight of the Industrial Siting Act."* Names the owners (**A.R.P. Hammond Hardware; Lumis Livestock Co.**), repeats the findings-of-fact demand. **Approved 6–3 (Wolf, Laybourn, Moody dissenting).**

### 11 · 6:46 — Item 15 (Highlands rezoning AG → BP, 3,340.7 acres)
Three-part objection:
1. **UDC classification** — a facility generating perimeter-discernible noise/intense utility loads is *heavy industrial*, not a "low-impact" business park; the developer's admitted data void makes BP a *"flexible political rubber stamp"* and creates a *"regulatory blind spot"* to evade the Industrial Siting Act.
2. **Live environmental failure** — the recent detection of a rare pathogen (rendered in captions as *"coupraitis gelardi"*) in the wastewater system, *"traced directly to a data center contractor,"* suspension of reclaimed-water irrigation, and bacteria reaching **Crow Creek**.
3. **Emergency-infrastructure breakdown** — the **Sept 21, 2025 Kinder Morgan pipeline rupture**, when the city *"failed to deploy" wireless emergency alerts to downwind residents — **time called** mid-list.

**Approved 6–3 (same dissenters).**

### 12 · 6:54 — Item 16 (Reed parcel, 387 acres south of I-80/east of College Dr., AG → BP; acknowledged on the record as Microsoft)
The housing argument: the **Planning Commission on June 1 recommended denial** of this very application; overruling it (which council had just done for Highlands) requires — under his "**Tonga doctrine**" — new substantial evidence and findings. He reads the city's own staff report (PUDC-26-121, p. 5, under UDC review criteria): *"Cheyenne has had documented housing challenges, and taking land anticipated for majority residential development and zoning that land into a business park would adversely affect the general welfare of the community."* *"You are literally deleting a 387-acre residential footprint during a documented housing crisis to grant an industrial entitlement for a data center ghost project…. The housing deletion is sealed. The Tonga violation is logged. Demand a no vote."* **Approved 8–1, Moody dissenting.** (Dan Reed, spokesperson for the four-generation Reed family, had just told the council Microsoft was the most reasonable buyer they'd dealt with in 40+ years of trying to sell.)

### 13 · 7:00 — Items 19 & 20 (resolution overruling the Planning Commission's disapproval; future-land-use-map amendment)
Invokes W.S. 15-1-506 for the override, calls tonight's vote quasi-judicial under the Teton/"Tonga" doctrine and W.S. 16-3-114(c): the staff memos justifying it are *"entirely procedural — zero new technical studies, zero updated infrastructure analysis, zero empirical data."* Cites the June 1 record (applicant's representative **Rachel Irwin** conceding no detailed design) and warns of detrimental-reliance litigation that will *"bankrupt the taxpayers of Cheyenne. Probably not bankrupt them, but… the Tonga violation is logged. The evidentiary void is sealed. I demand a no vote on items 19 and 20."* Staff clarified the Commission's outcome was a **quorum artifact** (only 5 of 7 members present; 4 yes-votes of the full board required), so council *must* overrule to proceed. **Both adopted 6–3 (Wolf, Laybourn, Moody dissenting).**

### 14 · 7:11 — Item 22 (final plat, Swan Ranch Rail Park 21st Filing)
Missed his chance on the previous item (*"I had my hand up… that was a pretty important deal"*). Objects that the plat was prepared by **AVI Engineering** — *"the exact same firm whose agent submitted the defective Highlands applications"* — and that a final industrial plat requires a statutory guarantee of adequate public facilities just as **BOPU banned closed-loop wastewater discharges** to the sanitary sewer, with no cumulative-load modeling. Praises them for pulling it from the consent agenda but insists they're *"not shielded from judicial review."* **Approved.**

### 15 · 7:14 — Item 26A (50-year ground lease to First & Union Apartments LP — 184-unit affordable-housing complex, Converse Ave./Carlson St.)
The **fiduciary-contradiction** argument: *"Moments ago during item 16 you voted to strip 387 acres of residential-designated land… Now you're attempting to put a taxpayer-funded band-aid on the exact housing crisis you just intentionally worsened."* Flags contract terms: rent deferral if the developer claims insufficient free cash flow (§3), and eventual transfer of the municipal land to the out-of-state (Utah) LP by special warranty deed (§4). *"You cannot logically or legally claim to be protecting the public welfare while executing this two-sided arbitrage against taxpayers."*
**The substitute-lease protest:** when Esquibel's amendment was described only in summary, Miller asks that the actual text be read; Esquibel reads the changes (title company, notice recipient, Exhibit A map, signature block — "nonsubstantive"). Miller asks whether the substitute was posted to the portal before the meeting: *"I don't think it was."* — *"Then this is a severe procedural and due-process violation… You cannot legally pass a binding municipal ordinance based on a ghost document."* Time called as he pivoted back to land use. **Substitute adopted; lease approved as amended, Moody dissenting.**

### 16 · 7:23 — Item 26B (2026–27 collective labor agreement, Cheyenne Firefighters Local 279)
*"I am absolutely not here to oppose our firefighters. I am here to place on the permanent administrative record exactly how this governing body is actively endangering them."* The mayor cuts him — *"You're way off topic"* — he recalovers by connecting the Industrial Siting Act's impact fees to fire-service funding: the council is *"legally obligating our firefighters to respond to catastrophic events — chemical coolants and high-voltage fires across a new 3,500-acre footprint — knowing definitively that you just threw away the state funding required to properly equip them for it…. Govern yourselves accordingly."* (The union president had just asked removal of a page-28 footnote; the council kept it after the mayor explained the 20-vs-22 minimum staffing issue, and corrected an EMT pay figure from 97¢ to 77¢.) **Approved as amended.**

### 17 · 7:31 — Item 27D (Christensen Road detention erosion mitigation; Legacy Construction bid, ≤$314,950)
Miller at his most mundane — asks staff to explain the item, learns it's erosion mitigation with a committee motion to **postpone two weeks** to negotiate with the railroad. He asks what exactly needs discussing; city engineer (through the mayor): after the bid, the railroad requested **full steel pipe through its right-of-way**, and he's exploring cheaper alternatives — *"You can come up to my office anytime and discuss any of these issues… but it's the **Union Pacific Railroad**, sir."* — *"Oh, very nice. Okay, thank you. That's all I have."* **Postponement approved.**

### 18 · 7:35 — Voucher report (~$3.7 million)
A "**systemic audit of tonight's discretionary spending against the city's statutory and environmental obligations**": the administration pleads poverty (water/sewer rate hikes, the $100M+ lead-pipe mandate) while the vouchers fund — per his read-aloud — **$70,000 to Suitcase Town Music** (concert advance), **$41,500 to Black 11 Touring** (Fridays on the Plaza headliner), **$34,725 to Colorado Jumps** (Super Day bouncy houses), **$8,148 for a Tim Meadows comedy-show settlement**, and **~$200,000+ to Lotus Engineering** (climate-action consulting). *"This is the definition of fiduciary sadism…. A municipal budget is a mathematical expression of intent"* — gesturing at what the captions render as a "**man-now liability claim**" (i.e., *Monell*). **Time called.**

### 19 · 7:39 — Other business: "the final constructive notice of the evening"
Three closing allegations:
1. **Unlawful delegation** — orchestrating the 3,500-acre annexation/heavy-industrial zoning strategy through **Cheyenne LEADS**, a private nonprofit shrouded in NDAs (*"You cannot launder public zoning decisions through a private 501(c)(6)"*).
2. **Conflict of interest** — the zoning applications' agent (**Brad Emmens for AVI Engineering**) appears on the same voucher report the body just approved: a **$10,548** payment from the 1% construction fund and **$9,859** under public-works contract 8325 directly to AVI.
3. **Global invalidity** — *"From the Tonga doctrine violations to the Industrial Siting Act bypass to the destruction of residential housing to the Monell liability of your luxury voucher spending, this entire proceeding is structurally defective and void ab initio…. **The administrative record is now closed. The trap is sprung. See you in district court.** Govern yourselves accordingly."* (Someone on the dais echoes: *"See district court."*) — Adjourned at **1:41 a.m.**
