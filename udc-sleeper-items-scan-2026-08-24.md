## S1. The small-farm tier is policed by a REVENUE test — the line moved from your land to your books ⭐ top find

The old boundary between small and large farming was **Limited Agriculture: "typically not occurring on lots or parcels larger than 10 acres"** — land size, a fact visible from a fence line.

The new boundary is the **Gardens and Homemade Foods** tier, defined by the W.S. 11-49-102 "producer" test: **≤250,000 products AND ≤$250,000 gross revenue annually**. And the draft adds the tripwire explicitly: *"Any agricultural use that exceeds the thresholds established by Gardens and Homemade Foods shall be considered General Agriculture"* — which is permitted by right only in AG and a few industrial districts, meaning **exceeding the revenue line makes your use illegal where you live.**

Enforcing that boundary requires evidence of sales volume and gross revenue. This imports a **financial-audit surface into zoning enforcement**: classification of your use depends on records only you hold. The summary sells the tier as "small farm activities… do not need a site plan" and never mentions the money cliff.

**Pattern match:** the compliance division's growth (106 "severe camps" cases, camper-ordinance backfire, noise citations "without a meter reading") + the qualified-audit regime ("audit-hostile by construction or neglect"). Every permission in this package arrives with a counting trigger — 45 days, 55 events, 2,000 sq ft, $250K — and this one counts *income*.

**Drop line:** *"The old code drew the line between small and large farming at ten acres — something you could see from the road. The new code draws it at $250,000 in gross revenue — something the city can only check by opening your books."*

## S2. This is the annexation fight's second front: protect the activity, keep the instruments

The city's own summary opens: *"During the County Pocket Annexation process many people had questions… City staff took these questions and found some old rules."* The package exists because of the pocket fight, and its protections are real — §6.08.020.F.7 (grazing/ag lawful in the year before annexation or rezoning), Gardens permitted in all 21 districts, gravel, fences.

But map the perimeter of what's protected: the **use** — not the **economics**. Unaffected by any of it: the blight map (the WY Fresh pocket pre-labeled BLIGHTED at the May 7, 2026 Urban Renewal Authority meeting), urban-renewal/TIF powers, the 2% BOPU assessment, franchise fees, city specials, and the city-initiated region-wide rezoning engine. And the mid-size operations that define county-style agriculture get nothing: **CFOs are banned outright in AR** (where rural-residential parcels sit) — only ≤$250K or a special-exception permit remain.

**Pattern match:** rescission removes ten pockets from the blight map while the designation machinery stays; rezonings arrive city-initiated; the Sept. 8 return includes an **AG→BP rezoning** — and see S3 for what BP now allows.

**Drop line:** *"They're offering the right to keep farming on land they already drew a blight line around. The grazing exemption protects the cows — not the property. Urban renewal, the two percent, and the specials all still reach it."*

## S3. Feedlots and slaughterhouses become sitable in industrial and airport districts — quietly

Table 5-1: **Concentrated Feeding Operations and Slaughterhouses = "S" (special exception) in AG, LI, HI, BP, and AD** — and General Agriculture is **permitted by right in BP and AD** (⚠ verify district abbreviations against UDC Table 3-1; AD is believed Airport Development).

So: industrial-scale livestock processing can be sited in light/heavy industrial, **business park**, and airport districts by governing-body special exception — venues with no residential neighbors to object — while the county pockets' own AR land is banned outright. The summary buries this in five words: *"Decide which zoning districts are the right places."*

**Pattern match:** the Sept. 8 AG→BP rezoning returning to committee — rezone ranch land to Business Park, and under the new code the business park takes agriculture by right and a feedlot by special exception. Low-visibility siting through council-level exceptions is the same channel as the perpetual ROW consents on tonight's consent agenda.

## S4. The gravel permission arrives welded to a new citation regime

New §6.2.8.d (all underlined — new text): every parking area, **"whether paved or surfaced with gravel,"** must be *"permanently maintained in good and safe condition,"* with repairs, drainage, visible striping, and — the load-bearing phrase — *"free and clear of weeds, junk, and nuisances."*

The summary presents gravel as permission ("Let farms, homes… use gravel"). It never mentions that the same section builds a standing **inspection basis over every gravel lot that uses the new right** — in "weeds, junk, and nuisances" language, the exact vocabulary of the compliance cases used against rural and nonconforming properties.

**Pattern match:** the camper ordinance that backfired ("flooded again"), the 106 cases, noise enforcement without meters. Give with one hand, build the citation surface with the other, in the same paragraph.

## S5. Site-plan exemptions for cemeteries — and "Military mission-oriented facilities" ⚠ verify

§2.2.3.b.5's no-site-plan list runs: (a) single-unit residential… (b) limited business operations… (c) [new] agricultural uses… **(d) Cemeteries. (e) Military mission-oriented facilities.** The (d)/(e) items sit exactly at the extraction's underline boundary — verify against the PDF whether they are additions. Either way, unexamined:

- If new: a carve-out exempting **military facilities from city site-plan/design review**, riding an agriculture bill, in an airbase/missile town in the Sentinel-modernization era — with no definition of "mission-oriented" in sight.
- If old: what HAS been approved under it? That's a records question worth filing on its own.

**Pattern match:** low-visibility federal/military entanglement; nothing about this appears in the summary.

## S6. Governance by bookmark — confirmed — and the numbers web

Confirmed in text: Gardens/Homemade Foods is defined **by reference to a state statute the ordinance won't print**, with the draft note *"For this draft (not to be included in the ordinance)"* — the Aug. 21 "keep the bookmark there" exchange, now visible. Any future amendment to W.S. 11-49-102 silently rewrites Cheyenne's most permissive tier.

Same genre: **55 times per year** (overflow parking), **2,000 sq ft gross leasable area** (farm sales cap), **45 consecutive days** (CFO trigger — see the swine-origin finding in `udc-draft-fulltext-analysis.md`). None has a published derivation; each creates a countable, citable, auditable trigger.

## S7. One appointed office holds three new switches

In this single package the Director gains/keeps: (1) the site-plan exemption gate — "significant customer impacts," verbatim from the work session where it was called "solely at your discretion"; (2) the gravel gate — "character of the surrounding area"; (3) parking-design exceptions via Administrative Adjustment. The elected body debates the numbers; the switches sit in one office, ungated by written criteria.

**Pattern match:** this is the structural version of the optics arc — decision-flow moved out of view. Aldrich flagged the symptom at the work session ("I find that really problematic"); the draft kept it.
