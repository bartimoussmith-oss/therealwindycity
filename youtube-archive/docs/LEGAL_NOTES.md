# Legal & Policy Notes — Read Before Publishing

> **I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice.** It is a layperson's
> summary of the issues to check, with sources to start from. If the channel
> matters to you, a one-hour consult with a Wyoming media-law attorney is cheap
> insurance.

## 1. Are these videos "public domain"?

**Your position is reasonable, but the law is more nuanced than "public
record = public domain":**

- **Federal rule doesn't cover cities.** 17 U.S.C. § 105 places *U.S. federal*
  government works in the public domain. It does **not** apply to state or
  local governments, which *can* hold copyright in principle.
- **Wyoming law is about access, not copyright.** The Wyoming Public Records
  Act (Wyo. Stat. §§ 16-4-201–205) defines public records broadly — expressly
  including sound recordings (§ 16-4-201(a)(v)) — and gives everyone the right
  to inspect and copy them. Minutes of non-executive sessions are treated as
  public records. But the Act grants *access*; it does not explicitly dedicate
  works to the public domain or waive copyright.
- **Practical factors strongly in your favor:**
  1. The city publishes these videos openly *and* provides direct **"MP4 Video"
     download links** to the public — strong implied license for downloading
     and reuse of public-meeting records.
  2. Recordings of public proceedings are overwhelmingly factual (thin or no
     copyrightable expression), and republication for transparency/comment is a
     classic **fair use** (purpose: civic information; nature: factual public
     proceedings).
  3. There is years-long precedent: the `cochwy` Internet Archive collection
     (773 items, some since 2012) and the city's own public YouTube channel
     operate openly; Wyoming courts have allowed recording/videotaping of
     public meetings where not disruptive.
  4. No Wyoming authority reviewed here asserts copyright over council meeting
     videos.
- **Residual risks to manage (not reasons to stop):**
  - Embedded third-party material: if a meeting ever plays a copyrighted film,
    song, or presentation, *that segment* could draw a Content ID claim. Handle
    per-video if it happens (YouTube's tools let you mute/trim).
  - Executive sessions are confidential by law — but those aren't in these
    public archives anyway. Never publish anything labeled executive/closed.
  - Don't claim *you* own copyright in the recordings (e.g. no © notices, no
    Content ID assertions on your uploads), and don't strip the city's
    attribution — the templates credit the source on every video.

**Bottom line for the channel:** describe the videos as "recordings of public
meetings posted as public records," credit the City of Cheyenne + Granicus link
on every video (the scripts do this), and avoid asserting ownership. That is
accurate without over-claiming "public domain" as a legal conclusion.

## 2. Granicus terms & scraping politeness

- Granicus hosts the archive *for the city*; the MP4 links are offered to the
  public with no login. Downloading them for civic archiving is consistent with
  their purpose.
- The download script is deliberately polite (sequential, delays, resume
  instead of re-fetch). Keep it that way: one machine, modest concurrency,
  off-hours for bulk pulls. If the city/IT ever asks you to slow down, comply
  promptly — goodwill matters more than speed.
- Granicus video streams are CloudFront-protected against datacenter scraping
  (observed: HTTP 403 from cloud IPs). That is anti-bot hygiene, not a ban on
  you — downloading from your home connection via the provided links is normal
  use.

## 3. YouTube policies that apply to this project

- **Impersonation:** a channel that looks like the official city government
  can be removed. Mitigations (all in the templates): unofficial-style name,
  "(Unofficial)" in About + banner, no city seal/logo avatar, links to the
  real city channel. See YouTube's impersonation policy.
- **Reused content & monetization:** mirrors of others' content are generally
  ineligible for the YouTube Partner Program. Expect **no monetization**; this
  is a civic project. Adding genuine value (accurate titles/dates, agendas
  linked, playlists, chapters, corrections) is both good practice and the right
  posture.
- **Copyright strikes / Content ID:** unlikely for meeting footage, possible
  for embedded third-party clips (see §1). If a claim arrives: read it, trim or
  dispute with a calm fair-use/public-record explanation, and never file a
  counter-notification without understanding the stakes (it exposes you to suit
  and requires your contact info).
- **Privacy:** public meetings have no expectation of privacy for on-mic
  participants, but be humane — don't editorialize titles to embarrass private
  citizens giving comment.

## 4. Practical checklist

- [ ] Channel name + About clearly unofficial; no city seal avatar.
- [ ] Every description links the Granicus/archived source (script default).
- [ ] No © claims on the meeting recordings; no Content ID disputes *against*
      the city or other archivists.
- [ ] Executive/closed-session material never uploaded.
- [ ] Polite downloading (defaults are fine).
- [ ] Consider a brief courtesy email to the City Clerk describing the archive
      project — not asking permission you may not need, but building goodwill
      and a contact for corrections.
