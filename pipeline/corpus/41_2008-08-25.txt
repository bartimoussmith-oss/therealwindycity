Wyoming House Representative Mary Throne, District 11, which is part of Ward I and Ward
III, a dvised a  g roup o f interested r esidents has been w orking d iligently to save several historic
residential structures located near the historic Governors’ mansion and expressed concerns that plans
by the property owner t o remove the structures from the area will have irreparable harm on the
Governors’ mansion as an historic site.  She reviewed the recent denial of a City demolition permit
through the Historic Preservation Board’s process and subsequent appeal to the Building Code Board
of Appeals, which upheld the denial; stated the Governors’ mansion neighborhood is a specially
protected historic area and stated her concerns with issuance of a City moving permit to relocate a
structure and urged the governing body to “slow down” the process to allow time for the group to
come up with options or solutions.  Speaking in support of the City intervening in the moving process
were:  J an Nelson Schroll, who read a letter received from former U.S. Senator Alan Simpson,
requesting  properties in the area be saved to preserve the history of Cheyenne; Nancy Abraham, who
requested dedicated historic districts receive respect and urged the City Council to use its power to
stop the moving of structures to maintain the integrity of the neighborhood, and Mary Ann Taliaferro,
who reviewed the area’s history and prominent individuals that had resided in the homes.  Speakers
requested the governing body intervene in the pending moving of a building within the historic area
to provide time to research options or alternatives; further requested that the governing body revisit
its regulations concerning moving permits relative to historic districts, and also discussed the historic
Governors’ Mansion neighborhood relative to possible sale of property in the area to the hospital that
purportedly may utilize the land for a parking lot.  Comments followed on the intention of the City’s
ordinance designating the Governors’ Mansion as an historic area; property owners’ rights; potential
hospital involvement; significance of area; interpretation of City Code permit requirements, a nd
Mayor and City Council authority to halt moving of a structure following issuance of a moving permit
through the Building Department. Mayor Spiker provided information available on moving permit
application procedures and status of the application that had been filed.  He explained the process a
moving permit applicant has to follow and compliance areas that have to be met.  Discussion included
whether the Mayor or City has legal authority to stop or intervene, either directly or through order
to the Police Chief, the moving process; moving permit application reviews and approvals required;
seeking a written legal review to determine Mayoral and City Council authority and options; process
utilized to review and approve demolition permits for designated historic areas vs. moving permits;
protection of the historic Governors’ Mansion area, and demolishing buildings vs. moving buildings
and effect of each on the historic integrity of the district.  City Attorney Claudia Angelos, stated her
concerns with mayoral and governing body authority to act upon the issue, and commented on state
statute and City Code provisions, which she indicated does not allow for the Mayor to stop the
issuance o f t he m oving p ermit o nce a pplication r equirements ha ve been met a nd a pproved by
necessary departments and the permit is issued.  U pon inquiry on whether there is a time frame
involved in issuing of moving permits, Ken Lewis, City Engineer, advised he was not aware of any
for moving permits. Ms. Throne referenced a section of City Code Chapter 15.50, Historic Design
Guideline (specific to the Governors’ Mansion protective area), regarding relevancy of a certificate
of appropriateness requirement for the property owner.  In conjunction with a question posed on
whether the City could file an injunction (with the court), Ms. Angelos advised if discussion continued
into issues that may involve potential litigation then the governing body needs to move into executive
session.  Following brief comments, Mr. Pierson moved to meet in executive session to discuss a
matter of potential litigation, seconded by Mr. Collins.  Motion carried.  Voting “yes” – all members
of the governing body with the exception of Mr. Laybourn voting “no”.  Mayor Spiker announced
the motion carried and members would meet in executive session, not open to the public, in Council
Chambers following a 10 minute recess;  that there may or not be any action taken by the governing
body as a result of the executive session, therefore, the meeting would reconvene and be open to the
public following closure of the executive session. He requested the City Attorney, City Clerk,  City
Engineer and Deputy City Attorney be present at the executive session. (Clerk’s Note: Executive
session began at 12:45 a.m.) Following conclusion of the executive session, Mayor Spiker reconvened
the meeting, open to the public at 1:10 a.m.  Mayor Spiker advised no action would be taken by the
governing body as a res ult of the executive session, but a s pecial meeting of the governing body
would be scheduled for Wed., August 27, 2008 at 12:00 p.m. (Noon) in City Council Chambers to
further review the concerns raised regarding removal of homes in the Governor’s historic district.
