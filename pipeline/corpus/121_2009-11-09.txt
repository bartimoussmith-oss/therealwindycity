Mr. Cole commented on construction material stored in the rights-of-way for property that has not

to t he curb line and explained the concern with run-off of certain materials into the storm sewer
system.  Discussion followed on a grace period involving the time frame for material storage in the
rights-of-way, and time frame flexibility for residents to incorporate landscaping projects relative
to a permit/fee.  Mr. Brown moved to amend the substitute as follows, seconded by Mr. Pierson:
Under Section 12.04.010, Subsections I., 3. and J., 3. strike the words “seventy-two (72) hours” and
replace with “30 days” so the subsections will read “Does not exceed a period of 30 days”.  Mr.
Beauheim expressed concern with the proposed amendment relative to any complaint that may be
received within the 30 day time period, and reviewed the process envisioned for a construction
project (ex: roof shingle replacement), indicating the intent of the ordinance is to create incentives
for individuals to place dumpsters on private property and if this can’t be done a permit would be
issued involving use of the right-of-way.  Discussion included the purpose of extending the permit
time to 30 days, environmental issues regarding run-off from materials, and concerns with blocking
sidewalks to users.  Upon inquiry, Martin Matsen, Development Director, provided information on
nuisance and zoning regulations, including setback requirements, and enforcement areas involving
storage of materials on private vs. public property, and Mr. Beauheim further explained when an
engineering encroachment permit is required.  Mr. Vetter advised the encroachment permit would
apply to work or construction being done in the right-of-way and if materials are not being used for
that purpose then a right-of-way permit for storage of materials would be needed.  Motion to amend
the substitute failed.  Voting “yes” – Mr. Brown, Mr. Pierson and Mr. Spiker.  Voting “no” – Ms.
Ash, Ms. Broyles, Ms. Case, Mr. Collins, Dr. Rinne, Mr. Valdez and Mayor Kaysen. Discussion
continued on encroachment permits, with Mr. Beauheim advising various sections of City Code
relate to t hese types of permits and explaining a developer is required to ob tain a permit prior to
construction, and concern with driveways being included in the proposed ordinance.  Mr. Collins
moved to eliminate references to driveways as noted throughout the substitute ordinance, seconded
by Mr. Pierson. During ensuing comments and upon inquiry, Mr. White reviewed existing City Code
Section 12.04.010 wording which does not prohibit obstructions of driveways and explained if the
amendment is adopted then materials could be placed in the area between the sidewalk and curb line
(i.e. driveway) which would potentially still be introducing materials into the storm water system
that are undesirable.  Discussion continued on Clean Water Act concerns, blockage of streets and
driveways, and allowable storage o f materials, equipment o r o ther t angible personal property if

City Co de Sect ion 12. 04.010 pro visions and i ntent o f t he i nitial o rdinance t o rest rict s torage
containers within rights-of-way.  Mr. Beauheim requested the word “driveway” be reinserted into

provide the authority to i ssue encroachment p ermits and are specific that a  permit is needed to
construct a driveway.  Ms. Broyles moved to amend by reinserting the word “driveway” into Section
12.04.010, Subsections F. and G., and Section 12.04.080, Subsection A., seconded by Ms. Ash.  Mr.
Cole ques tioned t he difference in fee s chedules for an  en croachment permit v s. a ri ght-of-way
