BACKGROUND 
 
Stemming from a detailed Hydraulic & Hydrologic assessment discussion with the City, HDR, CivilWorx, 
and GLM Design it was determined that a substantial scope change was needed to alter the outfall 
location of the proposed storm sewer interceptor being designed in the 18 th  Street Corridor. The new 
proposed interceptor routing will not include the design of an outfall structure.  Instead, it will include the 
design and routing of storm sewer interceptor south down Bent Avenue to 15 th  Street and will be designed 
to connect to the existing overflow structure located immediately north of UPRR property on an already 
developed wetlands and stormwater storage pond.  At the request of the City a new detention pond will 
be designed, in the future by others.  Finally, this scope modification also includes the removal of any 
remaining scoped work from Warren Avenue to House Avenue as it will be included in a future, separate, 
project. 
HDR has developed the below scope of services to accommodate the described additional work. This 
scope of services has been discussed with City Staff and represents HDR’s evaluation and interpretation 
of the level of effort required and the assumptions necessary to complete this additional work.

TASK SERIES 5.0 – FEMA Benefit-Cost Analysis 
HDR will prepare a benefit-cost analysis to support a future FEMA grant application for the stormwater 
portion of the project. The benefit-cost analysis will be prepared by HDR in accordance with FEMA 
guidelines. 
Task 5.1 – Data Gathering  
 
The completion of the benefit-cost analysis will require technical data to support the estimates of annual 
damages and annual benefits. The technical data required is as follows: 
Data Required for Estimating Annual Damages with Project 
• The flood events & depth raster for four recurrenc e intervals (corresponding to those evaluated in 
Task 2.8). 
• Estimated construction costs to implement the proj ect.  
• Estimated duration to implement the project. 
• The United States Army Corps of Engineers (USACE) National Structure Inventory (NSI) dataset 
to relate flood damages to building damages. 
• Change in annual or periodic maintenance costs for  the asset with and without the project. 
• Street closures and durations associated with floo ding events 
• Change in maintenance costs that would be incurred  after flooding events.  
o If provided by the City HDR will use provide eleva tion data to downtown business (i.e. 
doorway threshold elevations in flooding areas) to develop damage estimates. 
• HDR will extract and review structure data from US ACE’s NSI for the project area and structures 
affected by flooding events. A spreadsheet model will be constructed to combine NSI data with 
flooding depths to estimate property damages expected for flood events. 
 
Task 5.2 – FEMA BCA Modeling 
 
HDR will produce a Benefit-Cost Analysis following the FEMA BCA Toolkit. The model applies methods 
approved by FEMA, the NRCS, and USACE. Within the model, flood inundation depths from several 
recurrence intervals will be used to analyze each alternative. FEMA approved depth-damage curves will 
be used to estimate structure, contents, and inventory damage relative to the depths of flooding above 
foundation by building type. The analysis will be performed for each building and flood event in the project 
area. Damages will be aggregated and amortized to estimate the alternative’s total annual damage. 
Benefits will be estimated based on the difference between each proposed alternative and the no action 
alternative. Results of the spreadsheet model will be used in the FEMA BCA Toolkit model.
