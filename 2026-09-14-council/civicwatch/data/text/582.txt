BACKGROUND: 
 
This zone change application proposes changing the zoning from Harmony Valley PUD to NR-3 
Neighborhood Residential – High Density for roughly 6.50 acres of land. The land is currently vacant. The PUD 
allowed for high-density residential at this location, but limited the total number of units. Rather than amend 
the PUD to extend the unit cap, the applicant has instead requested a zone change to a high-intensity 
residential zone. 
 
SURROUNDING LAND USE AND ZONING: 
 
 
ANALYSIS OF APPLICATION CONFORMANCE WITH REVIEW CRITERIA: 
 
Should the Planning Commission wish to recommend approval of the Zoning Map Amendment, the 
Commission must make the following findings required in UDC 2.2.1.d. A detailed analysis is below: 
 
1) The application is in accordance with the development policies and physical 
patterns identified in the Comprehensive Plan.  (UDC 2.2.1.d.1) 
 
PlanCheyenne encourages opportunities for commercial and high-intensity residential growth 
within this area under this designation.

The Future Land Use Designation for this property is Mixed-Use Commercial. According to the 
comprehensive plan, this designation supports more commercial, light industrial, or live-work 
developments that could be “activity centers”. Residential use may be added to these areas in 
order to bring in more activity, but their development should be secondary to potential 
commercial development in the area. If residential developments are within the Mixed-Use 
Commercial area, there should be more than 8 dwelling units per acre. The ideal location for 
activity center is along principal arterial or minor arterial streets. This lot is located at the 
intersection of South Parsley Boulevard and West College Drive, two arterial streets. This results in 
higher volume of traffic than might be desired in residential areas.  
 
The optimal zone for this area is MUB Mixed-use Business Emphasis, although a mix of 
commercial and high-intensity residential zones may also accomplish the comprehensive plan 
objectives.  Based on the policies and future land use map, NR-3 is not the most appropriate zone 
for this area. The corridor is lacking in commercial zoning, and the mixed-use zone (within the 
PUD) to the east is already developed without commercial uses. The MUB zone in the UDC is 
essentially a combination of NR-3 and NB zones, the MUB even uses NR-3 lot types and 
residential design standards for purely residential development. Essentially, the proposed NR -3 
zone is the residential half of the optimal MUB zone. 
 
PlanCheyenne also supports flexibility to adjust to market demands and supports development of 
housing to meet demands, preferably with a variety of types and with access to amenities. 
Housing is needed in the Cheyenne, and this location has access to amenities and would allow for 
a variety of housing options. 
 
The proposed zone change largely complies with review criterion 1. 
 
2) The application is compatible with nearby property, specifically considering 
existing zoning, the Comprehensive Plan, and any other official plans and policies 
created under the guidance of that plan for these areas; (UDC 2.2.1.d.2) 
 
The proposed NR-3 zone would be isolated to this lot. To the north of the property is a 
combination of MR Medium Density Residential and Harmony Valley PUD. The Harmony Hill 
neighborhood is the residential north of this parcel. This development is comprised mostly of 
single-family dwelling units. East and northeast of this parcel is land zoned for Harmony Valley 
PUD, which it is currently being developed with duplexes, townhouses and multifamily 
development. To the south of the site is County Land Use, the land is vacant. To the west is 
County Light Industrial which has industrial operation on the land currently. The proposed NR-3 
zone is similar to the allowed uses of the Harmony Valley PUD to the east and northeast.   
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 2.

3) The suitability of the subject property for development according to the standards 
and uses of the existing zoning district, compared to the suitability of the subject 
property for development according to the standards and uses of the proposed 
zoning district (UDC 2.2.1.d.3) 
 
The NR-3 zone is a less intense zone than the existing zone. The PUD designates this area as High 
Density Residential within the PUD. This designation within the PUD allows for high density 
residential developments and commercial developments that would be allowed by right within 
the CB zone. The NR-3 would only allow for residential development to be permitted 
developments. While the current Harmony Valley allows for commercial uses, it also limits the 
total number of residential units. By shifting from Harmony Valley PUD to NR-3, the property is 
losing the ability to develop commercially, but gaining the ability to add more residential dwelling 
units. 
 
The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 3. 
 
4) The ability of the subject property and surrounding vicinity to be developed 
according to the Subdivision Standards in Article 4 considering the proposed 
zoning (UDC 2.2.1.d.4) 
 
No changes to the street network are proposed. Accesses will not be changed with the zone 
change. Infrastructure (streets and utilities) exist in the area. Water and sewer will likely need to be 
extended to the area. 
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 4. 
 
5) The ability of the application to support the Intent and Applicability statements of 
the proposed zoning district and achieve the Design Objectives of any specific 
standards. (UDC 2.2.1.d.5) 
 
The NR-3 district is intended to be used for high-density, urban residential use with consistent 
neighborhood charact. The regulations are meant to protect the neighborhood character of the 
district and protect the primarily residential use of land. 
 
The applicability to areas where more compact neighborhood development patterns are desired, 
specifically the Urban Residential category in the Cheyenne Comprehensive Plan.  
 
Typically, this district is located no more than ¼ mile or up to four blocks from any existing or 
planned Activity Center. Isolated application of this district without adjacency to activity center 
should be avoided so that significant concentrations of density do not exist without walking 
access to non-residential uses and quality urban amenities. This district should be supported by a 
highly connected street network with street design types that have a high degree of pedestrian 
amenities.  
 
The proposed zone is exchanging the Harmony Valley PUD for NR-3. The PUD outlines this area is 
to be High Density Residential. This designation allows for more intense development than the 
NR-3 zone allows. This parcel is not located near a commercial activity center, but is close to 
Harmony Valley’s park, various schools, and some limited commercial development south of 
PUDC-26-25   March 2. 2026

College. There are also employment centers to the west and northwest of this parcel, but due to 
topography and development patterns are not truly walkable destinations at this time.   
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 5. 
    
6) The ability of all potential future development to meet all applicable standards for 
development in the proposed zoning district, including other development options 
if any specific development proposed in association with the application is not 
executed after approval. (UDC 2.2.1.d.6) 
 
The proposed NR-3 zone would limit allowable development action within this area. The current 
zoning of Harmony Valley PUD designates this area High Density Residential, which under the 
PUD, allows for residential and commercial developments to be permitted by right. Changing to 
the zone NR-3 would lessen uses allowed to be built at this intersection. However, the PUD has a 
unit cap in the residential area, and changing to NR-3 allows for more flexible residential 
development. 
  
The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 6. 
 
7) The review and recommendations of other reviewing bodies. (UDC 2.2.1.d.7) 
 
No major comments received in regard to the proposed zone change.  
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 7. 
 
8) Whether the application will harm the public health, safety, or general welfare. 
(UDC 2.2.1.d.8) 
 
City staff is satisfied that this proposed zone change would not adversely affect the public health, 
safety, or general welfare. 
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 8.
