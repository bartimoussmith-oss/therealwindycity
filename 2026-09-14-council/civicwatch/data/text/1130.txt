Please find the attached Professional Service Agreement between the City and GLM Design Group 
for engineering services for a feasibility study, benefit-cost analysis, and preliminary design of the 
Cheyenne Dry Creek Union Pacific Railroad Project.   
 
The project is located along Dry Creek, both upstre am and downstream of the Union Pacific 
Railroad (UPRR). The Dry Creek Drainage Basin is approximately 14.6 square miles and extends 
from Warren Airforce Base to I-80 east of Cheyenne.  Development in basin has increased 
impervious area and associated stormwater runoff. I n a significant storm event, water impounds 
against the 25’ high UPRR track embankment east of Pershing Blvd and is released under the 
tracks through a historic 6’ by 8’ by 75’ masonry c ulvert. The impoundment of water against the 
UPRR embankment has led to more stringent drainage requirements in the basin than other basins 
in the city. 
 
The project will include a feasibility analysis rel ated to the boring of several culverts under the 
UPRR embankment and increased detention both west a nd east of the UPRR tracks. Several 
conveyance and detention scenarios will be investig ated and evaluated. The Dry Creek Master 
Plan Update (DMP) lists this as the top priority project for the Dry Creek Drainage basin. 
 
Engineering Staff evaluates fee proposals for engin eering services based on time and material 
estimates for the specific project.  Those costs ar e readily tracked and verified during design 
operations and the City only pays for the actual ti me expended. We believe this agreement will 
provide the required services to complete analysis and preliminary design portion of the project.

Exhibit A – Scope of Work 
Overview 
 
Consultant services for the Cheyenne Dry Creek Unio n Pacific Railroad Crossing are the development of 
a feasibility study, benefit cost analysis, and preliminary design plans for additional stormwater detention 
in Kiwanis Park and additional conveyance under the  Union Pacific Railroad (UPRR).  Any innovative 
solutions that are cost effective and meet project goals will be incorporated during project scoping 
negotiations and included in the analyses/design.  Work required for this project will include project  
management; field surveys, topographic data development, hydrologic/hydraulic analysis, and benefit cost 
analysis. 
The project requires a professional design team tha t includes a Civil Engineer registered in the State  of 
Wyoming with a minimum of 10 years of project desig n experience in one or more of the following 
disciplines: hydrologic/hydraulic analysis, and benefit cost analysis for major stormwater projects; with the 
ability to provide in-house or capacity to sub-consult for survey services and those disciplines required for 
the project.   
 
Project Description 
 
The project is located along Dry Creek, both upstre am and downstream of the Union Pacific Railroad 
(UPRR). The Dry Creek Drainage Basin is approximate ly 14.6 square miles and extends from Warren 
Airforce Base to I-80 east of Cheyenne. Development in basin has increased impervious area and associated 
stormwater runoff. In a significant storm event, wa ter impounds against the 25’ high UPRR track 
embankment east of Pershing Blvd and is released un der the tracks through a historic 6’ by 8’ by 75’ 
masonry culvert. The impoundment of water against t he UPRR embankment has led to more stringent 
drainage requirements in the basin than other basins in the city. 
The project will include a feasibility analysis rel ated to the boring of several culverts under the UP RR 
embankment and increased detention both west and ea st of the UPRR tracks. Several conveyance and 
detention scenarios to be investigated are presented later in this scope of work. 
The Dry Creek Master Plan Update (DMP) and the East  Park Greenway Underpass and Drainage Plan at 
the Union Pacific Railroad documents provide concep tual detention layouts and culvert configurations a t 
the now named Kiwanis Park for one of the scenarios  to be investigated. The DMP lists this as the top 
priority project for the Dry Creek Drainage basin.

Project Tasks and Deliverables 
 
The expected project tasks and project deliverables are as follows: 
Task 1: Feasibility Analysis 
• Feasibility analyses will be performed and will include in-depth discussions of the advantages 
and disadvantages of each scenario. Ultimately, the Consultant will provide recommendations on 
the preferred alternative. The following alternatives should be considered, at a minimum: 
o Increase detention in Kiwanis Park to minimize flow increases through the proposed 
culvert configuration under the UPRR tracks. This alternative will minimize downstream 
impacts through detention and attenuation solely on the west side of the UPRR line. 
o Allow for higher flow through the proposed culverts under the UPRR tracks and mitigate 
downstream impacts east of the UPRR line with minimal increases to detention west of 
the UPRR line.  
o Increase detention in Kiwanis Park with increased flow through the proposed culverts 
under the UPRR tracks and mitigation of downstream impacts east of the UPRR line. 
Mitigation of flooding will be accomplished on both sides of the UPRR line. 
o Optionally, incorporate any other mitigative measures not specifically identified in this 
scope of work. 
Task 2: Benefit Cost Analysis (BCA) 
• Provide a Benefit Cost Analysis (BCA) for the preferred alternative including a comparison to the 
existing un-modified site. Benefits should include the following, at a minimum: 
o Benefits to the City and Laramie County. 
o The potential for expanded future development in the Dry Creek Basin. 
o Benefits to the UPRR and interstate commerce.  
Task 3: Preliminary Design 
• Produce a preliminary plan (approximately 30% plan) of the preferred alternative. The plans 
should include the following, at a minimum: 
o Plan and profile of channel realignment. 
o Plan and profiles of conveyance structures. 
o Plan and cross sections of proposed detention features and dams. 
o Preliminary quantities and estimation of project probable cost.  
Implicate to the afore-mentioned tasks will be those steps necessary to complete the feasibility analyses, 
BCA, and preliminary plans. This will include, at a minimum, the evaluation and collection of 
topographical data; gathering of required geotechnical data; evaluation and supplementation of existing 
hydrologic and hydraulic models; preliminary design of the overall site, detention features, dams, and 
culverts under the UPRR tracks; preliminary construction cost estimation; and data gathering supporting 
benefit calculations.
