BACKGROUND: 
 
Frank Court was originally platted with Sunnyside Addition, 7th Filing in 1955. Since then, there has been 
several subsequent plats on either side of the platted road changing the layout of the land. Frank Court was 
not the original name of this section and it used to be a continuation of Monroe Avenue. Frank Court is not 
built to City street standards and is only used as access for two homes, one of which abuts Rock Springs 
Street.. This road will never extend to the north and is basically a large driveway. As right-of-way,  the City 
could be considered the appropriate maintenance agency.  
 
An east-west alley was once dedicated off of Frank Court, but later was vacated via a platting action. 
However, the owner next to the south half of the segment of the alley closest to Frank Court was not 
included in the plat, leaving the vacation status of the south half of that portion of alley ambiguous. This 
action also clearly vacates this potentially ambiguous right-of-way. 
 
City Staff is petitioning the Planning Commission and Governing Body for this vacation, as not all landowners 
adjacent to this street are actively desiring vacation. The property owner to the east would like to replat and 
would like to own the portion they utilize. The Governing Body heard approved the preliminary plat of the 
property to the east, which plat requires this street to be vacated.

ANALYSIS OF APPLICATION CONFORMANCE WITH REVIEW CRITERIA: 
 
Should the Planning Commission wish to recommend approval of the Street Vacation, the Commission 
must make the following findings required in UDC 2.2.4.d.  A detailed analysis is below:  
 
1) The vacation meets the purposes, intent, and applicable standards of Article 4.  (UDC 
2.1.4.d.1) 
 
Article 4 of the Unified Development Code is generally silent on right-of-way vacations of roads 
outside of downtown. Article 4, Section 4.1.1.a of the UDC seeks to “ensure that all City blocks and 
lots are served by necessary infrastructure services, including utilities, transportation, storm 
drainage, public safety, and community facilities, but recognize that necessary service levels may 
differ based on the context, character, and intensity of development.” The right-of-way proposed 
for vacation does not serve as a connection, and utilities utilizing this right-of-way will have 
easements provided.  
 
Article 4 also has minimum block size standards, and vacating a street could result in blocks which 
newly exceed these standards. This street does not provide any connections and Dry Creek and 
the development pattern of the neighborhood to the north makes any new connections that 
direction unlikely. 
     
Vacation of the property complies with this criterion.    
 
2) The ability of all existing and potential future development on adjacent lots to meet all 
applicable standards for development in the existing zoning district, including other 
development options in the event that any specific development proposed in assoc iation 
with the vacation is not executed after approval  (UDC 2.1.4.d.2). 
 
The proposed vacation is for an unbuilt street and does not substantively change the public -
private interface. Only two properties utilize this street and it is used mainly as a driveway for 
each. One of the two is abutting Rock Springs Street,. There will be an access easement granted to 
allow continued access to the parcel at the end of the street. The parcel closer to Rock Springs will 
receive half of the vacated street and will therefore be able to continue to use it as access to their 
property.  
 
Vacation of the property complies with this criterion.    
  
3) The vacation reflects sound planning and urban design principles with respect to existing 
potential future development in the vicinity, including consideration of current and future 
streets, open spaces, blocks and lots, and public utilities or facilities (UDC 2.1.4.d.3).  
 
The proposed vacation does not eliminate any publicly dedicated connections. Only one lot needs 
to use this property as access. All other adjacent lots have access to Rock Spring Street. The area 
to be vacated has utilities, but a utility easement is proposed to be included with the vacation.  
 
Vacation of the property complies with this criterion.

4) The application meets the public interest, and is not otherwise prohibited by other city, 
state, or federal laws (UDC 2.1.4.d.4). 
 
The portion of the right-of-way proposed to be vacated serves four owners, only two really utilize 
the street and it is mainly used as their driveway. If built, the expectation would be for the public 
to be responsible for street maintenance (through the City).    
 
Vacation of the property complies with this criterion.   
  
5) The review and recommendations of other reviewing bodies (UDC 2.1.4.d.5).  
 
The Board of Public Utilities made one comment regarding a main that they have stubbed up into 
Frank Ct along the western side that they would like an easement over or for the main to be 
abandoned and capped in Rock Springs. 
 
There are minor technical corrections that need to be made to the map prior to recording.  
 
Vacation of the property complies with this criterion, with conditions.    
   
CONFORMANCE WITH APPLICABLE PLANS: 
 
The project is located in an area covered by the following plans: 
• PlanCheyenne 
PlanCheyenne designates this area as Urban Residential. The Plan also encourages responsiveness to 
market demands and the responsible extension of infrastructure. Utilizing the resources required for the 
typical street build-out, and utilizing City resources for continued City maintenance after construction 
would not be responsible at this location. The vacation of this area will not adversely impact the ability for 
property to be accessed for residential uses, and may allow the property more flexibility in development.
