BACKGROUND: 
 
This zone change application proposes changing the zoning from County LI Light Industrial, AG Agricultural, 
and AR Agricultural Residential to LI Light Industrial for roughly 90 acres of land. The land is currently occupied 
by a tree nursery, rural residential development, and vacant land Outside of this application, discussion has 
occurred regarding development of a campground or recreational vehicle park; however, those intentions have 
not been formally stated. Campground and Recreational Vehicle Park land uses can be accommodated via 
conditional use process in the existing zone districts. 
 
The application has been submitted concurrently with a proposed future land use map amendment to 
change the subject property to Mixed-use Employment. Staff’s discussion and recommendation on the 
proposed future land use map amendment are found in that application’s staff report.  
 
The northernmost portion of land was annexed in 2015 and proposed to be platted as “Heiduck Addition”. The 
proposed zoning with the annexation and the plat was City LI Light Industrial. The underlying County zone was 
County LI and the City LI zone would be analogous to the County zone. There were some issues with the 
project that required corrective ordinances months later, and the plat never ended up getting recorded. 
Unfortunately, the Ordinance for the City zone was tied to the recording of the plat, not the recording of the 
annexation. The property still technically has not been given a City zone. However, as the original intent of the 
Council at time of annexation was LI zoning, and as representation on the zoning map for the City and County 
LI zones are identical, this property has been treated as zoned LI by previous staff. This zone change includes LI 
zoning for the northernmost parcel to clean-up the past issue. Should this zoning request zoning fail, staff will 
run a corrective ordinance for the northern area currently County LI.

ANALYSIS OF APPLICATION CONFORMANCE WITH REVIEW CRITERIA: 
 
Should the Planning Commission wish to recommend approval of the Zoning Map Amendment, the 
Commission must make the following findings required in UDC 2.2.1.d. A detailed analysis is below: 
 
1) The application is in accordance with the development policies and physical 
patterns identified in the Comprehensive Plan.  (UDC 2.2.1.d.1) 
 
The property is currently designated Mixed-use Residential by the Comprehensive Plan, but a 
comprehensive plan amendment has been submitted to change the designation to Mixed -use 
Employment. 
 
The current designation (Mixed-use Residential) has an optimal zone of MUR Mixed-use 
Residential, though a variety of residential zones and the NB zone may also be appropriate . The 
designation is intended for a mix of residential development with a mix of retail, offices, and light 
trade. Optimally, areas so designated would be less than one-third non-residential uses. The LI 
zone allows for no residential uses – encouraging instead warehousing, manufacturing, retail, and 
office uses. Some service uses (such as campgrounds or entertainment uses) are also permitted. 
The LI zone is largely incompatible with this future land use designation.   
 
The proposed designation (Mixed-use Employment) has an optimal zone of MUE Mixed-use 
Employment, though the BP, CB, and LI zones may also be appropriate depending on the context. 
The MUE designation encourages primarily office and light industry uses, with retail and service 
uses as secondary uses. Although not optimal, the LI zone would appropriate for this designation.  
 
Support for this application in policies in PlanCheyenne also hinge on the future land use map 
amendment proposal. For example, policies within PlanCheyenne encourage a mix of uses  in the 
Cheyenne region using the future land use map as a guide (1.1.A), the flexibility to respond to 
market demands (1.1.B), meeting community housing needs (2.5.A), dispersing housing

2) The application is compatible with nearby property, specifically considering 
existing zoning, the Comprehensive Plan, and any other official plans and policies 
created under the guidance of that plan for these areas; (UDC 2.2.1.d.2) 
 
Nearby property is zoned LI to the north, east and partly to the west. A PUD exists to the west as 
well, and LU and MUB zoning exist to the south.  
 
The PUD is the Overland Trails PUD which has designated this area of the PUD I Industrial. The 
Overland Trails PUD was originally adopted in 1984, and much of the PUD has been superseded 
by subsequent rezoning and new development plans. 
 
LI zoning is largely compatible with the current surrounding zoning.  
 
Nearby Future Land Use designations include Mixed-use Commercial to the northwest, Open 
Space and Parks to the north and west, Public and Quasi-public to the west, Mixed-use 
Employment to the east, and Mixed-use residential to the south and southwest. Based on the land 
use designations and existing zoning in the area, it would appear the comprehensive plan is 
expecting land west of the BNSF railroad to transition into housing and commercial development. 
The only designation which allows for industrial zoning and uses is across the barrier of the BNSF 
railroad. Based on the future land use plans for the area, LI zoning would not be compatible with 
surrounding properties. 
 
There is a request to change the future land use designation of the subject property from Mixed-
use Residential to Mixed-use Employment. Should this request be approved, the change would 
suggest that it would be acceptable to have light industrial uses ‘spill over’ from the east and the 
request would be compatible with the future land use designations in the area.  
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property does not comply with review criterion 2 
under the current future land use map designation but would comply if the concurrent 
application for future land use map designation changes is approved. 
   
3) The suitability of the subject property for development according to the standards 
and uses of the existing zoning district, compared to the suitability of the subject 
property for development according to the standards and uses of the proposed 
zoning district (UDC 2.2.1.d.3) 
  
The property is currently zoned County LI Light Industrial, AG Agricultural, and AR Agricultural 
Rural.  
 
The County zoning should be updated to a City zone prior to development.

The City AG zone is intended for agricultural and rural uses or as a holding zone for future 
development. The area currently zoned AG is inside the urban service boundary and has no 
natural features that would warrant limited development (such as a floodplain) – eventually this 
area would be anticipated to be changed to a more urban zone and develop with more urban 
uses. The proposed LI zone would allow for such development. 
 
The City AR zone is intended for rural residential uses and is one of the more restrictive zones in 
the City. The area zoned AR is within the urban service boundary and has no natural features that 
would warrant limited development (such as a floodplain). Almost any other City zone would 
allow for more urban and diverse development, including the proposed LI zone.  
 
The proposed LI zone has limited design standards and there are no site-specific issues that 
would limit the ability for the site to develop within applicable design standards.  
 
The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 3. 
 
4) The ability of the subject property and surrounding vicinity to be developed 
according to the Subdivision Standards in Article 4 considering the proposed 
zoning (UDC 2.2.1.d.4) 
 
Future development in the area will be highly constrained by I-80 and the BNSF railroad. 
Regionally, connectivity is also limited by I-25, College Drive, and the UP railroad. These barriers 
make developing to Article 4 standards for lots and blocks difficult for any development in any 
zone district. LI zoning generally has less expectations for an interconnected street network at the 
local-street level.  
 
A Major Collector Street is identified on the Transportation Master Plan immediately south of the 
area proposed for change. This street is and Extension of Allison Road between Southwest Drive 
and Parsley Boulevard. Right-of-way dedication and improvements will be required in conjunction 
with future development actions such as platting. 
 
No changes to the street network are proposed. Accesses will not be changed with the zone 
change. Infrastructure (streets and utilities) exist in the area. 
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 4. 
 
5) The ability of the application to support the Intent and Applicability statements of 
the proposed zoning district and achieve the Design Objectives of any specific 
standards. (UDC 2.2.1.d.5) 
 
The UDC indicates that the LI district is intended for environmentally-controlled manufacturing or 
intensive employment uses that may have accessory commercial, service or office uses. The LI 
district is applicable to larger land areas that are capable of providing substantial buffers to 
adjacent sites. This district is most appropriate in areas with access to major regional 
transportation infrastructure, specifically areas designated Industrial in the Comprehensive Plan, 
and in limited applications within Mixed-use Employment areas.

The subject property is a larger area capable of providing buffers and adjacent to an interstate 
and a railroad – with ready access to a second interstate. The site largely meets the intent and 
applicability as outlined in the UDC, with the exception that the LI zone is not compatible with 
Mixed-use Residential designated areas in the comprehensive plan. If the area is changed to be 
designated ‘Mixed Use Employment’, the LI zoning is fully compatible.  
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property largely complies with review criterion 5 
under the current future land use map designation but would comply if the concurrent 
application for future land use map designation change is approved. 
    
6) The ability of all potential future development to meet all applicable standards for 
development in the proposed zoning district, including other development options 
if any specific development proposed in association with the application is not 
executed after approval. (UDC 2.2.1.d.6) 
 
No specific development of the site has been noted with the formal rezoning application; 
however, unofficially it has been discussed that the applicant wishes to construct a campground 
or recreational vehicles park. These uses can be approved via conditional use approval in the AG 
and AR zones, but are permitted uses in the LI zone (and the LI zone is the only zone where these 
uses are permitted without conditional use review). 
 
Should the campground or recreational vehicle park use not come to fruition, the LI zone allows 
for a variety of limited industrial, office, and retail uses, and the site’s location and size would be 
versatile enough to allow a wide range of potential uses. 
 
The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 6. 
 
7) The review and recommendations of other reviewing bodies. (UDC 2.2.1.d.7) 
 
No major comments were received in regard to the proposed zone change that have not been 
covered under other review criteria.  
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 7. 
 
8) Whether the application will harm the public health, safety, or general welfare. 
(UDC 2.2.1.d.8) 
 
Cheyenne has had documented housing challenges and taking land anticipated for residential (or 
mostly residential) development and zoning that land industrial would adversely affect the 
general welfare of the community. However, if the future land use designation changes and the 
land is no longer anticipated for residential development, City staff is satisfied that this proposed 
zone change would not adversely affect the public health, safety, or general welfare.  
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property does not comply with review criterion 8 
under the current future land use map designation but would comply if the concurrent 
application for future land use map designation change is approved.

STAFF REVIEW: 
 
Through the review process, review comments were received expressing concerns about the 
appropriateness of the LI zone at this location. Staff comments also discussed the ability of a possible 
proposed use (campground or RV park) to be developed in the current zones (via conditional use).  Staff 
also mentioned drainage and traffic concerns that may become necessary to address with the site  plan. 
 
CONCLUSION: 
 
The application has been submitted concurrently with a proposed future land use map amendment to 
change the subject property to Mixed-use Employment. Staff’s conclusion on the proposal to zone land LI 
Light Industrial hinges on the appropriateness of the associated future land use map amendment 
proposal.  
 
Should the future land use map amendment be approved, the extension of ‘mixed -use employment’-style 
development across the BNSF railroad would be considered appropriate and the application would meet 
all review criteria. 
 
On the other hand, if the future land use map amendment is denied, this request would fail to meet 
review criteria 1, 2, and 8, and meeting review criterion 5 would be less clear-cut.
