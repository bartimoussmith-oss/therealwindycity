AMERICORPS SENIORS FOSTER GRANDPARENT PROGRAM

ITEMS.” 
 
 WHEREAS, the Governing Body of the City of Cheyenne (“City”) requests assistance in 
funding the AmeriCorps Seniors Foster Grandparent Program mentoring and enrichment 
program and related necessary support items (the “Project”); 
 
 WHEREAS, the City recognizes the need for this Project; 
 
 WHEREAS, the City desires to participate in the AmeriCorps Seniors Foster Grandparent 
Program grant program; 
 
 WHEREAS, the AmeriCorps Seniors Foster Grandparent Program has certain criteria 
that must be met as established by AmeriCorps, and to the best of the City’s knowledge, the 
proposed application satisfies those criteria; 
 
 WHEREAS, grant funding for this Project will be considered by  AmeriCorps after 
submission on April 3, 2026; 
 
 WHEREAS, the total estimated annual programming budget is Three Hundred Twenty-
six thousand Sixty-Eight ($326,068.00), and the City requests a grant in this amount; and 
 
 WHEREAS, the City is not required to provide a matching contribution for this grant. 
 
 NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED BY THE GOVERNING BODY OF THE 
CITY OF CHEYENNE, WYOMING , that the City supports the submission of a continuation 
application for the AmeriCorps Seniors Foster Grandparent Program grant to fund the Project; 
 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the Mayor of the City of Cheyenne is authorized to 
sign and submit an application for an AmeriCorps Seniors Foster Grandparent Program grant in 
the amount of Three Hundred Twenty-Six Thousand Sixty-Eight Dollars ($326,068.00) per year, 
for a total amount of Nine Hundred Seventy -Eight Thousand Two Hundred Four Dollars 
($978,204.00), to be submitted to AmeriCorps Seniors for renewal for three (3) years beginning 
July 1, 2026, through June 30, 2029, for assistance in funding the Project; and 
 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that  the Mayor of the City of Cheyenne is hereby 
designated as the authorized representative of the City to act on behalf of the Governing Body on 
all matters relating to this grant application, and, in the event grant funds are awarded, the Mayor 
is authorized to execute the grant agreement and any associated documents.

The City of Cheyenne Office of Youth Alternatives proposes to have 30-55 AmeriCorps 
Senior volunteers who will serve through tutoring and mentoring student interaction, 
promoting social/emotional development in Laramie, Platte, and Goshen counties. 
AmeriCorps Seniors volunteers will serve 36 Volunteer Service Years (VSY) each year. The 
primary focus area of this project is Education for school readiness and K-12 success.  
AmeriCorps Seniors volunteers will be responsible for approximately 540 students 
receiving services from Foster Grandparent volunteers. The AmeriCorps investment of 
$326,068.00 will be supplemented by an anticipated $51,272.00.00 in resources.

Wyoming, located in the mountain region of the Western United States, is the 10th largest 
state by area, totaling 97,814 square miles. It is also the least populous at six people per 
square mile and is the second most populated state with a total population of 587,618. Of 
the total population,111,865 are 65 and over a 12.1 % increase, with an additional 22%

Health, by 2030, over one-ﬁfth of the state's population is expected to be over age 65. 
Given that Wyoming is considered a rural state, characterized mainly by the mountain 
ranges and rangelands of the Rocky Mountains, obtaining necessary services such as 
healthcare becomes difficult for many of the state's residents. Wyoming Medicaid's long-
term care costs are projected to increase to $184 million by 2030, according to the 
Wyoming Department of Health's Annual Report. However, this may change due to

median income rates also fell, according to the Department of Health 2023 report. 17.9 %

Volunteerism improves health by strengthening the body, improving mood, and lessening 
stress in participants, according to Volunteer Hub. Volunteers are an asset and should be 
treated as such. The Nonproﬁt Hub suggested tips including the following: say thanks; 
build a community; equip them; respect their time; show them impact; and listen to their 
feedback. With these tips in mind, volunteers will be thanked in a variety of ways for the 
time they have served.  They must see their service time as helping the program achieve its 
annual target of required hours. This will be accomplished through shout-outs at monthly 
meetings where progress towards goals will be reported. Volunteers will receive years-of-
service pins, certiﬁcates of appreciation, lifetime service awards, and additional 
recognition as appropriate. Volunteers will be recognized as valued individuals through 
cards/ notes for accomplishments, birthdays, anniversaries, and personal sentiments 
when needed. Time will be allotted at monthly meetings for volunteers to become 
acquainted. Structured and unstructured "Get to Know You" activities will encourage a 
sense of belonging and ownership of the program. 
Making new friendships and staying socially engaged is vital to the emotional stability of 
older adults out of the workforce and who may have experienced a loss of friends and 
family or are socially isolated. Through ongoing contact, volunteers will feel supported by 
agency staff providing solution-focused ideas, coaching, and encouragement in difficult 
situations. Staff will work diligently to ensure volunteer assignments match the Foster 
Grandparents' skills and abilities. Through successful assignments, Foster Grandparents 
will know they are valued and their work is essential.  Foster Grandparents will be apprised 
of their impact through various modalities and will see the importance of their role in the 
program and how it improves their communities. A volunteer evaluation of the program will 
be conducted annually. The evaluation will seek feedback on how the program has 
impacted the volunteer's life and whether it meets their needs. The evaluation will allow 
the Foster Grandparent's to submit suggestions for improvement and future trainings. Two 
recognition events will be planned for the volunteers, including a Youth Alternatives annual 
Volunteer Appreciation event, and a Winter Appreciation event.  Volunteers, community 
representatives, volunteer agencies, elected officials, and others will be invited to these 
events to demonstrate the volunteers' importance and service. U.S. military Veterans will 
also be recognized at these events and receive special community recognition in 
conjunction with Veteran's Day.

Recruitment and Development:  Youth Alternatives Foster Grandparent Program recruits on 
an everyday basis. Recruitment annually has three to four billboards placed around 
Cheyenne, ﬂyers posted in multiple locations, multiple times in all counties. Presentations 
continue to be done in senior living complexes and every county. Public Service 
Announcements (PSA) are put in the local newspaper and the Trader's paper every other 
month, and postcards sent out to senior living spaces. Community volunteer fairs or events 
that bring people together are attended to for recruitment opportunities.  Recruitment 
priority is to have volunteers from all community areas, including  English Language 
Learners, individuals with disabilities, and veterans or military family members. The plan is 
to have speaking engagements to invite and educate all in the community about the Foster 
Grandparent opportunities and to invite the speciﬁc groups to speak at our trainings so 
they can educate us on how to get them more involved in the program.  Advertisements will 
be geared toward adults over 55 that will qualify and meet the AmeriCorps Guidelines. 
A comprehensive orientation is provided to qualifying applicants. Training is done on the 
sponsoring organization's mission and programs, program-speciﬁc rules and regulations, 
and working with children having special needs, building positive relationships with school 
professionals, and other applicable topics. Various methods will be used, including videos, 
guest speakers, YouTube Videos, and group discussions. Volunteers will receive a 
minimum of 20 hours of pre-service training. A combination of classroom instruction and 
job shadowing will be utilized. Ongoing instruction and continuous volunteer development 
are essential to ensure volunteer success. Therefore, monthly training on topics of interest 
identiﬁed by Foster Grandparents and current trends and issues will be provided.  
Local media, including newspapers, radio, television, and social media, will be utilized to 
recruit and retain individuals who qualify to be Foster Grandparent. In addition, 
participation in community events such as health fairs, Super Day in the Park, workforce 
service career fairs, and school functions will increase community awareness of the 
program and available opportunities.

Youth Alternatives Foster Grandparent Program plans and infrastructure to ensure 
management of volunteer stations will include Memorandums of Understanding (MOU) 
developed with LCSD #1, LCSD #2, GCSD #1, and PCSD #1. Additionally, MOUs will be 
developed with Wyoming Child and Family Development (Head Start), Boys and Girls Club. 
The MOUs will be negotiated between the Mayor, the Director of Youth Alternatives, the 
FGP Coordinator, and the appropriate volunteer agency personnel. The MOUs will outline 
the responsibilities and expectations of each party. The MOUs will include appropriate and 
inappropriate activities volunteers may and may not engage in.  The MOUs will also have an 
outline of expected paperwork, including Volunteer Assignment Descriptions, Volunteer 
Evaluations, Timesheet Approvals, and the culture of the volunteer station for safe spaces 
for the volunteers. The MOUs will also include any in-kind donations and the value to be 
provided by the volunteer stations. FGP staff will work directly with volunteer station staff 
such as principals, teachers, and other supervisory staff to assign volunteers in safe 
spaces. Meetings will be arranged with teachers, principals, and supervisory staff to 
provide station training on supervising volunteers and reviewing the station/ site training to 
include the FG Handbook. FGP staff members will make formal and informal visits to 
volunteer stations throughout the year to ensure that volunteers perform appropriate 
service activities and are in a culturally safe environment. Youth Alternatives Foster 
Grandparent Program will collect data to show the program's impact on academic 
achievement, graduation rates, and social/ emotional growth supported by the volunteers. 
The plan for addressing the culture of volunteer stations was discussed above in Youth 
Alternatives mission is to intervene in the lives of young people at an impressionable stage 
in their emotional growth and development when positive behaviors are more easily 
formed. Youth Alternatives is committed to equal treatment when addressing community 
needs and Performance Measures by providing fair treatment and access to service by 
meeting all the eligibility components and providing the opportunity to grow and advance in 
their service to the community and in reaching the Performance Measures.
