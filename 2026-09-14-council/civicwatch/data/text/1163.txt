Introduction:     
On June 21, 2021, the Governing Body adopted 
Resolution No. 6156 declaring portions of the 
city of Cheyenne as blight and authorizing the 
redevelopment of those areas utilizing the 
urban renewal tools authorized under state 
statutes. The initial blight declaration included 
three areas of the community, the Reed Rail 
Corridor, the Hitching Post area on West 
Lincolnway, and properties referred to as the 
Hynds and the Hole northwest of Lincolnway 
and Capitol Avenue. Since adoption, the blight 
study has been amended to include these four 
other areas of the community:  
• West 15th Street roughly between 
Bent and the Historic Depot 
(Resolution No. 6234 May 9, 2022),  
• Jolly Rogers northwest of Whitney 
Road and U.S. 30 (Resolution No. 
6334, July 10, 2023) 
• Expanded Downtown generally 
west of Warren Avenue and east of 
Bent Avenue (Resolution No. 6334, 
July 10, 2023) 
• Ridge and Holland which is an area 
of land west of Ridge Road ant 
Holland Court (Resolution No. 6364 
November 13, 2023).

Conditions of Blight 
City staff conducted a comprehensive survey 
using the guidelines outlined in Wyoming State 
Law to understand better which areas in 
Cheyenne may qualify for urban renewal. 
Specifically, the survey was based on nine 
conditions of blight listed in the Wyoming Urban 
Renewal Code (WY Stat. § 15-9-103(iii)). 
Although the law also defines slum conditions, 
these were not used separately in the survey 
since they closely overlap with the blight 
criteria. 
Staff developed the survey to assess the 
following nine conditions—plus an “Other” 
category: 
• Deteriorated or deteriorating 
buildings or structures 
• Poor or unsafe street layout 
• Faulty lot design (size, access, or 
usefulness) 
• Unsanitary or unsafe environmental 
conditions 
• Site deterioration or damaged 
infrastructure 
• Complicated property ownership or 
tax issues 
• Delinquent taxes that exceed the 
land’s value 
• Legal title problems or irregularities 
• Dangerous conditions that may 
endanger life or property (such as 
fire hazards) 
• Other: This covers a broader 
definition from state law that 
includes factors impairing 
community growth, discouraging 
housing development, or creating a 
risk to public health, safety, or 
welfare. 
For each condition observed, staff checked a 
box and wrote a brief description explaining 
what indicators were used to identify the issue. 
There was also space for notes on anything 
important that didn’t fit the predefined 
categories. Unlike some neighboring states, 
such as Colorado, Wyoming does not require a 
certain number of blight conditions for an area 
to qualify. That means the area can be 
considered blighted under state law if just one 
condition is found. 
Deteriorated or Deteriorating 
Structures 
In the context of a blight study, deteriorated or 
deteriorating structures refer to buildings and 
infrastructure that show visible signs of physical 
decline, neglect, or damage, which can 
negatively impact an area's overall quality, 
safety, and aesthetics. This criterion is critical 
because the condition of structures significantly 
influences property values, livability, and the 
community's ability to attract new investments 
or residents. 
  
 
 
Page 5 
 
Characteristics of Deteriorated or Deteriorating 
Structures: 
• Physical Damage: Includes issues like cracks 
in walls or foundations, broken windows, 
collapsing roofs, or unstable structural 
elements. 
• Neglect: Prolonged lack of maintenance 
leading to peeling paint, rusted fixtures, 
overgrown landscaping, or damaged 
exteriors.  
• Obsolete Design: Due to outdated building 
materials or layouts, structures no longer 
meet modern safety, efficiency, or use 
standards.  
• Unsafe Conditions: Problems such as 
exposed wiring, mold, or significant water 
damage that pose health and safety risks. 
• Poor Maintenance: Cracked pavement, 
potholes, faded markings, or obstructed 
signage that compromise the usability and 
safety of the streets. 
Findings 
Currently, the study area exhibits limited visible 
evidence of deteriorated or deteriorating 
structures. Most of the existing commercial and 
industrial buildings within the boundary appear 
structurally sound, and significant physical 
damage or noticeable signs of neglect are not 
readily apparent from aerial observations. 
However, the city recognizes several parcels, 
mainly vacant lots, unused parking areas, and 
underutilized buildings, present a lack of activity 
and investment. Over time, this ongoing 
inactivity, if not proactively addressed, could 
contribute significantly to deterioration criteria, 
including structural degradation, increasing 
neglect, and potential physical decline. 
Specifically, the presence of multiple vacant or 
minimally utilized lots, especially those adjacent 
to the railroad tracks, suggests that if left 
unmanaged, these areas could experience 
gradual deterioration through reduced property 
maintenance, deferred upkeep, and 
encroachment by overgrown landscaping or 
debris accumulation.  
Although immediate conditions in South 
Holliday do not yet strongly reflect 
deterioration, the City of Cheyenne recognizes 
that ongoing inactivity and underutilization in 
several vacant lots and buildings could, over 
time, increase vulnerability to structural 
degradation and broader blight characteristics.

Findings 
South Holliday exemplifies a range of urban 
challenges that inhibit its potential as a dynamic 
and functional urban environment. Issues in 
connectivity, design, infrastructure efficiency, 
and maintenance collectively constrain the 
area's growth and vitality.  
Lack of Connectivity

urban spaces with their surroundings, 
promoting accessibility, and fostering 
community cohesion. Unfortunately, South 
Holliday suffers from a notable lack of 
connectivity, which isolates it from key urban 
arteries and neighboring areas. 
• Limited Accessibility: The area's dearth 
of direct and coherent routes limits its 
integration with adjacent residential, 
commercial, and industrial zones, 
stifling economic and social interactivity. 
Access limitations include arterial 
streets within the area and the Union 
Pacific railroad to the south.  
• Fragmented Pathways: Incomplete 
sidewalks and the absence of 
multimodal transport options restrict 
pedestrian and cyclist movement, 
diminishing the area’s inclusivity. This 
includes sidewalks and greenway paths.

of South Holliday reflect a misalignment

of a diverse urban populace. 
• Lack of Cohesion: The absence of a

fragmented land use and undefined 
spaces, impeding the area’s potential to 
thrive as a harmonious urban district. 
• Neglect of Human Scale: A deficiency in 
walkable environments, active street-

undermines efforts to establish an 
engaging and vibrant urban 
atmosphere. 
• Underutilized Parcels: Numerous vacant 
or inefficiently utilized parcels reflect a 
missed opportunity for the area to 
perform as a productive urban asset.

Conclusions   
The South Holliday study area in Cheyenne 
demonstrates several criteria indicative of 
potential blight, despite exhibiting limited 
visible structural deterioration. Predominantly 
commercial and industrial, the district includes 
numerous large buildings, warehouses, parking 
lots, and multiple vacant or minimally utilized 
parcels, notably adjacent to railroad tracks, 
suggesting potential sites for redevelopment. 
 
Although immediate structural deterioration is 
minimal, the persistent inactivity and 
underutilization of several lots and structures 
pose long-term risks. Specifically, parcels near 
the railroad tracks have heightened vulnerability 
due to potential environmental contamination 
associated with railroad operations and 
historical industrial uses. The proximity to 
railroad infrastructure raises concerns about 
hazardous materials spills and contamination 
risks. 
Most of the South Holliday area is within the 
City-defined Holliday Basin 100-year floodplain. 
Beyond FEMA-designated flood hazard areas, 
the City of Cheyenne has proactively established 
local flood hazard zones based on site-specific 
flooding conditions, aiming to protect public 
health, safety, and welfare and reduce flood-
related damages. Poorly maintained or 
insufficient drainage systems amplify flooding 
risks, further exacerbating environmental 
vulnerabilities. Infrastructure issues also 
prominently affect this area. Inadequate street 
lighting potentially increases crime and accident 
risks, while irregular lot shapes and limited 
street connectivity pose accessibility challenges. 
Parcel configurations and existing roadway 
alignments, including significant curves and 
intersections, reduce sight distances, restrict 
safe ingress and egress, and complicate 
compliance with engineering spacing standards. 
 
The proximity to railroad infrastructure presents 
additional environmental risks due to potential 
contaminants associated with rail operations, 
hazardous material storage, and industrial 
activities, emphasizing the necessity for careful 
ecological assessment and proactive 
intervention. 
These factors—irregular lot configurations, 
access limitations due to engineering spacing 
standards, inadequate infrastructure, proximity 
to the railroad, and local floodplain conditions—
underscore the area's susceptibility to blight. 
Strategic, proactive urban planning 
interventions will be essential to mitigate these 
vulnerabilities and enhance South Holliday's 
long-term viability and resilience.
