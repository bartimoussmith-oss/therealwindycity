TO:  Honorable Mayor Patrick Collins and City of Cheyenne Council Members 
FROM:  Robin Lockman, City Treasurer 
DATE:  March 4, 2025 
SUBJECT: Investment Policy Review and Update 
 
 
On January 28, 2025, the City's Investment Committee voted to recommend approval of revisions 
to the City's Investment Policy . The voting members of the committee include Mayor Patrick 
Collins, City Council President Mark Rinne, City Council man Tom Segrave, and the City 
Treasurer. 
 
Mr. Jeffrey Prince, a local investment expert with Wells Fargo Advisors, serves as a non -voting 
member of this Committee and reviewed and provided feedback on the proposed policy changes. 
The City's Investment Advisor, Federated Hermes, also reviewed the policy to ensure compliance 
with Wyoming State Statutes. 
 
The proposed revisions enhance clarity  and align with best practices (e.g., collateralization 
standards, liquidity management).  Significant changes include the following: 
 
Page 5 Revisions 
 
Added Local Government Investment Pools 
• Wyoming CLASS and Wyoming Government Investment Fund  are now explicitly listed 
as authorized local government investment pools in Wyoming, alongside WyoSTAR I and 
II. 
Collateralization Requirements for Certificate of Deposits (CDs) 
• Collateral must equal 105% of the market value  of the principal and accrued interest for 
CDs, excluding FDIC insured amounts. 
• Monthly Verification: The City Treasurer must now confirm that pledged collateral 
maintains sufficient value to meet this requirement. 
 
Page 6 Revisions 
 
Diversification Limit 
• 5% Obligor Limit: No single obligor (excluding federal government, authorized pools, or 
CDs) may exceed 5% of the City’s total investment portfolio. 

2 
 
Liquidity Flexibility 
• Removed Six-Month Budgeted Expenditure Rule : The portfolio must remain sufficiently 
liquid to meet reasonably anticipated operating needs , rather than adhering to a rigid six-
month budgeted expenditure threshold.  
 
Page 7 Revisions 
 
Benchmark Clarification 
• Federated Hermes Benchmark: Updated to reflect Federated Hermes’ standard benchmark 
for accurate performance measurement. 
 
Pool Terminology 
• Replaced “WyoSTAR”  with “one of the local government investment pools” to reflect 
broader participation options. 
 
Reporting Requirements 
• Bi-Annual Reports : The City Treasurer must  now submit investment reports to 
the Investment Committee and Governing Body  every six months, ensuring transparency 
and accountability.

Investment Policy Statement 
 
 
The purpose of this  Investment Policy Statement is to establish investment objectives, provide 
investment guidelines, and set forth responsibilities and procedures necessary for the prudent 
management and investment of the funds of the City of Cheyenne (“City”).   
 
I. Governing Authority 
 
This Investment Policy is adopted pursuant to W.S. § 9 -4-831(h). The investment program 
governed by this Policy shall be operated in conformance with all federal, state, and local legal 
and policy requirements, including the requirements set forth in W.S. § 9 -4-816 through 
W.S. § 9-4-831. 
 
II. Scope 
 
This Policy applies to the activities of the City regarding investing the financial assets of all funds, 
except funds held in checking accounts and funds under the control of the City of Cheyenne Board 
of Public Utilities.   
 
Except for funds in certain restricted and special funds  or accounts, the City may commingle its 
funds to maximize investment earnings and to increase efficiencies regarding investment pricing, 
safekeeping and administration. Investment income will be allocated to the various funds based on 
their respective participation and in accordance with generally accepted accounting principles.   
 
This Policy also applies to all commingled funds. 
 
III. General Objectives 
 
Investment activities shall occur in a manner that strives for the highest possible risk-adjusted total 
return consistent with the following primary objectives, in priority order:  
 
1. Safety:  The safety and preservation of principal is the primary and foremost objective  of 
the investment program. Investments shall be made that seek to ensure the preservation of 
capital in the overall portfolio . The goal will be to mitigate credit risk , interest rate risk, 
and custodial risk.   
2. Liquidity:  The investment portfolio shall remain sufficiently liquid to meet all operating 
requirements that may be reasonably  anticipated. This shall be accomplished  by 
maintaining appropriate balances in investment vehicles that fall within the most liquid 
asset classes; and structuring the portfolio so that securities mature concurrent with cash 
needs to meet anticipated demands.  
3. Yield:  The investment portfolio shall be managed to maximize the return on investments  
consistent with the safety and liquidity objectives described above, with the goal of 
exceeding the stated benchmark returns over a full market cycle.  
4. Local Community Investment:   The investment portfolio shall be managed with a 
commitment toward s local economic development . Investments in local institutions, 
domestic instruments, or through local brokers, where possible, are preferred.

IV. Delegation of Authority and Responsibilities 
 
Governing Body :  The Governing Body of the City of Cheyenne retains ultimate fiduciary 
responsibility for the City’s financial assets, investment program and this Policy. Upon its request, 
the Governing Body shall be given reports about the City’s investments. A copy of this Policy 
shall be reviewed and approved by the Governing Body on, at least, a biennial basis. 
 
Investment Officer:  Authority to manage the City’s investment program is granted to the City 
Treasurer, also referred to herein as the City’s Investment Officer, who shall act in accordance 
with this Policy, established written procedures , and all internal controls established for the 
operation of the investment program.   
 
Investment Committee:  An Investment Committee shall be established to  assist the Investment 
Officer in managing the investment program and to facilitate direct communication to the 
Governing Body. The Investment Committee shall be composed of the following persons: 
 
1.   Two members of the City Council appointed by the President of the City  Council, for 2-
year terms. These members shall be voting members of the Committee and one member of 
the City Council shall be appointed Chair of the Investment Committee.  
2.   The Mayor or designee. The Mayor or his/her designee shall be a voting member of the 
Committee. 
3.   The City Treasurer. The City Treasurer shall be a voting member of the Committee. 
4.   The Deputy City Treasurer. The Deputy City Treasurer shall be a non -voting member of 
the Committee. 
5.   The City Attorney or designee. The City Attorney or his/her designee shall be a non-voting 
member of the Committee. 
6.   Two non-City employees with financial expertise shall be appointed by the Chairman of 
the Committee for 2 -year terms. These members shall be non-voting members of the 
Committee. 
7.   Administrative staff shall be provided by the City Council’s Office. 
 
The Investment Committee shall meet on a biannual basis at a time designated by the Chair of the 
Investment Committee . If needed, the Chair of the Investment Committee can call a special 
meeting. As part of its duties, the Investment Committee shall: 
 
• Undertake a biannual review of the City's investment activities , and recommend changes 
as needed; and 
• On a periodic basis, review, comment upon, and make changes to this Policy. 
 
Investment Advisor s:  The City may engage the services of one or more external investment 
advisors/managers to assist in the management of the City’s investment portfolio in a manner 
consistent with the objectives contained herein. Such external managers may be granted discretion 
to purchase and sell investment securities in accordance with this Policy. Such managers must be 
registered under the Investment Advisers Act of 1940 and are subject to all requirements relating 
to investment advisers under Wyoming law. The firm shall have at least a three (3) year verifiable 
track record and a total assets under management (AUM) of at least $500 million.

V. Standards of Care  
 
All participants in the investment process shall be held to the highest standards of professional and 
ethical conduct. Each participant should be committed to honesty, integrity, and accountability in 
all of his or her decisions and actions relating to the investment process. 
 
Compliance with Laws, Rules, Regulations and Policies:   All participants in the investment 
process must comply with any and all applicable laws, rules and regulations and th e terms and 
provisions of this Investment Policy. 
 
Prudence:  The standard of care to be applied by all participants in the investment process shall 
be the “prudent person” standard.   
 
Any person, persons, or third parties investing or managing the City’s financial assets, shall do so 
as a prudent investor would, by considering the purposes, terms, distribution requirements, and 
other circumstances, and exercising reasonable care, skill and caution in all decisions and actions. 
  
Conflicts of Interest:   All participants in the investment process have a duty to avoid financial, 
business or other relationships which might cause a conflict with the performance of their duties 
and could reasonably be expected to interfere with their duties or their ability to  make unbiased 
and objective recommendations and decisions. Each participant should conduct himself or herself 
so as to avoid even the appearance of such conflicts of interest. 
 
To avoid conflicts of interest, all participants in the investment process shall, at a minimum: 
 
• refrain from personal and business activity that could conflict with proper execution and 
management of the investment program, or that could impair their ability to make impartial 
investment decisions; 
• disclose to the Mayor of the City of Cheyenne any material interest in financial institutions 
with which they conduct business; and 
• refrain from undertaking any personal investment transactions with the same individual 
(e.g., investment advisor) with whom business is conducted on behalf of the City. 
 
Internal Controls:  The City Treasurer shall establish a written system of internal guidelines and 
controls, which shall be reviewed and approved by the investment committee and the City’s 
independent auditor. The controls shall be designed to prevent the loss of public funds arising from 
fraud, employee error, misrepresentation by third parties, unanticipated changes in financial 
markets, or imprudent actions by the Investment Officer and participants in the investment process. 
The guidelines and controls should include, but not be limited to: 
 
• frequent reconciliation of investment and accounting records; 
• written confirmation of transactions; 
• annual independent reviews by an external auditor to assure compliance with this policy;  
• appropriate training for participants in the investment process; and 
• a list of approved financial institutions, depositories, and broker/dealers (see below).

VI. Authorized Financial Institutions, Depositories, and Broker/Dealers  
 
The Investment Officer will maintain a list of approved financial institutions, depositories, and 
securities broker/dealers selected by a process that ensures due diligence in the selection process. 
The City will limit all security purchases to financial institutions and broker/dealers on the 
approved lists.  
 
Financial institutions and depositories eligible to transact investment business with the City are 
those listed in W.S. § 9-4-817 and must be federally insured through the Federal Deposit Insurance 
Corporation (FDIC). 
 
• banks incorporated under the laws of Wyoming; 
• national banks; 
• savings and loan associations incorporated under the laws of Wyoming; and 
• federal savings and loan associations and federal savings banks. 
 
All financial institutions who desire to become  qualified for investment transactions must supply 
the Investment Officer with the following information, as requested: 
 
• audited financial statements; 
• proof of Financial Industry Regulatory Authority certification; 
• trading resolution; 
• proof of state registration; 
• certification of having read the City's Investment Policy; and 
• depository contracts. 
 
All broker/dealers who desire to become qualified for investment transactions must supply the 
following: 
 
• proof of proper registration with their appropriate regulatory agency;  
• proof of insurance, either through private or regulatory sources, satisfactory to the 
Investment Committee;  
• proof of satisfactory record of past performance; and  
• proof of registration to do business in the State of Wyoming.   
 
An annual review of the financial condition and registration of qualified financial institutions and 
broker/dealers will be conducted by the Investment Officer.  
 
VII. Delivery, Payment and Safekeeping 
 
Delivery vs. Payment:   All trades of marketable securities entered into  by the City shall be 
conducted on a deli very-versus-payment (DVP) basis to ensure that securities are deposited in 
eligible financial institutions prior to the release of funds.

Safekeeping:  Securities shall be held by an independent third-party custodian institution selected 
by the City. All securities will be evidenced by safekeeping receipts in the City’s name.  The City 
must receive a safekeeping receipt on the same day as the deposit is made.   
 
VIII. Authorized Investments and Investment Guidelines 
 
Local Government Investment Pools :  The City will use available local government investment 
pools as the primary means of short -term investment management. These local government 
investment pools as of the approval date of this Investment Policy Statement include:  
 
• Wyoming Cooperative Liquid Assets Securities System (Wyoming CLASS) 
• Wyoming Government Investment Fund (WGIF) 
• WyoSTAR I  
 
The City may also use, at its discretion, other qualified short-term investment vehicles or programs. 
 
Other Investment Types:  The City shall invest only in those securities and investments authorized 
by W.S. § 9-4-831 and any other state law authorizing such investments. 
 
Credit Guidelines: The following credit guidelines shall be followed:   
 
• Bonds must be investment grade rated by at least two of the three Nationally Recognized 
Securities Rating Organization s (“NRSRO”) (Fitch Ratings, Inc., Moody’s Investors 
Service, Inc. and S&P Global Ratings). If the downgrade of a single bond forces the holding 
below the lowest rating allowed for the security, the City’s investment advisor will notify 
the City within a reasonable timeframe and a decision will be made whether to hold or sell 
the bond. 
• No non -investment grade bonds can be held for more than three months after the 
downgrade. No non-investment grade bonds can be bought. 
• Purchases or sales of securities shall be executed through firm(s) offering the best price 
and execution, unless otherwise directed by the City. All fixed income investments will be 
U.S. dollar denominated.  
• All securities in the short term/liquid portfolio that are not backed by the U.S. Government, 
its agencies, or its instrumentalities must conform to Securities and Exchange Commission 
(SEC) requirements for first tier money market investments. 
 
Authorized Collateral:  Collateralization is required for investments in certificates of deposits. To 
reduce market risk, the collateralization level will be 105% of the market value of principal and 
accrued interest, after any applicable FDIC insurance limits.  Only U.S. Treasury securities are 
permitted to be used as collateral.  
 
The City Treasurer shall verify monthly that the value of the collateral is sufficient to cover the 
deposits of investments discussed in this Investment Policy Statement. Acceptable instruments for 
collateralization of certificates of deposit shall be the same as set forth in W.S. § 9-4-820 and W.S. 
§ 9-8-821. In addition, collateralization is also required on the following types of investments:

IX. Investment Parameters 
 
Diversification:  The City shall mitigate credit risk—which is defined as the risk that a security or 
a portfolio will lose some or all of its value due to a real or perceived change in the ability of the 
issuer to repay its debt—by diversifying its investments as follows: 
 
• limiting investments in investment grade securities that have higher credit risks;  
• investing in short-term eligible securities to include institutional certificates of deposit and 
asset-backed commercial paper; 
• investing in securities with varying maturities;   
• limiting individual holdings, other than those backed by the U.S. Government, its agencies, 
or its instrumentalities, certificates of deposits, and authorized pools  to no more than 5% 
of the City’s total investment portfolio in any one single obligor; and 
• continuously investing a portion of the portfolio in readily available funds. 
  
Liquidity:  The City shall mitigate market risk—which is defined as the risk that the portfolio value 
will fluctuate due to changes in the general level of interest rates—by providing adequate liquidity 
for short-term cash needs, and by making longer -term investments only with funds that are not 
needed for current cash flow purposes. The investment portfolio shall remain sufficiently liquid to 
meet all operating require ments that may be reasonably anticipated.  To further mitigate market 
risk, the following strategies shall be followed: 
  
• the maximum percent of callable securities in the portfolio shall be 20%;  
• liquidity funds will be held in one of the local government investment pools in money 
market instruments maturing one year and shorter;  
• longer term/core funds will be defined as the funds in excess of liquidity requirements; 
• allow the use of money funds ; The duration of the portfolio will not exceed + 20% of the 
benchmark. 
• no index related funds are allowed. Examples of these prohibited index related securities 
are those whose interest rate is based on an index of commodities or equity securities. 
 
X. Performance Standards/Evaluation 
 
The performance of investments will be measured against the performance of a combination of 
benchmarks. Investment performance will be measured using “Total Rate of Return” 
measurement. The manager must provide monthly, quarterly, and annual performance reports 
against the stated benchmark on a net of fees basis.   
 
The assets of the portfolio managed by external advisors shall be benchmarked as follows: 
 
• Longer Term/CORE funds:  65% ICE BofA 1-5 Year Treasury-Agency / 30% ICE BofA 
US Mortgage Backed Securities / 5% Bloomberg US TIPS;

will also be posted to the City Treasurer’s public webpage.

IX. Investment Parameters 
 
Diversification:  The City shall mitigate credit risk—which is defined as the risk that a security or 
a portfolio will lose some or all of its value due to a real or perceived change in the ability of the 
issuer to repay its debt—by diversifying its investments as follows: 
 
• limiting investments in investment grade securities that have higher credit risks;  
• investing in short-term eligible securities to include institutional certificates of deposit and 
asset-backed commercial paper; 
• investing in securities with varying maturities;   
• limiting individual holdings, other than those backed by the U.S. Government, its agencies, 
or its instrumentalities, certificates of deposits, and authorized pools  to no more than 5% 
of the City’s total investment portfolio in any one single obligor; and 
• continuously investing a portion of the portfolio in readily available funds. 
  
Liquidity:  The City shall mitigate market risk—which is defined as the risk that the portfolio value 
will fluctuate due to changes in the general level of interest rates—by providing adequate liquidity 
for short-term cash needs, and by making longer -term investments only with funds that are not 
needed for current cash flow purposes. The investment portfolio shall remain sufficiently liquid to 
meet all operating require ments that may be reasonably anticipated.  To further mitigate market 
risk, the following strategies shall be followed: 
  
• a minimum level of six months of budgeted operating expenditures in short term  
investment shall be maintained, unless the budgeted disbursements are held in Wyo-Star I, 
to provide sufficient liquidity for expected disbursements; 
• the maximum percent of callable securities in the portfolio shall be 20%;  
• liquidity funds will be held in the Wyo-Star or one of the local government investment 
pools in money market instruments maturing one year and shorter;  
• longer term/core funds will be defined as the funds in excess of liquidity requirements; 
• allow the use of money funds ; The duration of the portfolio will not exceed + 20% of the 
benchmark. 
• no index related funds are allowed. Examples of these prohibited index related securities 
are those whose interest rate is based on an index of commodities or equity securities.

X. Performance Standards/Evaluation 
 
The performance of investments will be measured against the performance of a combination of 
benchmarks. Investment performance will be measured using “Total Rate of Return” 
measurement. The manager must provide monthly, quarterly, and annual performance reports 
against the stated benchmark on a net of fees basis.   
 
The assets of the portfolio managed by external advisors shall be benchmarked as follows: 
 
• Longer Term/CORE funds:  Bloomberg Barclays U.S. 1-3 Government/Credit Index; 65% 
ICE BofA 1-5 Year Treasury-Agency / 30% ICE BofA US Mortgage Backed Securities / 
5% Bloomberg US TIPS; 
• Liquidity Funds (excluding Wyo-Star): WyoSTAR I, Wyoming CLASS and Wyoming 
Government Investment Fund): Citigroup 3-month Treasury Bill Index. 
 
XI. Reporting 
 
The City Treasurer is charged with the responsibility of preparing a bi -annual investment report 
and providing it  to the Investment Committee and City Governing Body, showing the type of 
investment, institution, rate of interest, maturity date and amount of deposit. This investment report 
will also be posted to the City Treasurer’s public webpage.
