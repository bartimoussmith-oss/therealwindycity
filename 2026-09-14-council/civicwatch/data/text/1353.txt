TO: The Honorable Mayor Patrick Collins and City of Cheyenne Council Members 
FROM: Robin Lockman, City Treasurer 
DATE: January 7, 2025 
SUBJECT: Reappropriation to be considered for approval 
On January 13, 2025, the Governing Body will be referring a resolu
tion to the Finance Committee 
to reappropriate funds for th e Fiscal Year (FY) 2025 City of Cheyenne Budget per W.S. § 16-4-
112 and 16-4-113.  Below is more detailed information regarding this reappropriation request.  
GENERAL FUND – ADDITIONAL REVENUE AND RESERVES 
The Go verning Body approved Resolution 6442 on December 9, 2024, authorizing one-
time payments to permanent employees from FY 2024 budget savings.  Full-time employees 
received $2,000, while part-time staff received $1,000.  The resolution covers both employer 
and employee 
portions of FICA tax es but does not include federal withholding taxes.  
This reappropriation 
will increase budget line items by $1,130,143 from General Fund 
reserves 
and $491,650 from other fund reserves and grant revenues, totaling $1,621,793 in one-
time payments. 
In addition, there are several other General Fund requests included for your consideration: 
1. The Governing Body has approved various special projects to begin or continue during FY
2025.  A total of $3,363,807 will be transferred from General Fund reserves to pay for these
project expenses, including:
a. Design services for the restoration of the pumphouse ($439,539)
b. Construction of the new gymnastics’ facility ($2,073,049)
c. Railcar asbestos and lead paint abatement for the 15th Street project ($255,462)
d. Concrete repairs and repl acement of the public sidewalk on Vandehei Avenue
between Yellowstone Road and Stockman Street ($180,652)
e. Drainage enhancements for Childs Draw/Thomas Heights ($115,105)
f. Design and installation of a new boiler for the Johnson Pool facility ($300,000)
2. In October 2024 the Cheyenne Police Depart ment (CPD) launched the Critical Traffic
Control Area (CTCA) program, enabling co mmunity members to request reviews of
traffic-related concerns for potential temporary remedial action.  The program aims to
1
identify areas with unsafe or unlawful traffic behaviors and implement temporary measures 
to address these issues.   A new budget line item was created speci fically for overtime 
expenses pertaining to this program, and $100,000 will be transferred from General Fund 
reserves.  
  
3. The CPD has also contracted with the Horse Pa lace off-track betting facility to provide 
security services.  Overtime expenses for these services will be paid from the special event 
overtime line item, and $50,000 will also be  transferred from the police overtime 
reimbursements revenue line item to cover expenses. 
 
4. The Cheyenne/Laramie County Emergency Management Agency (EMA) recently learned 
that their 2025 Emergency Management Pl anning Grant Program (EMPG) funding was 
reduced by the Wyoming Office  of Homeland Security. As a result, EMA is requesting 
General Fund reserves to support the shortage  in their program ad ministration expenses. 
Laramie County and the City of Cheyenne have a cost-sharing agreement for EMA 
operations. After considering available gran t funds, the remaining operational costs are 
split equally between the county and city.

MEMORANDUM  
TO: The Honorable Mayor Patrick Collins  and City of Cheyenne Council Members 
FROM: Jason Sanchez, Community Recreation and Events Director 
DATE: December 2, 2024 
SUBJECT: Civic Center and Ice & Events Center Requests from General Fund Reserves 
At the close of last fiscal year, our team managed over 100 large -scale events within the 
Community Recreation & Events department. Nearly half of these events, including Fridays on 
the Plaza, Superday, Brew Fest, CFD Pancake Breakfast, Cheyenne Day, and m ore, were hosted 
outside of the Cheyenne Civic Center and were directly managed or facilitated by Civic Center 
staff.  
The
 Civic Center staff consists of five full -time staff members who are critical to the operations 
and success of these outside events due to their specialized skill sets in technical production, event 
management, booking, marketing and advertising, ticketing, and safety. With this team, we have 
seen our most successful Fridays on the Plaza to date, built stronger partnerships with third- party 
promoters and agents, cultivated deeper relationships with sponsors, and served as a critical 
resource for local groups and community organizations in the surrounding area.  
Wi
th our continued growth in the caliber of events we are bringing to the downtown community, 
we have garnered stronger support from businesses as it drives tourism, economic growth, and 
overall vitality for the City of Cheyenne.  
The
 Cheyenne Civic Center holds a special place in the hearts of our citizens, serving as a home 
for a wide range of events, from performances by local artists to nationally renowned acts, 
community events, conferences, training sessions, and so much more.  Its diverse offerings 
contribute significantly to the City's cultural fiber, vibrancy, and economic growth.  
How
ever, like any facility, the Civic Center requires ongoing investments to maintain its 
infrastructure, attract top -tier talent, and provide the best experience for both performers and 
audiences. It is my belief that redirecting funds toward the Civic Ce nter is a strategic move that 
will yield substantial returns in terms of community engagement, economic activity and overall 
quality of life for our residents.  
5
I am respectfully requesting your support to reappropriate $323,322 from the Community 
Recreation & Events $581,072 FY24 reversion to support the Civic Center. I would also ask for 
$27,855 to be reappropriated to support the Ice & Events Center expenses and $40,000 toward 
F
ridays on the Plaza expenses.   
I understand the allocation of funds is a complex process that requires careful consideration of 
va
rious priorities. However, I firmly believe that supporting the Cheyenne Civic Center and Ice & 
E
vents Center aligns with our City’s commitment to quality of  life and fostering a rich cultural 
landscape, supporting local business and community groups, as well as providing our residents 
with enriching experiences.
