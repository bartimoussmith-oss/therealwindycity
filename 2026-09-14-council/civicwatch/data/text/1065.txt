BACKGROUND: 
 
The City’s Unified Development Code (UDC) was adopted by the Governing Body on January 23, 2012, by 
Ordinance Number 3943 with an effective  date of April 30, 2012.  The Unified Development Code is the 
regulatory document carrying out the vision of PlanCheyenne and is reviewed annually pursuant to UDC 
1.1.7 [Annual Review Requirement] to determine if any particular standard or regulation is timely, 
relevant and adequately addresses current market conditions. In addition to the annual review, the 
Planning and Development Director and City Engineer are au thorized under UDC 2.4.1.b.3 to bring 
forward text amendments on their own initiative.  
 
The proposed text amendments are to the Park Impact Fees section of code. Currently, Park Impact Fees 
are charged at time of subdivision and at time  of permit. There are three fees cha rged: the Land 
Acquisition Fee, the Infrastructure Fee, and the Enhancement Fee. The first two fees go into one account 
(Park Acquisition and Infrastructure ) and the enhancement fee goes into a separate account ( Park 
Enhancement Account). The accounts are earmarked for specific purposes  – the Park Acquisition and 
Infrastructure requires fees to be used to acquire land or prepare land for use (such as grading, utilities, 
etc.); while the Park Enhancement Account  is reserved for the creation of amenities (playgrounds, ball 
fields, etc.).

The current arrangement of Park Fees creates 
challenges for both developers and Staff.  
 
For developers, Park Fees being due at time of 
subdivision leads to various landowners not being 
willing to plat all of their land , or to not request zoning 
of all of their land to the zone they intend it to be under 
eventually. This is because agricultural land and 
remainder lots are exempt from Park Fees, and not all 
developers can afford to pay Park Fees up front without 
a specific project in process . The most prominent 
example is Bison Business Park, which, although 
originally intended to be a business park with HI or LI 
zoning, contains multiple unplatted or AG zoned parcels. 
Because parcels are unplatted or zoned with agricultural 
zoning, any business coming in would need to go 
through a time-consuming process to get the land ready 
for development (zone change and/or platting actions 
add at least 3 months to a project).  
 
For Staff, the limitation s on the different Park Fee 
accounts has meant some accounts grow while City 
recreation needs go unmet. The account which is limited to park land acquisition or park land 
infrastructure improvements is limited such that it cannot be used for indoor recreation (such as an indoor 
soccer facility) or for park enhancements (such as parking, playgrounds, ballfields, or trees). 
 
This amendment proposes changing the timing of collection of fees to the time of building permit, 
proposes changing “Park Fees” to “Park and Recreation Fees”, and proposes putting all fees into one 
account to be used wherever the City needs to improve its recreation offerings to service a growing 
population. 
 
This proposed amendment does not propose changing the fees’ dollar amount or how the fees are 
calculated.

REVIEW CRITERIA: 
 
Review of a UDC text amendment shall be measured by the following three (3) criteria of Section 2.4.1.d: 
 
1. COMPREHENSIVE PLAN:  
“The amendment is in accordance with general goals and policies of the Comprehensive Plan, or 
is necessary to address an emerging issue not anticipated in the Comprehensive Plan and is not 
inconsistent with the plan.” 
PLTA-25-7   May 19, 2025 
 
PlanCheyenne encourages the local governments to “work with local and regional economic 
development, educational, and business partners to  position the Cheyenne Area as a leading 
place to live and do business in the Northern Front Range region.” (Policy 1.5.A) This amendment 
is based off of collaboration with business parks to increase the ability of the region to respond 
to incoming business opportunities. 
 
PlanCheyenne also encourages the region to provide “quality of life amenities that are 
competitive with those of  other communities in the Northern Front Range region ” – including 
parks and recreation facilities (Policy 1.5.C) . This amendment allows the City more flexibility in 
using recreational impact fees to provide the amenities and facilities needed by the community. 
Similarly, Policies 6.5.A encourages centrally located recreation facilities that can serve large 
numbers of people. 
  
2. STATE STATUTORY REQUIREMENTS:  
The amendment is consistent with the Wyoming Statutes, and specifically the Municipal Planning 
and Zoning statutes. The following Articles are all listed verbatim from Wyoming State Statute. 
 
WYO 15-1-103(a) The governing bodies of all cities and towns may: 
 … 
 (xi)  Take all necessary action to plan, construct or otherwise improve, 
modify, repair, maintain and regulate the use of streets, including the 
regulation of any structures thereunder, alleys, any bridges, parks, public 
grounds, cemeteries and sidewalks; 
 … 
 (xxii)  Establish and regulate parks, zoological gardens and recreation 
areas within the city limits… 
 
Assessment: This amendment proposes taking action to establish and improve park and 
recreation areas.  
 
3. HEALTH, SAFETY, AND PUBLIC WELFARE:  
“The amendment promotes the general health, safety and public welfare.” 
 
This amendment allows for better timing and usage of park and recreation impact fees. Providing 
appropriate recreational facilities is a key part of the public welfare for the Cheyenne area.   
 
 
CONCLUSION:  
 
Staff concludes that this text amendment meets the review criteria of UDC 2.4.1.d.
