BACKGROUND: 
 
The applicant is requesting approval of a preliminary plat for roughly 22.21 acres of land. The proposed plat 
would create two Lots for multi-family development and one remainder Lot for future development. Lots 2 and 
3 are currently proposed to be NR-3 – Neighborhood Residential High-density and the remainder Lot would 
be AG – Agricultural.  
 
SURROUNDING LAND USE AND ZONING: 
 
 
ANALYSIS OF APPLICATION CONFORMANCE WITH REVIEW CRITERIA: 
 
Should the Planning Commission wish to recommend approval of the Preliminary Plat, the Commission 
must make the following findings required in UDC 2.1.2.d  – a detailed analysis is below:

patterns identified in the Comprehensive Plan. (UDC 2.1.2.d.1) 
 
On the future land use map found in PlanCheyenne, the subject land has been designated as 
Mixed-use Commercial / Urban Residential. The Mixed-Use Commercial Emphasis category 
encompasses existing and future developments, many of which align with activity c enters. This 
category includes a wide variety of land uses and activities, focused primarily on commercial

activities, but also includes other employment, public, and residential uses. The Urban Residential 
designation encourages a range of more intensive residential uses, although the intent is not to 
change existing stable neighborhoods.  
 
Foundation 1 of PlanCheyenne encourages community growth. This development is in response 
to market demands and is within the Urban Service Boundary. The development patterns 
proposed by this plat are compatible with the designations found on the future land use map. 
 
PlanCheyenne encourages connecting neighborhoods with streets and sidewalks for the puproses 
of making walking an bicycling safe and convenient. Although the block provided is oversized, the 
plat proposes a pedestrian easement which would help connect neighborhoods in a way that is 
safe and convenient for pedestrians and cyclists (even if cars would have to go around the block).  
 
The application complies with this review criterion.  
 
2) The application reflects sound planning and urban design principles with respect to 
existing and potential future development in the vicinity, including consideration 
of current and future streets, open spaces, blocks and lots, and public utilities or 
facilities. (UDC 2.1.2.d.2) 
 
The proposed lot is to allow for multifamily development similar to the development to the south . 
All multifamily buildings will be under one ownership. The plat also extends E Carlson to  the 
remainder lot which will allow it to continue further west or connect into a new road in the future.  
 
Future development to the north or northwest may be limited by this plat’s lack of platted 
roadways. The plat does include a pedestrian easement to break up the larger block this plat 
would create, though such a connection would not necessarily allow vehicle connections. This 
particular developer has created integrated developments with properties they own, so internal 
circulation may be possible if they are the ones to develop north of this property. Additional 
access easements can also be provided as the development builds out.  
 
The application complies with this review criterion, with conditions/exception.   
   
3) The division of land meets the purposes, intent, and all applicable standards in 
Article 4. (UDC 2.1.2.d.3) 
 
Community Facility Fees (4.2): 
For fees based on acreage, the area of the plat is pro-rated to the nearest tenth of an acre. 
Lands reserved as unbuildable (for right-of-way, detention, parks, remainder lots, etc), are 
exempt from fees. Park fees are calculated at $700 per acre. All fees are due at the time of 
building permit. 
 
Per UDC Section 4.2.5, Table 4-2, Enhancement Fees are payable at the issuance of a building 
permit for each structure. The Park Enhancement Fee for residential is $400 per dwelling unit.  
 
Transportation Network (4.3): 
This plat is adding a small amount to an existing E Carlson right-of-way to allow further 
dedication in the future. No additional new streets are proposed with this plat.

Proposed blocks do not meet the block size requirements of the UDC. The proposed block is 
excessively sized compared to regulations for the area. Although there is room for streets 
around this development, the 'disconnected' block size is only allowed to be 60% of the 
maximum block size, and the 'disconnected' block created by this development is much larger 
than 60% of the maximum block perimeter required in "Urban Residential" areas. The 
applicant is proposing a pedestrian access easement between proposed Lots 2 and 3 which 
does mitigate some of the negative impacts of the oversize block. Additionally, the way that 
the applicant develops their sites does create internal access ways that connect their 
developments and could allow for future connections to other developments in the future. 
Should the Planning Commission and the Governing Body wish to approve this plat, an 
exception to block size is required. Staff proposes granting the “Oversized Parcel” exception 
as it allows for internal access connections for larger scale developments, such as multifamily.  
 
Open Space (4.4): 
Open space is required at 750sqft per unit or 8% of the gross development area, whichever is 
less. Due to the nature of the proposed development pattern, open space will be taken care 
of with future development actions. 
 
Required Improvements (4.5): 
This plat appears to be able to provide all required utilities to the lot within this development. 
Easements are not provided for BOPU utilities, these easements need to be provided to the 
satisfaction of the BOPU. An Administrative Plat will need to be provided at a later date 
providing all the required BOPU easements. Easements are provided to other utilities.   
 
Public Safety Fees (4.6): 
Public Safety Fees are required at the time of construction of any residential building on the 
proposed lot. Per UDC Section 4.6.3.b, Public Safety Fees are payable at the issuance of a 
building permit for each structure.  
 
The application complies with this review criterion, with conditions.   
 
4) Any phasing proposed in the application is clearly indicated and demonstrates a 
logical and well-planned development pattern. This may include consideration of 
amenities and public facilities of the subdivision within each phase compared to 
the anticipated timing or the likelihood of future phases being constructed as 
planned. (UDC 2.1.2.d.4) 
 
The plat proposes two buildable lots with a remainder lot. The remainder lot is not intended to be 
developed at this time and is the furthest point from the currently developed areas. The remainer 
lot is essentially a ‘future phase’ of development, However, there is no indication the area in the 
remainder lot would develop with the same development pattern as the buildable lots, so it is not 
truly a phase of this development. No specific phasing is proposed with the buildable lots in this 
project. 
 
The application complies with this review criterion..

requested to be prepared in association with the application, and whether the

Drainage: 
A drainage worksheet was submitted and reviewed. There were no concerns with drainage at the

6) Lots proposed for development shall be buildable lots according to the existing or 
proposed zoning district standards, result in design and development patterns that 
meet the Intent and Applicability statements of the existing or proposed zoning 
district, and not aggravate nonconforming setbacks or currently adopted building 
and fire code access and separation requirements. (UDC 2.1.2.d.6) 
 
The proposed application occurs in concert with a proposal to zone the land NR-3 Neighborhood 
Residential – High Density. The NR-3 zone is intended primarily for high-density, urban residential 
uses with consistent neighborhood character. The NR-3 zone allows multifamily development as a 
use by right. The proposed lot type would be MD1. 
 
The intent of the NR3 zone is to develop with a compact neighborhood development patterns 
and a context of “a highly connected street network with street design types that have a high 
degree of pedestrian amenities” is encouraged. The block proposed by this plat is oversized, but a 
connecting pedestrian amenity is proposed.  
 
The application complies with this review criterion, with conditions.   
 
7) All parcels not proposed as buildable lots and which are not dedicated to and 
accepted by the City shall have a clearly defined entity responsible for ongoing 
maintenance.  (UDC 2.1.2.d.7) 
 
Lots 2 and 3 are proposed to be buildable Lots. Lot 1 is proposed as a remainder Lot which will 
need to be replated in the future should that lot wish to be developed or sold.  
 
The application complies with this review criterion.   
 
8) The application will not create any undue influence on existing or potential 
development for adjacent property considering the existing zoning and 
Comprehensive Plan policies for that property.  (UDC 2.1.2.d.8) 
 
The street network in this area would generally require a street within this plat boundary, which 
would be dedicated with this plat. Additions to the street network would facilitate surrounding

development. Staffs understanding is that there are ongoing discussions with landowners to 
rearrange the street network in this area and no additional streets are proposed to be platted at 
this time. Streets could be platted as other lands surrounding this one are developed.  This plat 
will not otherwise create any undue influence on existing or potential development.  
 
The application complies with this review criterion, with conditions.  
 
9) The application is not harmful to the public health, safety, and welfare. (UDC 
2.1.2.d.9) 
 
An interconnected street system with smaller blocks reduces travel times and pressure on major 
roadways. UDC standards would expect a street through this plat area to reduce block sizes. The 
plat does propose a pedestrian easement, which would reduce some of the travel time issues for 
pedestrian and bicycle transportation. Staff is uncertain in how the development will be built at 
this time, so future easements could be provided at a later time.  
 
City staff is satisfied that this proposed plat would not otherwise adversely affect the public 
health, safety, or general welfare. 
 
The application complies with this review criterion, with conditions.

noted and described on all plats either graphically or by legal description. (UDC

Lot 1 is proposed to be a remainder lot and is labeled as such on the plat.  
 
The application complies with this review criterion.
