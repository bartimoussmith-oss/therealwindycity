INTRODUCTION 
Federal Transit Administration (FTA) requires that all direct and primary recipients document their 
compliance with DOT’s Title VI regulations by submitting a Title VI Program to their FTA 
regional civil rights officer once every three years or as otherwise directed by FTA. 
The City of Cheyenne, Wyoming (the City), is the designated recipient of Federal Transit 
Administration (FTA) funds on behalf of the Cheyenne Transit Program (CTP). The Cheyenne 
MPO is the designated Title VI officer for the CTP. 
To fulfill the general reporting requirements of Title VI, the Cheyenne Transit Program has 
developed a Title VI program to ensure supported transit services and related benefits are 
distributed by applicants, recipients, and sub-recipients of FTA assistance in a manner consistent 
with Title VI. 
PROVISION OF TITLE VI ASSURANCES 
In accordance with 49 CFR Section 21.7(a), every application for financial assistance from FTA 
must be accompanied by an assurance that the applicant will carry out the program in compliance 
with DOT’s Title VI regulations. The CTP Certification and Assurance is appendix “A”.

TITLE VI COMPLAINT PROCEDURE 
Title VI states that no person in the United States of America shall, on the grounds of race, color, 
national origin, sex, age, or disability be excluded from the participation in, be denied the benefit 
of, or be otherwise subjected to discrimination under any of our programs or activities. 
The City of Cheyenne hereby gives public notice that it is the policy of the City of Cheyenne to 
assure full compliance with the Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the Civil Rights 
Restoration Act of 1987, and related statutes and regulations in all programs and activities. 
The City of Cheyenne has adopted a grievance procedure providing for prompt and equitable 
resolution of complaints. The complaint should be in writing using the Title VI and ADA 
Complaint Form and should contain all information requested about the alleged discrimination 
5  
such as name, address, phone number of complainant and location, date, and description of the 
problem. Alternative means of filing complaints, such as personal interviews will be made 
available for persons with disabilities upon request. The complaint should be submitted by the 
complainant within sixty (60) calendar days of the alleged violation. The form can be found on the 
City of Cheyenne’s Website: 
https://www.cheyennecity.org/files/sharedassets/public/departments/public-works/title-vi-
complaint-procedures-and-form.pdf 
and at the Human Resources Office City of Cheyenne. The signed form shall be submitted to: 
Human Resources or ADA Coordinator 
2101 O’Neil Avenue, Front Lobby 
Cheyenne, WY 82001 
(307) 637-6340 
 
Resolution of a complaint at the lowest level is encouraged. 
In those cases where the complainant is dissatisfied with the resolution by the Cheyenne Transit 
Program or the City, the same complaint may be submitted to the Federal Transit Administration 
(FTA) or the Secretary, U.S. Department of Transportation (US DOT), for investigation. A 
complaint must be filed within 180 days after the date of the alleged discrimination, unless the 
time for filing is extended by the Secretary, US DOT. Unless otherwise permitted, the final 
determination of all Title VI complaints affecting programs administered by FTA will be made by 
the Office of the Secretary, US DOT. 
1. Notification of Disposition. The Director of Cheyenne MPO will notify the complainant and 
party charged of the disposition of the complaint, within 5 working days, by registered letter. 
a. Enforcement-Procedures. In cases in which all required means of remedial action have 
failed to bring the respondent into compliance, enforcement procedures will be initiated 
by the FTA Region VIII Director of Civil Rights. 
b. Request for Reconsideration. The respondent may request reconsideration of the findings 
within 30 days of the Notice of Disposition. This request should include any additional 
information or analysis the respondent considers relevant. The Director of Cheyenne 
MPO or FTA VIII Director of Civil Rights will inform the respondent of its decision to 
accept or reject the request within 30 days after its receipt. In cases in which a request 
for reconsideration is approved, the responsible investigator will reopen the investigation 
and proceed to process the complaint in the same manner described above.

LANGUAGE ASSISTANCE PLAN 
Introduction 
The purpose of this Limited English Proficiency policy guidance is to clarify the responsibilities of 
recipients of federal financial assistance from the U.S. Department of Transportation (DOT) and 
assist them in fulfilling their responsibilities to limited English proficient (LEP) persons, pursuant 
to Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964 and implementing regulations. It was prepared in 
accordance with Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964, 42 U.S.C. 2000d, et seq., and its 
implementing regulations provide that no person shall be subjected to discrimination on the basis 
of race, color, or national origin under any program or activity that receives federal financial 
assistance. 
Executive Order 13166 
Executive Order 13166 "Improving Access to Services for Persons with Limited English 
Proficiency," reprinted at 65 FR 50121 (August 16, 2000), directs each Federal agency that is subject 
to the requirements of Title VI to publish guidance for its respective recipients clarifying that 
obligation. Executive Order 13166 further directs that all such guidance documents be consistent with 
the compliance standards and framework detailed in the Department of Justice's (DOJ's) Policy 
Guidance entitled "Enforcement of Title VI of the Civil Rights Act of 1964- National Origin 
Discrimination Against Persons with Limited English Proficiency." (See 65 FR 50123, August 16, 
2000 DOJ's General LEP Guidance). Different treatment based upon a person's inability to speak, 
read, write, or understand English may be a type of national origin discrimination. 
9  
Executive Order 13166 applies to all federal agencies and all programs and operations of entities that 
receive funding from the federal government, including state agencies, local agencies, Metropolitan 
Planning Organizations (MPOs), governments, private and non-profit entities, and subrecipients. 
Plan Summary 
The Cheyenne Transit Program (CTP) has developed this Limited English Proficiency Plan (LEP) 
to help identify reasonable steps to provide language assistance for LEP persons seeking 
meaningful access to transportation services as required by Executive Order 13166. A Limited 
English Proficiency person is one who does not speak English as their primary language and who 
has a limited ability to read, speak, write, or understand English. 
This plan details procedures on how to identify a person who may need language assistance, the 
ways in which assistance may be provided, training staff, and how to notify LEP persons that 
assistance is available. 
In developing the plan while determining CTP’s extent of obligation to provide LEP services, CTP 
undertook a U.S. Department of Transportation four factor LEP analysis which considers the 
following: A brief description of these considerations is provided in the following section. 
Four Factor Analysis 
1. The number or proportion of LEP persons eligible to be served or likely to be 
encountered by Cheyenne Transit Program. 
According to the 2016 – 2020 American Community Survey 5-year Estimates for the 
Cheyenne metro area, the Hispanic population is 10,179 or, 15.9% of the total population and 
is the largest minority. Of the total Hispanic population only 222 speak English “not well”. See 
appendix “H”. 
2. The frequency with which LEP individuals come in contact with the CTP. 
The four transit routes cover approximately 85% of the urbanized area. Approximately 60% 
of the transit stops have shelters that contain maps with the QR codes for Spanish maps. 
Most non-English speaking residents either have an app on their smart phones for translation 
(i.e. Google Translate) or have other English-speaking family members with them. Many 
human services agencies are quite aware of the Cheyenne Transit program and can provide 
information about it to their clients in need of transportation. List is appendix “F”. 
3. The nature and importance provided by CTP to people’s lives. 
The CTP system is designed to serve persons who do not have driver licenses, seniors, the 
disabled and low-income individuals. If LEP individuals are included in one of these 
categories, then public transit is very important to them. 
10  
4. The resources available to CTP for LEP outreach, as well as the costs associated with that 
outreach. 
a. The US Census data website is the primary source of identifying the number of LEP 
populations within the Cheyenne area. Agencies that serve these individuals have been able 
to provide some information although it is only an estimate, because they do not formally 
collect this information. The 2023 American Community Survey 5-year Estimates for the 
Cheyenne metro area indicates that 1.5% of the Households in the Cheyenne Census 
County Division speak English less than “very well.” 
 
b.  
c. The FTA and FHWA planning funds are the source for 90.49% of the CTP planning 
budgets. Conducting the LEP research and outreach is done in-house by MPO staff. 
d. CTP has installed the Google Translate app on all staff phones including front office, 
dispatch and drivers so that if a non-English speaking person is encountered while 
providing transit service, they will be able to communicate with them. 
e. Many LEP individuals may use their own vehicles or carpool to get to and from work 
because it can be more convenient to them personally than the transit system and because of 
Cheyenne’s relative compact size. 
 
After analyzing the four factors, CTP has developed a LEP Plan outlined in the following section 
for assisting persons of limited English proficiency. 
Limited English Proficiency Plan 
1. How to Identify an LEP Person who Needs Language Assistance - Below are tools to help 
identify persons who may need language assistance: 
a. Examine records requests for language assistance from past meetings and events to 
anticipate the possible need for assistance at upcoming meetings; 
b. When CTP-sponsored workshops or conferences are held, post on the public notices the 
11  
contact information for people with special needs. Also set up a sign-in sheet table, have a 
staff member greet and briefly speak to each attendee. To informally gauge the attendee's 
ability to speak and understand English, ask a question that requires a full sentence reply; 
c. Use the Google Translate app if a non-English speaking individual is encountered. 
d. Frequently meet with staff to learn if they have had direct or indirect contact with LEP 
individuals. 
2. Language Assistance Measures - CTP has or will implement the following LEP procedures. 
The creation of these steps is based on the very low percentage of persons speaking other 
languages or not speaking English at least "well," in the Cheyenne metropolitan area: 
a. The CTP website has installed a translation application. 
12  
b. When the CTP hosts public meetings or conferences and a special need is identified in 
advance, every effort will be made to have a translator available at the meeting or someone 
with the Google Translate App. 
3. Staff Training - All CTP staff will be provided with the LEP Plan and will be educated on 
procedures to follow. This information will also be part of the Human Resources staff 
orientation process for new hires. Training topics are listed below: 
a. Understanding the Title VI policy and LEP responsibilities; 
b. What language assistance services the Cheyenne Transit Program offers; 
c. Documentation of language assistance requests; 
d. How to handle a Title VI and/or LEP complaint 
4. Outreach Techniques 
a. The availability of translated vital documents for any non-English proficient person eligible 
to be served or likely affected by the Cheyenne Transit program is a primary outreach 
technique. All documents will be available on the CTP website and with the translator app, 
they can all be converted to the language the user desires to view and print them with. 
Vital written documents include an ADA and Title VI complaint form, notice of a person’s 
rights under Title VI, and other documents that provide access to essential services. 
b. A summary of outreach techniques CTP has taken to ensure meaningful access for 
individuals who are Limited English Proficient (LEP) is outlined below: 
i. The City and CTP website has language translation app for persons needing Spanish or 
other language translation. 
ii. Posters or flyers are distributed to groceries that cater to ethnic populations; La Rosa 
Market - 804 Central Ave., Golden Dragon grocery– 1605 Seymour Ave., Tortilleria La 
Paz – 200 E. 18th St. 
iii. Spanish route map/schedules are available with a smart phone using the QR code. 
Printed maps in Spanish are available on request. 
iv. When running a general public meeting notice, staff will insert the clause, based on the 
LEP population and when relevant, that translates into "A (insert alternative Language) 
translator will be available". For example: "Un traductor del idioma espanol estara 
disponible." This means "A Spanish translator will be available". 
5. Monitoring and Updating the LEP Plan - This plan is designed to be flexible and is one that 
can be easily updated. At a minimum, CTP will follow the Title VI Program update schedule 
for the LEP Plan. Each update should examine all plan components such as: 
a. How many LEP persons were encountered? 
b. Were their needs met? 
c. What is the current LEP population in Cheyenne metropolitan area? 
13  
d. Are other languages becoming increasingly spoken in the Cheyenne area? 
e. Have CTPs available resources, such as technology, staff, and financial costs changed? 
f. Has CTP fulfilled the goals of the LEP Plan? 
g. Were any complaints received? 
6. Dissemination of the CTP Language Proficiency Plan – Cheyenne Transit Program includes 
the LEP Plan with its Title VI Policy and Complaint Procedures. The Cheyenne Transit 
“Notice of Rights under Title VI to the Public” is posted in the Transit office. Any person, 
including social service, non-profit, and law enforcement agencies and other community 
partners will be able to access the plan. Copies of the LEP Plan will be provided, on request, to 
any person(s) requesting the document via phone, in person, by mail or email. LEP persons 
may obtain copies/translations of the plan upon request. Any questions or comments regarding 
this plan should be directed to the Cheyenne Transit Title VI officer.

2. Vehicle Headway Standards 
Prior to April 2022, six routes operated on hourly headways, between 6:00am to 7:00pm, 
Monday through Friday and 10:00am to 5:00pm Saturdays. No service was provided on 
Sunday. In April 2020, route service was put on hold. In September 2023 the fixed route 
service was reinstated with four routes identified by the Transportation Development Plan. 
These routes operate on a 30 min headway between 6:00 am and 7:00 pm Monday thru 
Friday and 10:00 am to 5:00 pm on Saturday. There is no service on Sundays. On Time 
Performance Standards 
When route service was running prior to April 2020, ninety-five percent (95%) of the 
Cheyenne Transit Program's vehicles completed their established runs no more than 5 
minutes early or late in comparison to the established schedule/published timetables. 
With the return of fixed route service, on time performance returned to 90- 95% of 
vehicles running no more than 5 min early or late on average. 
3. Service Availability Standards 
Prior to April 2020, Cheyenne Transit provided transit service to at least 85% of those 
destinations most frequently traveled to by riders and those locations will have a bus stop 
within 1/4 mile. In 2023, with the return of fixed route service, Cheyenne Transit returned to 
providing transit service to at least 75% of the previous destinations. 
Service Policies 
1. Vehicle Assignment Policy 
The bus assignment policy will consider the operating characteristics of buses of various 
lengths, which are matched to the operating characteristics of the route. Routes with lower 
ridership may be assigned a smaller bus, if necessary and conversely, the routes that have 
higher ridership will be assigned the largest busses. 
15  
2. Transit Amenities Policy 
Installation of transit amenities along bus routes are based on the number of passenger 
boardings at stops along those routes.

City of Cheyenne 
Title II & Title VI Policy 
and Complaint Procedures 
Title II of the "Americans with Disabilities Accessibility Act" (ADA) is the title that applies to 
public entities like state and city governments. The Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990 is 
divided into a number of titles. Title II requires nondiscrimination on the basis of disability, in 
state and local government services. These "public entities" - including departments, agencies, or 
other instrumentalities - are required to comply with the ADA. 
 
Title VI states that no person in the United States of America shall, on the grounds of race, color, 
national origin, sex, age, or disability be excluded from the participation in, be denied the benefit 
of, or be otherwise subjected to discrimination under any of our programs or activities. 
 
The City of Cheyenne hereby gives public notice that it is the policy of the City of Cheyenne to 
assure full compliance with both Title II of the Americans with Disabilities Act and Title VI of 
the Civil Rights Act of 1964, the Civil Rights Restoration Act of 1987, and related statutes and 
regulations in all programs and activities. 
 
The City of Cheyenne has adopted a grievance procedure providing for prompt and equitable 
resolution of complaints. This Grievance Procedure is established to meet the requirements of 
the ADA. It may be used by anyone who wishes to file a complaint concerning access to City 
facilities, services, activities, programs, or benefits. 
 
The complaint process and form are posted on the City’s webpage under “report a concern” and 
ADA requests. The form is also available in Human Resources, 2101 O’Neil Avenue, Room 
103, Cheyenne, WY 82001. 
 
The complaint should be in writing using the Title VI and ADA Complaint Form below and 
contain all information requested about the alleged discrimination such as name, address, phone 
number of complainant and location, date, and description of the problem. Alternative means of 
filing complaints, such as personal interviews will be made available for persons with disabilities 
upon request. The complaint should be submitted by the complainant within sixty (60) calendar 
days of the alleged violation. The signed form shall be submitted to: 
 
City of Cheyenne 
ADA Coordinator, Human Resources 
2101 O’Neil Avenue, Front Lobby 
Cheyenne, WY 82001 
(307) 637-6340 
 
Receipt of a Complaint 
Upon receipt of a complaint, the ADA Coordinator will provide the appropriate Department 
Director or their designated representative with a copy of the complaint. Within fifteen (15) 
calendar days after receipt of the complaint, the ADA Coordinator will then conduct an 
investigation that may involve meeting with the complainant to discuss the complaint and 
possible resolutions. 

 
If an informal resolution is not reached with the complainant, within 60 calendar days after 
receipt of the complaint, the ADA Coordinator will respond in writing, and where appropriate, in 
a format accessible to the complainant, such as large print, Braille, or audio recording. The 
response will explain the position of the City and offer options for substantive resolution of the 
complaint. 
Retainment Policy 
All written complaints received by the City’s ADA Coordinator or his/her designee, appeals to 
the Mayor or his/her designee, and responses from these two offices will be retained by the ADA 
Coordinator for at least five years. 
 
The right of a person to a prompt and equitable resolution of the complaint filed hereunder shall 
not be impaired by the person's pursuit of other remedies, such as the filing of an ADA 
complaint with the responsible state or federal department or agency. Use of this complaint 
procedure is not a prerequisite to the pursuit of other remedies. 
 
This policy is printed and available from the ADA Coordinator’s office, the Cheyenne Transit 
Office and is published on the City’s website for easy access.
