6.5.7 Prohibited Signs 
 
The following specific application of sign types are prohibited in all zoning districts, regardless of 
whether they meet the sign allowances of this Section: 
 
a. Signs that have been abandoned for sixty (60) days or longer; 
 
b. Signs imitating or resembling government signs for traffic direction or any other public safety 
symbol, unless approved by the City for the purpose of traffic direction or public safety  in 
accordance with Chapter 10.20 of the Municipal Code of the City of Cheyenne; 
 
c. Signs placed on vehicles or trailers for the primary purpose of displaying the sign(s), when 
such vehicle or trailer is parked and visible from the public right-of-way and is not used in the 
normal day-to-day operations of the owner or a business; 
 
d. Signs placed in or encroaching upon a public right-of-way, except: 
1. Awnings, projecting canopies, and marquees which comply with the provisions of these 
regulations and are at least 8’ above the sidewalk; 
2. Bus stop benches and shelters, displaying signs on the backs of the benches, approved by 
the City through a request for proposal (RFP) process and a contract with a provider of the 
benches;  
3. Portable signs which comply with these regulations; and 
Page 3 of 4 
 
4. Signs located in the right-of-way directly between the property line  of the owner of the 
sign and the curb or edge of the roadway, with the review and approval of the City Engineer 
through applicable agreements or permits regarding use of the right-of-way. Any approved 
sign comes with the understanding that roadway or utility improvements may affect the 
sign and if the sign is required to be maintained, moved, removed or replaced it may be 
required to be done at the expense of the owner of the sign . If approved, such signs must 
meet code requirements for a sign placed 0’ to 5’ from the right-of-way. 
 
e. Building signs that extend vertically above the highest portion of the roof line or parapet, 
whichever is higher.  
 
f. Portable signs, except that one sign per building entrance may be allowed as a permitted 
Pedestrian Sign in any Mixed -Use, Commercial or Industrial zone district, subject to the 
following: 
1. The sign consists of non -illuminated chalkboards or similar devices mounted on tripods, 
easels or pedestals;  
2. The sign shall not exceed 12 square feet and shall not exceed 5’ height; 
3. The sign shall be located no more than 25’ from the principal entrance to the building and 
removed from the premises during non-business hours; and 
4. If located in the right -of-way it shall be located in a  manner that allows at least 6 ’ clear 
area for pedestrian flow and meet all sight line triangle requirements. 
 
g. Signs on corner lots within the sight line triangle, except:  
1. Signs which do not exceed 2.5’ above street grade, and  
2. Freestanding signs which have at least 12’ of clearance above street grade and which do 
not have a support pole larger than12” in diameter. 
 
h. Animated signs, except in the CB, BP, LI, or HI districts. 
 
i. Off-premises signs located on private property, unless approved as a billboard or as part of a 
temporary use permit (for off-premises temporary signage).  
 
j. Signs that are obscene or do not concern lawful activity. 
 
Section 4.  Sections, paragraphs, sentences, clauses, and phrases of this ordinance are 
severable. If any section, paragraph, sentence, clause, or phrase of this ordinance is declared 
unconstitutional or otherwise unlawful, such declaration shall not affect any of the  remaining 
sections, paragraphs, sentences, clauses, and phrases. 
 
 Section 5.  This ordinance shall be in full force and effect upon approval and publication.

BACKGROUND: 
 
The City’s Unified Development Code (UDC) was adopted by the Governing Body on January 23, 2012, by 
Ordinance Number 3943 with an effective  date of April 30, 2012.  The Unified Development Code is the 
regulatory document carrying out the vision of PlanCheyenne and is reviewed annually pursuant to UDC 
1.1.7 [Annual Review Requirement] to determine if any particular standard or regulation is timely, 
relevant and adequately addresses current market conditions. In addition to the annual review, the 
Planning and Development Director and City Engineer are authorized under UDC 2.4.1.b.3 to bring 
forward text amendments on their own initiative.  
 
The proposed text amendment s are to the sign code. During the Annual Review process, there was a 
public request to allow for larger window signs and to allow for off -premises signage. This item was 
discussed at a Council work session, and concerns were expressed about window sign size li mitations,  
off-premises signage, and inappropriate content on signs. 
 
Currently window signs are limited to 25% of the window area. This proposed amendment would allow 
window signage at 100% of the window area, as long as the sign was designed to allow light penetration. 
 
Off-premises signage is prohibited by the current UDC, except as a temporary sign with a temporary use 
permit. Based on the discussion at the work session, this section of code is effectively remaining the same, 
although some clarification is proposed to be added.

Content regulation is a very tricky aspect of sign codes. The United States Supreme Court case Reed v. 
Gilbert (2015) established the basic test that signs could not be read to determine compliance with code 
requirements, with only a few exceptions. A few broad categories appear to be acceptable, such as 
commercial v. noncommercial signs, on-premises v. off-premises signs, and where the government has a 
compelling interest in limiting speech  (for example, Justice Thomas, writing the opinion of the court , 
stated “An ordinance narrowly tailored to the challenges of protecting the safety of pedestrians, drivers, 
and passengers—e.g., warning signs marking hazards on private property or signs directing traffic—might 
also survive strict scrutiny”).  
 
Content-neutral regulations are appropriate (sign elements where the sign does not have to be ‘read’ to 
determine compliance – such as height, area, lighting, portability, limits on placing on public property, 
etc.).  
 
Certain forms of speech are generally not considered ‘protected speech’ (in any context) and may also be 
limited by sign code. In an attempt to address some of the concerns expressed by Council regarding sign 
content, a new section “j” underneath 6.5.7 has  been added to specifically prohibit types of speech that 
are classically considered unprotected. Although such sign content may be effectively prohibited under 
other sections of municipal code (or state or federal regulations), the rejection of these types of speech 
in the zoning code would explicitly allow the zoning official to reject signs in violation, and allow another 
avenue of enforcement against signs in violation. 
 
While 6.5.7 Prohibited Signs was opened for review, Staff added some clarifications to sections based on 
experience with the code: 
- One section of Prohibited Signs is a section intend ed to prevent businesses from creating 
confusion by putting up advertisements that look exactly like traffic control devices. Imagine a 
realistic ‘stop’ sign in the middle of Dell Range to try and get people to visit a retail store adjacent 
to it! However, technically the current code as written would prohibit private developments from 
installing traffic control devices where they are being used properly (e.g. a ‘stop’ sign on an 
internal street) – a clarification to code allows the City to approve needed traffic control signage 
on private property. 
- One section prohibits signs from encroaching in the right -of-way. However, there are places in 
the City where right-of-way is extra-large and it may be appropriate for a sign to be closer to the 
roadway than at the property line. Currently, if the right-of-way is extra-large or misrepresented 
by GIS, a business owner may need a survey or a partial vacation action to get the sign where they 
want it. This amendment proposes allowing signs in the right -of-way if the area of adjacent  
right-of-way is appropriate for signage (as r eviewed and approved by the City Engineering 
department).  
- A clarification to building signage to allow wall signs to be on parapets. On a flat -roofed building 
where a parapet is the highest point, it would be difficult to locate and enforce if the sign could 
not exceed the height of the flat roof behind the parapet 
- Allowance of portable signs in all non-residential zones. Portable signs are currently prohibited in 
the CB zone – but commercial establishments in strip malls in the CB zone may want to use these 
signs. Portable signs in the CB zone are likely to be less obstructing than in the CBD (downtown) 
or other mixed-use zones.

REVIEW CRITERIA: 
 
Review of a UDC text amendment shall be measured by the following three (3) criteria of Section 2.4.1.d: 
 
1. COMPREHENSIVE PLAN:  
“The amendment is in accordance with general goals and policies of the Comprehensive Plan, or 
is necessary to address an emerging issue not anticipated in the Comprehensive Plan and is not 
inconsistent with the plan.” 
 
PlanCheyenne encourages the local governments to “work with local and regional economic 
development, educational, and business partners to  position the Cheyenne Area as a leading 
place to live and do business in the Northern Front Range region.” (Policy 1.5.A) This amendment 
is based off of collaboration with business parks to increase the ability of the region to respond 
to incoming business opportunities. 
 
PlanCheyenne also encourages the region to provide “quality of life amenities that are 
competitive with those of  other communities in the Northern Front Range region ” – including 
parks and recreation facilities (Policy 1.5.C) . This amendment allows the City more flexibility in 
using recreational impact fees to provide the amenities and facilities needed by the community. 
Similarly, Policies 6.5.A encourages centrally located recreation facilities that can serve large 
numbers of people.
