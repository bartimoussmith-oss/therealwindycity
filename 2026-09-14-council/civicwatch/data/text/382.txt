ANALYSIS OF APPLICATION CONFORMANCE WITH REVIEW CRITERIA: 
 
Should the Planning Commission wish to recommend approval of the Zoning Map Amendment, the 
Commission must make the following findings required in UDC 2.2.1.d. A detailed analysis is below: 
 
1) The application is in accordance with the development policies and physical 
patterns identified in the Comprehensive Plan.  (UDC 2.2.1.d.1) 
 
PlanCheyenne encourages opportunities for a range of industrial employment uses as well as 
encourages working with community partners such as LEADS. LEADS oversees the Cheyenne 
Business Park.  
 
The Future Land Use Designation for this area is Industrial and Mixed-use Employment. According 
to the comprehensive plan the optimal City zones for the subject areas future land use 
designation is any of our industrial or commercial zones (for the areas designated mixed use 
employment). The BP zone is a mix of both our industrial and commercial uses and would be 
suited for this area of the community.  
 
Based on the policies and future land use map, BP zoning is appropriate at this location.  
 
The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 1. 
 
2) The application is compatible with nearby property, specifically considering 
existing zoning, the Comprehensive Plan, and any other official plans and policies 
created under the guidance of that plan for these areas; (UDC 2.2.1.d.2) 
 
Much of the area is zoned LI, HI or CB. The BP zone is intended to be a combination of these 
three zones. The area is also developed with industrial and commercial type uses and would 
continue to develop with these types of uses based on the comprehensive plan. Nearby 
properties are zoned HI, LI (in the County), and CB. These areas are compatible with BP zoning 
and the BP may even extend to these areas in the future.  
 
The County PUD zone to the east is a large-scale civic area distant from the business park, but the 
BP zone would be compatible with the anticipated development patterns of this property. The 
County PUD is developed with a more industrial-style development which is compatible with BP 
zoning.  
 
Large-acreage parcels surrounding the development to the south include AG zoning in the City 
and LU and P zoning in the county. These parcels would not be adversely affected by the 
proposed BP zone. 
 
Properties to the north include MR zoning and County LU zoning developed with largely 
residential land uses. However, these parcels are across the railroad from the proposed zone 
change and are currently adjacent to land zoned HI. These parcels would not be adversely 
affected by the proposed BP zone. 
 
 
PUDC-26-29 / 30   May 4, 2026

There is a City PUD developed with residential uses to the southwest of the proposed zone 
change, but the neighborhood is currently surrounded by HI zoning on one of 3 sides and LI 
zoning on the other 2 sides. These parcels would not be adversely affected by the proposed BP 
zone. 
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 2. 
   
3) The suitability of the subject property for development according to the standards 
and uses of the existing zoning district, compared to the suitability of the subject 
property for development according to the standards and uses of the proposed 
zoning district (UDC 2.2.1.d.3) 
  
The BP zone is a combination of the LI, HI and CB zones which will allow this area to develop with 
mixes of industrial and commercial without the need to do additional zone changes. Over the 
years there have been several parcels that had switched from LI to CB back to LI or vice versa. The 
BP zone also allows more flexibility when it comes to landscaping and property coverage.  
 
The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 3. 
 
4) The ability of the subject property and surrounding vicinity to be developed 
according to the Subdivision Standards in Article 4 considering the proposed 
zoning (UDC 2.2.1.d.4) 
 
No changes to the street network are proposed. Accesses will not be changed with the zone 
change. Infrastructure (streets and utilities) exist in the area. Generally speaking, business parks 
are not anticipated to conform to block sizes, where generally CB zoned areas would be. 
Changing areas from CB to BP would still allow for commercial uses while recognizing the 
difficulty of creating traditional urban blocks in this area of the community. 
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 4. 
 
5) The ability of the application to support the Intent and Applicability statements of 
the proposed zoning district and achieve the Design Objectives of any specific 
standards. (UDC 2.2.1.d.5) 
 
The Business Park (BP) district is intended to support small and large-scale industrial development 
while providing essential commercial services along regional transportation routes in an industrial 
park setting. The district integrates manufacturing, processing, research and development, and 
shipping activities in addition to retail, dining, and convenience stores to enhance functionality 
and appeal for businesses and employees.  
 
The BP district is applicable to areas on the edge of the community that are intended to support  
large-scale industrial, manufacturing, shipping, research and development, and data center uses. 
The district is typically found close to major transportation routes, making commercial uses 
appropriate. The district shall contain not less than 320 acres of land (interrupted only by roads, 
railroads, highways and natural features (such as ridges and waterways). Specifically, large -scale 
areas within the future land use designations in the comprehensive plan of Community Business, 
Mixed-Use Employment, and Industrial – including Community/Regional Activity Centers. Areas 
PUDC-26-29 / 30   May 4, 2026

currently designated Agricultural/Rural (either inside or outside of the Urban Service Boundary) 
may also be appropriate for this zone district if near to regional transportation routes and if 
development of the area extends appropriate infrastructure. 
 
The proposed zone change is to take two existing business parks along regional transportation 
routes and change them to the BP zone. The area proposed to be rezoned is over the required 
320 acres.  
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 5. 
    
6) The ability of all potential future development to meet all applicable standards for 
development in the proposed zoning district, including other development options 
if any specific development proposed in association with the application is not 
executed after approval. (UDC 2.2.1.d.6) 
 
The BP zone is a mix of the LI, HI and CB zone which allows for many different industrial and 
commercial uses. The BP zone will allow undeveloped properties in the area to develop without 
the need to change between the LI, HI or CB zone. The BP zone also provides flexibility in terms of 
landscaping and property coverage.    
 
The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 6. 
 
7) The review and recommendations of other reviewing bodies. (UDC 2.2.1.d.7) 
 
No major comments were received in regard to the proposed zone change.  
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 7. 
 
8) Whether the application will harm the public health, safety, or general welfare. 
(UDC 2.2.1.d.8) 
 
City staff is satisfied that this proposed zone change would not adversely affect the public health, 
safety, or general welfare. 
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 8.

be approved or denied based on the review criteria (listed above). Public comments relevant to the review 
criteria may assist the review and discussion on the application.

- Legal Notice in the Wyoming Tribune Eagle published on April 18, 2026.

affect their land. The project page on the website has received 3 views and no comments.

Through the review process, review comments were received expressing the appropriateness of the  
BP zone at this location. 
 
CONCLUSION: 
 
Staff is satisfied that the application meets the review criteria set forth in UDC 2.2.1 .(d).  The BP zone is 
appropriate based on the future land use map, surrounding zoning, surrounding development patterns, 
and proposed development.
