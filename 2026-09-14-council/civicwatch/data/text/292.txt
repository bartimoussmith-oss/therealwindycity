Re: PUDC-26-29 / PUDC-26-30 — Request to Exclude Lot 1, Block 1, Cheyenne Business Parkway, 11th 
Filing from BP Rezone 
 
Dear Mr. White, 
On behalf of Christensen Rd Phase 1 LLC, owner of Lot 1, Block 1, Cheyenne Business Parkway, 11th 
Filing, we respectfully request that our parcel be excluded from the proposed rezone from Heavy 
Industrial (HI) to Business Park (BP). We very much appreciate the time you and your colleagues have 
taken to walk us through the proposal, and we want to be constructive participants in the City’s broader 
planning effort. After working through how the change would actually apply to our parcel, however, we 
do not believe it can comfortably be reconciled with the criteria the City would need to find under UDC 
§2.2.1.d. 
Our property operates under the conditional use permit and Certificate of Compliance issued in Case 
PUDC-23-203 on June 12, 2024 for “General Industrial with Outdoor Storage – Commercial Warehouse,” 
with the approved yard covering essentially the full site (see Exhibit A). Under HI, that use is fully 
conforming and entitled. Under BP, as we understand the planner’s reading of UDC §1.3.11.c, the same 
use would convert to legal nonconforming. That is a meaningful downgrade for us in three respects: 
1. A continuous 12-month vacancy would permanently terminate the use, where today vacancy 
carries no clock. 
2. Any expansion or material change — including restructuring how the yard is leased (for 
example, bifurcating yard from building tenancies) — would move from a permitting question 
with available paths under HI to a discretionary nonconforming-use-expansion proceeding 
under BP with no guaranteed path.

3. The “legal nonconforming” designation will surface in every future buyer and lender diligence 
review and discount the asset relative to a fully conforming HI comp. 
A side-by-side summary of the property’s rights under HI versus BP is provided at Exhibit B for ease of 
reference. 
The upsides BP offers — relaxed landscaping, additional by-right commercial uses — do not apply to our 
parcel. We are already landscape-compliant, and our tenant base is industrial and depends on the yard. 
Given that, we respectfully ask the City to consider, as applied specifically to our parcel, the findings 
required by UDC §2.2.1.d — in particular §2.2.1.d.3 (relative suitability of the property for development 
under the existing versus proposed district) and §2.2.1.d.8 (whether the change harms the public 
welfare). We believe excluding our parcel is the cleanest way to satisfy both, and it preserves the City’s 
optionality as well: if our use or strategy ever changes, we retain the ability to petition for BP at that 
time. 
If exclusion is not available, we would appreciate the opportunity to discuss, and ideally to have 
reflected in the record, written confirmation that (a) the entitlement issued under PUDC-23-203 survives 
the rezone intact and runs with the land, (b) ordinary tenant transitions and yard restructuring will not 
be treated as nonconforming-use expansion, and (c) the abandonment provisions of UDC §1.3.11.c will 
not be asserted against the approved use. We would also, as a matter of standard practice, want to 
preserve our rights as an affected landowner under Wyoming law, including those provided under W.S. 
§§ 15-1-602 and 15-1-603, and we intend to participate fully in the public hearing process. 
Thank you again for the helpful correspondence to date, and please let us know if anything further 
would be useful from us. 
 
Respectfully,
