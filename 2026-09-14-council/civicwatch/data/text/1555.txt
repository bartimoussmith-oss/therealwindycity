BACKGROUND:
The applicant is requesting approval of a preliminary plat for roughly 3.17 acres of land. The proposed plat 
would create three lots, two of which have existing single-family residences on them. The applicant had 
submitted for expedited review, meaning the final plat had also been submitted. Through staff’s review, there 
were several issues that arose, such as ownership and the proposed street vacation. Due to these issues, staff is 
not processing this application as an expedited plat and only the preliminary plat is moving forward at this 
time.

On the future land use map found in PlanCheyenne, the subject land and surrounding area has 
been designated as Urban Residential. The Urban Residential designation encourages a range of 
residential uses. The proposed plat creates two lots for the existing single-family homes and a 
third lot for residential development. 
Foundation 1 of PlanCheyenne encourages community growth. This development is in response 
to market demands and is within the Urban Service Boundary. The development patterns 
proposed by this plat are compatible with the designations found on the future land use map.
The application complies with this review criterion. 
2) The application reflects sound planning and urban design principles with respect to 
existing and potential future development in the vicinity, including consideration 
of current and future streets, open spaces, blocks and lots, and public utilities or 
facilities. (UDC 2.1.2.d.2)
The proposed plat subdivides one lot into two and also proposes vacating right-of-way and 
platting that into the adjacent parcels. With the proposed vacation, staff believes that the entirety 
of Frank Court needs to be vacated and not just a portion. A partial vacation creates a street 
section that does not meet UDC standards and is not a benefit. Vacation with a plating action 
requires all owners adjacent to the right-of-way to sign on the plat application. Not all owners 
with interest in the proposed-to-be-vacated rights-of-way have signed on the plat.
The application complies with this review criterion, with conditions.  
3) The division of land meets the purposes, intent, and all applicable standards in 
Article 4. (UDC 2.1.2.d.3)
Community Facility Fees (4.2):
For fees based on acreage, the area of the plat is pro-rated to the nearest tenth of an acre. 
Lands reserved as unbuildable (for right-of-way, detention, parks, remainder lots, etc), are 
exempt from fees. Park fees are calculated at $700 per acre. All fees are due at the time of 
building permit.
Per UDC Section 4.2.5, Table 4-2, Enhancement Fees are payable at the issuance of a building 
permit for each structure. The Park Enhancement Fee for residential is $400 per dwelling unit.
Transportation Network (4.3):
This plat proposes vacating portions of Frank Court via the platting action. The proposed 
vacation by the applicant only vacates half the street. Staff believes that if any portion of 
Frank Court is to be vacated, the entire street should be vacated. Staff does not support a 
partial vacation. Full vacation may be done by including all owners in the plat or by the City 
initiating a vacation ordinance process.
This plat includes the east-west section of Frank Court which at this time has some ownership 
and vacation questions that surround it. There are issues with the resolution that is referenced 
on the plat and more research may be needed to determine if this section has been vacated 
and who actually owns it.

Although this plat proposes vacating streets, the streets to be vacated are undeveloped and 
would not provide meaningful street connections even if built. Due to Dry Creek, block 
connectivity is limited in this area and reducing block size by extending the current streets or 
by creating new streets is not reasonable. Staff recommends granting an exception to block 
size.
Open Space (4.4):
Open space is required at 750sqft per unit or 8% of the gross development area, whichever is 
less. Due to the nature of the proposed development pattern, open space will be taken care 
of with future development actions.
Required Improvements (4.5):
Utilities are available within this area already. The applicant needs to provide information that 
future connections can be made should any of the lots need to connect to City water and 
sewer. Should lot 1 ever need water and sewer, easements may be needed and are not 
provided at this time. 
Public Safety Fees (4.6):
Public Safety Fees are required at the time of construction of any residential building on any 
of the proposed lots. Per UDC Section 4.6.3.b, Public Safety Fees are payable at the issuance 
of a building permit for each structure. 
The application complies with this review criterion, with conditions.

8) The application will not create any undue influence on existing or potential

Comprehensive Plan policies for that property.  (UDC 2.1.2.d.8)
The previous development pattern in the area utilized wells and septic systems. The utility plan 
submitted by the applicant indicated that both houses are currently on wells and septic systems. If 
this is the case, the creation of the new Lot 3 may interfere with the locations and functions of 
these systems. Further information is needed to determine where existing facilities are prior to 
authorizing a final division of land. 
If existing wells and septic systems are abandoned and easements for utilities are extended, this 
plat will not create any undue influence on existing or potential development. Frank Court is an 
under utilized right-of-way that only servers one home. The surrounding lots are developed and 
have access to other rights-of-way. 
The application complies with this review criterion, with conditions.
