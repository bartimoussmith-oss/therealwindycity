BACKGROUND: 
 
The applicant is requesting connection to BOPU sanitary sewer services for property outside City limits. There 
is currently existing sewer in the area. City sanitary sewer is currently within the property along the 
southwest corner. City water is not proposed to be included in the Outside User Agreement. Water is over 
1,000 feet away from the portion of the site proposed to be developed.  
 
The standard Outside User Agreement contract includes language where the applicant consents to 
annexation at the Citys request, with no specific date to complete the annexation. 
 
SURROUNDING LAND USE AND ZONING: 
 
 
The property is currently zoned AR Agricultural Residential in the County.  
 
ANALYSIS OF APPLICATION CONFORMANCE WITH REVIEW CRITERIA: 
 
The request for an outside user agreement has no formal review criteria; however, the following 
elements should be considered when reviewing outside user agreements: 
- BOPU capacity 
- Needed infrastructure improvements 
- Fiscal cost/benefit to the City and the BOPU 
- Population served 
- Considerations on the effect on future annexation(s), including proximity to current and future 
City services 
- Conformance with the Comprehensive Plan 
 
 The proposed 
use of the properties is for outdoor recreation. Sanitary sewer is available along Dry Creek to the 
southwest of the property.

Property tax would increase, but the City would not have any income from the property tax in the area
at this time. The proposed use is not residential and would not change distribution of funds based on 
population. The BOPU may charge a higher rate for outside users.  
 
City limits exist to the north, west, south and east of this property  the property is only not in a County 
pocket due a corridor to the northeast. Non-infrastructure City services, such as fire, police, and 
sanitation could relatively easily be extended to the property. City sewer runs through the property. City 
water, on the other hand, would require a significant extension (partially due to the need to provide two 
connections for water). While this sort of extension of water lines would be feasible for large-scale 
development, the fractured ownership and existence of a floodplain make large-scale development and 
cost-effective water provision to the site difficult. Annexation was considered for the property (as City 
limits are adjacent to the property across the railroad tracks), but the extension of City water to this 
location would be difficult to do in a cost-effective manner. Future annexation of this property may 
result in a site that is in City limits but substandard compared to typical City requirements, including fire 
code requirements. 
 
The Comprehensive Plan designates this area as Open Space  and the proposed use in this location is 
consistent with that anticipated development pattern.
