MEMO
To: Honorable Mayor Patrick Collins and Members of the City Council 
From: Seth Lloyd, AICP, Senior Planner 
Subject: An Ordinance amending weeds language in municipal code to allow native grasses to grow 
over 6 inches in height 
Date: September 22, 2025 
Background: 
The item for consideration is an amendment to municipal code and is approved by ordinance. The proposed 
Governing Body review schedule is as follows: 
• Introduction and Referral:  September 22, 2025 
• Public Services Committee:  October 6, 2025 
• Second reading:   October 13, 2025 
• Public Services Committee:  October 20, 2025 
• Third and final reading:   October 27, 2025 
The Unified Development Code (UDC) allows for native seed landscaping. Recent amendments to the UDC have 
increased allowances for native seed landscaping. Native seed is often used on unused areas on industrial sites 
and to revegetate open spaces and vacant parcels. The City code, on the other hand, includes language making 
all grasses over 6 inches in height a nuisance requiring abatement by the City. Native grasses are healthier, grow 
better, outcompete weeds better, and look better when allowed to grow over 6 inches. Strict application of the 
code would require hundreds of acres of industrial parks to mow their native seed prairie grasses, which would 
generally encourage weed growth and would not maintain a natural landscape look. 
This ordinance proposes removing the height limit on grasses for specific cases: 
• The first case would be when native grass is clearly identified as the ground cover on the site plan.
• The second case is to allow open spaces designated as natural areas to retain the look and feel of a
natural area.
• The third is to allow natural areas on residential and agricultural lots over 8/10ths of an acre. Larger
residential lots often do not irrigate and mow the entire parcel, and upon study by Staff, it appears most
residential parcels with 8/10ths of an acre or more leave at least a portion of their parcel native. Larger
parcels north of the City outside of City limits appear to irrigate about 8/10ths of an acre on average.
Agricultural areas often have grasses grow over 6 inches prior to grazing.
• The fourth case is to clearly allow City-managed parcels to utilize grasses over 6 inches. Although City
parcels could likely fit into one of first two categories above, there could be cases where applicability is
ambiguous.
The code currently allows for grasses to be over 6 inches in Greenway corridors and in cases where residents 
cultivate grasses for aesthetic purposes. Those exceptions will remain in code.

8.60.050 - Weeds as a nuisance. 
 
A. Six Inches in Height. All weeds, tall Except as provided in subsection (C) of this section, 
all grasses, weeds, and rank, and other troublesome and harmful vegetation growing upon 
any lot or parcel of land outside the traveled portion of any street or alley in the city which 
that exceed a height of six (6) inches are a nuisance. , unless shown to have been cultivated 
for aesthetic purposes. The chief of police shall make the determination as to whether or 
not vegetation is cultivated for aesthetic purposes. The greenway, set out in the greenway 
development plan, is exempt from this requirement upon approval by the chief of police. 
 
B. Noxious Weeds.  All noxious weeds within the city are a nuisance.  As used in this 
subsection, "noxious weeds" means any plant species designated by the Wyoming Board 
of Agriculture and the Wyoming Weed and Pest Council. 
 
1. Defined. "Noxious weeds" shall be designated by the Wyoming Board of Agriculture 
and the Wyoming Weed and Pest Council. 
 
2. Noxious Weeds a Nuisance. All noxious weeds within the city are a nuisance. 
 
C. Subsection (A) of this section shall not apply to: 
 
1. Native or naturalized vegetation, including but not limited to grasses, flowers, shrubs, 
or other plant species not classified as noxious weeds, that are: 
 
a. Authorized for a subject lot or parcel of land  as part of the site plan approved by 
the City in accorda nce with  the requirements imposed under Cheyenne Unified 
Development Code; 
 
b. Located within any lot, parcel or portion thereof formally designated as a natural 
area in accordance with the Cheyenne Unified Development Code;

c. Located within any residentially or agriculturally zoned lot or parcel having a total 
area of eight-tenths (0.8) of an acre or greater; or 
 
d. Located on a lot or parcel  or portion thereof  managed by the City under a 
sustainable landscape plan intended to promote native species, reduce irrigation and 
chemical use, mitigate weed growth, and control erosion. 
 
2. Any lot, parcel or portion thereof cultivated and maintained for aesthetic purposes, as 
determined by the compliance department; or 
 
3. Any portion of the greenway, as identified in the City’s greenway development plan, 
upon the approval by the compliance department.
