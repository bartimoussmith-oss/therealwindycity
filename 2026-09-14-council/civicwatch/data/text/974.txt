for engineering services for the Clear Creek Inline Stormwater Detention Facility Project. 
 
The project is located along Clear Creek, directly upstream of the confluence of Crow and Clear 
creeks. The Clear Creek Drainage Basin is approximately 24 square miles and extends southwest 
of Cheyenne to the confluence of the creeks at Ames  Underpass just south of the Union Pafic 
Railroad tracks. Development downstream of the UPRR  has significantly altered the historical 
alignments of Clear Creek and Crow Creek. The histo ric channel within the corporate limits of 
Cheyenne has been filled and replaced with a small low-flow channel. The original confluence 
with Crow Creek was realigned in the 1930s and now exacerbates inundation and backwater issues 
through   the Ames Avenue underpass. Within David R omero Park, the floodplain is still intact 
and there is an expansive area on the south and east sides of the park, along the historic alignment 
of the channel, that can be used for significant st orage and flood relief for the Ames Avenue 
underpass. Storage at this location would assist in mitigating flooding in the downstream properties 
and backwater impacts at Ames Underpass.

Scope of Work 
Overview 
C
onsultant services for the Cheyenne Clear Creek Inline Stormwater Detention Facility project are the 
development of a design that will evaluate and identify a feasible, constructible inline storage facility within 
the corporate limits of Cheyenne.  Any innovative solutions that are cost effective and meet project goals 
will be incorporated during project scoping negotiations and included in the analyses/design. The work 
required for this project will include project management, field surveys, hydrologic/hydraulic analysis, 
storm drainage and detention design, geotechnical investigations, etc. 
The project requires a professional design team that includes a Civil Engineer licensed in the State of 
Wyoming with a minimum of 10 years of project design experience in one or more of the following areas 
of expertise: hydrologic/hydraulic analysis and the design of major stormwater projects; with the ability to 
provide in-house or capacity to sub-consult for survey and other services required for the project.   
Project Description 
T
he project is located along Clear Creek, directly upstream of the confluence of Crow and Clear creeks. 
The Clear Creek Drainage Basin is approximately 24 square miles and extends southwest of Cheyenne to 
the confluence of the creeks at Ames Underpass just south of the Union Pafic Railroad tracks. Development 
downstream of the UPRR has significantly altered the historical alignments of Clear Creek and Crow Creek. 
The historic channel within the corporate limits of Cheyenne has been filled and replaced with a small low-
flow channel. The original confluence with Crow Creek was realigned in the 1930s and now exacerbates 
inundation and backwater issues through   the Ames Avenue underpass. Within the Dave Romero Park, the 
floodplain is still intact and there is an expansive area on the south   and east sides of the park, along the 
historic alignment of the channel, that can be used for significant storage and flood relief for the Ames 
Avenue underpass. Storage at this location would assist in mitigating flooding in the downstream properties 
and backwater impacts at Ames Underpass.  
The selected consultant will use an iterative approach with the hydrological and hydraulic modeling of 
project alternative designs, resulting in design alternatives to be evaluated. This approach will lead to a 
cost-effective project recommendation for the community. There will be multiple alternatives considered 
in the scoping/design effort including natural-based solutions such as expanded floodplain functionality for 
Clear Creek, expanded and new wetlands within the drainage basin, and new detention/bio-retention 
features to enhance the overall functionality of the creek while providing much-needed flood control for 
our community. The existing connection to Crow Creek and impacts with the proposed detention area(s) 
will also be evaluated for larger storm events. This proposed project is intended to explore mitigation of 
flood events from Crow Creek that can be incorporated into the design to the extent practical.  This approach 
will result in cost-effective and feasible mitigation measures that meet FEMA’s objectives of utilizing 
nature-based solutions and addressing both climate change and future conditions in the basin. Any 
additional value engineering recommendations that evolve during the scoping process will be given 
consideration by City staff.

Project Tasks and Deliverables 
 
At a minimum, the following tasks will be included in the analysis and design of the project: 
1)  Field Surveys and Reconnaissance for collection and  evaluation of pertinent data needed for 
hydraulic modeling of design alternatives including conveyance pipes for a realignment of the Clear 
Creek confluence with Crow Creek.  
2)  Identify existing utilities and adjust or relocate if necessary. 
3)  Digital topographic data development for use in hydraulic and geotechnical modeling efforts.  
4)  The development of a working hydraulic model to eva luate and design alternative storage areas 
and conveyance pipes using a reach specific model. The model will incorporate  non-proprietary 
computer modelling such as the Environmental Protection Agency SWMM model, USACE HEC-
HMS, and/or the USACE’s HEC-RAS model into the desi gn and provide review copies with the 
project deliverables. The consultant will supplement these models with a specific culvert analysis 
model as necessary. The underlying hydrologic input  will initially be taken from the current 
effective regulatory hydrologic models.  The consul tant will provide recommendations for a 
corrected effective model, or a full replacement hydrological model for Clear Creek with the scope 
of the project.   
5)  Preparation of Cost Estimates/BCA Development per F EMA guidelines. The consultant will 
conduct BCA’s of each alternative hydraulic analysis and potential mitigation strategies to ensure 
the constructability and cost-effectiveness of the recommended strategy. 
6)  The evaluation and design of a detention storage fa cility (or multiple facilities working together) 
to provide flood relief for both the Ames Avenue underpass and downstream residential properties 
located in the historic flow path of Clear Creek that has been inundated with larger flood events. 
7)  Preparation of construction plans, special provisions to the standard specifications, and engineer’s 
estimates for construction cost for the preferred alternative. 
8)  Complete a FEMA CLOMR/LOMR for changes to the flood plain and FIRM. Finalization of the 
LOMR can be completed after the FEMA period of performance ends. 
9)  Update the City/County floodplain mapping.
