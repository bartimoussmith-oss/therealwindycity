 
MEMORANDUM 
TO:  City of Cheyenne Council Members 
FROM:  Robin Lockman, City Treasurer 
DATE:  November 18, 2025 
SUBJECT: Cost-of-living Wage Increase Proposed for Approval 
 
I am submitting for your review and consideration a resolution proposing a cost-of-living 
salary adjustment (COLA) for all full -time and part- time employees of the City of 
Cheyenne, effective January 1, 2026. 
Background 
The City of Cheyenne recognizes that escalating cost-of-living pressures continue to affect 
the financial stability of City employees and their families. The State of Wyoming 
Economic Analysis Division’s Cost of Living Index for the second quarter of 2025 reported 
a statewide inflation rate of 4.2%, with the Southeast Wyoming region , including 
Cheyenne, reflecting similar inflationary pressures. Addressing these higher costs is 
essential to maintaining a competitive and equitable compensation structure for City staff. 
Summary of Resolution 
The attached resolution requests a three percent (3%) cost-of-living salary adjustment for 
all employees, while balancing the City’s commitment to fiscal responsibility. This salary 
adjustment will apply to all full -time and part- time City employees employed as of 
November 30, 2025, except elected officials and Board of Public Utilities staff.  
The estimated six-month cost ( including benefits ) of this request for full -time only 
employees is $555,345 from General Fund reserves and $160,858 for all other City funds. 
Final costs may fluctuate depending on any new hires before November 30, 2025, or 
terminations occurring before January 1, 2026. Additionally, the variable schedules of 
part-time employees prevent an accurate estimate at this time; therefore, a 
reappropriation at fiscal year-end for part-time staff will be requested if necessary.   
Requested Action 
Please review and consider adoption of the attached resolution.

WHEREAS, the City of Cheyenne recognizes that increases in the cost  of living 
continue to affect the financial well-being of its employees and their families; 
 
WHEREAS, maintaining a fair, transparent, and competitive compensation structure is 
vital to the City’s ability to attract, retain, and reward a skilled and dedicated workforce 
committed to providing high-quality public service; 
 
WHEREAS, cost -of-living adjustments represent a fiscally responsible means of 
offsetting inflationary pressures and ensuring that employee compensation remains aligned 
with regional economic conditions; 
 
WHEREAS, on November 4, 2025, the State of Wyoming Economic Analysis 
Division released the Wyoming Cost of Living Index for the second quarter of 2025, reporting 
a statewide increase in inflation compared to the same period in the previous year; 
 
WHEREAS, the regional inflation rate for Southeast Wyoming, which includes 
Albany, Carbon, Goshen, Laramie, Niobrara, and Platte Counties, was reported at four and 
two-tenths percent  (4.2%), reflecting a continued trend of higher living costs within the 
region; and 
 
WHEREAS, the Governing Body of the City of Cheyenne values fiscal stewardship 
and seeks to balance competitive employee compensation with long -term budgetary 
sustainability. 
 
NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED  BY THE GOVERNING BODY OF THE 
CITY OF CHEYENNE , WYOMING, that effective January 1, 2026, all full -time and part -
time employees employed by the City as of November 30, 2025, excluding elected officials 
and employees of the City of Cheyenne Board of Public Utilities, shall receive a three  percent 
(3%) cost-of-living salary adjustment; 
 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED , that the City Treasurer is hereby directed to prepare 
and submit a budget reappropriation allocating the necessary funds from available reserves, 
operational efficiencies, and eligible grant funding sources to implement this cost -of-living 
adjustment in a manner consistent with sound fiscal management; and 
 
BE IT FINALLY RESOLVED, that the Governing Body continues to affirm its 
commitment to maintaining a stable and sustainable  municipal workforce through periodic 
review of compensation policies and prevailing market conditions.
