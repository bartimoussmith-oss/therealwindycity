BACKGROUND: 
This zone change proposes changing roughly 8 blocks of the northwest portion of downtown from P zoning 
and MUB zoning to CBD zoning. There has been some interest in making changes to the development 
pattern on the block of the Municipal Building, and in researching options, it was discovered the property is 
nonconforming in multiple ways in the current P zone. The same issues specific to this area of town which  
create challenges for the redevelopment of the Municipal Building would also create issues for other 
governmental agencies on land currently zoned P Public. While the impetus behind the change was the 
municipal building, the County property was looped in. 
 
The new zoning, should it be adopted, would leave an MUB enclave surrounded by CBD zoning to the south 
of the proposed zone change. This enclave is bordered on two sides by the P zoning proposed to be changed. 
That block was therefore included as well. 
 
To the northeast, there is a federally-owned building that it is Staff’s understanding will be disposed of in the 
near future. Non-public entities are not supposed to use P Public zoning and including this block would round 
out the area becoming CBD to a nine-block square.

ANALYSIS OF APPLICATION CONFORMANCE WITH REVIEW CRITERIA: 
 
Should the Planning Commission wish to recommend approval of the Zoning Map Amendment, the 
Commission must make the following findings required in UDC 2.2.1.d. A detailed analysis is below: 
 
1) The application is in accordance with the development policies and physical patterns 
identified in the Comprehensive Plan.  (UDC 2.2.1.d.1) 
 
PlanCheyenne encourages revitalizing downtown and including a mix of uses (Policy 1.4.C and 
Principal 3.2), including retail, restaurants, employment, commercial, office, residential, and 
civic uses. By changing these properties to CBD zoning, a wider variety of uses are allowed and 
there will be more options for redevelopment.  
 
PlanCheyenne has a future land use designation of “Central Business District”. The definition of 
this future land use designation includes the language that the area is “the community’s core 
business and civic area” (emphasis added). The description also states that the designation 
“covers the entire downtown area”. Although many properties in the area proposed to be 
rezoned are designated Public Quasi-public instead of Central Business District on the future 
land use map, the map results in a patchwork around this area of downtown when the Central 
Business District is intended to be all of downtown. Also, the original future land use map used a 
parcel-based analysis that resulted in any tax-exempt property being designated Public  
Quasi-public even if properties were not governmental agencies or even if properties were HUD 
single-family homes surrounded by the Urban Residential designation. As such, the future land 
use designation of Public Quasi-public should be taken with caution and surrounding future land 
use designations should be considered. The future land use designation surrounding this area of 
Public Quasi-public is largely Central Business District. 
 
The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 1. 
 
2) The application is compatible with nearby property, specifically considering existing zoning, 
the Comprehensive Plan, and any other official plans and policies created under the guidance 
of that plan for these areas; (UDC 2.2.1.d.2) 
 
Surrounding properties to the east and south are zoned CBD, as is one property to the 
northwest and a few enclaves within the subject area. The CBD zone for the subject property is 
compatible with the region. 
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 2. 
   
3) The suitability of the subject property for development according to the standards and uses of 
the existing zoning district, compared to the suitability of the subject property for 
development according to the standards and uses of the proposed zoning district (UDC 
2.2.1.d.3) 
  
Generally speaking, P zone regulations are for more suburban areas of the community – requiring 
large setbacks and limiting the mixture of uses available. For example, the P zone does not allow 
residential uses or food service uses.  
 
PUDC-25-197   September 15, 2025 
On the other hand, the CBD zoning specifically includes governmental uses as part of the diverse mix 
of uses allowed, and has no setbacks. 
 
The block zoned MUB would be subject to stricter use allowances and design requirements than all 
surrounding properties if it were not included with this zone change. The nearby MUB-zoned parcels 
to the south and west are all in the Urban Use overlay zone which also relaxes some of the zone’s 
typical design standards.  
 
The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 3. 
 
4) The ability of the subject property and surrounding vicinity to be developed according to the 
Subdivision Standards in Article 4 considering the proposed zoning (UDC 2.2.1.d.4) 
 
The zone change is taking place in a previously-developed area of the community and a robust 
street network already exists. Public improvements (such as sidewalks and open spaces) also 
already exist in the area, though some routine maintenance may be needed. 
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 4. 
 
5) The ability of the application to support the Intent and Applicability statements of the 
proposed zoning district and achieve the Design Objectives of any specific standards. (UDC 
2.2.1.d.5) 
 
The CBD zone’s applicability states that the zone is “intended for a wide range of Retail, 
Employment, Service, Civic, Residential, and Public uses forming the financial, governmental, 
and cultural core of the community.” (emphasis added) Adding public, governmental properties 
to the CBD zoning is appropriate. The CBD zone is applicable to the central urban core of the 
City, and while the subject property is northwest of the most central parts of the core, CBD 
zoning exists on multiple blocks both north and west of the subject property. 
 
The CBD zone does not have design or dimensional standards. 
 
The proposed zone change for the subject property complies with review criterion 5. 
    
6) The ability of all potential future development to meet all applicable standards for 
development in the proposed zoning district, including other development options if any 
specific development proposed in association with the application is not executed after 
approval. (UDC 2.2.1.d.6) 
 
Although there are no design or dimensional standards specific to the CBD zone, certain  
city-wide requirements will still be in force with the change in zoning, for example, street trees 
are required in all zones, as is parking lot landscaping (when parking lots above a certain size are 
provided). 
 
The CBD zone allows for a wide variety of uses in a more urban environment, including 
residential and mixed uses. The specific potential development behind this zone change is 
revamping the Municipal Building, but should that project never be executed, the CBD zoning 
and its variety of allowed uses are appropriate for this location.   
 
The proposed zone change complies with review criterion 6.
