Background:  
 
The item for consideration is a contract for professional services to update the Community Character 
element of the Cheyenne Comprehensive Plan (PlanCheyenne). The Governing Body approval schedule 
is as follows:    
• Introduction and Referral   August 24, 2026 
• Finance Committee   September 9, 2026 (Wednesday) 
• Final Consideration  September 14, 2026 
 
Eleven firms responded to the Request for Proposals (RFP). Three submittals were disqualified for failing 
to meet submittal requirements. The remaining eight submittals were evaluated by a selection 
committee comprising representatives from the City Council, Planning and Development, Engineering, 
the MPO, Laramie County Planning and Development, the Planning Commission, and a local developer. 
 
The committee shortlisted and conducted in-person interviews with four firms: 
• Cushing Terrell  
• Houseal Lavigne Associates LLC  
• Kimley-Horn  
• Logan Simpson Design Inc.  
 
Additional submittals evaluated but not shortlisted included Design Workshop, Glenn A. Rick 
Engineering & Development Company, Lotus Engineering and Sustainability, LLC, and Orion Planning + 
Design.  
 
Following competitive evaluations and in-person firm presentations, Houseal Lavigne ranked highest 
among the competing consultants. None of the submitting firms qualified for local preference. 
 
Through contract negotiations, staff worked with Houseal Lavigne to optimize both the schedule and 
fee. The scope was refined to condense the project timeline from 18 months down to 14 months 
(targeting final adoption in November 2027) while incorporating land-use scenario planning and 
Placer.ai mobile device data to analyze regional commuting patterns. 
 
The resulting professional services agreement is for a not-to-exceed amount of $351,790. 
 
  
 
Project Scope & Deliverables 
 
Project timing and milestones are detailed in Exhibit A of the contract. Robust Steering Committee and 
public engagement will drive the process throughout. 
 
The final deliverable will be a clear, concise, easy-to-read Comprehensive Plan designed to guide 
Cheyenne’s future growth under current and projected development conditions. The plan will feature 
user-friendly goals and actionable statements to evaluate local development regulations and guide 
future land-use and policy decisions. Supporting deliverables include clear graphics, an implementation 
framework, and key maps covering future land use, active transportation networks, development 
barriers, and residential and technology growth suitability. 
 
Funding Sources 
 
The total contract price has been negotiated to be $351,790.00 with a completion date of in November 
of 2027. Funding sources come from the $240,000 budgeted as a one-time expense for the 
comprehensive plan update and a roll over of unspent County Pocket annexation budget totaling 
$112,900 (Totaling $352,900).

Re: CheyenneNext Comprehensive Plan Update – Revised Scope, Fee, and Timeline 
Houseal Lavigne appreciates the opportunity to continue refining the scope of services for the 
CheyenneNext Comprehensive Plan Update. Based on discussions with the City, Houseal Lavigne has 
updated the attached Scope of Services, Fee Schedule, and Project Timeline to align with the City’s 
priorities and anticipated project outcomes. 
The revised scope maintains a comprehensive community-engagement and planning process while 
establishing a streamlined 14-month schedule through adoption.  
Key revisions and additions to the scope include: 
▪ Refined public-engagement program. It is understood that all online engagement will be
conducted through the City’s Connect Cheyenne platform. The revised scope includes an
online community survey, map.social, up to 16 stakeholder interview or focus-group sessions,
DIY engagement kits, a Business Community Workshop, a Community Kick-off Open House,
and a Community Visioning Workshop. Houseal Lavigne will attend one pop-up event
scheduled in conjunction with another project trip and will prepare reusable materials for
additional City staff-led pop-up outreach.
▪ Clarified Steering Committee involvement. The scope includes four Steering Committee
meetings aligned with key project milestones: project kickoff, review of existing conditions,
evaluation of the vision and land-use scenarios, and review of the draft Comprehensive Plan
Update.
▪ Reorganized existing-conditions analysis. Related public-service, infrastructure,
environmental, cultural, and community-facility topics have been consolidated under a
coordinated Services, Infrastructure, and Community Facilities assessment. The revised
scope also clarifies the intended policy-level review of public safety, utilities, parks and
recreation, environmental conditions, cemetery planning, and arts and culture.

Scope of Work 
Step 1: Project Kick-off 
Meetings will be conducted with key City staff, department heads, and the Steering Committee before undertaking the primary community outreach 
activities.  
1a. Staff Coordination Call and Data Collection 
The Project Team will host an initial coordination call with City staff to review the Scope of Work, project timeline, and key deliverables. As needed, check-
in meetings with City staff will be held throughout the planning process to ensure clear and consistent communication. 
The Project Team will outline data needs including, but not limited to, GIS data regarding parcels, building footprints, existing land use, zoning districts and 
overlays, community facilities (including City-owned properties, schools, parks, etc.), traffic volumes, traffic signals, transit, bike routes and trails, etc.; City 
branding guidelines and photos; past plans and studies; and a list of service providers with contact information. 
1b. Staff Kick-Off Meeting 
A kick-off meeting will be held with the City staff assigned to the Comprehensive Plan Update project. This first face-to-face meeting will allow us to review 
the project goals, scope, timeline, and deliverables. The Project Team will also address any outstanding data needs and discuss next steps. 
1c. Department Heads Meeting 
Immediately following the kick-off meeting with City staff, the Project Team will host a meeting with representatives from other City departments. The 
Comprehensive Plan Update will affect a variety of City-wide policies and support from City departments will be essential to plan implementation. The 
Project Team will work with City staff to engage other department heads throughout the process to ensure that plan recommendations are meaningful and 
actionable for all City departments. 
1d. Staff-led Study Area Tour 
The Project Team will conduct a study area tour with City staff to learn more about Cheyenne’s issues, recent projects, and development opportunities. 
1e. Steering Committee Meeting – Project Kick-off Workshop (Mtg #1) 
The City will identify members of the community to serve on the Steering Committee for the Comprehensive Plan Update process. The Steering 
Committee will serve as a community sounding board to advise on issues and provide overall planning direction. Steering Committee meetings are 
identified at key moments throughout the planning process to ensure that the planning process reflects the goals and values of the Cheyenne community. 
Steering Committee member participation will also help develop champions for the Plan and ensure that the adoption process moves smoothly. 
Before planning work begins, this project initiation meeting will be held with the Steering Committee to set the foundation for the planning process and 
review and discuss the overall direction and policy issues facing the community. The purpose of the project initiation meeting will be to: (a) discuss the 
Steering Committee’s role for the project; (b) review overall project objectives; (c) refine the work program for the project; and (d) establish a preliminary

Step 2: Upfront Public Engagement 
Anticipating a high level of participation from an active and engaged community, our proposed outreach process for the Comprehensive Plan Update 
includes both traditional (face-to-face) and innovative web-based activities. Outreach summaries will be prepared for each public engagement activity.  
2a. Internet-based Public Engagement 
The Project Team is committed to using the internet to maximize the participation and communication between the City and its residents. We understand 
that all online engagement for the Comprehensive Plan Update will be conducted through the City’s Connect Cheyenne platform, built on PublicInput, and 
that no independent project website will be created. The City will provide the Project Team with administrative access to the designated project pages, 
with any features outside those pages coordinated with City staff. The Project Team will work with the City to post meeting dates; display draft documents; 
address frequently asked questions; provide an online community survey, and host map.social as a link or button on Connect Cheyenne.  
Online Community Survey 
The Project Team will prepare an online community survey for the residents and business owners of Cheyenne to offer a community-wide opinion on a 
range of topics and issues. The Project Team will provide the survey language to load into the Connect Cheyenne platform. The business component of 
the survey will include the opportunity to provide specific input on those issues and concerns most important to the City’s business community. At the 
close of the survey, the Project Team will review and summarize results in the Existing Conditions Memorandum as a gauge of community issues and key 
themes.  
map.social (Online Map-Based Engagement Platform) 
The Project Team will work with City staff to host map.social (via link or website button), a web-based community issues mapping tool, on the Connect 
Cheyenne platform. Developed by Houseal Lavigne, this tool allows users to identify, map, and comment on geographic areas of concern and valued City 
amenities. map.social simplifies the mapping process and familiarizes users with all areas of the City in a manner that is intuitive, interactive, and effective. 
Input from users allows us to create a composite map of Cheyenne issues to assist with the establishment of Comprehensive Plan Update goals and 
policies. 
2b. Key Stakeholder Interviews and Focus Groups (up to 16 sessions) 
Confidential interviews/focus group discussions (two to four participants each) will be conducted to obtain additional information regarding local issues and 
opportunities. The Project Team will work with City staff and elected officials to identify those individuals or groups to be interviewed. The Project Team 
recommends a broad sampling of interviewees who may possess unique perspectives or special insights into the community. Interviewees could include 
selected property owners, new or lifelong residents, local business owners, school district officials, the City commissions, developers, HOA 
representatives, service providers, economic development entities and local nonprofits, and relevant taxing bodies and government agencies. Examples of 
potential interviewees could include representatives from Laramie County, the Cheyenne MPO, F.E. Warren Air Force Base, and the VA Hospital. The 
interviews and focus group discussions will be conducted in person during scheduled visits related to other outreach events or via web meeting during a 
specific scheduled day for such activities.  
2c. DIY Kits 
The Project Team will make DIY workshop kits available to City staff, as well as community groups (e.g., partner organizations, homeowners associations, 
places of worship, community organizations, neighborhood groups) throughout the City. DIY workshop kits allow City staff and residents to facilitate their 
own workshops and gather input from specific segments of the population that may not otherwise participate in more formal planning activities. City staff 
will play a key role in helping distribute DIY workshop materials to target groups and then provide summary information to the Project Team. DIY 
Workshops can either be conducted by staff or volunteers as in-person events or hosted online using a small group virtual event format such as Zoom or 
Microsoft Teams. Houseal Lavigne will summarize the results of up to 15 DIY kits.

Mobile Device Location Data Collection and Analysis. Given the relationship of Cheyenne to other Front Range communities and the large proportion 
of residents who commute out and into the City every day, the City’s function as a regional hub at the convergence of I-25 and I-80, and increased traffic

impacts of new industrial and residential developments, and potential travel behavior changes (e.g. route shifts, mode shifts). Mead & Hunt will use probe-

existing Replica License; no additional data purchases are anticipated. Specific pattern insights will include major origins and destinations, commute 
sheds/ employee/ employer origin-destination, trip purpose, trip length, mode choice, trip maker demographics, destination trip/ visitation levels, trip diurnal 
distribution to inform the Land Use and Development, Economic Development, and Transportation elements of the Comprehensive Plan. This analysis will

Development

4a. Community Visioning Workshop  
The community visioning workshop will allow residents and stakeholders to tell us what they think, before plans and recommendations are crafted. The 
community visioning workshop will involve the Project Team, City staff, elected and appointed officials, Planning Commission members, and community 
stakeholders and residents.  
The workshop will begin with a brief presentation providing an overview of the planning process, major themes and takeaways from the Public 
Engagement Key Themes and Takeaways Summary and the Existing Conditions Memorandum, and directions for the visioning exercise. Participants will 
then break into small groups for a mapping exercise where they will put pen to paper and work to develop their “vision” for the future of the community. 
The workshop will conclude with general agreement regarding the community’s issues and opportunities, key planning themes and principles, and projects 
and improvements that will be desirable in the future. 
4b. Vision Statement and Goals 
The Comprehensive Plan Update needs to establish an overall “vision statement” for the future of Cheyenne that can provide focus and direction with 
goals based on analysis and themes identified during community outreach. Following the community visioning workshop, the Project Team will summarize 
all feedback received during the previous steps of the planning process and prepare a vision statement for the Cheyenne Comprehensive Plan Update. 
The vision statement will be prepared using feedback from the community visioning workshop, community outreach activities, and observations garnered 
from the Existing Conditions Memorandum. As part of the task, the Project Team will develop goals to provide more specific focus and direction for 
planning recommendations.  
4c. Land Use Scenario Development (Three Scenarios) 
The Project Team will develop up to three alternative land use scenarios and work with City staff and the Steering Committee to evaluate them and select 
a preferred scenario serve as the basis for the CheyenneNext Comprehensive Plan. 
The scenarios will reflect adopted plans, existing regulations, current development patterns, and other ongoing planning studies. The Project Team will 
begin by working with City staff to identify areas of the community that may be appropriate for future growth, redevelopment, or other change. The City 
and Project Team will also identify key measures for comparing the scenarios, such as future population, number of households, and number of jobs.  
These measures will be estimated using current development trends, demographic data, GIS information, and other available datasets. The Project Team 
will map and analyze each scenario to confirm that it represents a realistic pattern of future development. 
Potential Revenue Generation Analysis  
As part of the land use scenario development, this analysis allows the City and Steering Committee to compare the scenarios based not only on 
population, households, and jobs, but also on their relative potential to generate property tax and sales tax revenue for the City. This analysis will provide 
planning-level estimates intended to compare the scenarios and support decision-making. 
Estimate of property tax revenue. The Project Team will use parcel-level information from the Laramie County Assessor to calculate the average 
assessed value per acre for different land use types. These values will then be applied to the land uses shown in each scenario to estimate the total 
assessed value of development under that scenario. The City’s mill levy will be applied to estimate the resulting property tax revenue received by the City. 
Estimate of sales tax revenue. The Project Team will estimate taxable sales for different commercial and retail land uses. These estimates will be based 
on sales-per-square-foot assumptions developed with the City’s Economic Development Director and taxable sales data from the Wyoming Department of 
Revenue. The City’s share of sales tax revenue will then be applied to estimate the revenue generated under each scenario. 
This analysis will provide planning-level estimates intended to compare the scenarios and support decision-making and will focus on potential revenue. 
The analysis will rely on available data and will inform policies addressing data center siting, infrastructure investment, residential land preservation, and 
long-term fiscal sustainability. Preparation of a detailed cost-of-services or net fiscal impact analysis is not included.

Step 5: Draft Comprehensive Plan Update 
This step will focus on preparing draft City-wide policies and recommendations for the core elements of the Comprehensive Plan Update. The draft 
Comprehensive Plan Update prepared in this step will reflect the revised Vision Statement, Goals, and Preferred Land Use Scenario  developed in 
previous steps of the planning process. 
5a. Draft Comprehensive Plan Update Elements 
We will prepare the Draft Comprehensive Plan Update using input obtained during the previous steps of the project. The draft Comprehensive Plan 
Update will be well-organized and user-friendly, highly illustrative, and visually compelling rather than being too heavy on narrative. Concise, well-written 
text will be combined with attractive and easy-to-understand maps, graphics, illustrations, and photos to effectively communicate the draft Comprehensive 
Plan Update’s policies and recommendations. The following sections will be developed as part of the draft Comprehensive Plan Update: 
Introduction and Context 
This section will include the introduction for the Comprehensive Plan Update, describing how to use it and how the Plan was created. It will also 
summarize components from the Existing Conditions Memorandum, including the regional context and study area. Additional content may include concise 
summaries of relevant past plans, studies and reports; a demographic profile snapshot; and abbreviated summaries of public engagement events along 
with key themes and takeaways. This will be a concise section meant to set the foundation for the Comprehensive Plan Update’s policies and 
recommendations. 
Vision and Goals 
This section will include the revised Vision and Goals that will serve as a framework for decision-making, resource allocation, and policy development. 
This section will inform and integrate the following Plan sections to collectively achieve the community’s vision for the future. 
Land Use and Development 
This section will include policies and recommendations for all land use areas in the City and its planning area, including residential neighborhoods, 
commercial areas, medical, open space, natural environment, and public and semi-public uses. This section will identify and address a range of land use 
topics such as desired development patterns, new growth areas, potential annexation opportunities, land use compatibility issues, commercial and mixed-
use development, and conservation areas. This section will include the Recommended Future Land Use Map (based on the Preferred Land Use 
Scenario).

Housing and Neighborhoods – Residential Areas 
This section will provide policies and recommendations for maintaining and improving Cheyenne’s established neighborhoods while supporting housing 
supply, affordability, diversity, and reinvestment. Recommendations will address infill development, residential land capacity, neighborhood compatibility, 
and a range of housing types, including missing-middle, multifamily, mixed-use, manufactured, and other attainable housing options needed to serve 
current residents and anticipated workforce and population growth. The Project Team will review the City’s current housing initiatives and recently 
completed housing-demand and growth analyses to determine whether changing conditions or updated information warrant modifications to existing 
assumptions or policy direction. Recommendations will align housing and land use policies with housing demand, affordability, workforce growth, 
infrastructure availability, and long-term residential land needs. 
The section will consider applicable federal and state housing legislation in effect during preparation of the Plan, including the 21st Century ROAD to 
Housing Act, where relevant to local land use regulations, development processes, housing production, neighborhood reinvestment, funding opportunities, 
and Comprehensive Plan implementation. 
Economic Development – Commercial and Employment Areas 
This section will focus on the City’s commercial areas providing policies and recommendations designed to strengthen employment, job creation, business 
attraction and retention, and to provide and grow a diverse and thriving tax base for the City. This section will provide high-level recommendations for the 
City’s commercial areas.   
Data Center and Large-Footprint Technology Development. Based on the Land Use Scenario analysis, this subsection will establish policies and 
considerations for data centers and other large-footprint technology uses. Recommendations will address land suitability, compatibility with residential and 
sensitive land uses, infrastructure requirements, buffering and site-design practices, and preservation of land needed for long-term residential and 
community growth. The Project Team will prepare a Technology and Residential Growth Suitability Map to evaluate the relationship between large-
footprint technology development and future residential growth. Using available data, the map will identify broad areas that may be suitable for data 
centers and similar technology uses, areas that should be prioritized or preserved for residential growth, and potential conflict or transition areas where 
technology development could consume land needed for housing or create compatibility, infrastructure, noise, traffic, or buffering concerns. 
Transportation 
Building on existing conditions analysis and community input, Mead & Hunt will lead the development of the Transportation chapter, translating findings 
into actionable goals, policies, and implementation strategies. The chapter will address multimodal connectivity, roadway function and capacity, active 
transportation improvements, and Safe Systems-based safety policies. Recommendations will support connections between neighborhoods, employment 
centers, downtown, the Greenway, F.E. Warren Air Force Base, and Cheyenne Regional Airport while addressing growth pressures from annexation, data 
centers, and regional growth trends. The chapter will include clear, user-friendly goals, policies, and action steps identifying priorities, responsible parties, 
timing, and potential funding opportunities.  
 
As part of the development of the Transportation element, the Project Team will produce an updated Recommended Greenway Master Plan Map and a 
Recommended Bicycle Master Plan Map that will supersede the 2012 plans. These will be produced as standalone documents that can either be 
included in the Transportation chapter in the final CheyenneNext Comprehensive Plan Update or appended to the plan. 
Services, Infrastructure, and Community Facilities 
This element will provide policy-level recommendations addressing public services, infrastructure, and community facilities needed to support existing 
development and future growth. 
Public Safety. Recommendations will address police, fire, emergency services, service areas, facility coordination, and growth-related service 
considerations. 
Utilities and Infrastructure. Recommendations will address water, wastewater, stormwater, utilities, outside-user connections, infrastructure capacity, 
development-related improvements, and coordination of infrastructure investment with land-use planning. 
Parks, Recreation, and Community Facilities. Recommendations will address parks, recreation, open space, community facilities, arts and cultural 
resources, historic preservation, and cemetery planning. 
Environmental Conditions. Recommendations will address relevant environmental conditions and constraints, including flood risk, resource 
conservation, and applicable policies identified through the City’s Community Climate Action Plan and other adopted plans. 
Intergovernmental and Interagency Coordination. This subsection will provide recommendations for coordination among City departments and with 
Laramie County, the Cheyenne MPO, state and federal agencies, F.E. Warren Air Force Base, the Cheyenne VA Medical Center, service providers, 
nonprofit organizations, economic-development entities, and other partner organizations.
