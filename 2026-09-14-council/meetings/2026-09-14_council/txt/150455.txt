 
 
RESOLUTION NO. __________ 
 
ENTITLED: “A RESOLUTION GRANTING SPECIAL USE APPROVAL FOR A 
GAMING CENTER PURSUANT TO UNIFIED DEVELOPMENT CODE 
SECTION 2.2.7 AND GRANTING LOCAL APPROVAL OF A SITE -
SPECIFIC APPLICATION FOR A SIMULCASTING PERMIT TO BE 
ISSUED BY THE WYOMING GAMING COMMISSION PURSUANT TO 
WYO. STAT. § 11 -25-210 FOR PROPERTY LOCATED AT 1734 
MEADOWLAND DRIVE” 
 
 WHEREAS, Bally’s Wyoming LLC, d/b/a Bally’s Cheyenne  (the “Applicant”) has 
submitted an application (the “Application”) under Section 2.2.7 of the Cheyenne Unified 
Development Code (“UDC”) for Special Use approval to establish a Gaming Center, as described 
in UDC Subsection 1.4.4.c, on property located at 1734 Meadowland Drive (the “Property”), 
which is zoned CB Community Business;  
 
 WHEREAS, pursuant to UDC Section 2.2.7.c, notice of the Application was posted, 
published, and mailed in the manner and time required by law;  
 
 WHEREAS, pursuant to UDC Section 5.1.4 and Table 5 -1, a Gaming Center is a use 
permitted after Special Use review in the zone district in which the Property is located;  
 
 WHEREAS, City staff has reviewed the Application for compliance with the review 
criteria set forth in UDC Section 2.2.7.d and the Gaming Center development standards of UDC 
Section 5.7.13, including the applicable district standard of UDC Subsection 5.7.13 .a, and 
determined that the proposed use satisfies the applicable review criteria and lot, building, 
development, and design standards of the UDC; 
 
 WHEREAS, after considering the Application, the accompanying staff report, the evidence 
and public comment in the record, and each review criterion set forth in UDC Section 2.2.7.d, the 
Governing Body finds that the record supports the approval provided in  this Resolution and 
discloses no material concern under those criteria warranting denial; 
 
 WHEREAS, the Applicant also seeks the City’s site-specific approval of its application to 
the Wyoming Gaming Commission (the “Commission”) for a simulcasting permit, including 
historic horse racing terminals, under Wyo. Stat. § 11 -25-210, and because the p roposed 
simulcasting would occur within the City’s corporate limits, the Governing Body serves as the 
“local approving authority” pursuant to Wyo. Stat. §§ 11-25-102(a)(xxiii) and 11-25-210(d)(i); 
 
WHEREAS, before this action, the Applicant filed its local-approval application, paid the 
notice-publication cost, and identified the specific location and the number of historic horse racing 
terminals proposed for that location, as required by Wyo. Stat. § 11-25-210(e); 
 
 WHEREAS, in accordance with Wyo. Stat. § 11 -25-210(f), notice of the simulcasting 
permit application was published once a week for two (2) consecutive weeks in a newspaper of 
general circulation and posted to the City’s official website, and the notice identified the date, time, 
 
 
and place of the Governing Body meeting designated for the receipt of protests, which meeting 
occurred before the Governing Body took action on the Application as required by Wyo. Stat. § 
11-25-210(f) and (g)(i); 
 
 WHEREAS, Wyo. Stat. § 11 -25-210(d)(iii) affords the Board of County Commissioners 
of Laramie County thirty (30) days to review and object to the simulcasting permit application, 
which was timely submitted to the Board and no comments or objections were received within that 
period; and 
 
 WHEREAS, having considered the Applicant's site-specific application, the accompanying 
staff report, the evidence and public comment in the record, and the requirements, standards, and 
limitations of Wyo. Stat. § 11 -25-210, and no timely objection having been received, the 
Governing Body, acting as the local approving authority, finds that the record supports granting 
the City's local approval of the Applican t's simulcasting permit application, subject to the 
reasonable conditions set forth herein, which were negotiated in good faith with the Applicant in 
accordance with Wyo. Stat. § 11-25-210(g)(iii). 
 
 NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED BY THE GOVERNING BODY OF THE CITY 
OF CHEYENNE, WYOMING, that the Application to operate a Gaming Center as a Special Use 
at 1734 Meadowland Drive is approved under UDC Section 2.2. 7, subject to the following 
conditions of Special Use approval: 
 
1. The Gaming Center shall be developed and operated in substantial compliance with the 
conceptual site plan depicted in Exhibit A which is attached hereto and incorporated 
herein; 
 
2. The Special Use approval is valid for three (3) years from the effective date of this 
Resolution, unless it terminates earlier under UDC Subsection 2.2.7.c.4 upon 
expiration of the associated Commission -issued simulcasting permit. If the permit is 
suspended, revoked, surrendered, or otherwise ceases to authorize simulcasting, 
simulcasting at the Property shall cease, and the City may enforce or suspend the 
Special Use approval pursuant to UDC Article 1.3; and 
 
3. Gaming Center shall operate only during the hours of 7:00 a.m. to 2:00a.m. 
 
 BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that, acting solely as the local approving authority under 
Wyo. Stat. § 11 -25-210, the Governing Body grants the City’s local approval of the Applicant’s 
site-specific application for a simulcasting permit to be issued by the Commission for the Property, 
provided that historic horse racing terminals may be located only within the area(s) of the permitted 
premises depicted on Exhibit B, which is attached hereto and incorporated herein; 
 
 BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the City’s local simulcasting approval does not replace 
or limit the Applicant’s independent obligation to comply with the UDC, the Cheyenne Municipal 
Code, and all other applicable law, as amended. A violation of an independent ly applicable 
requirement shall be enforced under the law establishing that requirement and, standing alone, 
shall not constitute a violation of the local simulcasting approval unless the requirement is 
 
 
expressly identified in this Resolution as a negotiated condition under Wyo. Stat. § 11 -25-
210(g)(vii)(A); 
 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the City’s local simulcasting approval takes effect 
only upon the Commission’s issuance of the associated simulcasting permit for the Property and 
remains effective for the same term as that permit, as required by Wyo. Stat. § 1 1-25-210(g)(v). 
The local simulcasting approval does not extend the term of the separate Special Use approval 
permitted under UDC Subsection 2.2.7.c.4. and simulcasting may occur only while both approvals 
and all other approvals required by law are in effect; 
 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the Governing Body may revoke the City's local 
simulcasting approval, or deny its renewal, only for good cause as limited to the grounds set forth 
in Wyo. Stat. § 11-25-210(g)(vii)(A) through (D), subject to the following: 
 
1. With respect to a breach of a reasonable condition imposed by the City or the Wyoming 
Gaming Commission, the City shall provide the permitholder written notice identifying 
the breach and a reasonable opportunity to cure. Good cause under Wyo. Stat. § 11-25-
210(g)(vii)(A) shall not exist unless the permitholder fails to take reasonable steps to 
cure the breach within ten (10) business days after receiving written notice from the 
City or the Commission, or by a later date agreed upon by the permitholder and t he 
City or the Commission, as applicable. 
 
2. Upon a showing of good cause by the permitholder, the Governing Body, together with 
the Commission, may extend the two (2) -year period for commencement of 
simulcasting for one (1) additional year as provided in Wyo. Stat. § 11 -25-
210(g)(vii)(C). 
 
3. A renewal application shall be filed and processed in accordance with Wyo. Stat. § 11-
25-210(e) and (f), including the requirement that the application be filed not later than 
ninety (90) days prior to expiration of the permit, that the City publish notice  of the 
application and hold the designated meeting for protests not later than thirty (30) days 
prior to expiration, and that the City not approve or deny the renewal before the date 
set for the designated meeting. A denial of renewal shall be for good ca use as limited 
by Wyo. Stat. § 11 -25-210(g)(vii)(A) through (D) and shall be determined in 
accordance with paragraph 4 of this section. 
 
4. Before revoking the City's local approval or denying its renewal on any ground set forth 
in Wyo. Stat. § 11 -25-210(g)(vii)(A) through (D), the City shall provide the 
permitholder written notice of the proposed action and the grounds supporting it, and 
shall issue written findings and conclusions in support of any such decision, as required 
by Wyo. Stat. § 11-25-210(g)(vi). This Resolution establishes no additional hearing or 
adjudicatory procedure for such action; the City may adopt any such procedure by 
separate ordinance. 
 
5. Nothing in this Resolution creates any additional right of administrative appeal from a 
final decision of the Governing Body. Judicial review of a decision denying renewal or 
 
 
revoking the City's local approval shall be governed by Wyo. Stat. § 11-25-210(h) and, 
as applicable, § 11-25-210(j). 
 
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED, that this Special Use approval is a land-use approval under 
the Unified Development Code and shall terminate by expiration as provided above. Any 
enforcement of the conditions of this Special Use approval shall proceed under the Uni fied 
Development Code and the Cheyenne Municipal Code. The good-cause standard of Wyo. Stat. § 
11-25-210(g)(vii) governs only the local simulcasting approval and does not enlarge or diminish 
the City's authority over the Special Use approval. 
 
 BE IT FINALLY RESOLVED, t he Mayor, City Clerk, and Director of Planning and 
Development are hereby authorized to take all actions and execute all documents reasonably 
necessary to carry out and transmit this approval, including notification to the Wyoming Gaming 
Commission.

The development of Gaming Center requires Special Use approval pursuant to UDC 5.1.4, and the

BACKGROUND: 
 
The applicant is requesting Special Use approval for a proposed Gaming Center in the CB - Community 
Business zone district. The previous use of the site is a restaurant. The new proposed use is gaming parlor that 
falls under Gaming Center by the Unified Development Code.  
 
This property previously submitted an application for Conditional Use approval through the Board of 
Adjustment. The application was submitted just prior to the Special Use code changes taking effect and was 
processed under the Conditional Use application process. The Board of Adjustment denied the application as 
the Board deemed the application, as submitted, did not meet all Conditional Use review criteria. The 
Conditional Use review criteria and Special Use criteria are similar, but not identical.

The subject property contains an existing building that was previously used as a restaurant. The 
applicant’s proposal is to keep the building while remodeling the interior to accommodate the 
gaming center use. The applicant is proposing minimal exterior changes – most of which are 
focused on maintaining the original site layout. 
 
The UDC has Gaming-Center specific regulations in UDC 5.7.13. This section requires Gaming 
Centers to be adjacent to a Collector, Minor Arterial, or Principal Arterial. The property at 1734 
Meadowland Drive has 42’ of frontage along Stillwater Avenue which is a Major Collector. The 
UDC does not require vehicular access onto the major street. This application meets this 
requirement. 
 
UDC design standards include parking and access, landscaping, lighting, signage and façade 
design. The property is expected to be able to meet all design regulations applicable to property 
re-use in the CB zone. Due to the existing nature of the site, some design requirements may not 
be able to be fully met (but this would be broadly applicable to any use proposing to reuse the 
site) – for example, the standard for new construction would be to include a sidewalk that is 
detached from the curb with a 7’ tree boulevard space, and to have the parking lot setback 6’ 
from the property line adjacent to the street. Due to how this site was previously developed, a 
detached sidewalk would remove existing mature trees, and a 6’ setback would remove an entire 
row of parking. The applicant has already submitted a formal site plan for Staff review. The 
submitted site plan has addressed the challenges presented by the re-use of the existing site and 
the applicant has proposed some improvements. 
 
The design standard requires a 10-foot landscape buffer between the CB and HR zoning districts. 
The property to the east consists of a 55-foot-wide drainage swale and is zoned both CB and HR. 
Because the CB-zone is located within the drainage swale, the required buffer would technically 
be located on the neighboring property. However, although a buffer is not required on the 
applicant’s property, the applicant has voluntarily provided a 10-foot landscape buffer on the site 
plan, consistent with typical buffer standards. The proposed buffer will provide a visual break and 
help minimize potential visual impacts associated with the proposed use.  
 
Staff’s analysis is that the proposed site plan substantially meets requirements as shown on the 
applicant site plan and does not require any special design standards. Staff recommends 
substantial compliance with the site plan submitted be a condition of approval. 
 
The proposed special use complies with review criterion 1, with conditions. 
 
2) Whether the operating characteristics of the use are complimentary to the 
character of the surrounding area in terms of hours of operation, visible and 
audible impacts, and traffic patterns caused by the use; (UDC 2.2.7.d.2) 
 
The surroundings to the north, south, and west are commercial uses that a change of use would 
have minimal impact on. There is a residential subdivision to the east of the site.  The operating 
characteristics of this use are complimentary to the surrounding area. Hours of operations are 
expected to be similar to other businesses within the area and similar to the previous use. There 
are no significant changes in traffic expected. There are no expected visible and audible impacts 
to the site.

4) Whether the specific application of the use to the site supports the general intent 
and applicability of the zoning district, and does not compromise the general 
intent and applicability of adjacent zoning districts; (UDC 2.2.7.d.4) 
 
The property is zoned CB Community Business. The CB zone is intended for Retail, Employment, 
Service and Civic uses that may serve a number of residential neighborhoods or other districts in 
meeting daily needs, discretionary needs, or other business functions. The zone is applicable to 
areas where moderate- or large-scale business uses are appropriate.  
 
The proposed Gaming Center does not compromise the general intent or applicability of the 
adjacent HR High Density Residential district. The intent of the HR District is to accommodate a 
range of higher-density urban residential lots and building types, while protecting the urban 
character of the district and maintaining the primarily residential use of land. The HR District is 
intended to support more compact neighborhood development patterns and is not intended to 
prohibit nonresidential uses on adjoining properties where appropriate land use and site design 
measures are provided. 
 
The existing drainage swale provides physical separation between the proposed use and the HR -
zoned area. The applicant is also providing a 10-foot landscape buffer, which exceeds what is 
technically required on the applicant's property and is consistent with typical buffer standards. 
The buffer will provide a visual separation between the use and the adjacent residential district 
and help minimize potential impacts to the residential character of the HR District.  
Given the existing separation, proposed landscaping, and site design, the Gaming Center will not 
compromise the intent of the HR District to protect its urban character and primarily residential 
land use. The proposed special use is therefore consistent with the general intent and applicability 
of the adjacent zoning district. 
 
The proposed special use complies with review criterion 4. 
 
5) The duration of the proposed Special Use is relevant to the applicability 
requirements of the zoning district and adjacent zoning districts. (UDC 2.2.7.d.5)

On behalf of Bally’s Wyoming, LLC d/b/a Bally’s Cheyenne, this letter provides supplemental 
information regarding the Special Use criteria set forth in UDC Section 2.2.7.  The information below is 
intended to assist in the review of the application and provide additional context regarding issues 
discussed during the Board of Adjustment proceedings.    
1. Whether the use meets applicable provisions of the UDC, including applicable development and 
design standards. In meeting these criteria, the reviewing body may consider design standards 
contained in the UDC that are not otherwise applicable in the zoning district, where the Design 
Objectives associated with such standards are relevant to the specific situation under review. 
 
The planning and development staff provided guidance and requirements during the requisite work 
session process.  The existing building complies with applicable UDC standards for setbacks, height, 
façade articulation, and materials, and no exterior modifications are proposed that would alter its 
established scale or form. The site is located within the CB zoning district, where a mix of commercial 
and service uses is intended, and surrounding properties are developed with similar uses. The existing site 
design meets UDC parking and landscape requirements and remains consistent with the character and 
functionality of the area. The proposed change in use does not alter the building footprint or overall site 
layout. The addition of a pedestrian connection to the public right-of-way enhances accessibility and 
supports UDC design objectives for connectivity without changing the established site character. 
Although the proposed used differs operationally from the prior restaurant use, the site itself remains 
substantially unchanged.  The existing building, parking layout, access points, and buffering continue to 
serve the property without expansion of the building footprint or intensity of site development.   
2. Whether the operating characteristics of the use are complimentary to the character of the 
surrounding area in terms of hours of operation, visible and audible impacts, and traffic patterns 
caused by the use. 
 
There are (3) existing operating restaurants and fast -serve facilities to the north and directly adjacent the 
applicants property. Operating hours for the fast-serve are from 7:00am to 2:00am, 7 days a week. There 
are no exterior sound system or outdoor activities planned around the proposed use. Existing exterior 
sound equipment is being removed to reduce any audible impacts to surrounding areas.  In addition, 
privacy film is being added to existing windows to reduce any visual impacts from the interior spaces to 
the surrounding areas. The parking count remains the same. The seat count for the planned operation is 
reduced from the prior use, and the bar and kitchen operations have also been consolidated. There is no 
anticipation that there will be any impacts to traffic circulation around the property. Staff concurred with 
this assessment. While no formal traffic or noise study was required during the review process, the 
proposed use includes a reduced seating count, no outdoor entertainment areas, removal of exterior sound 
equipment, and no increase in parking demand, all of which support the conclusion that impacts will 
remain compatible with surrounding uses. Accordingly, the operating characteristics of the proposed use 
are compatible with the existing commercial character of the surrounding area.   
 
3. Whether the use, compared with the suitability of the subject property for other uses permitted by 
right in the zoning district. 
 
The subject property previously operated as a high-traffic restaurant and entertainment venue, making it 
well suited for the proposed Entertainment, Amusement, Recreation - Other use within the CB zoning 
district. The CB district accommodates a mix of commercial and service uses, and the property is already 
developed with a building and site layout appropriate for this use. The existing structure, parking, and 
access configuration support the proposed use with minimal modification. Given that the site previously 
operated as a restaurant, which generally involves higher activity and traffic levels, the site is equally or 
better suited for the proposed Service Use. The property is therefore as suitable for this use as other uses 
permitted within the district. 
4. Whether the specific application of the use to the site supports the general intent and applicability 
of the zoning district, and does not compromise the general intent and applicability of adjacent 
zoning districts. 
 
The subject property’s prior use was a highly themed sports bar and restaurant entertainment venue. The 
proposed use is compatible with the location history. 
Per UDC 5.4.2, the CB District is intended for Retail, Employment, Service and Civic uses that may serve 
a number of residential neighborhoods or other districts in meeting daily needs, discretionary needs, or 
other business functions. This proposed Entertainment, Amusement, Recreation - Other use falls under 
the Service use category per UDC 1.4.4, therefore complying with the general intent of the CB zoning 
district. 
Per UDC 5.4.2, the CB District is applicable in areas where moderate or large-scale business uses are 
appropriate. The proposed Entertainment, Amusement, Recreation - Other use will be a moderately sized 
business with no added square footage to the existing vacant building. 
Per UDC 5.3.5 the adjacent HR District is intended for a range of higher density, urban residential lots 
and building types within a neighborhood. All uses require full access to public infrastructure and city 
services. The regulations are designed to protect the urban character of the district and protect the 
primarily residential use of land. The proposed Entertainment, Amusement, Recreation - Other use does 
not impact the aforementioned intent of the HR District. The residential use of land is protected and 
separated from the proposed Entertainment, Amusement, Recreation - Other use by the drainage easement 
that prevents patron overflow onto residential properties. 
Per UDC 5.3.5 the adjacent HR District is applicable to areas where more compact neighborhood 
development patterns are desired and is typically used only in areas that are no more than ¼ mile from 
Activity Centers. Activity Center is defined by the UDC as places where economic and social activity are 
focused. The existing CB district and proposed Entertainment, Amusement, Recreation - Other use 
reasonably can be labeled as an Activity Center. Thus, the proximity of the proposed Entertainment, 
Amusement, Recreation – Other use to the adjacent HR District is consistent with the development 
pattern contemplated by the UDC.   
5. The duration of the proposed Special Use relevant to the applicability requirements of the zoning 
district and adjacent zoning districts. 
 
The proposed Special Use is intended to be a long-term investment and occupancy of the property and is 
consistent with the long-range planning framework for the site and surrounding area.  The CB zoning 
district is intended to support a mix of commercial and service uses over the long term, and the proposal 
aligns with this role. The use is intended as a permanent occupancy and is comparable in longevity to 
surrounding commercial and service uses. The area is characterized by a stable mix of these uses, and the 
proposal represents a continuation of this established pattern. No significant shift in area character is 
occurring that would make the use incompatible. The change from a restaurant to a service use maintains 
the existing function of the site and is consistent with anticipated future development within the CB 
district. 
6. Whether any additional site-specific conditions associated with the application are warranted 
and sufficient to prevent any potential harm to the public health, safety or general welfare. 
 
The existing vacant building is detrimental to the community with no day-to-day property management or 
supervision. Additionally, unhoused individuals have been observed occupying areas around the vacant 
building. The proposed Entertainment, Amusement, Recreation - Other use will bring 24/7 surveillance 
and consistent building and site maintenance providing a safer condition for the community.  In addition, 
the Owner is creating an accessible pedestrian pathway from Stillwater Ave onto property to improve safe 
passage of pedestrians, as well as a new accessible ramp at the corner of Stillwater Ave. and Meadowland 
Dr. to improve safe passage of pedestrians. Lastly, the owner has added trees along the eastern property 
line to provide additional screening elements from the apartments. Collectively, the enhanced 
surveillance, ongoing site management, pedestrian improvement, ADA accessibility upgrades, and 
additional landscape screening constitute site-specific measures intended to promote public safety, 
improve compatibility with neighboring properties, and protect the general welfare.   
  
7. Whether the use is in conflict with other applicable City, State or Federal regulations. 
 
The proposed use complies with applicable City, State, and Federal regulations. 
 
8. The adequacy of infrastructure supporting the site. 
 
Existing utilities and parking support the proposed use, no added impacts to public utilities and 
infrastructure are anticipated.  Previous work sessions with staff have not resulted in requests for 
additional infrastructure other than the noted repairs. The owner is proactively implementing those repairs 
now ahead of the change-of-use approval. Existing public infrastructure is sufficient to support the 
proposed use without material upgrades or public expenditure.   
  
9. Whether the proposed use contributes demonstrable public benefits, including but not limited to 
affordable housing, additional recreational opportunities, preservation or provision of open 
space, cultural amenities, or other improvements that serve the general welfare of the 
community.  
  
As part of the proposed use, the building is being enhanced and upgraded with new paint, new roofing, 
new landscaping, repaired public walkways, replacement signage and awnings, and repairs to the building 
exterior.  The dilapidated exterior trellis is also being removed to improve the exterior aesthetic and curb
