BACKGROUND: 
 
The applicant is requesting amending the Future Land Use Map for roughly 652.67 acres of land from 
Agricultural/Rural, Open Space and Parks, and Mixed-Use Employment to Community Business to 
accommodate a zone change for future commercial campus development. 
 
The property is shown as mostly Agricultural/Rural and Open Space and Parks in the Future Land Use Plan. 
The Agricultural/Rural and Open Space and Parks category promotes single family residences on individual 
large lot, farming, ranching, and other agriculturally related activities. The Agricultural/Rural considers the any 
of the agricultural zones districts as the optimal zone district.  
 
The major street plan for the area is designed to support traffic demands based on the uses anticipated by the 
future land use designations of the subject property and surrounding properties. 
 
A concurrent application for a zoning map amendment has been submitted. The zoning map amendment will 
accompany the future land use amendment at City Council. If the application for the zoning map amendment 
is approved, the applicant will be required to submit a site plan application for the new development.

ANALYSIS OF APPLICATION CONFORMANCE WITH REVIEW CRITERIA: 
 
Should the Planning Commission wish to approve an amendment to the Future Land Use Map of the 
Comprehensive Plan, the Commission must make the following considerations outlined in the 2014 
Community Plan (Build Chapter page 3-3).  A detailed analysis is below: 
 
1) BENEFICIAL OR ADDRESSES ISSUES - The proposed amendment is beneficial to the 
community or addresses issues not foreseen during the planning process.    
 
The proposed amendment has been requested to allow BP Business Park zoning. The Land use 
category requested support small and large-scale development while providing essential 
commercial services along regional transportation routes in an industrial park setting. The district 
integrates manufacturing, process; similar development patterns of Swan Ranch, North Range 
Business Park, and Highlands Business Park. Lower intensity commercial and service uses are 
typically secondary uses and only in support of the industrial developments of the park. 
Higher‑intensity employment uses are encouraged near major roadways and residential 
development are not supported in these districts.    
 
Business Park will undergo a change in the allowable agricultural uses. The proposed end use for 
this area will need a use that allows for equestrian activities. Under current code, equestrian 
related developments are not allowed in the BP zone district. The change would permit for the full 
development of the proposed development.  
 
The proposed Future Land Use amendment largely complies with review criterion 1. 
 
2) COMPATIBLY - The proposed amendment is compatible with the surrounding area, and the 
goals and policies of the Plan.  
 
The current situation surrounding this includes Overland PUD on the subject property and to the 
south and east. Agricultural zoning bisects the two parcels. Properties to the north are across the 
railroad track and development is across the interstate. Surrounding development patterns in the 
surrounding area includes commercial development to the north and east, parcels to the south 
and west are vacant.

One use has been identified for this area. The evaluation of this land use plan amendment and the 
related zoning should be based on all the possible uses for the area. Once this property is 
changed to Community Business, it would allow for zone change to a zone district that would 
accommodate the proposed development in the area. Once this proposed development is 
established, it could lead to more commercial development in the area. If more development was 
to happen in the area, there would need to be further build out of infrastructure and 
transportation network. This would promote public welfare because it would bring an increase in 
jobs, development of water and sewer systems, and the transportation network in this area.  
 
Policies in PlanCheyenne are mixed on this proposal, but, on the balance, probably would 
encourage maintaining the current land use designation.

STAFF REVIEW: 
 
Through the review process, review comments were received expressing concerns about the 
appropriateness of the Mixed-use Employment designation versus the Community Business designation 
at this location. Staff also mentioned drainage and traffic concerns that may become necessary to address 
with the site plan. 
 
CONCLUSION: 
Although there are multiple reasons to both approve or deny the request, on the balance, Staff 
recommends approving changing the future land use designation of this area to Community Business due 
to the changing nature of land development in the area.  
 
Rationale for approving the request: 
The subject properties are adjacent to the interstate interchange to the southwest, commercial and 
industrial development to the east and south of the parcels. This area is at an optimal spot along the 
interstate corridor to develop a regional activity center for Cheyenne. While the area could be a good 
location for further industrial growth, a commercial activity center would be just as ideal. A commercial 
activity centered around a single commercial development could spur similar or support development in 
the area. With easy access to and from either surrounding interstate, a commercial campus development 
could become a regional activity center, becoming a driving force of Cheyenne economy and 
development.   
 
Rationale for denying the request: 
The area currently has no connectivity to services, including water and sewer connections. This 
commercial development has precured money for extension of services to the around. With its easy 
access to the interstate interchange would make the area more ideal for large commercial or industrial
